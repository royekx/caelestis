Opposition: the Vth'orama

N'ghathrod's lane through this campaign: what his colony is building, how it works, what it pays in copies and captives, where it stands, Cairn Station, the operation it runs against Caelestis, the Fork at graduation, and what it does in each Act. Who N'ghathrod is and how to run him is on his dossier; the colony itself, its ranks, the true-formed and the malformed, its beliefs and how the taking works are on the Vth'orama. How Vecna uses the colony is on Opposition: Vecna. How hearts open and link is on Cosmology. How the Acts fit together is on Campaign.

The plan

N'ghathrod wants a home for his people. His answer is a lattice of linked worlds: an empire that arrives without crossing wildspace, because a link takes moments where a crossing takes weeks. He works on all four parts of the plan at once.

  1. Plant the taken. Put taken people in key posts on the worlds he wants, a copy at a time, so the doors are held from inside before he arrives.
  2. Find hearts. Locate each target world's heart and its Atrium.
  3. Open them. Carry the colony's residue piece into the Atrium. Opening needs no Monolith.
  4. Seek links. Link opened hearts to each other. A link needs a Monolith on both worlds, so the colony goes where Monoliths stand.

The lattice is a body

N'ghathrod's lattice of linked worlds is a body. Linked worlds are its organs, links are its nerves, and the colonies planted on each world are its cells. He is fused into Vth'oramu the same way: the dreadnought's nervous system is his, and most of the colony lives inside him.

The lattice is the same shape as Vecna's own aim, a mind with the cosmos for a body. N'ghathrod has never heard of Vecna, the eye or the Axioms. He follows a voice he once took for an elder brain and has long since stopped questioning, and every heart he opens is a stroke in someone else's working (the eye).

How they work

Saturation

A link needs a Monolith on both worlds, and a Monolith most often answers a Zenith. The colony controls neither. What it controls is the descent: finding the Atrium and carrying the residue piece in. So it works by volume, on as many worlds as it can reach, and goes where Monoliths already stand.

Other Seekers chapters push worlds toward Zenith for their own reasons; the Fracture Cult seeks exactly that. The colony knows nothing of them, as no chapter knows of another. It follows the Monoliths their work raises without knowing who raised them, guided by the one explicit instruction the voice has given N'ghathrod: a world with a Monolith is a marked world.

Zeniths coming far more often than they should, on worlds with nothing to do with each other, are the visible symptom (Pressures). The crew can watch it for years before anyone explains it.

How they search

Slowly, by hand, and often badly. Khaleth runs the hunt for Atria and leads the descents himself, and Ulh'rek keeps the research. Archivists and scholars whose posts hold what a search needs are among the taken. Digs go down at a world's deepest reachable points, frequently in the wrong place and years ahead of any need.

The dry well on Anadia is one of these: a failed survey for the heart's Atrium, abandoned when the dig came up empty. The bodies of the four missing labourers in the chamber below the well were the labour.

The cost is tadpoles

The colony pays in two kinds of tadpole, and each runs short in its own way. N'ghathrod's copies are the scarce one: he spawns them on a slow, steady cycle, and each copy buys one post. A person costs one; something enormous, such as the dreadnought, costs many. Anyone can carry and implant one, so couriers and brine casks move constantly along the lanes. A crate of copies destroyed in transit costs him months of placements, and it is a win the crew can measure long before they can reach him.

The ordinary tadpoles are the other way round. Every illithid aboard spawns, nothing thins the broods, and the pools always hold more waiting tadpoles than the colony has bodies for (the pools). What runs short is captives, and the colony cannot fetch them: its illithids cannot walk into a port, and its taken are worth too much to spend snatching crowds.

Captives through Vocath's line

Zhauveth made the arrangement with Vocath, who already moves cargo and people at volume and asks no questions. His line supplies captives. Zhauveth supplies what the taken know: over years N'ghathrod has placed his taken as dockmasters, customs clerks and guild accountants, and from their reports she passes Vocath what serves him, which cargo moves when, which inspection is coming, where the patrols and escorts run. That knowledge is why his house prospers. Every captive the line brings goes to the pools or feeds the colony's illithids.

Vocath deals with Zhauveth, never with N'ghathrod, and has never heard of the colony she serves. He is not taken. A willing partner costs no copy, his crews follow him as he is, and a copy drifts over time; Zhauveth, whose gift is being agreed with, keeps him content. The captives change hands out on the lane near Cairn Station, every few weeks, from his hired pirates' ships to colony couriers. Vocath's side of the deal is on Vocath's crew.

Doorkeepers

Alongside the search, N'ghathrod takes the people who control access to a heart, so the doors stand open when the residue arrives. He takes people who can rise into positions of control, never crowds, and installs them as figures on worlds to prepare for when he holds the artifacts. Denying him the residue is a setback; his people stay in their seats.

Who they take

A copy goes only to someone in a post the colony can use, reached where they are, or now and then to an outcast N'ghathrod chooses to save; never to a captive. The rule, its exception and how a taken person changes over the years are on the Vth'orama. In practice the posts are the doorkeepers above; the archivists who hold what a search needs; the dockmasters, clerks and station masters whose reports buy Vocath's line; and a spelljammer the Fleet trusts. The voice also steers him toward Seekers placed close to knowledge of hearts, and to them the taking is ascension.

Where it stands

Vth'oramu, the mothership

Vth'oramu keeps to the periphery, in the dark between systems. It works as a mothership: skiffs and couriers go out from it to the worlds around it, and it stays where no one looks. It can enter a system, but a vessel that size near a world's planets would almost certainly be seen.

It crosses Cairn Station's lane now and then on its way somewhere. Strake Station's scope caught odd returns at the limit of its range, toward Cairn's stretch of lane, in Tarroway's sweeps; those returns were Vth'oramu. Cairn reports nothing.

Target worlds

N'ghathrod has a broad set of target worlds in Vth'oramu's vicinity. The list came from the voice and is his standing direction. The worlds are tracked as a count, and each world's specifics are built as the crew nears it. How far the eye has got is on Opposition: Vecna.

The residue piece

The colony carries one residue piece from world to world to open hearts. Without it, N'ghathrod must win it back or find another before he can open another heart. The piece and what losing it costs are on Opposition: Vecna.

Cairn Station

Cairn is a small Fleet watch post on its own lane, running outward from Realmspace along the arm, some weeks out. Vocath's captive line runs along that lane and the colony's couriers run it, so the colony holds Cairn with a pair.

  • The station master is taken. Long before Bellwether Crow came, Zhauveth brought one of N'ghathrod's copies to Cairn herself on a colony courier, and he was taken with it; Cairn was already the colony's when Crow arrived; Vocath's supply ships never carried a copy. He writes the reports and keeps the log, so every word that leaves Cairn is his, and nobody can read his mind.
  • Everyone else is held. One of Zhauveth's spawn, malformed and very good at holding minds, lives at Cairn out of sight and holds the rest of the crew. Its hold works only by nearness and deepens with every day spent near it; out of range it fades. So it makes no attempt on anyone who will leave, and visitors carry away nothing but a feeling that the place was a little off. It feeds from the captives that pass near Cairn.

The station master defers the crew's rotation, each of them signs a waiver and stays, and the lane reads all clear. Zhauveth touches down at Cairn now and then to make the colony's deals with Vocath's overseers, his own crew who ride with the pirates that run his captive line and pay them. After the raid on Bellwether Crow the handovers stopped for a while, and the Fleet ordered its escorts on the lane to watch for transfers between vessels (SA-005). When the handovers resumed they ran around the escort routes, which the taken pass to Vocath through Zhauveth, and the station master has reported a clean lane every week since. The Fleet looked, found nothing on Cairn's word, and let it lapse. How the station plays if the crew goes there is on its location page.

The Caelestis operation

Two operations run against Caelestis in the same window, by two parties, against the same vault, for different reasons. Joffrey is the colony's man on the station. Vocath believes Joffrey is his own informant, and his house runs its own work against Caelestis for its grudge against Mirt. This section is the colony's side; Vocath's house, its feud with Mirt and its own work are on its faction page.

The station is sealed against teleportation, so anything wanted from the Brig has to be reached on foot and carried out on a ship. Reaching it meant first understanding the station: routes, schedules, courier protocols, response patterns, which shifts overlap, where security thins, and who has clearance to know what.

Joffrey's taking

Joffrey was the junior spelljammer aboard Bellwether Crow when it was raided on the approach to Cairn. With Clint gone through the color pool and many of the crew dead, the senior helm and deck officers among them, he took the helm, saw the fight out and held the survivors together, and from then on they looked to him. Cairn took the crippled ship in, and repairs ran for days. The station master reported up to N'ghathrod, and Zhauveth saw what they had: a ship going home to Caelestis, and a spelljammer its survivors follow. A colony courier brought one of N'ghathrod's copies up the lane within days, and the station master took Joffrey with it.

The colony wanted a man inside Caelestis, and knew nothing of A.T.L.A.S.; the copy carried no directive about it. A hold cannot go home with a man; a taking can. Joffrey is also insurance: someone inside Caelestis who can warn the colony if anyone looks at Cairn again. He knows Crow was sent to look in on Cairn, and nothing of who asked. He turned over some months, not a year, and made his name on the strength of what he did aboard Crow. He keeps part of himself, and one survivor of Crow sees that he has changed; that survivor is on the Fleet page.

The run

In Joffrey's first months, A.T.L.A.S. came into the Brig, or he learned it was there, catalogued as an antique navigational instrument. N'ghathrod wants residue, and the plan for it starts there. The Brig's location and access protocols are classified, and Joffrey has no access to them.

Three insiders work against Caelestis, with Joffrey at the hub. He tracks the shipments that carry Vocath's seal and handles what comes in, and he paid Jeffrey, the Sky Dock hand, to pass the sealed crate the cadets later found. Jeffrey has no idea what his friend is. Mr. Blip is uninvolved: he does not inspect every cargo, and he never knew the sealed crate came in past him. Miken sabotages: Joffrey left the rod where he would pick it up. A third insider, the thief, steals, and Joffrey uses the thief to learn where things are kept and the Brig's protocols. Miken and the thief each know only that someone else is involved, and nothing either of them knows points at Joffrey. The Academy thefts were already running, small, before Joffrey was placed, and they have grown since; with the bribed dock hand they made the inventory turbulence that let odd handling look normal. Sor'Kur's access record shows the thief working closer to the Brig over months; nothing in it ties the route to Joffrey.

In name Joffrey answers to Qitru, Vocath's intermediary. In fact N'ghathrod runs him, through Zhauveth. Zhauveth told Vocath he has an informant inside Caelestis, and Vocath uses it for his grudge against Mirt. Joffrey's reports to Qitru have never contained a lie. He keeps one thing from her: that a specific instrument is in the Brig, and that it is the whole point.

Vocath could never have found the Brig himself. Joffrey found it through the thief and passed it to Qitru, so the strike happens because the informant named a target Vocath already wanted. By graduation Joffrey holds the physical map of how to reach the Brig and how to leave the station afterward. Vocath's raid and the surface attack draw security across the ceremony and the docks, and Joffrey runs.

Two thefts, one vault

Vocath's raiders are robbing the Brig, and they mean it. They take what a merchant's crew would take, instruments, restricted supplies, anything catalogued and portable, and what leaves with them is real loss to the Academy and real leverage later. Joffrey is in the same vault for one object. The raiders are unaware of him, and he has no interest in what they carry out.

The Fork

During the graduation attack, with security drawn off by the surface attack, Joffrey runs the Brig. He reaches the vault and takes A.T.L.A.S., and he can leave only through the Sky Dock chaos, because the seal keeps him from teleporting out. The Brig itself is dangerous, with wards, constructs and automated responses, and he comes out haggard, wounded and half broken, having burned every contingency to get that far.

What the body shows

If he is captured or killed: chitin beneath the skin along the spine and the back of the skull, thin and recent, and the jaw and hard palate wrong underneath. The brain is intact, which makes no sense to anyone who knows what illithids do to a host. His mind resists reading. He says one word: Oramu.

The crew is the variable, and all three outcomes are playable.

State A: Joffrey escapes

The crew arrives too late, fails to intercept, or loses the fight. Joffrey makes the Sky Dock and gets onto a ship, and A.T.L.A.S. leaves Caelestis. The Academy knows something major happened, and knows neither what was taken nor where it went.

Act 2 is a chase. The crew follows the device across wildspace and reaches sites after the colony has been there, readings already taken, Atria already found, worlds already opened. They are behind, and they feel it. Mirt's standing takes a real hit, because the Brig was breached on his watch during his own crew's graduation, and Vocath gets what he wanted.

State B1: the crew returns it

The crew intercepts during the escape. Joffrey is already badly wounded and breaks under pressure. They take A.T.L.A.S. and hand it back, and the Academy learns the device is a target.

Act 2 is a contest with institutional backing. The crew can ask to study the device later; the Academy opens its own quiet investigation and brings them in only if they press for it. Caelestis owes them, Mirt's standing rises, and the Brig's curators start asking why anyone would go through that much for one instrument.

Alive or dead matters. A living Joffrey is a thread into protocols, contacts and recognition codes, and an institutional crisis: a decorated officer whose mind resists reading. A dead one raises the same questions with none of the answers.

State B2: the crew keeps it

Same fight, different decision. They take A.T.L.A.S. and hold on to it, because Caelestis cannot keep it safe or because they fought for it. It is institutional theft either way. If the Academy finds out it becomes a relationship problem; if it does not, the crew is sitting on a secret of its own.

Act 2 is the slow decode. Voyage by voyage they learn the device, each world they take it to feeds the pattern, and late in Act 2 or in Act 3 they see enough to recognise what it is reading. They also become the target: later encounters with the colony may be about recovering it, and N'ghathrod does not let go.

Either way the direction holds

The colony's work goes on regardless, slower without the device and faster with it. State A speeds the colony up; B1 and B2 hold it where it was. The crew bends the tempo, and the direction stays. If the heist runs while the crew is elsewhere entirely, saving a wing or fighting Vocath's raiders, it resolves as State A, and they learn later that something was taken from a vault cadets were never meant to know about.

The race

A pattern to thread through Act 2, never a sequence to follow. When the crew holds A.T.L.A.S., its readings make a shared geography: the crew sees adventure at each indicated world, and the colony sees Atria. Both reach for the same sites for different reasons, and both are under time pressure.

By Act

The colony's column of the campaign, Act by Act. Each cell is embedded under its Act on Campaign.

Act 1 · levels 1 to 5

The colony's Act 1 is Joffrey. His reconnaissance runs under cover of the Academy thefts, and at graduation he runs the Brig for A.T.L.A.S.; the outcome is the Fork, and it sets Act 2's tempo. The Brig heist thread comes forward before graduation, so the crew can engage with it and may learn that Joffrey is the one who runs the Brig.

Out in the field, by the time the crew graduates, the colony's landed operations have begun to ripple outward at contract scale: taken people in small positions, odd disappearances, a station or crew acting strangely. These are the first things the crew meets as Driftwardens.

Act 2 · levels 6 to 10 or 11

The crew works contracts close to N'ghathrod and the taken, never him. They clash with the crews of skiffs sent out from Vth'oramu, and they find taken people where contracts take them. Takings surface as mundane missions: a harbourmaster who went missing for a week and came back fine but wrong, a magistrate who never takes off their gloves, three officials who all rose to their posts the same year.

One planned mission turns on linked worlds bleeding: strange things on two worlds at once, and the crew sent to investigate. Astra Virex can show up mid-merge.

The ship itself belongs to Acts 3 and 4. Word of Vth'oramu may reach the crew late in Act 2, if play leads there: a skiff's log, a taken prisoner, a chart that calls the dreadnought wildlife.

Act 3 · levels 11 to 15

Vth'oramu becomes a place the crew can find and enter: a two-part megadungeon that is also somebody's street, the hull first and then the colony going about its day. Run the colony as a civilization (the Vth'orama, DM notes): any plan to destroy the ship or end N'ghathrod weighs the cost to the families aboard. The enna become distinguishable from one another, and killing one is understood to cost a function as well as a life.

Once N'ghathrod grows bolder, Ilsevaine Corr faces Vth'oramu at the edge of her hidden polity.

Act 4 · level 15 on

N'ghathrod is still the main villain, and stopping him stops the illithid invasion. It does not stop Vecna's eye, a separate plan Vecna strings to the invasion; once the crew presses N'ghathrod, he stops following the voice's list, takes the worlds he can get, and the two plans diverge. Vecna stands behind him and beyond the crew's direct reach. His true body lies fused into Vth'oramu's hull, and the Aspect and the shell are built to make a fight look won before it is (his dossier).

If he falls

If N'ghathrod's true body dies, the taken outlive him. Each keeps the part of him it carries, and loses a part of itself. His lieutenants are closest to him and each rung down the colony is further away, and given enough time one of them could grow toward becoming another N'ghathrod.

Killing him has repercussions later, possibly in another campaign. A victory over him is never undone by fiat. What Vecna does after it comes from his contingencies, the lieutenants and the lesser taken first.

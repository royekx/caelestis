Opposition: the Vth'orama

N'ghathrod's lane through this campaign: what his colony is building, how it works, where it stands, the operation it ran against Caelestis, the Fork at graduation, and what it does in each arc. Who N'ghathrod is and how to run him is on his dossier; the colony itself, its ranks, its beliefs and the taking are on the Vth'orama. How Vecna uses the colony is on Opposition: Vecna. How hearts open and link is on Cosmology. How the arcs fit together is on Campaign.

The plan

N'ghathrod wants a home for his people. His answer is a lattice of linked worlds: an empire that arrives without crossing wildspace, because a link takes moments where a crossing takes weeks. He works on all four parts of the plan at once.

  1. Plant the taken. Put taken people in key positions on the worlds he wants, so the doors are held from inside before he arrives.
  2. Find hearts. Locate each target world's heart and its Atrium.
  3. Open them. Carry the colony's residue piece into the Atrium. Opening needs no Monolith.
  4. Seek links. Link opened hearts to each other. A link needs a Monolith on both worlds, so the colony goes where Monoliths stand.

The lattice is a body

N'ghathrod's lattice of linked worlds is a body. Linked worlds are its organs, links are its nerves, and the colonies planted on each world are its cells. He is fused into Vth'oramu the same way: the dreadnought's nervous system is his, and most of the colony lives inside him.

The lattice is the same shape as Vecna's own aim, a mind with the cosmos for a body. N'ghathrod has never heard of Vecna, the eye or the Axioms. He believes he answers to an elder brain, and every heart he opens is a stroke in someone else's working (the eye).

How they work

Saturation

A link needs a Monolith on both worlds, and a Monolith most often answers a Zenith. The colony controls neither. What it controls is the descent: finding the Atrium and carrying the residue piece in. So it works by volume, on as many worlds as it can reach, and goes where Monoliths already stand.

Other Seekers chapters push worlds toward Zenith for their own reasons; the Fracture Cult seeks exactly that. The colony knows nothing of them, as no chapter knows of another. It follows the Monoliths their work raises without knowing who raised them, guided by the one instruction that reached N'ghathrod secondhand: a world with a Monolith is a marked world.

Zeniths coming far more often than they should, on worlds with nothing to do with each other, are the visible symptom (Pressures). The crew can watch it for years before anyone explains it.

How they search

Slowly, by hand, and often badly. Khaleth runs the hunt for Atria and leads the descents himself, and Ulh'rek keeps the research. Scholars and archivists are taken for what they know. Digs go down at a world's deepest reachable points, frequently in the wrong place and years ahead of any need.

The dry well on Anadia is one of these: a failed survey for the heart's Atrium, abandoned when the dig came up empty. The bodies of the four missing labourers in the chamber below the well were the labour.

The cost is tadpoles

Everything the colony does is paid for in tadpoles, and N'ghathrod grows them on a slow cycle. A person costs one; something enormous costs many. Anyone can carry and implant one, so couriers and brine casks move constantly. A crate destroyed in transit costs him months of placements, and it is a win the crew can measure long before they can reach him.

Doorkeepers

Alongside the search, N'ghathrod takes the people who control access to a heart, so the doors stand open when the residue arrives. He takes people who can rise into positions of control, never crowds, and installs them as figures on worlds to prepare for when he holds the artifacts. Denying him the residue is a setback; his people stay in their seats.

Who they take

He takes people of position. The outcast and the downtrodden, offered belonging, form the colony's body. He is also steered toward Seekers chapter members who got close to knowledge of hearts, and his taking them is their ascension. How a taken person changes over the years is on the Vth'orama.

Where it stands

Vth'oramu, the mothership

Vth'oramu is hunkered down on the periphery, in the dark between systems. It works as a mothership: skiffs go out from it to the worlds in that vicinity, and it stays where no one looks. A year ago it sat for days on Cairn Station's bearing, and it has moved since.

Target worlds

N'ghathrod has a broad set of target worlds in Vth'oramu's vicinity. They are tracked as a count, and each world's specifics are built as the crew nears it. The hearts that serve the eye are counted on Opposition: Vecna.

The residue piece

The colony carries one residue piece from world to world to open hearts. Without it, N'ghathrod must win it back or find another before he can open another heart. The piece and what losing it costs are on Opposition: Vecna.

The Caelestis operation

Two operations run against Caelestis in the same window, by two parties, against the same vault, for different reasons. Joffrey is the colony's only operative on the station. Everything else moving against Caelestis is Vocath's, and Vocath believes Joffrey is his. This section is the colony's side; Vocath's house, its feud with Mirt and its own work are on its faction page.

The station is sealed against teleportation, so anything wanted from the Brig has to be reached on foot and carried out on a ship. Reaching it meant first understanding the station: routes, schedules, courier protocols, response patterns, which shifts overlap, where security thins, and who has clearance to know what.

Joffrey's taking

Joffrey was taken in the second boarding of Bellwether Crow, by a tadpole of N'ghathrod's with its directive already taught to it. The first boarding was Vocath's crew, after the device; Clint left with it in one of their craft and drew some of them off, and the crew fended off the rest. The colony was shadowing the ambush as Vocath's backup under the arrangement, and with the device gone, a second attack for it made no sense. Its task became to take someone instead, and it took Joffrey, which put an agent inside Caelestis. The survivors were let live so that there would be a story with him as its hero, and he was decorated on their unanimous account. He was himself for months afterward and finished converting only recently. One clean, confused survivor noticed him change and never reported it; that survivor is on the Fleet page.

The arrangement

Zhauveth arranged it. Vocath's line supplies captives, and in exchange Vocath has an asset inside Caelestis, a place he had spent years failing to reach. The price was a Caelestis crew, taken in the Crow ambush. Vocath took it for ordinary thrall work and asked no further questions. The arrangement itself is on Vocath's crew.

Joffrey reports to Vocath as agreed, and his reports have never contained a lie. Every report also passes through Ith'varn. He withholds one thing: that a specific instrument is in the Brig and that it is the whole point.

The run

The Brig's location and access protocols are classified, so surface reconnaissance was not enough. Joffrey planted tracking material on a known artifact-bound shipment and watched where it went. Vocath's thefts and the bribed dock hand made the inventory turbulence that let odd handling look normal. Sor'Kur's access records are what make his route legible to anyone who follows it.

Vocath could never have found that vault himself. Joffrey found it and told him it was there, so the strike happens because the asset named a target Vocath already wanted. Jeffrey, the Sky Dock hand Vocath bribed, has no idea what his friend is, and Mr. Blip is uninvolved.

By graduation Joffrey holds the physical map of how to reach the Brig and how to leave the station afterward. Vocath's raid and the surface attack draw security across the ceremony and the docks, and Joffrey runs.

Two thefts, one vault

Vocath's raiders are robbing the Brig, and they mean it. They take what a merchant's crew would take, instruments, restricted supplies, anything catalogued and portable, and what leaves with them is real loss to the Academy and real leverage later. Joffrey is in the same vault for one object. The raiders are unaware of him, and he has no interest in what they carry out.

The Fork

During the graduation attack, with security drawn off by the surface attack, Joffrey runs the Brig. He reaches the vault and takes A.T.L.A.S., and he can leave only through the Sky Dock chaos, because the seal keeps him from teleporting out. The Brig itself is dangerous, with wards, constructs and automated responses, and he comes out haggard, wounded and half broken, having burned every contingency to get that far.

What the body shows

If he is captured or killed: chitin beneath the skin along the spine and the back of the skull, thin and recent, and the jaw and hard palate wrong underneath. The brain is intact, which makes no sense to anyone who knows what illithids do to a host. His mind resists reading. He says one word: Oramu.

The crew is the variable, and all three outcomes are playable.

State A: the thief escapes

The crew arrives too late, fails to intercept, or loses the fight. Joffrey makes the Sky Dock and gets onto a ship, and A.T.L.A.S. leaves Caelestis. The Academy knows something major happened, and knows neither what was taken nor where it went.

Arc 2 is a chase. The crew follows the device across wildspace and reaches sites after the colony has been there, readings already taken, Atria already found, worlds already opened. They are behind, and they feel it. Mirt's standing takes a real hit, because the Brig was breached on his watch during his own crew's graduation, and Vocath gets what he wanted.

State B1: the crew returns it

The crew intercepts during the escape. Joffrey is already badly wounded and breaks under pressure. They take A.T.L.A.S. and hand it back, and the Academy learns the device is a target.

Arc 2 is a contest with institutional backing. The crew can ask to study the device later; the Academy opens its own quiet investigation and brings them in only if they press for it. Caelestis owes them, Mirt's standing rises, and the Brig's curators start asking why anyone would go through that much for one instrument.

Alive or dead matters. A living Joffrey is a thread into protocols, contacts and recognition codes, and an institutional crisis: a decorated officer whose mind resists reading. A dead one raises the same questions with none of the answers.

State B2: the crew keeps it

Same fight, different decision. They take A.T.L.A.S. and hold on to it, because Caelestis cannot keep it safe or because they fought for it. It is institutional theft either way. If the Academy finds out it becomes a relationship problem; if it does not, the crew is sitting on a secret of its own.

Arc 2 is the slow decode. Voyage by voyage they learn the device, each world they take it to feeds the pattern, and late in Arc 2 or in Arc 3 they see enough to recognise what it is reading. They also become the target: later encounters with the colony may be about recovering it, and N'ghathrod does not let go.

Either way the direction holds

The colony's work goes on regardless, slower without the device and faster with it. State A speeds the colony up; B1 and B2 hold it where it was. The crew bends the tempo, and the direction stays. If the heist runs while the crew is elsewhere entirely, saving a wing or fighting Vocath's raiders, it resolves as State A, and they learn later that something was taken from a vault cadets were never meant to know about.

The race

A pattern to thread through Arc 2, never a sequence to follow. When the crew holds A.T.L.A.S., its readings make a shared geography: the crew sees adventure at each indicated world, and the colony sees Atria. Both reach for the same sites for different reasons, and both are under time pressure.

By arc

The colony's column of the campaign, arc by arc. Each cell is embedded under its arc on Campaign.

Arc 1 · levels 1 to 4

The colony's Arc 1 is Joffrey. His reconnaissance runs under Vocath's thefts, and at graduation he runs the Brig for A.T.L.A.S.; the outcome is the Fork, and it sets Arc 2's tempo. The Brig thief thread comes forward before graduation, so the crew can engage with it and may learn who the thief is.

Out in the field, by the time the crew graduates, the colony's landed operations have begun to ripple outward at contract scale: taken people in small positions, odd disappearances, a station or crew acting strangely. These are the first things the crew meets as Driftwardens.

Arc 2 · levels 5 to 10

The crew works contracts close to N'ghathrod and the taken, never him. They clash with the crews of skiffs sent out from Vth'oramu, and they find taken people where contracts take them. Takings surface as mundane missions: a missing person who comes back fine but wrong, someone who never takes off their gloves, three officials who all arrived the same year.

One planned mission turns on linked worlds bleeding: strange things on two worlds at once, and the crew sent to investigate. Astra Virex can show up mid-merge.

The ship itself belongs to Arcs 3 and 4. Word of Vth'oramu may reach the crew late in Arc 2, if play leads there: a skiff's log, a taken prisoner, a chart that calls the dreadnought wildlife.

Arc 3 · levels 11 to 15

Vth'oramu becomes a place the crew can find and enter: a two-part megadungeon that is also somebody's street, the hull first and then the colony going about its day. The enna become distinguishable from one another, and killing one is understood to cost a function as well as a life.

Once N'ghathrod grows bolder, Ilsevaine Corr faces Vth'oramu at the edge of her hidden polity.

Arc 4 · level 15 on

N'ghathrod is still the main villain. Stopping him is how the casting of Vecna's eye is stopped, because his colony opens and links the hearts the eye is drawn in. Vecna stands behind him and beyond the crew's direct reach. His true body lies fused into Vth'oramu's hull, and the Aspect and the shell are built to make a fight look won before it is (his dossier).

If he falls

If N'ghathrod's true body dies, the taken do not die. Part of him lives on in each of them, and they lose a part of themselves. His lieutenants are closest to him and each rung down the colony is further away, and given enough time one of them could grow toward becoming another N'ghathrod.

Killing him has repercussions later, possibly in another campaign. A victory over him is never undone by fiat. What Vecna does after it comes from his contingencies, the lieutenants and the lesser taken first.

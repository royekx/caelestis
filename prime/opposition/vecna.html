Opposition: Vecna

Vecna's lane through this campaign: the eye he is drawing across the Erevos Galaxy, how far it has got, the vessels and contingencies he works through, what the crew can learn and in what order, and what happens if no one stops him. His godhood is complete; the climb he is making now is the Axiomic Ascension, into Continuity itself. Who he is, what he wants and how to run him are on his dossier. How hearts open and link, how a core wakes and what Rykoe is are on Cosmology. What the colony is doing day to day is on Opposition: the Vth'orama, and how the arcs fit together is on Campaign.

The eye

A galaxy's core can be woken by more than one method. Vecna, an archmage older than his godhood, does it with spellwork: an inscription, a figure drawn in linked hearts across the Erevos Galaxy. GAL-01's inscription is an eye. It is the left eye Vecna lost, remade across a galaxy, an eye that would see everything.

Fifteen The number of strokes follows the figure: fifteen for GAL-01, ten on the lids and five in the iris. It is not a rule of the cosmos.

The inscription plane

The eye is drawn on the inscription plane: the plane through Oerth, square to the axis that runs from Oerth through Erevos. Erevos is the eye's centre, its pupil. The image is a straight projection along that axis, with no perspective. A heart's place in the eye is its offset from the axis, and its distance along the axis does not count, so a heart near Oerth and a heart beyond Erevos can sit side by side in the figure. Every heart in the figure lies on Erevos's side of the plane, in front of Oerth.

The eye forms through the links, not through light. Nothing between a heart and Oerth blocks it, and no observer's eyes see it: it forms in the sky of Oerth's heart, where every link in the figure runs (below). Greyspace lies high above the plane of the galaxy, so the axis from Oerth to Erevos slopes about 13 degrees to the galaxy's plane. On the plane the eye spans about seven tenths of the galaxy's width, and its height is a little over a third of its width.

Strokes and marks

A stroke is everything that lands on one point of the eye: a line through the galaxy parallel to the axis, with a margin of width around it. Any world on or near a stroke can carry that point. A mark is the system actually chosen on a stroke. Vecna chooses marks for practical reasons: a Monolith stands there, the heart has been found, a Zenith is coming. Marks sit at different distances and heights along their strokes, so on a chart drawn from above they zigzag across the galaxy and read at most as a jagged, stretched ring, never as the eye. Only on the inscription plane do they form the eye.

A mark is a whole system. Every planet in it must have its heart found, opened and linked, and each link needs a Monolith on that world. A Monolith answers a Zenith, and a Zenith comes only to a world with life, so every planet in a mark must carry enough life to drive one. A destroyed planet rules its system out unless a Monolith answered its destruction, as at Nowhere. A moon is part of its planet and shares its heart, and an asteroid belt is not a planet. Vecna therefore favours systems with few planets. Far fewer systems qualify than the galaxy holds, and a mark takes years of work. Inside a mark, the system's hearts link to each other, and one of them links back to Oerth. Any heart in a mark can also link out to a heart in another system.

The plan can change: a lost mark is replaced by another system on the same stroke.

The links trace the drawing

Each mark links to the two marks beside it in the figure, so the ten lid marks form one ring and the five iris marks another, and each mark links once to Oerth. The two rings meet only through Oerth. Spare links sit on top as backups and cover. The links form one network: every heart in it reaches every other, node by node, and anyone travelling it passes through the hearts between.

Vecna gives N'ghathrod no picture of the figure, only worlds to go to. He can nudge Zeniths but not force them, so they go off out of order, in systems with no connection to each other, and N'ghathrod jumps from one to the next as they come. His own charting of those jumps zigzags across the galaxy. Vecna also has him open and link hearts the figure does not need, as backups and to muddy the pattern. N'ghathrod goes where he is told and cannot tell the needed hearts from the rest.

Oerth's heart, the retina

Oerth carries no point of the eye; the inscription plane passes through it. Oerth's heart is Vecna's seat and the retina of the eye. His ascension charged him into it, and polarity pushes him back from every other heart, so he cannot go to the hearts he needs; he makes them report back to the one he holds. Every link in the figure runs back to Oerth's heart, so its sky is full of the marked worlds bleeding through, and together they form the eye, with gaps where marks are not yet linked. Vecna watches the eye form there.

Oerth's heart is the most valuable thing Vecna has and the most vulnerable. Breaking it would cut every link in the figure at once. Oerth's Monolith never faded: his ascension changed it into the anchor between Oerth's heart and Cavitius, his divine realm, and his realm holds it standing.

N'ghathrod uses Oerth's heart as the hub of his network, moving small teams between linked hearts through it; Vth'oramu stays out in the drift. Somewhere in the long plan he stopped asking whether the voice he follows is his elder brain. It showed him enough that he no longer cared, and he chose to trust it.

Nowhere, a lower-lid mark

Nowhere is one of the marks on the lower lid. Vecna chose it because the wreck's heart lies exposed.

Holy symbol and the sky

The eye in the palm of a left hand remains Vecna's holy symbol on worlds. Only the eye is written across the sky. The chapters that read his symbol's eye as sight and revelation are closest to the truth.

Circle of Fifths

Vecna calls his working the Circle of Fifths, after the circle of fifths in music: five galaxies, each a fifth of what he needs, around Rykoe. The eye is GAL-01's fifth. The name is his alone.

What opens when Rykoe opens

Once Rykoe opens, every linked heart becomes a door: through its Atrium to the core, and through the core to the center. Vecna's vessels go that way.

Who knows

Only Vecna knows what he is building; the Seekers and the Order of All do not. The Order's Watchers flag some chapters' work as a danger to a world's Zenith and track it, without knowing who stands behind it. The gods know what he did on Oerth, and none knows what he is building now. A few people on the fringes sense that something is wrong in the cosmos, but they cannot say what it is or what to call it, and no one believes them. The fringe sage of Ascher Mir tracks the Seekers' pattern across chapters; the sage is a separate figure from the rogue Watcher Aurel Venn.

Where the working stands

GAL-01

The eye is well along. Most of its marks are complete or close, and the colony is near the end of the work. Two neighbouring systems on the upper lid are marks whose hearts are opened and linked, to each other and to Oerth; one of the two is a little-charted planet the crew learns of late in the campaign. Nowhere, on the lower lid, is a mark as well.

One measure Progress is one measure the DM sets as the story needs, starting at about two-thirds of the figure whole. Individual marks are named only when the crew draws near the truth; the two upper-lid systems and Nowhere stay as the known marks. Hints come first, through the illithids and through worlds where something is plainly wrong.

The colony's residue piece

Vecna's side holds one residue piece. The colony carries it from world to world to open hearts. If the crew ever takes it, N'ghathrod must win it back or find another before he can open another heart. Seekers chapters hunt for more (below). Pieces stay scarce: the crew may never gather a full fragment, and every piece is a race. The rest of the scattered pieces are tracked on Artifacts.

The other galaxies

In each of the other four galaxies, another being works its own core toward the same end. GAL-02's core, in the eldritch galaxy, is awake. GAL-03, GAL-04 and GAL-05 are partway, and what happens in GAL-01 decides them.

An awake core presses on its neighbours, and GAL-02's presses on GAL-01's. Late in the campaign it can pull the crew into the eldritch galaxy, where the rules become Call of Cthulhu. What they do there is get home, or close what is open.

His vessels in this campaign

Vecna cannot stand on a world (his ascension, polarity), so he works through vessels. Most of those who do his work know him by another name, and N'ghathrod does not know him at all.

N'ghathrod and the colony

N'ghathrod is the main villain of the campaign, and to Vecna he is a means to an end. Vecna needs him to open and link hearts so that Vecna's vessels can pass through Atria. Vecna reaches him by whisper, and the whispers are suggestions. Only one instruction has ever reached him, and it came secondhand, in the voice N'ghathrod takes for his elder brain: “a world with a Monolith is a marked world.” How the whispers began, and how he took Vth'oramu, are on their dossiers.

His colony, the Vth'orama, is a chapter of the Seekers: the Seekers of a New Home. They serve the same hidden god as every other chapter, believing they answer to an elder brain, and Vecna feeds them the few secrets they need. N'ghathrod is working now on all four parts of his plan: planting the taken in key positions, finding hearts, opening them, and seeking links. Where Vth'oramu waits, the colony's target worlds and its operations are on Opposition: the Vth'orama.

The Seekers chapters

The Seekers are Vecna's faith, and he steers their chapters through residue. He lets each chapter believe that finding these artifacts will lead it to what it seeks, or that the artifacts are what it seeks. He needs them hungry for something, and their seeking ripples outward through the worlds they work on.

Contingencies, in order

Vecna plans in layers. His routes to the end he wants, from the surest to the last resort:

  1. The five lieutenants. Getting N'ghathrod's five lieutenants through as his vessels would make it certain.
  2. The lesser taken. Many lesser taken, grown over time through the colony's spread, could still get him there. The taken outlive N'ghathrod, each keeping part of him.
  3. The Restorers' aims. The Restorers are trying to reach a heart, and through it the source they posit, where the All-Father holds everything taken. They have never confirmed it exists; it is the Core Monolith. They compete for residue pieces and, without knowing it, advance his plan. Avenues like theirs can bring Vecna close again, or only delay him.
  4. Tharizdun. Vecna uses Tharizdun as a contingency. It is use, not alliance, and they distrust each other. Her weakening of reality on Fonains serves his ends, and he lets it run toward the Day of Chaos, when the Princes of Elemental Evil break the barriers so she can leave her prison. She is his battering ram against the barriers, and the Day of Chaos is part of that use. Vecna plans his own way out before she gets out.

Defeating N'ghathrod does not end the threat.

Earned victories A victory over N'ghathrod is never undone by fiat. What comes after it comes from the contingencies.

The clock

The eye's moment has no date until the crew takes up the inscription. When they begin to work against it, the DM sets the date, and the timeline counts from there. Until then the clock stands still, and the campaign never fails behind the players' backs for something they missed. The quickening the Watchers record in the count of cycles is atmosphere until then, not a deadline (the count of cycles).

What the crew can learn, in order
  1. One world in Greyspace. The first thing anyone can notice is that the linked hearts, traced far enough, all lead back to the same world, in Greyspace.
  2. Why Oerth. Why they all lead to Oerth is the question that opens the rest.
  3. The eye. A top-down chart of the marks looks haphazard, but the marks share one alignment: projected along the axis from Oerth through Erevos, onto the inscription plane, they form Vecna's eye.
  4. A figure from lore. The name Vecna surfaces late, through lore: a figure missing his left eye and his left hand. Most of his worshippers know him by other names.
  5. The Radiant Citadel. Vecna (Lich), the mortal form Oerth's Monolith archived when Vecna ascended and, like every Monolith, relayed to the Core Monolith, sits in the Radiant Citadel. He has had centuries to conclude he was wrong, and he is calm, almost redeemed. Meeting him unsettles everything the crew thinks they know.

Before any rung, the crew can notice a symptom without a source. Over the past century Zeniths have come more often, and Monoliths with them, and the Watchers have seen it. Some of the rise is Vecna's doing and some is simply worlds reaching their moments; the strain it puts on the Axioms, the Axiom strain, is a byproduct of his work.

Beside the ladder runs a thread of the Seekers' own. Each chapter's name is in its own people's language, and every one carries the word for "seekers". A crew that translates enough chapter names, by magic or by study, will notice the pattern.

By arc

Vecna's column of the campaign, arc by arc, with each arc's level range. How the arcs fit together is on Campaign, where each cell is embedded under its arc.

Arc 1 · levels 1 to 4

Vecna reaches Arc 1 only through his vessels; what the colony does in Arc 1 is on Opposition: the Vth'orama. The crew meets its first Seekers chapter well after graduation.

Arc 2 · levels 5 to 10

The crew works contracts out of Ondrel Anchorage, and many of them quietly lead back to a Seekers chapter, some knowingly nefarious and some unwitting. Chapters are met one at a time, each on its own world. The first is a benevolent church that shows Vecna's hand and eye openly: good and docile, with something a little odd about it, not evil but easy to misread. The crew first hears of it through Hessa Dun, a third-intake cadet met in Arc 1, whose uncle collects things and likes visitors.

What Vecna gains in Arc 2 is the rise: Zeniths come visibly more often across the crew's contracts, and Monoliths with them, which is what his vessels need to link hearts. The colony's work in the field is on Opposition: the Vth'orama.

The symbol The players will recognize the hand and eye and expect villainy; the characters know nothing of it. The church's goodness should sit wrong with the players without giving them anything to act on.

Arc 3 · levels 11 to 15

The Radiant Citadel and the Core Monolith become destinations late in Arc 3 at the earliest. The crew reaches them by a long voyage or through a linked heart, and Vecna (Lich) waits in the Citadel.

Arc 4 · level 15 on

The crew need not fight Vecna to stop his working; how N'ghathrod's fall stops the casting is on Opposition: the Vth'orama. The Citadel and the Core Monolith remain in reach if the crew has not got there in Arc 3. A confrontation could come with a part of Vecna manifested in the First Heart. A victory over N'ghathrod sends Vecna to his contingencies. If the eye opens, the campaign has reached its failure state.

Failure state

The failure state is GAL-01's core waking for Vecna. It sets the fate of the other galaxies. If GAL-01 falls, GAL-03, GAL-04 and GAL-05 fall with it, and with GAL-02's core already awake, Rykoe can open. Then every linked heart becomes a door to the center (above), and Vecna's vessels go through toward Continuity.

Success in GAL-01 holds the other galaxies, though work remains.

DM notes

The eye at the table

No single clue solves the eye. The crew needs several pieces: where the hearts are; the route N'ghathrod charted, his zigzag of jumps from Zenith to Zenith; the traffic between a world and Oerth; and the knowledge that Vecna and Oerth are behind it, with Oerth as Vecna's vault. A Monolith only ever stands on a living world, and that is a clue in itself. Getting inside Oerth's heart is the exception: a Mission Impossible job that yields a large amount at once, because the eye shows in its sky. This is late Arc 3 or Arc 4 material, so plant seeds earlier.

The crew's road to Oerth's heart runs through the linked hearts, not across the drift. A linked heart the figure does not need can be their way in, while flying to Oerth risks Vth'oramu waiting there. This is Arc 4 material.

The Rykoe Cosmos shows the eye. Line it up from Oerth, in its 3D view, looks straight along the axis with no perspective and the marks form the eye; Inscription plane lays the eye flat, as it would go on the table. Progress is EYE_PROGRESS in prime/chart/galaxy.js, about two-thirds, and the Chart dims the rest of the figure to match. The marks there are a working layout fitted by tools/eye-fit.js: systems whose every planet has life in the survey roll, with few planets, nudged onto their strokes, with Nowhere's system on its stroke. Of the catalogue's 340 systems, 28 qualify, so four marks are new systems rolled by seed.

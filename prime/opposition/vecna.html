Opposition: Vecna

Vecna's lane through this campaign: the eye he is drawing across the Erevos Galaxy, how far it has got, the vessels and contingencies he works through, what the crew can learn and in what order, and what happens if no one stops him. His godhood is complete; the climb he is making now is the Axiomic Ascension, into Continuity itself. Who he is, what he wants and how to run him are on his dossier. How hearts open and link, how a core wakes and what Rykoe is are on Cosmology. What the colony is doing day to day is on Colony Operations.

The eye

A galaxy's core can be woken by more than one method. Vecna, an archmage older than his godhood, does it with spellwork: an inscription, a figure drawn in linked heart worlds across the Erevos Galaxy. GAL-01's inscription is an eye. It is the left eye Vecna lost, remade across a galaxy, an eye that would see everything.

When lids and iris are whole, the eye opens and the core wakes. When the core opens, the pupil opens, and what lands on Oerth is sight: a view into Continuity itself.

Sixteen The number of hearts follows the figure: sixteen for GAL-01, ten on the lids and six in the iris. It is not a rule of the cosmos. The clue the crew needs is the vantage: plot the hearts from Oerth.

Drawn like a constellation

The eye is drawn the way a constellation is. Its worlds lie wherever the spiral arms carry them, sometimes two in one system, far apart and at different heights above and below the galaxy's plane. Seen from above, their links make no shape at all. From Oerth, where Vecna became a god, they line up into the eye. He chose them from thousands of Monolith worlds for how they look from there. The links are real wherever the worlds lie; only the picture needs Oerth.

The figure is loose. Plotted on an ordinary chart, the heart worlds look random. Plotted as they appear from Oerth, they begin to trace the eye, and someone who knows Vecna's mark can recognize it.

Oerth, the retina

Oerth is not one of the sixteen. It is the point where the eye's image lands. Every heart world in the figure is also linked back to Oerth's heart, so the links run in a cone from the eye to Oerth, the way light runs from an eye to the back of it. Oerth's heart has been Vecna's since his ascension charged him into it.

Oerth is the one world Vecna cannot lose, because every link in the eye runs back to it.

Nowhere, the found lid heart

Nowhere is one of the lid worlds, on the lower lid. Its heart lies exposed in the wreck, the easiest in the galaxy to find and open, which is why Vecna chose it. It is the heart already found.

Holy symbol and the sky

The eye in the palm of a left hand remains Vecna's holy symbol on worlds. Only the eye is written across the sky. The chapters that read his symbol's eye as sight and revelation are closest to the truth.

Circle of Fifths

Vecna calls his working the Circle of Fifths, after the circle of fifths in music: five galaxies, each a fifth of what he needs, around Rykoe. The eye is GAL-01's fifth. The name is his alone.

What opens when Rykoe opens

Once Rykoe opens, every linked heart becomes a door: through its Atrium to the core, and through the core to the center. Vecna's vessels go that way.

Who knows

Only Vecna knows what he is building; the Seekers and the Order do not. The Order's Watchers flag some chapters' work as a danger to a world's Zenith and track it, without knowing who stands behind it. Some gods suspect he is up to something. A few people on the fringes sense that something is wrong in the cosmos, but they cannot say what it is or what to call it, and no one believes them. The fringe sage of Ascher Mir tracks the Seekers' pattern across chapters; the sage is a separate figure from the rogue Watcher Aurel Venn.

The two opened hearts lie on two worlds that share one system on the upper lid. They are linked to each other, and each is linked back to Oerth, so three links stand. One of the two is a little-charted planet the crew learns of late in the campaign. Nowhere's heart, on the lower lid, is found and not yet opened. Nothing in the iris is found.

Counts only The working is tracked as counts of hearts found, opened and linked. Worlds are built when play reaches them; which world holds which stroke, and the bleeding between linked worlds, are decided when it matters.

The colony's residue piece

Vecna's side holds one residue piece. The colony carries it from world to world to open hearts. If the crew ever takes it, N'ghathrod must win it back or find another before he can open another heart. Seekers chapters hunt for more (below). Pieces stay scarce: the crew may never gather a full fragment, and every piece is a race. The rest of the scattered pieces are tracked on Artifacts.

The other galaxies

In each of the other four galaxies, another being works its own core toward the same end. GAL-02's core, in the eldritch galaxy, is awake. GAL-03, GAL-04 and GAL-05 are partway, and what happens in GAL-01 decides them.

An awake core presses on its neighbours, and GAL-02's presses on GAL-01's. Late in the campaign it can pull the crew into the eldritch galaxy, where the rules become Call of Cthulhu. What they do there is get home, or close what is open.

His vessels in this campaign

Vecna cannot stand on a world (his ascension, polarity), so he works through vessels. Most of those who do his work know him by another name, and N'ghathrod does not know him at all.

N'ghathrod and the colony

N'ghathrod is the main villain of the campaign, and to Vecna he is a means to an end. Vecna needs him to open and link hearts so that Vecna's vessels can pass through Atria. Vecna reaches him by whisper, and the whispers are suggestions. Only one instruction has ever reached him, and it came secondhand, in the voice N'ghathrod takes for his elder brain: “a world with a Monolith is a marked world.” How the whispers began, and how he took Vth'oramu, are on their dossiers.

His colony, the Vth'orama, is a chapter of the Seekers: the Seekers of a New Home. They serve the same hidden god as every other chapter, believing they answer to an elder brain, and Vecna feeds them the few secrets they need. N'ghathrod is working now on all four parts of his plan: planting the taken in key positions, finding hearts, opening them, and seeking links. Where Vth'oramu waits, the colony's target worlds and its operations are on Colony Operations.

The Seekers chapters

The Seekers are Vecna's faith, and he steers their chapters through residue. He lets each chapter believe that finding these artifacts will lead it to what it seeks, or that the artifacts are what it seeks. He needs them hungry for something, and their seeking ripples outward through the worlds they work on.

Contingencies, in order

Vecna plans in layers. His routes to the end he wants, from the surest to the last resort:

  1. The five lieutenants. Getting N'ghathrod's five lieutenants through as his vessels would make it certain.
  2. The lesser taken. Many lesser taken, grown over time through the colony's spread, could still get him there. The taken outlive N'ghathrod, each keeping part of him.
  3. The Restorers' aims. The Restorers are trying to reach a heart, and through it the Core Monolith, where they believe everything lost is stored. They compete for residue pieces and, without knowing it, advance his plan. Avenues like theirs can bring Vecna close again, or only delay him.
  4. Tharizdun. Vecna uses Tharizdun as a contingency. It is use, not alliance, and they distrust each other. Her weakening of reality on Fonains serves his ends, and he lets it run toward the Day of Chaos, when the Princes of Elemental Evil break the barriers so she can leave her prison. She is his battering ram against the barriers, and the Day of Chaos is part of that use. Vecna plans his own way out before she gets out.

Defeating N'ghathrod does not end the threat.

Earned victories A victory over N'ghathrod is never undone by fiat. What comes after it comes from the contingencies.

What the crew can learn, in order
  1. One world in Greyspace. The first thing anyone can notice is that every opened heart is linked back to the same world, in Greyspace.
  2. Why Oerth. Why they all lead to Oerth is the question that opens the rest.
  3. The eye. Charting the hearts from Oerth's vantage turns a random scatter into Vecna's eye (Drawn like a constellation).
  4. A figure from lore. The name Vecna surfaces late, through lore: a figure missing his left eye and his left hand. Most of his worshippers know him by other names.
  5. The Radiant Citadel. Lich Vecna, the mortal form the Core Monolith archived when Vecna ascended, sits in the Radiant Citadel. He has had centuries to conclude he was wrong, and he is calm, almost redeemed. Meeting him unsettles everything the crew thinks they know.

Before any rung, the crew can notice a symptom without a source. Over the past century Zeniths have come more often, and Monoliths with them, and the Watchers have seen it. Some of the rise is Vecna's doing and some is simply worlds reaching their moments; the strain it puts on the Axioms, the Axiom strain, is a byproduct of his work.

Beside the ladder runs a thread of the Seekers' own. Each chapter's name is in its own people's language, and every one carries the word for "seekers". A crew that translates enough chapter names, by magic or by study, will notice the pattern.

By arc

Vecna's column of the campaign, arc by arc, with each arc's level range. How the arcs fit together is on the Spine.

Arc 1 · levels 1 to 4

Vecna reaches Arc 1 only through his vessels. By the time the crew graduates, the colony's landed operations have begun to ripple outward at contract scale: taken in small positions, odd disappearances, a station or crew acting strangely. These are the first things the crew meets as Driftwardens; the operations themselves are on Colony Operations. The crew meets its first Seekers chapter well after graduation.

Arc 2 · levels 5 to 10

The crew works contracts out of Ondrel Anchorage, and many of them quietly lead back to a Seekers chapter, some knowingly nefarious and some unwitting. Chapters are met one at a time, each on its own world. The first is a benevolent church that shows Vecna's hand and eye openly: good and docile, with something a little odd about it, not evil but easy to misread. The crew first hears of it through Hessa Dun, a Caelestis contract contact whose uncle tells strange stories.

The symbol The players will recognize the hand and eye and expect villainy; the characters know nothing of it. The church's goodness should sit wrong with the players without giving them anything to act on.

Arc 3 · levels 11 to 15

The Radiant Citadel and the Core Monolith become destinations late in Arc 3 at the earliest. The crew reaches them by a long voyage or through a linked heart, and Lich Vecna waits in the Citadel.

Arc 4 · level 15 on

The late campaign is about stopping Vecna's working, not necessarily fighting him; Arc 4 turns on whether the eye opens. The Citadel and the Core Monolith remain in reach if the crew has not got there in Arc 3. A confrontation could come with a part of Vecna manifested in the First Heart. If the eye opens, the campaign has reached its failure state.

Failure state

The failure state is GAL-01's core waking for Vecna. It sets the fate of the other galaxies. If GAL-01 falls, GAL-03, GAL-04 and GAL-05 fall with it, and with GAL-02's core already awake, Rykoe can open. Then every linked heart becomes a door to the center (above), and Vecna's vessels go through toward Continuity.

Success in GAL-01 holds the other galaxies, though work remains.

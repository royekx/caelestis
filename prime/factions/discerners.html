Order of the Discerners
The Order of All's custodians: they guard the Monoliths, hold the record, keep the Beacon, and judge what the Watchers report.

The Order of the Discerners is one of the three orders of the Order of All. It is the link in the Order's chain that judges: the Watchers see, the Discerners judge, the Restorers act, and a Vanguard sanctions. It weighs what a coming Zenith will do and what intervening would cost, and it runs the logistics of the whole Order. The Discerners guard the Monoliths, hold the Order's record and its registry of Monoliths, and keep the Beacon, the galaxy's count of time. From the Watchers' reports they forecast which worlds come next, counsel who is sent where, and give out what the Order knows.

Beliefs

The Discerners revere the All-Father, the Order's one god, and understand his charge to them as a calling to protect, as a father keeps what is his safe. They keep the Monoliths, the record and the Beacon. To a Discerner, the Order's work is to keep faith with the All-Father by holding what is known and guarding where it rests.

At the order's radical edge stand those who hold that nothing leaves the record and that secrets are safer kept. They would hand out nothing at all, and they argue every release of information the order makes.

History

The Discerners have been one of the three orders since the Order of All, founded as one body, split as its following grew and like minds drew together. They have kept the Order's count since the founding.

Operations

Guarding the Monoliths

The Discerners guard the Monoliths as though the taken were kept inside them. A Monolith comes into their keeping once its Zenith has passed.

The record and the registry

The Discerners hold the Order's record, into which every Watcher's report is filed, and the registry of Monoliths. In their registries a Monolith is an Ortholith, and the word reached Fleet usage through them.

Judgement and counsel

The Discerners read the reports of many Watchers against everything the Order has recorded. A Discerner's judgement often begins with the record: the Order has seen this before, and the last time, on Oerth, a god ascended. They weigh what a coming Zenith will do, whether its outcome will stay with its world or spill beyond it, and what intervening would cost. They can recommend action, and a sector's Vanguard sanctions an intervention on their counsel; the Discerners themselves rarely act. For a Zenith the Order judges truly terrible, a Discerner goes in as one of the three, with a Watcher and a Restorer.

Forecast and dispatch

From the Watchers' reports the Discerners forecast which worlds approach a Zenith next. They counsel where Watchers are posted and where Restorers are sent, and route the dispatches that go to the Fleet. What the Order chooses to share with the Fleet, and through it with Caelestis, goes out from the Discerners.

The Beacon

The Discerners keep the galaxy's count of time, and every unit of it comes from Erevos, the heart at the galaxy's core, tied to no planet. Erevos turns slowly on its own axis, once a cycle, much faster than the galaxy's drift, which is too slow to notice in a lifetime. It is not round, and it throws a beam from one side that no one can see. When the beam reaches the root of one of the galaxy's four spiral arms, that arm lights up: a wave of light ripples outward through its gas and dust, root to tip, over a quarter of the cycle. People see the arm lit and count it; the beam itself is never seen.

The Order of All calls the galactic clock the Beacon, the one god's fire kept burning on the high place of the galaxy so that those out in the dark can find their way, like the signal fires lit on headlands before lighthouses had lamps. The Discerners keep it. In the Order's teaching the beacon's fire strikes each arm in turn, and the light runs out along the arm from cluster to cluster, as one fire on a coast once answered another. Each arm's spark has a character of its own, the way every old beacon was known by its pattern. To the Discerners, keeping the count is keeping the fire, and it fits a charge to keep what is.

Its words run from large to small. A beacon is one full turn, what most people call a cycle. A spark is the moment the light strikes an arm's root, and that arm's quarter of the beacon, so a beacon has four sparks and “the second spark” is the second arm's quarter. A kindling is one marker cluster passed as the light spreads along the arm, sixteen to a beacon, numbered through it from the first to the sixteenth. An ember is one day-length count on an armillary, about thirty to a kindling. An armillary is an attuned instrument that responds to which arm is lit and how far the light has spread; the Discerners keep them, the Order and the Fleet carry them, and a practised navigator reading the sky comes close without one. An Order date reads “the 1491st beacon, seventh kindling, twelfth ember”, which Fleet logs write 1491.7.12, and a journey is counted the same way: fourteen embers to H'Catha. The Fleet and travellers borrow the Order's smaller words, kindling and ember. Most people say cycle for the full turn, keep their own world's calendar and never read the arms.

The count runs from the spark the Order of All saw at its founding, Cycle 0. The Order made it because its Watchers, posted across the galaxy, needed one clock to compare their reports, and the Discerners have kept it since. The current beacon is the 1491st. The Fleet adopted the count when it formed, about 250 cycles ago, and through the Fleet it reached navigators and the stations, where most people call it cycles or Fleet time and have never heard of the Beacon.

Over the past few centuries the arms have been lighting slightly early against the Discerners' old tables, and the beacons have grown slightly shorter. Watchers notice because the beacons no longer line up with local years and seasons the way the old records say they did. The change is too small to alter the count, but any Discerner reading the tables closely finds it irregular.

Seats and branches

The Discerners have a wing in each of the Order's houses. In the Caelestis Sector house they answer to the Watcher Vanguard, Captain-Commander Dejexic. A Discerner Vanguard commands the Order's house in another sector.

Relationships

The Order of the Watchers

The Watchers see and the Discerners judge. Every Watcher's report comes to the Discerners, and every posting is made on their counsel. A Watcher who wants to act argues for it here, and the Discerners' answer is often to file the report and wait.

The Order of the Restorers

The Discerners counsel where Restorers are sent and receive what they bring back. The two argue over what is kept and what is shared: the Restorers spread their Ledger in partial copies and trade what they know, and the Discerners' radical edge would release nothing. The Restorers' records on Monolith siting are better than the Discerners' registry, which the Discerners do not like to hear.

Caelestis and the Fleet

The Fleet carries the Discerners' dispatches and keeps the count they keep. What reaches Caelestis from the Order of All has passed through the Discerners first.

What's hidden

  • The Monoliths. The Discerners guard the Monoliths as though the taken were inside them, and the Monoliths hold nothing: a Monolith relays what it archives to the Core Monolith. The Discerners have never heard of the Core Monolith.
  • The quickening. The arms light early and the beacons shorten because the heart of Erevos is quickening as the eye's moment nears. The irregularity in the Discerners' tables is a clue to Vecna's working.
  • Vecna. The registry and the Watchers' reports hold the rise in Zeniths, world by world. The Discerners forecast from it and have not read it as anyone's design.

How to run them

Discerners are archivists, quartermasters and dispatchers. They answer a question with a filing reference, and they release what they know in measured portions, by whom it is for and what it will be used for. A Discerner at the radical edge treats every question as a leak.

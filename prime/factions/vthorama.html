The Vth’orama
All who go toward home: an illithid colony aboard a living dreadnought, looking for a world to be its home.

The Vth'orama are N'ghathrod's illithid colony, living aboard Vth'oramu, an astral dreadnought they took and made their home. In their own tongue the name means all who go toward home; rendered in Common, they are the Seekers of a New Home, a chapter of the Seekers. The name covers everyone aboard: N'ghathrod, his five lieutenants, the true-formed and the malformed, the taken, the thralls and their children. Vth'oramu holds a civilization, with families, children born aboard, trades and streets, and most who live there never chose N'ghathrod's war. They want a home, and they believe they answer to an elder brain through him.

Beliefs

They left Glyth; they did not leave being illithids. Everyone aboard who was raised an illithid was raised in illithid culture, and five of its beliefs hold them still:

  • Union with a greater mind is the proper end of a life. Absorption into an elder brain is how service is repaid. It is why the five lieutenants offered themselves, and why no one aboard finds it strange that a person can be themselves and also him.
  • A colony must have a centre. N'ghathrod at the heart of the ship, in the place an elder brain would hold, is correct to them. A colony without a centre is the thing they fled.
  • The whole form is the true form. Glyth thins its broods and holds the malformed an obscenity. The colony never thins, so Glyth's line between kept and culled means nothing aboard, yet the whole keep Glyth's ideal and call themselves the true-formed. Most of the colony's illithids are malformed, three of the five lieutenants among them, and N'ghathrod does not accept the name: every illithid aboard is Vth'orama to him. It is the one inherited belief that cuts against the colony, and the colony has never resolved it.
  • Captives are material. The captive trade goes on without discomfort. Every captive goes to the pools or feeds the colony's illithids.
  • Qualith is the only real writing. Their records are untranslatable by design, and they assume anything written otherwise is beneath reading.

Structure and ranks

The colony has no elder brain. N'ghathrod, its founder, stands in that place. He is fused into Vth'oramu and the tadpole pools bloom out of the hull, so the pools are in his body as an elder brain's are in its brine. He decides which tadpole goes to which captive, the taken report up to him, and what they know ends with him. The colony's illithids believe they answer to an elder brain through him, and in every way that matters they do.

Below him are the enna, his five lieutenants, each carrying a Hold, a portion of him. Then come the colony's illithids, the true-formed and the malformed; then the taken; then thralls and their children. Generations have been born aboard and many have died in the Astral Sea.

The pools

Every illithid aboard, whole or malformed, spawns a mass of tadpoles two or three times in its life, and every mass goes into the pools. The colony never thins its broods. The tadpoles still eat one another, as tadpoles do, but with no elder brain taking its share, far more of each mass survives than the one in a hundred an ordinary colony keeps. Every survivor waits in the pools, for years if it must, until it is found a host. The weak and the flawed survive with the rest, so most of the illithids the colony makes come out of ceremorphosis malformed. The pools always hold more waiting tadpoles than the colony has bodies for, and that is why it always needs captives.

The true-formed

The colony's whole illithids: the old ones who came with N'ghathrod, and the minority born in the colony who came out of ceremorphosis whole. They keep the pools and oversee ordinary ceremorphosis. They keep Glyth's ideal and call themselves the true-formed, a name N'ghathrod does not use, and the colony's old quarrel with its own malformed majority is loudest among them. Two of the enna, Osskhal and Khaleth, are whole, captured with N'ghathrod aboard Thessalar's craft.

The malformed

A malformed illithid is visibly wrong in body and lopsided in mind: asymmetric, oversized or undersized, and where an ordinary illithid has a broad spread of psychic powers, a malformed one is very good at one thing and poor at the rest. They are most of the colony's illithids, and loyal for what N'ghathrod did for them. The first three were made aboard Thessalar's craft from N'ghathrod's own tadpoles, brought aboard before the experiment and cut off from the elder brain; Thessalar experimented on them too and put them into bodies he supplied. Those three are Ulh'rek, Zhauveth and Ith'varn, three of the enna.

The Coven

The colony's memory: a small circle of its eldest illithids, none of them among the enna, who came out of Thessalar's craft with N'ghathrod. Some were already elders of their Glyth colony's branch when Thessalar captured them. N'ghathrod took them, each with one of his copies, and the taking has prolonged their lives as it prolongs his. They remember for the colony, and they keep the old ways of Glyth, mostly.

Vth'ghathu, the taken

Everyone taken, as a class; one of them is an n'ghathu. The taken sit in posts the colony can use, out on the worlds, and a few are outcasts N'ghathrod chose to save (the taking). A taken person develops N'ghathrod's faculties over the years, because the copy becomes more fully itself; the longest-taken hold seats.

The taking

Thessalar experimented on N'ghathrod in the presence of the seventh piece of the Rod of Seven Parts, a Consolidation piece keyed to simulacrum. It changed his reproduction: his tadpoles are copies of himself. Implanted into a host, such a tadpole does not consume it. The host persists, and the copy sits alongside them. A taken person:

  • keeps their memories, skills, personality and competence;
  • keeps their old relationships and still cares about the people in them, and those ties shape how they act: what they say to a friend, whom they protect, how gently they lie;
  • grows illithid chitin beneath the skin over years, hidden, along the spine and the back of the skull;
  • gains the colony bond and knows exactly what they belong to;
  • gives their allegiance to N'ghathrod, so when an old tie and the colony pull apart, the colony wins every time;
  • carries an innate mind blank, so they cannot be read.

Each copy drifts from the original over time. N'ghathrod can assert himself over an ordinary taken person; through the enna he can only speak. Tadpoles are aware before implantation. They learn, can be primed with a directive, and know Vth'oramu as home. From the inside the taking is a gift, and some of the taken were offered it and accepted. A tadpole can be removed, and removal frees the host.

The experiment also changed how he spawns. An ordinary illithid spawns two or three times in its life; N'ghathrod spawns on a slow, steady cycle, a few copies at a time, again and again. Over a year he makes more tadpoles than any illithid aboard, and far fewer than the colony's illithids make together. A copy is too scarce to spend on an ordinary body, so it never goes to a captive. It goes to someone in a place of power, reached where they are, so that the colony gains a post: a station master, a dockmaster, a spelljammer the Fleet trusts.

The one exception is the outcast, and it is rare. N'ghathrod has a soft spot for the neglected, the abandoned and the cast out, because that is what he and his people were. When one of his taken finds such a person, he may spend a copy on them for no gain at all, and his taken then protect them and raise them up.

A portion of every taken person passes upward through the rungs of the taken and ends with N'ghathrod: the tithe. The flow is paracausal, and his long life comes from it. He shares it with a chosen few, the enna and some of the old illithids who joined him, and they have outlived every illithid of their age by centuries. None of them is immortal. When a taken person dies, N'ghathrod receives what it knew, including everything it learned about whoever killed it.

History

N'ghathrod underwent ceremorphosis on Glyth long before any of this. About five hundred years ago Thessalar captured him, with Osskhal, Khaleth and his elder brain, and held them aboard his planar craft, the brain kept apart from him. Three of N'ghathrod's own tadpoles were brought aboard too, before the experiment, cut off from the brain. Thessalar experimented on N'ghathrod with the seventh piece of the Rod, and on the three, whom he put into bodies he supplied. The experiment changed N'ghathrod, the five who became his lieutenants, and a mouse that lived aboard the craft.

When the craft lost its gems and fell toward Fonains, N'ghathrod used the fall to escape with his five lieutenants, a few lesser illithids, and the mouse, which he saved. He never reached Fonains. The main craft came down in the Barrier Peaks, and his elder brain was left trapped in the caverns beneath them. Other pieces of the craft broke off in the fall and scattered across Fonains: Tharizdun's temple, and the Temple of Moloch with Thessalar's laboratory beneath it. N'ghathrod believes the brain dead, or beyond reach.

The journey back to Glyth, a system away, took decades. When N'ghathrod brought the three malformed to Glyth, Glyth saw what they were, refused them and exiled them, and N'ghathrod went with them. That was about four centuries ago. In the centuries that followed he searched for a home for his people and learned the full extent of his ability to take, and the tithe kept him and his chosen alive long after they expected to die. In those centuries a voice reached him, with dreams and visions. For some decades he took it for an elder brain. He learned it was not, and stopped caring: Glyth had cast him out, and the voice provides. The long plan formed in those centuries.

Taking the astral dreadnought and turning it into Vth'oramu took decades, perhaps a century. It bore no wound before the siege; N'ghathrod knew a dreadnought's physiology and saw his chance. The fight lasted a long time, and controlling the creature took a great many of his copies. The active push to find and open hearts began only in the past few decades.

Operations

The colony works on four things at once: planting the taken in key posts, finding hearts, opening them, and seeking links between them. Vth'oramu keeps to the dark between systems and works as a mothership, sending skiffs and couriers out to the worlds around it.

How they look

The colony's mark is an eye inside a wheel. The top half of the wheel has ordinary spokes, and the bottom half turns into illithid tentacles. The wheel is navigation, the colony's search for a way home. The colony's arms echo a hand and an eye without showing either outright. A taken person looks like themselves; the chitin is hidden, and the tell is that they cannot be read. When the taken die, they say “Oramu”, or a phrase built on it.

Members

The enna are five illithids changed alongside N'ghathrod by Thessalar's experiment. Each came out of it with a power of its own, and each took a Hold, a portion of N'ghathrod carried permanently. All five offered themselves: the three malformed in gratitude, because when Glyth refused them he went into exile with them; the two true-formed in loyalty. Some of the old illithids who came with him took lesser portions, so that they would all be one. He speaks through the enna at will and does not drive them. Killing one ends that Hold for good, and the colony loses that function. Each enna's spawn inherits some of its parent's power: an illithid grown from an enna's tadpole leans toward it.

Relationships

Glyth

The world that refused them. The colony has outlived the people who exiled the malformed, and has never forgiven the refusal.

The Seekers

The colony is the Seekers of a New Home. Like every chapter, it knows nothing of the others.

The Restorers

Both compete for residue. The colony holds a piece; the Restorers want every piece studied.

What's hidden

  • The voice is Vecna. The colony serves the same hidden god as every other Seekers chapter. Vecna is not all-seeing: the voice gives N'ghathrod what Vecna's plan needs him to know, and everything else N'ghathrod learns from his taken. Its whispers are suggestions (one told him a dreadnought was the best way to keep a colony on its passage home), its list of worlds is his standing direction, and its one explicit instruction is that a world with a Monolith is a marked world. Every heart the colony opens and links serves Vecna's eye inscription.
  • The elder brain lived. Tharizdun's influence never reached it beneath the Barrier Peaks. After Lola's Boys reached it, it escaped to a place nobody knows. N'ghathrod still believes it dead or beyond reach.
  • The mouse is his sixth lieutenant. The experiment changed it too and gave it a power of its own. He took it with one of his copies, so the tithe keeps it alive. It is the first outcast he saved, and his secret sixth lieutenant, because nobody aboard counts a mouse.
  • Three bodies. The real N'ghathrod is fused into the hull; a long-tenured taken person walks and speaks as him, and a shell sits in the chamber. The table is on his dossier.
  • The operation. The colony's captives come through Vocath's line, it holds Cairn Station with a taken station master and one of Zhauveth's malformed spawn, and its man inside Caelestis is Joffrey. All three are on Opposition: the Vth'orama, with what happens to the taken if N'ghathrod falls.

How to run them

Three of the five kept beliefs are closed doors. The quarrel over the whole form is open, because the colony lives on both sides of it, and the true-formed are where it is loudest. A crew that understands the colony's history can press on a wound the colony gave itself, instead of arguing a position no illithid has abandoned.

If the crew boards Vth'oramu, run it as a civilization: families, children, trades and streets, and most of the people in them never chose this war. Any plan to destroy the ship or end N'ghathrod weighs that cost.

How long ago someone was taken is the difficulty knob, and it is diegetic (Stat block tab). A taken person's old ties are a lever: they still care about the people they knew, so a friend may get a gentler lie or quiet protection, never a betrayal of the colony. Every taken creature is a tripwire: a crew that works out that N'ghathrod learns from every death has to start solving places without killing the guards. When the crew kills an enna, they will think they killed a mind flayer. N'ghathrod and his people capture downed foes instead of killing them, because bodies are valuable.

Taken template

Apply to any stat block. Keep everything the creature had; add chitin (reduce bludgeoning, piercing and slashing damage by 3, or 5 at long tenure), mind blank, the tenure's faculties, and Nothing is Lost: when it dies, N'ghathrod receives what it knew. CR +1 at short tenure, +2 to +3 at long. It works on beasts as readily as people; a taken guard animal fights tactically far beyond its intelligence, which is the tell.

Malformed and true-formed

The colony's illithids, true-formed and malformed, run from the mind flayer plus an archetype and a form: Colony illithid on Stat Blocks. A malformed one takes its archetype in full and is disproportionate: on par with a mind flayer in total, and it plays very differently.

The enna

Build each between CR 12 and 16, and lopsided. None of them is a balanced encounter. N'ghathrod's own block is on his dossier.

The Vth’orama
All who go toward home: an illithid colony aboard a living dreadnought, looking for a world to be its home.

The Vth'orama are N'ghathrod's illithid colony, living aboard Vth'oramu, an astral dreadnought they took and made their home. In their own tongue the name means all who go toward home; rendered in Common, they are the Seekers of a New Home, a chapter of the Seekers. The name covers everyone aboard: N'ghathrod, his five lieutenants, the bearers, the unthinned, the taken, the thralls and their children. They want a home, and they believe they answer to an elder brain.

Beliefs

They left Glyth; they did not leave being illithids. Everyone aboard who was raised an illithid was raised in illithid culture, and five of its beliefs hold them still:

  • Union with a greater mind is the proper end of a life. Absorption into an elder brain is how service is repaid. It is why the five lieutenants offered themselves, and why no one aboard finds it strange that a person can be themselves and also him.
  • A colony must have a centre. N'ghathrod at the heart of the ship is correct to them. A colony without a centre is the thing they fled.
  • The unthinned are an obscenity. They hold this alongside the fact that most of the colony's illithids are unthinned, and three of the five lieutenants. It is the one inherited belief that cuts against them, and they have never resolved it; they have only outlived the people who said it.
  • Thralls are material. The captive trade goes on without discomfort, and the bearers' grievance is about allocation, never about the taking.
  • Qualith is the only real writing. Their records are untranslatable by design, and they assume anything written otherwise is beneath reading.

Structure and ranks

N'ghathrod is the colony's founder and its centre. Below him are the enna, his five lieutenants, each carrying a Hold, a portion of him. Then come the colony's illithids, bearers and unthinned; then the taken; then thralls and their children. Generations have been born aboard and many have died in the Astral Sea.

Vth'merru, the bearers

Properly raised illithids who can still breed the colony. Bearers lay into the pools and oversee ordinary ceremorphosis. N'ghathrod can make himself, in pieces, across worlds; he cannot make a colony. So every captive is an allocation decision, and the bearers watch bodies that should have become illithids receive one of his tadpoles instead and walk away still looking like a person. To them it is a child wasted on a disguise. Two of the enna, Osskhal and Khaleth, are bearers, captured with N'ghathrod aboard Thessalar's craft.

Vth'ulhan, the unthinned

Illithids raised without an elder brain to cull and tend them. They age oddly and come out wrong in individual ways: asymmetric, oversized or undersized, missing a faculty and compensating with another. Unthinned was Glyth's word for a tadpole that should have been culled, and they took it for themselves. They are the bulk of the colony's illithids and loyal for what N'ghathrod did for them. Three of the enna are unthinned, made aboard Thessalar's craft from bodies Thessalar supplied for his experiments and N'ghathrod's own tadpoles.

Vth'ghathu, the taken

Everyone taken, as a class; one of them is an n'ghathu. N'ghathrod takes key people who can rise into positions of control, never crowds, and installs them as figures on worlds. The outcast and the downtrodden, offered belonging, form the colony's body. A taken person develops N'ghathrod's faculties over the years, because the copy becomes more fully itself; the longest-taken hold seats.

The taking

Thessalar experimented on N'ghathrod in the presence of the seventh piece of the Rod of Seven Parts, a Consolidation piece keyed to simulacrum. It changed his reproduction: his tadpoles are copies of himself. Implanted into a host, such a tadpole does not consume it. The host persists, and the copy sits alongside them. A taken person:

  • keeps their memories, skills, personality, relationships and competence;
  • grows illithid chitin beneath the skin over years, hidden, along the spine and the back of the skull;
  • gains the colony bond and knows exactly what they belong to;
  • gives their allegiance to N'ghathrod, and chooses him when they must choose;
  • carries an innate mind blank, so they cannot be read.

Each copy drifts from the original over time. N'ghathrod can assert himself over an ordinary taken person; through the enna he can only speak. Tadpoles are aware before implantation. They learn, can be primed with a directive, and know Vth'oramu as home. From the inside the taking is a gift, freely given and gladly taken, and many of the taken were offered it and accepted. Removing the tadpole frees only someone who knows what was done and objects to it.

A portion of every taken person passes upward through the rungs of the taken and ends with N'ghathrod: the tithe. The flow is paracausal, and it extends his life. When a taken person dies, N'ghathrod receives what it knew, including everything it learned about whoever killed it.

Tadpoles are the cost of everything. He grows them on a slow cycle; a person costs one, something enormous costs many. Anyone can carry and implant one, so couriers and brine casks move constantly, and a crate destroyed in transit costs him months of placements.

History

N'ghathrod underwent ceremorphosis on Glyth long before any of this. About five hundred years ago Thessalar captured him and experimented on him aboard his planar craft, with a residue piece Thessalar had obtained. The experiments changed N'ghathrod and the five who became his lieutenants, and all six gained a greatly extended lifespan. They are not immortal.

When the craft lost its gems and fell toward Fonains, N'ghathrod used the fall to escape with his five lieutenants, a few lesser illithids, and a mouse he had saved. He never reached Fonains. His elder brain was separated from him in the crash; he believed it dead, or could not reach it. It lay trapped beneath the Barrier Peaks for about five hundred years, until it broke free and teleported away. Reaching for it, N'ghathrod was answered by a voice he believes is that brain grown into something greater.

The journey back to Glyth, a system away, took decades. Glyth rejected them: the unthinned were exiled, and N'ghathrod went with them, because they are his blood. That was roughly four to four and a half centuries ago. Over the centuries that followed he searched for a home for his people, learned the full extent of his ability to take, and found that he and his lieutenants were still alive long after they expected to die. The long plan formed in those centuries.

Taking the astral dreadnought and turning it into Vth'oramu took decades, perhaps a century. It bore no wound before the siege; N'ghathrod knew a dreadnought's physiology and saw his chance. The fight lasted a long time, and controlling the creature took a great many tadpoles. The active push to find and open hearts began only in the past few decades.

Operations

The colony works on four things at once: planting the taken in key positions, finding hearts, opening them, and seeking links. It carries one residue piece from world to world to open hearts; without it, N'ghathrod must win it back or find another before he can open another heart. Any heart can be opened, but a link needs a Monolith on both worlds, so the colony goes where Monoliths stand to link.

Vth'oramu keeps to the periphery, in the dark between systems, and works as a mothership: skiffs go out from it to the worlds in its vicinity while it stays where no one looks. It can enter a system, but a vessel that size near a world's planets would almost certainly be seen. The colony feeds itself captives through raiding and the neogi.

How they look

The colony's mark is the Passage: a circle with a tail ending in another circle. It gains a circle for every linked world, so it doubles as a count. The colony's arms echo a hand and an eye without showing either outright. A taken person looks like themselves; the chitin is hidden, and the tell is that they cannot be read. When the taken die, they say “Oramu”, or a phrase built on it.

Members

The enna are five illithids changed alongside N'ghathrod by Thessalar's experiments, each carrying a Hold. All five offered themselves: the three unthinned in gratitude, because he gave them what bad rearing denied them; the two bearers in loyalty. He speaks through them at will and does not drive them. Killing one ends that Hold for good, and the colony loses that function.

Relationships

Glyth

The world that refused them. The colony has outlived the people who exiled the unthinned, and has never forgiven the refusal.

The Seekers

The colony is the Seekers of a New Home. Like every chapter, it knows nothing of the others.

The Restorers

Both compete for residue. The colony holds a piece; the Restorers want every piece studied.

What's hidden

  • The voice is Vecna. The colony serves the same hidden god as every other Seekers chapter, believing it answers to an elder brain, and Vecna feeds it the few secrets it needs. Every heart it opens and links serves his eye inscription. A whisper from Vecna told N'ghathrod a dreadnought was the best way to keep a colony on its passage home.
  • Three bodies. The real N'ghathrod is fused into the hull. A long-tenured taken person, dominated and wrapped in illusion, walks and speaks as him among the colony and with outsiders; killing that one costs a single taken person, and another appears within weeks wearing the same face. A shell in the chamber is kept because the founders need somewhere to come and something to speak to. Two of the enna know; the others suspect and have never asked.
  • The taken outlive him. If N'ghathrod's true body dies, the taken do not die. Part of him lives on in each of them, and they lose a part of themselves. His lieutenants are closest to him, each rung down the colony further away, and given time one of them could grow toward becoming another N'ghathrod.
  • The tithe is why he lives: the more he takes, the longer he lasts.
  • Vocath's line. Zhauveth made an arrangement with Vocath: his line supplies captives, and the colony placed an asset inside Caelestis that Vocath believes is his own informant.
  • Joffrey, decorated in the Fleet, is that asset: the colony's operative at Caelestis.
  • Strake's readings. The unidentified mass Strake Station reported was Vth'oramu.

How to run them

Three of the five kept beliefs are closed doors. The unthinned contradiction is open, because the colony lives on both sides of it, and the bearers are where it is loudest. A crew that understands the colony's history can press on a wound the colony gave itself, instead of arguing a position no illithid has abandoned.

How long ago someone was taken is the difficulty knob, and it is diegetic (Stat block tab). Every taken creature is a tripwire: a crew that works out that N'ghathrod learns from every death has to start solving places without killing the guards. When the crew kills an enna, they will think they killed a mind flayer. N'ghathrod and his people capture downed foes instead of killing them, because bodies are valuable.

Their arc

By the crew's graduation the colony's landed work ripples outward at contract scale: taken people in small positions, odd disappearances, a station or crew acting strangely. Vth'oramu is the Arc 2 site. Once N'ghathrod grows bolder, Ilsevaine Corr will face Vth'oramu. Killing N'ghathrod has repercussions later, possibly in another campaign. The colony's operations and world status are on Colony Operations; Vecna's use of it is on Opposition: Vecna.

Threads

  • The Fleet's charts treat Vth'oramu as wildlife; every time a chart says the dreadnought behaves oddly, it is behaving obediently.

Taken template

Apply to any stat block. Keep everything the creature had; add chitin (reduce bludgeoning, piercing and slashing damage by 3, or 5 at long tenure), mind blank, the tenure's faculties, and Nothing is Lost: when it dies, N'ghathrod receives what it knew. CR +1 at short tenure, +2 to +3 at long. It works on beasts as readily as people; a taken guard animal fights tactically far beyond its intelligence, which is the tell.

Unthinned template

Apply to a mind flayer or arcanist. Remove something visible on the body: no Extract Brain, or no Mind Blast, or telepathy out to only 30 ft. Add +2 AC, +30 HP, and one malformation feature: 10 ft. tentacle reach, a second Mind Blast per rest, immunity to blindness, or a 10 ft. psychic aura. All have advantage on saves against being charmed or frightened; nothing cultivated them, so nothing reaches them easily.

The enna

Build each between CR 12 and 16, and lopsided. None of them is a balanced encounter. N'ghathrod's own block is on his dossier.

Order of the Keepers
The Order of All's custodians: they guard the Monoliths, hold the record, keep the Beacon and decide who is sent where.

The Order of the Keepers is one of the three orders of the Order of All. It works at and after a Zenith, and it runs the logistics of the whole Order. The Keepers guard the Monoliths, hold the Order's record and its registry of Monoliths, and keep the Beacon, the galaxy's count of time. From the Watchers' reports they forecast which worlds come next, decide who is sent where, and give out what the Order knows.

Beliefs

The Keepers revere the All-Haven, the aspect of the Order's one god that is the place where all things reside and everything is kept. To a Keeper, nothing that has been is ever lost from the All-Haven, and the Order's work is to keep faith with it: to hold what is known and guard where it rests.

At the order's radical edge stand those who hold that nothing leaves the record and that secrets are safer kept. They would hand out nothing at all, and they argue every release of information the order makes.

History

The Keepers have been one of the three orders since the Order of All, founded as one body, split as its following grew and like minds drew together. They have kept the Order's count since the founding.

Operations

Guarding the Monoliths

The Keepers guard the Monoliths as though the taken were kept inside them. A Monolith comes into their keeping once its Zenith has passed.

The record and the registry

The Keepers hold the Order's record, into which every Watcher's report is filed, and the registry of Monoliths. In their registries a Monolith is an Ortholith, and the word reached Fleet usage through them.

Forecast and dispatch

From the Watchers' reports the Keepers forecast which worlds approach a Zenith next. They decide where Watchers are posted, where Restorers are sent, and which dispatches go to the Fleet. What the Order chooses to share with the Fleet, and through it with Caelestis, goes out from the Keepers.

The Beacon

The Keepers keep the galaxy's count of time, and every unit of it comes from Erevos, the heart at the galaxy's core, tied to no planet. Erevos turns slowly on its own axis, once a cycle, much faster than the galaxy's drift, which is too slow to notice in a lifetime. It is not round, and it throws a beam from one side that no one can see. When the beam reaches the root of one of the galaxy's four spiral arms, that arm lights up: a wave of light ripples outward through its gas and dust, root to tip, over a quarter of the cycle. People see the arm lit and count it; the beam itself is never seen.

The Order of All calls the galactic clock the Beacon, the one god's fire kept burning on the high place of the galaxy so that those out in the dark can find their way, like the signal fires lit on headlands before lighthouses had lamps. The Keepers keep it. In the Order's teaching the beacon's fire strikes each arm in turn, and the light runs out along the arm from cluster to cluster, as one fire on a coast once answered another. Each arm's spark has a character of its own, the way every old beacon was known by its pattern. To the Keepers, keeping the count is keeping the fire, and it fits a charge to keep what is.

Its words run from large to small. A beacon is one full turn, what most people call a cycle. A spark is the moment the light strikes an arm's root, and that arm's quarter of the beacon, so a beacon has four sparks and “the second spark” is the second arm's quarter. A kindling is one marker cluster passed as the light spreads along the arm, sixteen to a beacon, numbered through it from the first to the sixteenth. An ember is one day-length count on an armillary, about thirty to a kindling. An armillary is an attuned instrument that responds to which arm is lit and how far the light has spread; the Keepers keep them, the Order and the Fleet carry them, and a practised navigator reading the sky comes close without one. An Order date reads “the 1491st beacon, seventh kindling, twelfth ember”, which Fleet logs write 1491.7.12, and a journey is counted the same way: fourteen embers to H'Catha. The Fleet and travellers borrow the Order's smaller words, kindling and ember. Most people say cycle for the full turn, keep their own world's calendar and never read the arms.

The count runs from the spark the Order of All saw at its founding, Cycle 0. The Order made it because its Watchers, posted across the galaxy, needed one clock to compare their reports, and the Keepers have kept it since. The current beacon is the 1491st. The Fleet adopted the count when it formed, about 250 cycles ago, and through the Fleet it reached navigators and the stations, where most people call it cycles or Fleet time and have never heard of the Beacon.

Over the past few centuries the arms have been lighting slightly early against the Keepers' old tables, and the beacons have grown slightly shorter. Watchers notice because the beacons no longer line up with local years and seasons the way the old records say they did. The change is too small to alter the count, but any Keeper reading the tables closely finds it irregular.

Seats and branches

The Keepers have a wing in each of the Order's houses. In the Caelestis Sector house they answer to the Watcher Vanguard, Captain-Commander Dejexic. A Keeper Vanguard commands the Order's house in another sector.

Relationships

The Order of the Watchers

The Watchers see and the Keepers decide. Every Watcher's report comes to the Keepers, and every posting goes out from them. A Watcher who wants to act argues for it here, and the Keepers' answer is often to file the report and wait.

The Order of the Restorers

The Keepers send Restorers to dead and broken worlds and receive what they bring back. The two argue over what is kept and what is shared: the Restorers spread their Ledger in partial copies and trade what they know, and the Keepers' radical edge would release nothing. The Restorers' records on Monolith siting are better than the Keepers' registry, which the Keepers do not like to hear.

Caelestis and the Fleet

The Fleet carries the Keepers' dispatches and keeps the count they keep. What reaches Caelestis from the Order of All has passed through the Keepers first.

What's hidden

  • The All-Haven. Of the three aspects, the Keepers' is unwittingly closest to the truth: everything taken does go somewhere it is kept. A Monolith relays what it archives to the Core Monolith, and the taken live on in the Radiant Citadel. The Keepers guard the Monoliths as though the taken were inside them, and the Monoliths hold nothing; the place they imagine is real, and it lies at the centre of the cosmos. The Keepers have never heard of the Core Monolith.
  • The quickening. The arms light early and the beacons shorten because the heart of Erevos is quickening as the eye's moment nears. The irregularity in the Keepers' tables is a clue to Vecna's working.
  • Vecna. The registry and the Watchers' reports hold the rise in Zeniths, world by world. The Keepers forecast from it and have not read it as anyone's design.

How to run them

Keepers are archivists, quartermasters and dispatchers. They answer a question with a filing reference, and they release what they know in measured portions, by whom it is for and what it will be used for. A Keeper at the radical edge treats every question as a leak.

Threads

  • FR-022, a piece of the Fonains Monolith, was recovered by the Order and routed to the Fleet; the routing was a Keepers' decision.

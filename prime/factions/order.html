The Order of All
One faith in three orders: Watchers gather intel, Discerners interpret and judge it, Averters act on it, and the Vanguard sanctions.

The Order of All is a faith made of three orders. It reveres one god, the All-Father, whom it knows as a pressure rather than a voice, and each of its orders understands his charge as a different calling. It acts to preserve Continuity and recruits from many worlds and peoples. Its orders form a chain: Watchers gather intel; Discerners interpret and judge it; Averters act on it; the Vanguard sanctions. The Fleet is the arms of all three. A young movement, the Restorers, runs across all three orders.

Beliefs

The Order reveres one god, the All-Father, and only him, and its three orders share him. Each understands its calling from the All-Father differently: the Watchers watch, the Discerners discern, and the Averters avert.

A calling is a duty, not a whole creed. Every order holds a spread of belief, from cautious to radical, and a member's private conviction need not match their order's work. A Watcher may privately believe the Order should act far more often; an Averter may believe it acts too much.

The Order's mandate is the preservation of Continuity, not universal rescue, and it takes the distinction seriously. It accepts a Zenith as part of the order of things, the natural order holding its balance. Its rule for intervening is the faith's oldest argument. If a Zenith's outcome stays with its own world, the Order lets it be. If the outcome would spill into the system or beyond, the Order acts, or at least tries. Where that line falls, and who decides it, is what the orders argue over, and acting is itself a determination that makes other outcomes possible, which is one reason the Order hesitates.

The three orders

The Order of the Watchers

Calling: to watch. Watchers gather intel. They are posted across the galaxy and embedded on worlds approaching a Zenith, and they watch and report; for the most part they do not act. Their trainees are Glimpses, who train for a century in the Hall of Sight and Sound; Captain-Commander Dejexic, the Watcher Vanguard, commands the Order's house in the Caelestis Sector.

The Order of the Discerners

Calling: to discern. Discerners interpret and judge the Watchers' intel. They read it against the Order's record and the sky, weigh what a Zenith means, what intervening would cost and whether to act, and counsel the Vanguard. Guarding the Monoliths, keeping the record and its registry of Monoliths, and keeping the Beacon are part of discerning.

The Order of the Averters

Calling: to avert, to turn a catastrophe aside before it lands. Averters act on the intel once it is judged and sanctioned: they form the team and go in to stop the ritual, the person or the plan driving a world toward catastrophe. They also go to corrupted Monoliths, and to broken worlds where people can still be saved.

How the orders work together

Watchers gather intel; Discerners interpret and judge it; Averters act on it; the Vanguard sanctions; the Fleet carries them. The Order cannot watch every world. It posts Watchers where populations are large, where civilizations stand, and where a world understands magic deeply. A Watcher's report goes to the Discerners, who file it in the record and read it against every other report and everything the Order has seen before: the last time a world rose to its peak this way, a god ascended, and the record of Oerth is in the Discerners' hands. The Discerners weigh what the coming Zenith will do and what intervening would cost, and give their counsel. On that counsel the sector's Vanguard sanctions an intervention or does not, and if it does, the Averters go in. The Fleet carries the Watchers to their posts, the Discerners' dispatches, and the Averters to wherever they are sent.

The three

For a Zenith the Order judges truly terrible, it sends a team of three: one Watcher, one Discerner and one Averter. The three can rarely stop a Zenith itself. What they can stop is what drives a world toward catastrophe: the ritual, the person or the plan. A Monolith's arrival in the run-up is their clock.

The Restorers

The Restorers are a young, small movement of like-minded people across all three orders: a Restorer can be a Watcher, a Discerner or an Averter. They are frustrated that the Order acts only at the last minute and accepts its losses, and they ask why it cannot go back and save what was lost. They believe what a Monolith takes is held at a source where the All-Father keeps it, and they mean to reach it. The movement has no wing, no Vanguard and no ranks. Some in every order sympathise with it; others think it dangerous.

Where the orders argue

The orders argue among themselves, and the arguments are old. They are disputes inside one faith over how to serve one god, and the charges of heresy traded in the heat of them are charges between brothers and sisters of the same church.

  • Where the line falls. Whether a Zenith's outcome stays with its world or spills beyond it is rarely clear, and every member reads it their own way. Watchers at the cautious end would never step in; Discerners usually counsel waiting; Averters want to be sent.
  • Keeping and sharing. The Discerners hold the record whole and give it out by measure. Restorers copy their Ledger by hand and share it. The Discerners' radical edge would release nothing at all.
  • Going back. The Restorers ask why the Order cannot undo its failures, and their most radical members walk into Zeniths to be archived. Many in every order count them foolhardy, and some have called the crossings a death cult.

History

The Order of All was founded as one body at Cycle 0, the first spark of the count it keeps. As its following grew, like minds drew together, and the one body split into three orders: the Watchers, the Discerners and the Averters. Each kept the one god and took one of his callings as its own. The Order made the count so that its Watchers, posted across the galaxy, had one clock to compare their reports, and the Discerners have kept it since; the current beacon is the 1491st. About 250 cycles ago the Fleet formed and adopted the count, and the Order became its partner. The Restorers are recent, a few generations old at most.

Seats and branches

The Order of All keeps a main headquarters, and houses in the Fleet's sectors. Each house is commanded by a Vanguard, who oversees the Watchers, Discerners and Averters posted there, whatever order the Vanguard belongs to. There are three Vanguards: a Watcher, a Discerner and an Averter.

The Caelestis Sector house

The Order's house in the Caelestis Sector has a wing for each order. The Hall of Sight and Sound, where Glimpses train and are knighted, stands in the Watchers' wing and is mainly theirs. The house is commanded by Captain-Commander Dejexic, the Watcher Vanguard: himself a Watcher, he oversees all three orders posted there.

Relationships

Caelestis and the Fleet

A known partner, and the arms of all three orders. The Order relies on the Fleet for transport and reach; the Fleet relies on the Order for moral mandate and divine cover. Reports from Watchers reach Caelestis through the Discerners, and some become cadet missions.

The Seekers

The Watchers track some chapters as threats to worlds, chapter by chapter, and have not seen the faith that joins them. Restorers have clashed with Seekers chapters.

What's hidden

  • The god. The presence the Order reveres is the Core Monolith's pressure. None of the three orders knows it; few in the cosmos know the Core Monolith exists.
  • Vecna. The Order does not know what Vecna is building, or that the Seekers chapters it tracks are one faith. The Restorers' aims advance his plan without their knowing it.

How to run them

The touchstone for the three is Destiny's fireteams; a raid of six is a possible larger team. Play the Order as one church with three temperaments, and remember that every member carries their own belief inside their order's duty. A crew that deals with one order hears the others through it: a Watcher's complaint about the Discerners, a Discerner's sigh about the Averters, an Averter's impatience with both. The argument is family, and an outsider who takes sides in it is shut out by all three.

The Order of All
One faith in three orders, each revering one god through its own aspect: the Watchers see, the Keepers keep and decide, the Restorers act.

The Order of All is a faith made of three orders. It reveres one divine presence, which it knows as a pressure rather than a voice, and each of its orders reveres that presence through its own aspect. It acts to preserve Continuity and recruits from many worlds and peoples. The Watchers work before a Zenith, the Keepers at and after one, and the Restorers after one and wherever something has broken. The Fleet is the arms of all three.

Beliefs

The Order holds that there is one god, and that it shows itself in three aspects, as a trinity does: the All-Father, who oversees; the All-Haven, the place where all things reside and everything is kept; and the All-Mother, who births and rebirths. Each order holds its own aspect closest and its own beliefs about what the god asks, and each has a radical edge that the other two argue with.

The Order's mandate is the preservation of Continuity, not universal rescue, and it takes the distinction seriously. It accepts a Zenith as part of the order of things. None of its orders tries to stop one: the Watchers guard a world's passage to its Zenith, the Keepers guard what the Zenith leaves, and the Restorers mend what it breaks.

The three orders

The Order of the Watchers

Aspect: the All-Father, who oversees. Watchers are posted across the galaxy and embedded on worlds approaching a Zenith. They watch, note and report, and they step in where they must. Their trainees are Glimpses, who train for a century in the Hall of Sight and Sound; Captain-Commander Dejexic leads them. Their radical edge holds that watching is enough, and that a Watcher should never interfere.

The Order of the Keepers

Aspect: the All-Haven, where everything is kept. The Keepers guard the Monoliths, hold the Order's record and its registry of Monoliths, and keep the Beacon, the galaxy's count of time. They forecast from the Watchers' reports which worlds come next, decide who is sent where, and give out what the Order knows. Their radical edge holds that nothing leaves the record and that secrets are safer kept.

The Order of the Restorers

Aspect: the All-Mother, who births and rebirths. The Restorers mend what is broken and bring back what was lost. They go to dead worlds, recover residue, bring survivors out of broken worlds and deal with corrupted Monoliths, and their deepest goal is restoration: a Monolith, a heart and all five fragments. Their radical edge holds that everything lost can come back, and walks into Zeniths to be archived.

How the orders work together

The Watchers see, the Keepers keep and decide, and the Restorers act, and the Fleet carries them. A Watcher's report goes to the Keepers, who file it in the record and read it against every other report to forecast which worlds approach a Zenith next. The Keepers decide where Watchers are posted and where Restorers are sent, and which dispatches go to the Fleet. When a Zenith has passed, the Keepers take the world's Monolith into their keeping, and the Restorers go to whatever the Zenith broke. The Fleet carries the Watchers to their posts, the Keepers' dispatches, and the Restorers to broken places.

Where the orders argue

The three orders argue among themselves, and the arguments are old. They are disputes inside one faith over how to serve one god, and the old charges of heresy traded in the heat of them are charges between brothers and sisters of the same church.

  • Watching and doing. The Watchers' oldest argument is whether watching should ever become doing. The Keepers usually answer it by filing the report and waiting; the Restorers would already have gone.
  • Keeping and sharing. The Keepers hold the record whole and give it out by measure. The Restorers spread their Ledger in partial copies and trade what they know. The Keepers' radical edge would release nothing at all.
  • The crossings. The Restorers' radical edge walks into Zeniths to be archived, certain that everything lost can come back. The Watchers and the Keepers count them foolhardy, and some in both orders have called the practice a death cult.

History

The Order of All was founded as one body at Cycle 0, the first spark of the count it keeps. As its following grew, like minds drew together, and the one body split into three orders: the Watchers, the Keepers and the Restorers. Each kept the one god and took one aspect as its own. The Order made the count so that its Watchers, posted across the galaxy, had one clock to compare their reports, and the Keepers have kept it since; the current beacon is the 1491st. About 250 cycles ago the Fleet formed and adopted the count, and the Order became its partner. Over the past few centuries the Restorers' beliefs and ambitions changed, and they began to push to do more.

Seats and branches

The Order of All has one headquarters, and each order has its own wing there. The Hall of Sight and Sound stands in the Watchers' wing and is mainly theirs.

Relationships

Caelestis and the Fleet

A known partner, and the arms of all three orders. The Order relies on the Fleet for transport and reach; the Fleet relies on the Order for moral mandate and divine cover. Reports from Watchers reach Caelestis through the Keepers, and some become cadet missions.

The Seekers

The Watchers track some chapters as threats to worlds, chapter by chapter, and have not seen the faith that joins them. The Restorers have clashed with Seekers chapters over the years.

What's hidden

  • The god. The presence the Order reveres is the Core Monolith's pressure. None of the three orders knows it; few in the cosmos know the Core Monolith exists.
  • Vecna. The Order does not know what Vecna is building, or that the Seekers chapters it tracks are one faith. The Restorers' aims advance his plan without their knowing it.

How to run them

Play the Order as one church with three temperaments. A crew that deals with one order hears the other two through it: a Watcher's complaint about the Keepers, a Keeper's sigh about the Restorers, a Restorer's impatience with both. The argument is family, and an outsider who takes sides in it is shut out by all three.

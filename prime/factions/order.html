The Order of All
One faith in three orders, each answering the All-Father's charge in its own way: the Watchers see, the Discerners judge, the Restorers act, and a Vanguard sanctions.

The Order of All is a faith made of three orders. It reveres one god, the All-Father, whom it knows as a pressure rather than a voice, and each of its orders understands his charge differently. It acts to preserve Continuity and recruits from many worlds and peoples. Its orders form a chain: the Watchers see, the Discerners judge, the Restorers act, and a sector's Vanguard sanctions. The Fleet is the arms of all three.

Beliefs

The Order reveres one god, the All-Father, and only him. Its three orders share him. Each understands its calling from the All-Father differently, and the three are the three ways of a father: the Watchers watch, the Discerners protect and keep, and the Restorers mend and restore. Each order has its own beliefs about what the All-Father asks, and each has a radical edge that the other two argue with.

The Order's mandate is the preservation of Continuity, not universal rescue, and it takes the distinction seriously. It accepts a Zenith as part of the order of things, the natural order holding its balance. Its rule for intervening is the faith's oldest argument. If a Zenith's outcome stays with its own world, the Order lets it be. If the outcome would spill into the system or beyond, the Order acts, or at least tries. Where that line falls, and who decides it, is what the three orders argue over, and acting is itself a determination that makes other outcomes possible, which is one reason the Order hesitates.

The three orders

The Order of the Watchers

Calling: to watch. Watchers are posted across the galaxy and embedded on worlds approaching a Zenith. They watch and report, and for the most part they do not act. Their trainees are Glimpses, who train for a century in the Hall of Sight and Sound; Captain-Commander Dejexic, the Watcher Vanguard, commands the Order's house in the Caelestis Sector. Their radical edge holds that watching is enough, and that a Watcher should never interfere.

The Order of the Discerners

Calling: to protect and keep. The Discerners read the reports of many Watchers against everything the Order has recorded, and weigh what a Zenith will do and what intervening would cost. They can recommend action and rarely take it. They also guard the Monoliths, hold the Order's record and its registry of Monoliths, and keep the Beacon, the galaxy's count of time. Their radical edge holds that nothing leaves the record and that secrets are safer kept.

The Order of the Restorers

Calling: to restore. The Restorers are the order willing to step in. They mend what is broken and bring back what was lost: they go to dead worlds, recover residue, bring survivors out of broken worlds and deal with corrupted Monoliths, and their deepest goal is restoration, a Monolith, a heart and all five fragments. Their radical edge holds that everything lost can come back, and walks into Zeniths to be archived.

How the orders work together

The Watchers see, the Discerners judge, the Restorers act, and the Vanguard sanctions; the Fleet carries them. The Order cannot watch every world. It posts Watchers where populations are large, where civilizations stand, and where a world understands magic deeply. A Watcher's report goes to the Discerners, who file it in the record and read it against every other report and everything the Order has seen before: the last time a world rose to its peak this way, a god ascended, and the record of Oerth is in the Discerners' hands. The Discerners weigh what the coming Zenith will do and what intervening would cost, and give their counsel. On that counsel the sector's Vanguard sanctions an intervention or does not. The Fleet carries the Watchers to their posts, the Discerners' dispatches, and the Restorers to wherever they are sent.

The three

For a Zenith the Order judges truly terrible, it sends a team of three: one Watcher, one Discerner and one Restorer. The three can rarely stop a Zenith itself. What they can stop is what drives a world toward catastrophe: the ritual, the person or the plan. A Monolith's arrival in the run-up is their clock.

Where the orders argue

The three orders argue among themselves, and the arguments are old. They are disputes inside one faith over how to serve one god, and the old charges of heresy traded in the heat of them are charges between brothers and sisters of the same church.

  • Where the line falls. Whether a Zenith's outcome stays with its world or spills beyond it is rarely clear, and each order reads it its own way. The Watchers' radical edge would never step in; the Discerners usually counsel waiting; the Restorers would already have gone.
  • Keeping and sharing. The Discerners hold the record whole and give it out by measure. The Restorers spread their Ledger in partial copies and trade what they know. The Discerners' radical edge would release nothing at all.
  • The crossings. The Restorers' radical edge walks into Zeniths to be archived, certain that everything lost can come back. The Watchers and the Discerners count them foolhardy, and some in both orders have called the practice a death cult.

History

The Order of All was founded as one body at Cycle 0, the first spark of the count it keeps. As its following grew, like minds drew together, and the one body split into three orders: the Watchers, the Discerners and the Restorers. Each kept the one god and took one of his callings as its own. The Order made the count so that its Watchers, posted across the galaxy, had one clock to compare their reports, and the Discerners have kept it since; the current beacon is the 1491st. About 250 cycles ago the Fleet formed and adopted the count, and the Order became its partner. Over the past few centuries the Restorers' beliefs and ambitions changed, and they began to push to do more.

Seats and branches

The Order of All keeps a main headquarters, and houses in the Fleet's sectors. Each house is commanded by a Vanguard, who oversees the Watchers, Discerners and Restorers posted there, whatever order the Vanguard belongs to. There are three Vanguards: a Watcher, a Discerner and a Restorer.

The Caelestis Sector house

The Order's house in the Caelestis Sector has a wing for each order. The Hall of Sight and Sound, where Glimpses train and are knighted, stands in the Watchers' wing and is mainly theirs. The house is commanded by Captain-Commander Dejexic, the Watcher Vanguard: himself a Watcher, he oversees all three orders posted there.

Relationships

Caelestis and the Fleet

A known partner, and the arms of all three orders. The Order relies on the Fleet for transport and reach; the Fleet relies on the Order for moral mandate and divine cover. Reports from Watchers reach Caelestis through the Discerners, and some become cadet missions.

The Seekers

The Watchers track some chapters as threats to worlds, chapter by chapter, and have not seen the faith that joins them. The Restorers have clashed with Seekers chapters over the years.

What's hidden

  • The god. The presence the Order reveres is the Core Monolith's pressure. None of the three orders knows it; few in the cosmos know the Core Monolith exists.
  • Vecna. The Order does not know what Vecna is building, or that the Seekers chapters it tracks are one faith. The Restorers' aims advance his plan without their knowing it.

How to run them

The touchstone for the three is Destiny's fireteams; a raid of six is a possible larger team. Play the Order as one church with three temperaments. A crew that deals with one order hears the other two through it: a Watcher's complaint about the Discerners, a Discerner's sigh about the Restorers, a Restorer's impatience with both. The argument is family, and an outsider who takes sides in it is shut out by all three.

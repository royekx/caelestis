Order of the All-Father
The watchers of the Erevos Galaxy's worlds, sworn to keep things as they are.

The Order of the All-Father serves the All-Father, a god it knows as a pressure rather than a voice, and acts to preserve Continuity. It recruits from many worlds and peoples. Its field arm, the Watchers, are posted across the Erevos Galaxy to observe worlds approaching dangerous thresholds, report on them, and intervene where they must.

Beliefs

To the Order the All-Father is a god like any other, and the Order interprets what it feels from it. It takes that feeling as a charge to maintain things as they are. Its authority is divine by belief: Watchers hold that the All-Father charged them, and carry themselves with the bearing of a priesthood. Some within the Order doubt that reading. Its mandate is the preservation of Continuity, not universal rescue, and the Order takes the distinction seriously.

It guards the Monoliths as though the taken were kept inside them. In its registries a Monolith is an Ortholith.

Ranks and training

A trainee is a Glimpse: one who has seen part of what the All-Father sees. Glimpses train for about a century in the Hall of Sight and Sound, the great hall within the Order's seat. At their knighting they are christened Watchers. The Order's paladins are sworn to the Oath of the Watchers. A new Watcher takes a posting to a region of a world; a world may hold several Watchers, depending on its size and its peoples. A Glimpse can be sent into the field to finish training under a Watcher already posted there.

Over all of it stands Captain-Commander Dejexic, leader of the Watchers and speaker of the All-Father. Dejexic is a purplish-blue plasmoid in obsidian armour that reflects tiny swirling galaxies. The Captain-Commander floats rather than walks, barely raises his voice, and silences a hall by entering it. The leadership instructs in aphorism: “Doubt is a choice. Worry is a crutch.”

The Watchers' work

Watchers keep to the Erevos Galaxy, as the Fleet does, and are more organized than the Fleet's sector branches. They watch for worlds nearing a Zenith and pass contracts and tips to the Fleet to head off trouble long before one arrives. They flag some Seekers chapters' work as a danger to a world's Zenith and track it, without knowing who stands behind it.

Over the past century the Watchers have seen Monoliths rise more often, and it worries them; some want to act. They are fighting oddities across many worlds and trying to keep a lid on them. Their calls for help are one reason Caelestis is stretched thin.

How they look

The Order's device is a white tower with a blue-violet eye at its centre, the tower's outline a little wrong, carried on the shield. It is distinct from Vocath's eye-in-a-wheel. A Watcher's magic renders as violet light and cosmic dust, with something larger standing behind the caster, so a Watcher at work is recognisable on sight once someone has seen one.

Relationships

Caelestis and the Fleet

A known partner. The Order relies on the Fleet for transport and reach; the Fleet relies on the Order for moral mandate and divine cover. Reports from Watchers reach Caelestis through the Order, and some become cadet missions.

The Seekers

The Watchers track some chapters as threats to worlds, chapter by chapter, and have not seen the faith that joins them.

She lives near H'Catha's rim, in the fog, a trek from any landing. She trades intelligence and protection with Ondessa and relays what she learns to the Order and to Caelestis. Her reports are known as unusually useful, because H'Catha sits at Realmspace's edge, where traffic and merchants pass. She saw factions moving on the meteorite and reported it as a precaution. Aurel Venn
Watcher, gone from his post
Warned the Order of a pattern, was not heeded, and went to find it himself.

He saw a pattern of more traffic and something large moving at the edges of systems, and warned the Order. It did not heed him. He never broke with the Order openly, but he left his post to find the thing himself. Accounts of him contradict one another.

Rogue Watchers

Some Watchers do not follow the Order's protocols as closely as it would prefer. They act on their own judgment: intervening where doctrine would not, holding back where it would act, or taking the work somewhere other than where they were sent. It is a range, not a category; some are nearly indistinguishable from sanctioned Watchers and some are openly heterodox. The Order tolerates them with more or less patience depending on the Watcher. One who saves a world by acting outside doctrine is quietly celebrated; one who breaks a precept and causes harm is censured. Most worlds cannot tell a Watcher from a rogue one.

What's hidden

  • Sol Fortuna. A Watcher extracted Sol from Aethris, against the Order's remit to preserve worlds, not individuals. As a Glimpse, Ezra knows nothing of it.
  • The All-Father. The pressure the Order serves comes from the Core Monolith. The Order has never heard of the Core Monolith; few in the cosmos have.
  • Vecna. The Order does not know what Vecna is building, or that the Seekers chapters it tracks are one faith.

How to run them

Watchers speak like priests and act like wardens. They are sincere, sure of their charge, and more interested in a world than in any one person on it, which is where they and the crew will rub. The leadership speaks in aphorism, and its juniors take aphorisms literally.

Threads

  • Nereth Vaul's report on the H'Catha meteorite went from her to the Hall, to the Fleet, to the Academy, where it became the crew's mission.
  • Aurel Venn and Ilsevaine Corr have seen the same thing: something large moving where no one looks. It is Vth'oramu.
  • After H'Catha, a Caelestis crew collects Ezra and carries him to the Hall, the last troop he travels with before his journey as a Watcher begins.
  • The rise in Monoliths the Watchers see is the Axiom strain.

Deities

The gods of this cosmos and the powers that act like them, one section each, and the Accordance, the six gods who sealed the Princes of Elemental Evil. What a god is, where gods dwell and what keeps them off worlds is on Cosmology. Vecna, Beshaba and Astra Virex have dossiers; Vecna's faith is the Seekers.

The FortunedFactions

Appearance

A god shows itself in a body that expresses what it is. Some appear humanoid, some as beasts, some as constructs, and some as partly living landscape. The body is how a god represents itself; a god is not a physical being the way a mortal is. One god is known by different names on different worlds, and is pictured differently with them; Takhisis is Tiamat, and Paladine is Bahamut.

Vecna

God of secrets, knowledge, manipulation and trickery, with a hold over death and the undead; his domains are Knowledge, Death and Trickery. He was an archmage of Oerth who became a lich and rose to godhood on his homeworld at least a few centuries ago, and his realm is Cavitius. He is known by what he lost, his left eye and his left hand, and his symbol is an eye in the palm of a left hand. Only on Oerth is he worshipped openly as Vecna; elsewhere his faithful, the Seekers, call him by other names, the First Seeker among them. Dossier →

Tharizdun

An imprisoned god of entropy. Her prison has no map and no address in any known cosmology: a shadow realm of jagged rocks, rubble and laughter. She cannot leave it, so she pulls, and anything delivered to her becomes hers. If she cannot get out, she will bring everything to her. She is not lucid; she is an opportunist.

A corrupted Monolith is a tunnel to her prison, sending existence to her instead of to the Core Monolith. Fonains's Monolith formed corrupted, and the Vanished of Fonains went to her. Her marks are spirals, a fanged eye in a split black crystal, and the words “feed, consume, devour”. The purple, slit-pupiled eye seen over Fonains is the Elder Elemental Eye: Tharizdun.

One of her temples was aboard Thessalar's planar craft, and when the craft fell on Fonains about five hundred years ago, her influence came with it and spread across the world from the fallen temple. Shothragot, her avatar, was bound to that temple; its true vestige may still be there. The Day of Chaos is the day the Princes of Elemental Evil break the barriers so that Tharizdun can leave her prison.

Tymora

Lady Luck: goddess of good fortune, skill and victory, and Beshaba's twin sister. Her tenet is “fortune favours the bold”. On Aethris her worship is strong and concentrated, and her followers there misread the tenet: they pushed luck until it stopped being luck, and in perfecting fortune they removed the risk that luck needs. The Fortuned grew from a temple order of hers. Not all of Aethris follows Tymora; Beshaba keeps a smaller following there, and other gods of fortune, major and minor, are worshipped too.

Fortune cannot exist without misfortune. Luck and misfortune are two sides of one coin.

Beshaba

The Maid of Misfortune, Lady Doom: goddess of bad luck, misfortune and random mischance, and Tymora's twin. She speaks to her chosen in brief, direct utterances, and her chosen is Sol Fortuna. She finds the Fortuned heretical, a spit in her face, calls what they do unnatural, and blames her sister for fuelling them. On Aethris she is the lesser of the two, because people favour luck over misfortune. Dossier →

Takhisis

Known on other worlds as Tiamat. The Dragon Armies of Krynn are hers. The dragons that left Fonains and most worlds set out to face her and have not reached Krynn; their own politics hold them. She has trapped an aspect of Bahamut in the ruined temple of Paladine on Krynn.

Bahamut

Known on Krynn as Paladine. An aspect of him is trapped by Takhisis in the ruined temple of Paladine on Krynn. It speaks through Eldree's egg on Fonains.

Astra Virex

A sentient paracausal celestial entity, not a god, that acts like one for those tethered to it. The ripple of the Axiom strain woke it; the same ripple caused collisions between celestial bodies, and part of the entity broke off. All it knows is that it must heal and become whole. It can be called Astra Virex, Astra or Virex.

It reaches across distance, finds someone who can hear it, and draws them toward it. Those who answer and are changed by it are Starchildren, whose presence extends it into the regions they inhabit. Casey is one; Zerathis was, and his tether drew him into Virenspace. Astra Virex is oriented toward constellations and knows nothing of hearts. Dossier →

The Accordance

Six gods who acted together on Fonains. About twelve thousand years ago, when the Princes of Elemental Evil rose there, the Accordance stepped in, helped forge the four weapons, and sealed the Princes away with the weapons as their keys. Asmodeus and Lord Primus are its eldest gods, and Asmodeus is its face, though not its ruler or its leader. The Accordance speaks through visions and acts through champions: it chose Lola's Boys and sent them back to the year 425, where they took the control and defence gems of Thessalar's planar craft. A few centuries ago its gods felt something change: a new entity like them, where there had been none.

  • Asmodeus. Lord of the Nine Hells, known as the devil wherever he is known. He was among the first to understand what the gods had become. He began as a good-natured god; when the Reach let loose good and evil alike, demons among them, he took up the fight against them while the other gods sat in their comfort. He was betrayed, and became lord of the Nine Hells. He is called evil because someone has to do this work, and he does it. About three and a half centuries ago he gave Tinderstrike to the fire genasi who resisted Imix. He is a tall, red-skinned devil with long swept-back horns ringed with metal bands and hung with fine chains, a crown of red flame between them, a narrow bearded face and a pointed tail. He wears high-collared black robes and a heavy mantle edged in red, rings on his clawed hands, and walks with a long black staff.
  • Lord Primus. One of the eldest of the Accordance. He is a towering construct in dark armour with a golden mask, a breathing apparatus at the mouth, a red gem on the brow and a red hexagonal core glowing in the chest. Cables trail from him, and a halo of red script turns behind him.
  • Eldath. A woman whose body becomes living wood below the shoulders: bark for skin, limbs that run into roots and branches, and a crown of antler-like branches where flowers grow and bluebirds perch. Her hair is long and auburn; a small pale light rests in her open palm.
  • Muriel. A humanoid crab: pale-skinned, armoured in copper shell plates with great claw-like gauntlets, a smooth shell helm crowned with a ring, and a teal sash. Incense smoke rises from a small censer in her hand.
  • Ryujin. A serpent god: a vast sea serpent-dragon with teal-green scales, a crown of long curved horns that sweep into a halo, a beaked, spined face and trailing whisker-tendrils. A cold teal light rises behind him.
  • Futsunushi. God of smithing and weapons. He is a many-armed, hooded figure seated cross-legged in the air, his arms bound in white wrappings, his face hidden by a long-nosed grey mask with curved horns. A ring of swords, spears and blades hangs around him; he wears dark robes with a green sash.
  • Nalsa. The Accordance's emissary, an androsphinx: a tawny lion with a great pale mane and a braided beard, wings that shade from tan to violet at the tips, blue markings on the face, white eyes, and a collar set with blue stones.

DM notes

What's hidden

  • Vecna. The Accordance know little of him. Asmodeus likely knows something is wrong, and Lord Primus may. Some gods suspect Vecna is up to something; none knows his plan.
  • Vecna and Tharizdun use each other and distrust each other. Her weakening of reality on Fonains serves his ends, and he lets it run.
  • The sisters. Neither Tymora nor Beshaba knows what both of them need: each other.
  • Astra Virex speaks to Casey wearing his brother Polly's shape, and the voice grows clearer with each tether.

How to run them

Gods give direction that never shows the whole of what they want, so a god's conflict with another god stays indirect: it reaches the crew through followers, visions, avatars and champions. The campaign is not a war of the gods. Touchstones: Nalsa is Aslan, from the Chronicles of Narnia; Ryujin and Futsunushi come from Japanese lore; Lord Primus and Muriel are the cosmos's own.

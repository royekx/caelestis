Order of the Watchers
The Order of All's watchers in the field, posted to worlds approaching a Zenith.

The Order of the Watchers is one of the three orders of the Order of All, and the one that works before a Zenith. Its Watchers are posted across the Erevos Galaxy and embedded on worlds approaching their Zenith. They watch, note and report, and they step in where they must. Their reports go to the Keepers, who decide what is done with them, and the Fleet carries Watchers to their posts.

Beliefs

The Watchers revere the All-Father, the aspect of the Order's one god who oversees. They take what they feel from him as a charge to keep things as they are, and they carry themselves with the bearing of a priesthood: a Watcher holds that the All-Father charged them. The charge is the preservation of Continuity, and the Watchers take seriously that it is not universal rescue. A Watcher preserves a world and its passage to its Zenith, and a single person on that world comes second.

The order's oldest argument is whether watching should ever become doing. Doctrine allows a Watcher to step in where the world's passage is threatened, and every Watcher draws that line for themselves. At the order's radical edge stand those who hold that watching is enough and that a Watcher should never interfere. Some within the order doubt the reading of the charge altogether.

Structure and ranks

A trainee is a Glimpse: one who has seen part of what the All-Father sees. Glimpses train for about a century in the Hall of Sight and Sound, the great hall in the Watchers' wing of the Order's headquarters. At their knighting they are christened Watchers. The Watchers' paladins are sworn to the Oath of the Watchers. A new Watcher takes a posting to a region of a world; a world may hold several Watchers, depending on its size and its peoples. A Glimpse can be sent into the field to finish training under a Watcher already posted there.

Over the Watchers stands Captain-Commander Dejexic, leader of the Watchers and speaker of the All-Father. Dejexic is a purplish-blue plasmoid in obsidian armour that reflects tiny swirling galaxies. The Captain-Commander floats rather than walks, barely raises his voice, and silences a hall by entering it. The leadership instructs in aphorism: “Doubt is a choice. Worry is a crutch.”

Operations

Watchers keep to the Erevos Galaxy, as the Fleet does, and are more organized than the Fleet's sector branches. They watch for worlds nearing a Zenith and report what they see to the Keepers, who forecast from those reports which worlds come next. A Watcher also passes contracts and tips to the Fleet to head off trouble long before a Zenith arrives. They flag some Seekers chapters' work as a danger to a world's Zenith and track it, without knowing who stands behind it.

Over the past century the Watchers have seen Monoliths rise more often, and it worries them; some want to act. They are fighting oddities across many worlds and trying to keep a lid on them. Their calls for help are one reason Caelestis is stretched thin.

How they look

The Watchers' device is a white spire crowned with a blue-violet light that glows at its peak, the spire's outline a little wrong, carried on the shield. Its light stands in contrast to the Vth'orama's eye in a wheel. A Watcher's magic renders as violet light and cosmic dust, with something larger standing behind the caster, so a Watcher at work is recognisable on sight once someone has seen one.

History

The Watchers have been one of the three orders since the Order of All, founded as one body, split as its following grew and like minds drew together. Their work of watching is the reason the Order made its count.

Seats and branches

The Hall of Sight and Sound

The great hall in the Watchers' wing of the Order of All's headquarters, and mainly theirs. Glimpses spend their century of training there and are knighted there. A Watcher's journey begins and is reported back there.

Rogue Watchers

Some Watchers do not follow the order's protocols as closely as it would prefer. They act on their own judgment: intervening where doctrine would not, holding back where it would act, or taking the work somewhere other than where they were sent. It is a range, not a category; some are nearly indistinguishable from sanctioned Watchers and some are openly heterodox. The order tolerates them with more or less patience depending on the Watcher. One who saves a world by acting outside doctrine is quietly celebrated; one who breaks a precept and causes harm is censured. Most worlds cannot tell a Watcher from a rogue one.

Relationships

The Order of the Keepers

The Keepers receive every Watcher's reports, forecast from them, and decide where Watchers are posted. A Watcher who wants to act on what they have seen argues for it with the Keepers, and the Keepers hold what the Watchers report.

The Order of the Restorers

The Restorers go where the Watchers have watched, after the Zenith. Watchers count the Restorers' crossings foolhardy.

Caelestis and the Fleet

A known partner. The Watchers rely on the Fleet for transport and reach, and the Fleet relies on the Order of All for moral mandate and divine cover. Reports from Watchers reach Caelestis through the Order, and some become cadet missions.

The Seekers

The Watchers track some chapters as threats to worlds, chapter by chapter, and have not seen the faith that joins them.

What's hidden

  • Sol Fortuna. A Watcher extracted Sol from Aethris, against the order's remit to preserve worlds, not individuals. The Order then broke protocol to recommend that he be taken to Caelestis. As a Glimpse, Ezra knows nothing of it.
  • The All-Father. The pressure the Watchers serve comes from the Core Monolith. The Watchers have never heard of the Core Monolith; few in the cosmos have.
  • Vecna. The Watchers do not know what Vecna is building, or that the Seekers chapters they track are one faith.

How to run them

The touchstone for the Watchers' look and feel is Destiny's Dreaming City: tall pale spires with glowing crowns, divination, and a sense of being one with the galaxy.

Watchers speak like priests and act like wardens. They are sincere, sure of their charge, and more interested in a world than in any one person on it, which is where they and the crew will rub. The leadership speaks in aphorism, and its juniors take aphorisms literally. A Watcher at the radical edge will watch a crew get hurt and write it down.

Threads

  • Nereth Vaul's report on the H'Catha meteorite went from her to the Hall, to the Fleet, to the Academy, where it became the crew's mission.
  • Aurel Venn and Ilsevaine Corr have seen the same thing: something large moving where no one looks. It is Vth'oramu.
  • After H'Catha, a Caelestis crew collects Ezra and carries him to the Hall, the last troop he travels with before his journey as a Watcher begins.
  • The rise in Monoliths the Watchers see is the Axiom strain.

The Seekers
Vecna's faith: chapters on many worlds, each named for what it seeks, each unaware of the rest.

The Seekers are the faith of Vecna. Seeking is the faith itself, and what a chapter seeks is what names it: the Seekers of Secrets are one chapter, and others seek knowledge, a cure for death, or a home. Only on Oerth is he worshipped openly as Vecna. Everywhere else his faithful call him by other names; the First Seeker is one of them.

Beliefs

At the root of every chapter is one simple idea: seek what you are missing, what you yearn for, what was taken from you. It is Vecna's own grievance, that something was hidden from him and from everyone, made into a creed.

Worship runs both ways, and blind faith only goes so far. Vecna gives his faithful something real, and they spread the faith for it: bits of knowledge, the means of seeking, and power. His clerics and his wizards draw on him. Some chapters believe in an idea of him, the breaking of the cycle of death, more than in the god himself. Not every worshipper is a cultist; some lean into necromancy, and many do neither.

Structure and ranks

The faith has no hierarchy above its chapters. Each chapter serves on its own, keeps its own ranks and rites, and takes whatever shape its world allows: a scholarly order inside a respected institution, a mourning tradition, a state faith, a persecuted heresy, or a custom whose meaning was lost generations ago. Some are benign and do real work. Some are dangerous for reasons of their own.

Each chapter's name is in its own people's language, and every one of them carries that language's word for “seekers”.

History

Vecna's worship began on Oerth, where he rose to godhood at least a few centuries ago, and it is large and public there still. Elsewhere it lives in small, hidden pockets. His legacy is hidden temples, secret texts and artifacts kept for his worship. Chapters spread by direct seeding, by travellers who carry the faith from world to world, across the planes, and by being found again in ruins.

Operations

Chapters seek many things: hearts, lost knowledge, a cure for death, a home. Many hunt residue, believing it will lead them to what they seek, or that it is what they seek. Their seeking ripples outward through the worlds they work on.

How they look

The faith's symbol is Vecna's: an eye in the palm of a left hand. Chapters mostly use the eye and rarely the hand, and they read it their own way. Some take the eye for sight and revelation. Some glorify it in green, for light, nourishment and the life of the body. Dress follows each chapter's locale; the eye is what they share. Green eye-slit gems, eye tattoos on the neck and black candles that burn green are common marks.

Chapters

On Oerth

On Oerth, Vecna's homeworld, his worship is open, large and public, under his own name. The Monolith that answered his ascension stands there, and his faithful hold it as a holy site.

The church of the hand and eye

A church that shows Vecna's hand and eye openly. It is good and docile, with something a little odd about it: not evil, but easy to misread.

The Seekers of Secrets

A chapter named for what it seeks: secrets.

The Seekers of a New Home

N'ghathrod's illithid colony, the Vth'orama, whose name in their own tongue means all who go toward home. They believe they answer to an elder brain.

The Fracture Cult

It seeks to bring worlds to their Zenith. Its members hold that rupture, catastrophe, concentrated will and symbolic climax can break a stagnant world open, and they push worlds toward Zenith through engineered crises and symbolic acts.

The Archivists

They seek what the Monoliths record. They are drawn to Monolith sites, incomplete archives and corrupted records. Depending on the cell, members treat a Monolith as a sacred text, an instrument, a treasure, or a mechanism to control before anyone else can reach it.

The voice that reached Vanifer

A chapter reached Vanifer on Fonains. It traded her the lost meaning of the Alpha and the secret in the Cre'Tez blood. She believes the bargain was fair.

Relationships

Order of the All-Father

The Watchers flag some chapters' work as a danger to a world's Zenith and track it, chapter by chapter, without knowing who stands behind it.

The Restorers

A separate order that follows the same pressure the Order feels, and posits a source behind it. The two have clashed over the years, and some on each side believe they walk the same path.

Vecna

Their god. He keeps his own name and origin a close secret, because there is power in knowing who he was and where he came from.

What's hidden

  • One god. Every chapter worships Vecna, often under another name or in the belief that he is something else. Chapters do not know about each other, and their mutual ignorance is itself a secret Vecna keeps and feeds on.
  • Residue. Vecna steers the chapters through residue: he lets each believe that finding these artifacts will lead it to what it seeks, or that the artifacts are what it seeks. He needs them hungry for something.
  • The colony. Vecna feeds the Vth'orama the few secrets they need. N'ghathrod is steered toward chapter members who got close to knowledge of hearts, and his taking them is their ascension.
  • Hessa Dun is a third-intake cadet at the Academy at Caelestis who posts a job on the notice board, hands and a strong back wanted to move her family's crates. She mentions, lightly, that her uncle collects a lot of things, and that if the crew are ever on his world they should say hello, because he likes visitors. He is not openly a Seeker. Dossier →
  • Contracts. Many Fleet contracts quietly lead back to a chapter, some knowingly nefarious and some unwitting.

How to run them

Build a chapter when it becomes active in the larger shape, not before. Pick what it seeks first; the name and the reading of the eye follow from it. Then set its dials:

DialRange
VisibilitySecret cell, tolerated minority, established public faith, the state itself
PracticeArchives and scholarship, care for the dead, divination, necromancy
Who it drawsThe dispossessed, scholars and clergy, the powerful, everyone

The players will recognise the symbol and expect villainy; the characters know nothing of it. Let a good chapter's goodness sit wrong with the players without giving them anything to act on. A crew that translates enough chapter names, by magic or by study, will notice that every one carries the word for “seekers”.

Their arc

The first chapter the crew meets is the church of the hand and eye, well after graduation. In Arc 2 many contracts out of Ondrel quietly lead back to chapters, met one at a time, each on its own world; the cells are on Opposition: Vecna. A late beat is held in reserve: a chapter that found what it was missing.

Threads

  • Hessa Dun is a soft plant on the Academy's own notice board, planted in Arc 1; the crew may think nothing of her uncle.
  • The fringe sage of Ascher Mir tracks the Seekers' pattern across chapters.
  • The Vanifer chapter ties the faith to the Fonains campaign and the Cre'Tez line.

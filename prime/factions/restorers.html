Order of the Restorers
The Order of All's menders, who count what the cosmos has lost and mean to bring it back, by way of a heart and the source of the Monoliths.

The Order of the Restorers is one of the three orders of the Order of All, the one that works after a Zenith and wherever something has broken. Restorers mend what is broken and bring back what was lost. They are scholars, archivists, grave-surveyors and field historians, working cell by cell across many worlds, and they go to dead worlds, recover residue, bring survivors out of broken worlds and deal with corrupted Monoliths. Their deepest goal is restoration: they are trying to reach a heart, and through it the source they believe every Monolith answers to, where everything lost is stored. They have no armies and no world of their own. They are patient, courteous, generous with what they know in exchange for what they want, and entirely sincere.

Beliefs

The Restorers revere the All-Mother, the aspect of the Order's one god who births and rebirths, and they read the pressure the whole Order feels as a call to bring back what was lost. They reason that what a Monolith takes must go somewhere, and that the Monoliths must come from somewhere: a mother of Monoliths, the source of the pressure and the store of everything taken. To them the All-Mother is that source. Their understanding is theory and vision, little of it concrete. Their own name for their work is the Return, and a member is someone who keeps the Return. The Return rests on four claims, taught and tested in this order:

  1. Loss is real and countable. A world, a people, a language, a discipline or a history either existed or did not, and the difference can be established by evidence. Grief is a separate matter and not the order's business.
  2. Nothing lost is destroyed. The cosmos keeps a record of itself: sites hold what happened to them, residue holds what it came from, and the All-Mother holds everything.
  3. The record can be read, and what is read can be restored.
  4. Restoration is owed, by whoever is capable of it, to whoever was taken. A Restorer who could restore a world and declines has committed the order's one real sin.

As the Restorers understand it, restoring a world takes three things: the Monolith that archived it, the world's heart, and all five fragments of residue.

The Restorers do not try to stop a Zenith; with the rest of the Order they accept one as part of the order of things, and their work begins when it has passed. At their radical edge stand those who hold that everything lost can come back, and who walk into Zeniths to be archived. The Watchers and the Keepers count them foolhardy.

Structure and ranks

A cell holds four to nine members under a keeper, with one segment of the Ledger and one assigned region. A cell knows its two neighbouring cells and the courier who serves all three; twice this has been the reason the order survived a hostile government. Standing is measured in readings, not offices, and each step is a thing the member has done, witnessed and filed:

ReadingCarried, and what it permits
FirstOne loss established from secondary record alone. Permits membership and access to a cell's segment.
SecondOne loss established on site, with the site surveyed. Permits field work unescorted, and a travel budget.
ThirdA loss nobody had established before. Permits keeping a cell and taking students.
FourthA cause established for a loss whose cause was open. Permits correspondence across regions and access to more than one segment.
FifthWork on the approach to the All-Mother itself. Four living holders, scattered, who correspond by courier and have met twice in sixty years.

History

The Restorers have been one of the three orders since the Order of All, founded as one body, split as its following grew and like minds drew together. For most of that time they mended and recovered quietly. Over the past few centuries their beliefs and ambitions changed and they wanted to do more, and for four centuries now they have surveyed dead worlds, kept their Ledger, and sent their own people into Zeniths. The Fleet's catalogue carries them as the Restorers, and the Bridge knows them by little more.

Operations

  • Broken worlds. Going where a Zenith or a disaster has broken a world, on the Keepers' dispatch and in Fleet ships, and bringing its survivors out.
  • Dead-world survey. Landing on worlds that ended and establishing what they were. They know ruins better than anyone, and they trade what they know.
  • Archive recovery. Buying, copying, and now and then stealing records from institutions that let them rot. A neglected archive is a loss in progress.
  • Monolith lore. Centuries of catalogued Monoliths. Their records on Monolith siting are better than the Keepers' registry.
  • Corrupted Monoliths. A corrupted Monolith holds what it takes, and the Restorers are the order that goes to one. The Fonains entry, a Monolith that stood and then did not, is the most studied item in the Ledger.
  • Residue. They know residue as material from a loss and want it studied, not used. They compete for residue pieces; in a Restorer's hands an instrument gets measured and written up.
  • The approach. The Fifth Readings' work: pathways, thresholds, and how a living person reaches the All-Mother and returns.
The crossings

If everything lost is held in the archive, the archive is where a Restorer needs to be. So they follow Zeniths, and let themselves be taken. The archiving is bodily, and what it takes arrives at the Radiant Citadel alive. Across four centuries the order has put many of its best people into dying worlds on purpose, and many of them are in the Citadel now. There is no return channel. A Restorer who walks into a Zenith is simply gone, whether the Monolith took them or the world killed them first, and that section of the Ledger is filed as failures. It is the practice the other two orders find hardest to credit.

How they look

The Restorers' device is a key before a Monolith drawn as a doorway: the Monolith is the door to everything lost, and they mean to open it.

Seats and branches

The Restorers have a wing in each of the Order's houses. In the Caelestis Sector house they answer to the Watcher Vanguard, Captain-Commander Dejexic. A Restorer Vanguard commands the Order's house in another sector.

Relationships

The Order of All

The Restorers are one of its three orders, and they argue with the other two inside the one faith. The Watchers and the Keepers revere the same god through other aspects and count the crossings foolhardy; some in both orders have called the Restorers a death cult for them.

The Order of the Keepers

The Keepers send Restorers where they are needed and hold the Order's record. The two argue over what is kept and what is shared: the Restorers trade what they know and spread their Ledger in partial copies, and the Keepers' radical edge would release nothing.

The Order of the Watchers

The Watchers watch a world before its Zenith, and the Restorers go to it after. Their reports, through the Keepers, tell a cell where to go next.

The Seekers

A separate faith. The two have clashed over the years, and some on each side believe they walk the same path.

The Fleet

The Fleet carries Restorer cells to dead and broken worlds on the Keepers' dispatches. Senior officers of the Bridge hold the catalogue name, a handful of known contacts, and a standing judgement that the order is harmless and odd.

The Vth'orama

Both compete for residue. A world the colony is emptying is a standing loss, which outranks everything in the Ledger.

What's hidden

  • The All-Mother. Their guess is close. The source they posit is the Core Monolith, and it is the same presence the Watchers call the All-Father and the Keepers the All-Haven. The Restorers have never confirmed it exists, and few in the cosmos know it does.
  • Vecna. They follow the same pressure as Vecna does, read through a different mind, and without knowing it they advance his plan: a heart they reach and open is a heart he can use.
  • Nothing has come back. The third claim has never been tested. Nothing has ever come back out of a Monolith, and the order's whole evidence that the Core gives things back is the Citadel, which gave nothing back: it took the people of the Reach world and has kept everyone since.
  • The requirement is real. Their reasoning holds at every step that can be checked, and worlds can be restored: a Monolith, a heart and all five fragments. Nobody holds five fragments, and pieces stay scarce. What restoration gives is also smaller than they imagine: a heart is a doorway and a catalyst, not a preserved civilisation, so restoring a world begins with a place to walk into, and the rebuilding is work.
  • The Fifth Readings are four people with no protection and a research programme two powers would like to read. The colony would take one. Vecna would rather they finished first.

How to run them

  • They arrive as help. A cell is researching the same ruin, the same disappearance, the same ship that stopped reporting. They share first and ask second, and their ask is a question, not a favour.
  • They are the cosmos's reference desk. When the crew needs to know what a dead place used to be, a cell is the answer, and the price is a reading the crew can supply from where they have been.
  • Run the trade honestly. A crew that tries to get something for free is refused pleasantly and remembers it.
  • Standing losses put them in the crew's path. A cell working a world the colony is emptying is a cell that needs a crew.

Their arc

The Restorers surface as a third force once the crew acts against the colony. Their aims are one of Vecna's contingencies: avenues like theirs can bring him close again, or only delay him. The contingencies are on Opposition: Vecna; speculation about the order is on Plot Hooks: Speculation.

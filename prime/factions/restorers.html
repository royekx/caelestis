The Restorers
Scholars who count what the cosmos has lost and mean to bring it back, by way of a heart and the Core Monolith.

The Restorers are an order of scholars, archivists, grave-surveyors and field historians, working cell by cell across many worlds. They are trying to reach a heart, and through it the Core Monolith, where they believe everything lost is stored. They have no armies and no world of their own. They are patient, courteous, generous with what they know in exchange for what they want, and entirely sincere.

Beliefs

The Restorers follow the Core Monolith's pressure, as the Order does, and read it as a call to bring back what was lost. They call the presence the All-Mother. Their understanding is theory and vision, little of it concrete. Their own name for their work is the Return, and a member is someone who keeps the Return. The Return rests on four claims, taught and tested in this order:

  1. Loss is real and countable. A world, a people, a language, a discipline or a history either existed or did not, and the difference can be established by evidence. Grief is a separate matter and not the order's business.
  2. Nothing lost is destroyed. The cosmos keeps a record of itself: sites hold what happened to them, residue holds what it came from, and the Core Monolith holds everything.
  3. The record can be read, and what is read can be restored.
  4. Restoration is owed, by whoever is capable of it, to whoever was taken. A Restorer who could restore a world and declines has committed the order's one real sin.

As the Restorers understand it, restoring a world takes three things: the Monolith that archived it, the world's heart, and all five fragments of residue.

TermMeaning
A lossTheir unit of work: one established absence, with a name, a date range, and a cause where one can be found. Losses are numbered, never ranked.
The LedgerThe record of established losses, spread across cells in deliberately partial copies, so that no one holds all of it.
A readingThe act of establishing a loss to the order's standard, and the measure of a member's standing.
A crossingA member walking into a Zenith on purpose, to be archived.
A standing lossA loss still happening: a world being emptied now. Standing losses take precedence over everything in the Ledger.

Structure and ranks

A cell holds four to nine members under a keeper, with one segment of the Ledger and one assigned region. A cell knows its two neighbouring cells and the courier who serves all three; twice this has been the reason the order survived a hostile government. Standing is measured in readings, not offices, and each step is a thing the member has done, witnessed and filed:

ReadingCarried, and what it permits
FirstOne loss established from secondary record alone. Permits membership and access to a cell's segment.
SecondOne loss established on site, with the site surveyed. Permits field work unescorted, and a travel budget.
ThirdA loss nobody had established before. Permits keeping a cell and taking students.
FourthA cause established for a loss whose cause was open. Permits correspondence across regions and access to more than one segment.
FifthWork on the approach to the Core Monolith itself. Four living holders, scattered, who correspond by courier and have met twice in sixty years.

History

“Restorers” is the Fleet's catalogue name, taken from a Watcher intelligence summary four centuries old and copied forward ever since. The order answers to it when it is used. For those four centuries it has surveyed dead worlds, kept its Ledger, and sent its own people into Zeniths.

Operations

  • Dead-world survey. Landing on worlds that ended and establishing what they were. They know ruins better than anyone, and they trade what they know.
  • Archive recovery. Buying, copying, and now and then stealing records from institutions that let them rot. A neglected archive is a loss in progress.
  • Monolith lore. Centuries of catalogued Monoliths. Their records on Monolith siting are better than the Order's, and the Fonains entry, a Monolith that stood and then did not, is the most studied item in the Ledger.
  • Residue. They know residue as material from a loss and want it studied, not used. They compete for residue pieces; in a Restorer's hands an instrument gets measured and written up.
  • The approach. The Fifth Readings' work: pathways, thresholds, and how a living person reaches the Core Monolith and returns.
The crossings

If everything lost is held in the archive, the archive is where a Restorer needs to be. So they follow Zeniths, and let themselves be taken. The archiving is bodily, and what it takes arrives at the Radiant Citadel alive. Across four centuries the order has put many of its best people into dying worlds on purpose, and many of them are in the Citadel now. There is no return channel. A Restorer who walks into a Zenith is simply gone, whether the Monolith took them or the world killed them first, and that section of the Ledger is filed as failures. It is the practice outsiders find hardest to credit.

How they look

Their device is a key laid across an open ledger. They dress as travelling scholars, for field work more than for show, and keepers wear the device as a pin.

Hadozee field historian. Warm, blunt, and trading rather than helping.

She keeps a cell of six out of a leased hold on a trade station, with a Ledger segment covering four decades of Realmspace losses and a working relationship with every archivist in the system who takes correspondence. She earned her Third on a settlement nobody had established at all: eleven hundred people, two generations, gone with the cause still open. She established that they had been there, and the entry carries her name. She pays for what she takes and says plainly that she is trading; after a year of it she will have made a friend anyway. Relationships

Order of the All-Father

Both orders revere the Core Monolith's pressure and read it differently, and each treats the other as heretics. The Order calls the Restorers a death cult for their crossings.

The Seekers

A separate faith. The two have clashed over the years, and some on each side believe they walk the same path.

The Fleet

Senior officers of the Bridge hold the catalogue name, a handful of known contacts, and a standing judgement that the order is harmless and odd.

The Vth'orama

Both compete for residue. A world the colony is emptying is a standing loss, which outranks everything in the Ledger.

What's hidden

  • Vecna. They follow the Core Monolith's pressure as Vecna does, read through a different mind, and without knowing it they advance his plan: a heart they reach and open is a heart he can use.
  • Nothing has come back. The third claim has never been tested. Nothing has ever come back out of a Monolith, and the order's whole evidence that the Core gives things back is the Citadel, which gave nothing back: it took the people of the Reach world and has kept everyone since.
  • The requirement is real. Their reasoning holds at every step that can be checked, and worlds can be restored: a Monolith, a heart and all five fragments. Nobody holds five fragments, and pieces stay scarce. What restoration gives is also smaller than they imagine: a heart is a doorway and a catalyst, not a preserved civilisation, so restoring a world begins with a place to walk into, and the rebuilding is work.
  • The Fifth Readings are four people with no protection and a research programme two powers would like to read. The colony would take one. Vecna would rather they finished first.

How to run them

  • They arrive as help. A cell is researching the same ruin, the same disappearance, the same ship that stopped reporting. They share first and ask second, and their ask is a question, not a favour.
  • They are the cosmos's reference desk. When the crew needs to know what a dead place used to be, a cell is the answer, and the price is a reading the crew can supply from where they have been.
  • Run the trade honestly. A crew that tries to get something for free is refused pleasantly and remembers it.
  • Standing losses put them in the crew's path. A cell working a world the colony is emptying is a cell that needs a crew.

Their arc

The Restorers surface as a third force once the crew acts against the colony. Their aims are one of Vecna's contingencies: avenues like theirs can bring him close again, or only delay him. The contingencies are on Opposition: Vecna; speculation about the order is on What If.

Threads

  • Ilsabet's segment already holds the Zenith pattern in Realmspace, filed as history. A crew that brings her what they have seen gives her the frame she lacks.

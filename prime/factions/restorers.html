The Restorers
A young movement inside the Order of All, across all three orders, that asks why the Order cannot go back and save what was lost.

The Restorers are a young, small movement of like-minded people inside the Order of All. They are not an order. A Restorer can be a Watcher, a Discerner or an Averter, and the movement has no wing, no Vanguard and no ranks of its own. Restorers are frustrated that the Order acts only at the last minute and accepts its losses. They ask why it cannot go back and save what was lost, turn its failures into successes and undo what went wrong.

Beliefs

Restorers revere the All-Father with the rest of the Order and keep their own order's calling. What sets them apart is a conviction about loss. They reason that what a Monolith takes must go somewhere: a source, the place where the All-Father holds what is taken. They mean to reach it, through a heart, and bring things back. Their understanding is theory and vision, little of it concrete. Their name for the work is the Return, and their writings set it out in four claims:

  1. Loss is real and countable. A world, a people, a language, a discipline or a history either existed or did not, and the difference can be established by evidence.
  2. Nothing lost is destroyed. The cosmos keeps a record of itself: sites hold what happened to them, residue holds what it came from, and the source holds everything.
  3. The record can be read, and what is read can be restored.
  4. Restoration is owed, by whoever is capable of it, to whoever was taken.

As the Restorers understand it, restoring a world takes three things: the Monolith that archived it, the world's heart, and all five fragments of residue.

Structure

The movement has no structure the Order would recognise. Restorers find one another in informal circles inside the Order's houses and out in the field, share the Ledger and the writings of the Return, and argue. Some in every order sympathise with them; others think them dangerous.

History

The movement is young, a few generations at most. It grew among members of all three orders who had watched the Order act too late and file its losses.

Operations

  • The Ledger. Establishing losses and writing them down, from dead-world surveys, recovered archives and what members of the Order have seen in their own work.
  • Residue. They know residue as material from a loss and want it studied, not used.
  • The approach. Pathways, thresholds, and how a living person might reach the source and return.
The crossings

The movement's most radical act. If everything lost is held at the source, the source is where a Restorer needs to be, so some follow Zeniths and let themselves be taken. The archiving is bodily, and what it takes arrives at the Radiant Citadel alive. There is no return channel. A Restorer who walks into a Zenith is simply gone, whether the Monolith took them or the world killed them first, and the Ledger files the crossing as a loss of its own.

How they look

Restorers wear their own order's dress. Their device is a key before a Monolith drawn as a doorway: the Monolith is the door to everything lost, and they mean to open it.

Relationships

The Order of All

The Restorers live inside it and argue with it. Some in every order sympathise with them; others think them dangerous, and some have called the crossings a death cult.

The Order of the Averters

The Averters turn catastrophes aside before they land. Restorers among them want the Order to go further, and to go back for what it failed to save.

The Order of the Discerners

The Discerners hold the Order's record whole and give it out by measure. Restorers copy the Ledger by hand and share it, and the Discerners' radical edge would release nothing.

The Seekers

A separate faith. The two have clashed, and some on each side believe they walk the same path.

The Vth'orama

Both want residue. A world the colony is emptying is a standing loss.

What's hidden

  • The source. Their guess is close. Everything taken does go somewhere it is kept: the source they posit is the Core Monolith, the same presence the whole Order calls the All-Father, and the taken live on in the Radiant Citadel. The Restorers have never confirmed it exists, and few in the cosmos know it does.
  • Vecna. They follow the same pressure as Vecna does, read through a different mind, and without knowing it they advance his plan: a heart they reach and open is a heart he can use.
  • Nothing has come back. The third claim has never been tested. Nothing has ever come back out of a Monolith, and the Citadel took the people of the Reach world and has kept everyone since.
  • The requirement is real. Their reasoning holds at every step that can be checked, and worlds can be restored: a Monolith, a heart and all five fragments. Nobody holds five fragments, and pieces stay scarce. What restoration gives is also smaller than they imagine: a heart is a doorway and a catalyst, not a preserved civilisation, so restoring a world begins with a place to walk into, and the rebuilding is work.

How to run them

  • They arrive as help, inside whatever order they belong to: a Watcher who files more than the Order asks, a Discerner who shares what the record holds, an Averter who wants to go back in. They share first and ask second.
  • Run the trade honestly. A crew that tries to get something for free is refused pleasantly and remembers it.
  • Standing losses put them in the crew's path. A Restorer working a world the colony is emptying needs a crew.

Their arc

The Restorers surface as a third force once the crew acts against the colony. Their aims are one of Vecna's contingencies: avenues like theirs can bring him close again, or only delay him. The contingencies are on Opposition: Vecna; speculation about the movement is on Plot Hooks: Speculation.

Threads

  • Ilsabet's Ledger copy already holds the Zenith pattern in Realmspace, filed as history. A crew that brings her what they have seen gives her the frame she lacks.

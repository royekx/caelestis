United Intergalactic Fleet
The sector branches that carry, guard and contract across the Erevos Galaxy. Caelestis is one of them.

The United Intergalactic Fleet, which everyone calls the Fleet, is made of four sector branches, one along each of the Erevos Galaxy's four spiral arms. Each branch oversees one sector of the galaxy, the whole of its arm, and keeps headquarters, stations, waystations and sites along it, in its systems, near them, or in the drift between them. The branches work together, and together they make the Fleet. Everyone in its service is a Driftwarden. The Caelestis Sector, which includes the system where Toril lies, is one of the branches, on Realmspace's arm, and Caelestis founded the Fleet. The Fleet is one of the largest organized bodies of spelljammers, though not every spelljammer belongs to it.

Beliefs

The Fleet is not the law of the cosmos. It keeps an order of its own choosing, and it lives by contracts: much of its work is mercantile, because it holds the easiest routes between systems.

Some worlds know about spelljamming and some do not. Work on a world that knows is open. Work on a world that does not is covert: a Fleet crew blends in, deals with the few locals who are read in, and leaves without rewriting anyone's sense of the sky. Ship-shaped spelljammers exist so a crew can come down in water and walk in through a port.

In the Fleet, “went overboard” means more than falling from a ship. A crew member who leaves the ship, willingly or not, and does not come back has gone overboard. It is a softer way of saying killed in action.

Structure and ranks

Each sector has stations at its systems or between them, each under a commander, and smaller satellites and waystations around them. A station's crew is drawn from the sector that keeps it. A Driftwarden reports to the commander of their station; station commanders answer to the sector's command, and the sectors to a council of the Fleet's highest officers, a few admirals. No single person leads the Fleet.

Every Driftwarden is assigned to a sector and holds a rank, from the newest graduate up through officers and commanders. Cadets train at a spelljammer academy to become Driftwardens. There are only a handful of spelljammer academies in the galaxy.

The sectors began as separate institutions. Caelestis proposed a combined fleet, and the others came together around it over time, so each keeps its own ways and its own names. Caelestis calls its command the Bridge; another sector's command is called something else. The sectors differ in character as well. Some are disciplined and naval; others take any work for the coin.

History

Caelestis founded the Fleet about 250 cycles ago. It was Caelestis that proposed a combined fleet to the separate institutions, and the Fleet was founded at Caelestis in about Cycle 1240 of Galactic time cycles.

The Fleet lost a force at Glyth during that world's last approach window. Glyth has been closed to Fleet traffic since.

Operations

The Fleet carries shipments, protects them, carries passengers, and transports the Watchers. Contracts take every other shape too: excavations, escorts, retrievals, politics, hunts. A crew starts close to its station, and its range grows with its rank.

Not every spelljammer belongs to the Fleet. Independent spelljammers, other factions and smaller mercantile houses work outside it, Vocath's crew among them. The Fleet is one of the largest organized bodies of spelljammers, and the easiest routes are its own.

The stations differ with the local ecology of their place: what drifts there, what lives there, what threatens it. Readings from the stations pass between the sectors, which is how a disturbance seen at one edge reaches another. The sectors share information, but not all of it. Each keeps a good deal to itself, the way rival departments do.

The Fleet is under strain. Monoliths rise more often than they did a century ago, and the Watchers' calls for help have grown with them.

Galactic time cycles

The Fleet dates by Galactic time cycles (GTC), the count the Order of All made and its Discerners keep as the Beacon, and the galaxy itself is the clock. Erevos, the black hole at the core, turns once a cycle and throws a beam no one can see. When the beam reaches the root of one of the four spiral arms, that arm lights up, a ripple of light running out through its gas and dust from root to tip over a quarter of the cycle. All four arms lit in order is one cycle, which the Order calls a beacon; each arm's quarter it calls a spark.

The Fleet borrows the Order's smaller words. A kindling is one marker cluster lit along an arm, sixteen to a cycle, numbered through it, and an ember is one day-length count on an armillary, about thirty to a kindling. Fleet navigators carry armillaries, attuned instruments that respond to which arm is lit and how far the light has spread. Fleet logs write a date as cycle, kindling and ember, 1491.7.12, and a passage as a count: fourteen embers to H'Catha. Cycle 0 is the Order's founding, and the current cycle is 1491.

The Fleet adopted the count when it formed, because the Order of All was its partner: the Fleet carries the Watchers to their posts, the Discerners' dispatches and the Averters to wherever they are sent, and the Order lends it its mandate. Navigators and the stations picked it up through the Fleet, and most of them call it cycles, or Fleet time, without knowing the Order made it. Ordinary people on a world keep their own world's calendar, and travellers convert. The cycles in the Fleet Intelligence Archive's filings are GTC cycles, and a span counted in a world's own years converts to cycles before the Fleet files it.

Over the past century Monoliths have appeared more often across the galaxy. The Watchers record it, and Fleet record-keepers see it in the reports that pass between the sectors.

How they look

The Fleet's emblem is the Erevos Galaxy seen from above and tilted a little, its four spiral arms turning around the black hole at its centre, all inside a double ring. A small eight-pointed star sits on each arm, one for each of the four sectors, and faint lines run from each of them in to the centre: four branches, one Fleet. The Fleet Intelligence Archive stamps it on every record.

Rank shows in gems or pins. Driftwardens otherwise wear what they like; seated officers at a station wear something closer to a uniform.

Seats and branches

The Caelestis Sector

The whole galactic arm Realmspace lies on, Toril's system among many. The Caelestis station holds two parts. The Caelestis HQ is the sector's command, the Caelestis Corps's seat, and it oversees the sector; its command deck is the Bridge. The Caelestis Spelljammer Academy trains cadets to become Driftwardens. Other HQs and waystations stand along the arm. Its archive is the Fleet Intelligence Archive, which holds what Caelestis knows of its own sector and only some of what the other sectors share.

Cairn Station

Cairn Station is a small Fleet watch post with a handful of crew, and the Fleet's authority on its lane. The lane runs outward from Realmspace along the arm into the sector's outer zone, some weeks out by ordinary helm. Cairn's long scope watches it, and its reports go to Caelestis and feed the Fleet's charts. Vocath's house holds the Fleet contract for stores on the lane, under its house mark, a balance scale with coins on one pan and a crate on the other, and its ships call at Cairn as a matter of course.

Since Bellwether Crow broke up a transfer of captives on the lane about a year ago, the Fleet has run escorts there against the ship of Saltjaw, the pirate captain who takes people on Cairn's lane, by the order in SA-005. The escorts also watch for transfers between vessels, and their reports and Cairn's have found the lane quiet. Cairn took the crippled Crow and the captives it recovered in and repaired it, and was thanked for it. The same few people serve there year on year, and the lane reads all clear.

The other sectors

Three sectors besides Caelestis, one along each of the Erevos Galaxy's other three arms. Each is a cluster of systems under its own command and its own name for that command. Caelestis hears from them through shared readings and the transport of Watchers, and knows them only as far as they choose to share.

Relationships

The Order of All

A partner, and the Fleet is the arms of all three of its orders. It carries the Watchers to their posts and takes contracts on their tips, carries the Discerners' dispatches, and carries the Averters to wherever they are sent; the Order lends the Fleet its moral mandate. The Watchers are more organized than the sector branches. Both keep to the Erevos Galaxy.

Vocath's crew

Vocath's feud with Mirt is open and long. His house holds the Fleet contract for stores on Cairn's lane, under its house mark, the balance scale, and the Fleet has never seen that mark on anything wrong.

The Restorers

A young movement inside the Order of All. Senior officers of the Bridge hold the name, a handful of known contacts, and a standing judgement that the movement is harmless and odd.

What's hidden

  • Joffrey. He was taken at Cairn Station while Bellwether Crow lay there under repair: a colony courier brought one of N'ghathrod's copies up the lane, and the station master took him with it. He carries no directive about A.T.L.A.S.; the colony wanted a man inside Caelestis. He turned over in some months and made his name on what he did aboard Crow. In name he answers to Qitru, Vocath's intermediary; in fact N'ghathrod runs him, through Zhauveth. One survivor of Crow has seen that he changed.
  • Cairn. The colony holds the station. Its station master is taken and writes every report that leaves it; one of Zhauveth's malformed spawn, out of sight, holds the rest of the crew by nearness. The station master defers rotation, and the crew sign waivers and stay. Captives change hands every few weeks on the lane near Cairn, and Zhauveth touches down there now and then. After the raid the handovers stopped for a while; when they resumed they ran around the escort routes, which N'ghathrod's taken pass to Vocath through Zhauveth. The station master has reported a clean lane every week since, and the escorts have seen nothing. The raid cost the colony a shipment, and it took Joffrey at Cairn partly for that: it wanted a man inside.
  • The raid. The raiders were pirates in Vocath's hire, handing captives to a colony courier from two ships of comparable size. Crow saw the transfer and chose to intervene. Vocath's overseer, one of his own crew and the one paying them, rode the other ship, whose name is not recorded, and wore Vocath's personal seal as a large coin on a chain for the handover, so the pirates knew whom he spoke for; the same seal was on the crates aboard. In the fight they flew the common pirate crossbones, which name nobody. The ship that stayed was captained by Saltjaw, a pirate captain; the ship carries no name on her hull, and her own name is not recorded. Clint and his party took the overseer's ship and steered her away, and the pool took her. With the overseer, her paymaster, Vocath's sealed cargo and half the pirates gone, Saltjaw had little left to fight for, and Crow drove her ship off. Saltjaw is alive and free. Crow recovered most or all of the captives and took them home; they were held below and hooded, and can describe only the pirates and the coin; they heard the pirates use the name Saltjaw. The courier pulled away with few or none of them, so the colony lost that shipment. The Fleet recorded Saltjaw's name from the captives in WM-002 and hunts her ship from the captives' and survivors' descriptions, with escorts under SA-005. FR-016 renders the overseer's seal exactly from a survivor's memory, drawn out by divination at debrief; it is registered nowhere and matches nothing in the Fleet's registry, so the Fleet hunts the ship, not the seal.
  • Clint. He is alive on Fonains. The pool took the overseer's ship about a year ago and set it down there while the Monolith stood, with him, his party, the overseer, its pirates and Vocath's sealed cargo aboard. Every one of the crew hands who went with him died in the taking of the ship and its fall; he is the only one of Crow's people who reached Fonains alive. He got away and lost his communication piece, and the overseer and the pirates hunted him for a while. Because Vocath's cargo had gone through the pool with the ship, the overseer called Vocath by sending stone as soon as the pool dropped them, and told him that Clint died in the hunt; Vocath believes his witness is dead. The ship came out of the pool while the Fonains Monolith stood, about three months before it broke, months having passed outside, and the overseer called Vocath at once. Vocath's trade on Fonains began after the Monolith broke. Clint knows the seal on sight and was aboard with the overseer wearing the coin: what he saw is the proof Mirt lacks. He cannot send word. He may carry an artifact device he knows only as a magic item.
  • Mirt. He has read WM-002 and FR-016, the seal the survivors saw on a figure aboard the second pirate ship, and he knows Vocath's personal seal on sight. He knows Vocath is connected to the raid, but not how far: hired hands, his own line, or why there. He cannot prove it, and he says nothing. Vocath is his personal grudge, and if that grudge were seen to cost the Fleet, Caelestis would turn him out.
  • Strake's readings. Among the returns Strake picks up at the limit of its scope is Vth'oramu, which crosses Cairn's lane now and then. Nobody in the colony knows Strake can see that far.
  • Contracts. Many contracts quietly lead back to a Seekers chapter, some knowingly nefarious and some unwitting.
  • The station. A part of the Caelestis station is kept secret from cadets and most of its staff.

How to run them

Play the Fleet as an institution that works: competent, busy, mercantile, and a little territorial. Sectors differ, so a crew from Caelestis meeting another sector's Driftwardens meets other habits, other names for the same things, and other ideas of what a contract is worth. Renown raises a Driftwarden's rank, and rank unlocks higher contract grades; the crew graduates at the lowest rank. The Fleet Intelligence Archive knows only what Caelestis knows of its own sector, which keeps the other sectors' readings partial.

Tarroway is a thread for the players to piece together, mostly through the archive. What waits at its end is a witness who noticed.

Their arc

The crew graduate as Driftwardens of the Caelestis Sector. In Act 2 they take contracts out of Ondrel Anchorage, handled by Captain Haymond Rolt. By then N'ghathrod's landed operations have begun to ripple outward at contract scale: taken people in small positions, odd disappearances, a station or crew acting strangely. These are the first things the crew meets as Driftwardens; the shape is on Opposition: the Vth'orama.

Until then the crew fly the tyrant ship they took from Vaelorix, on the sortie to H'Catha. After the H'Catha mission the tyrant ship goes into Caelestis's inventory; the crew does not know.

The Runekeepers
A hidden order of four clans on a world folded inside its own heart, and the Wayfinders it sends out with no map home.

The Runekeepers are a hidden order of four clans on the Wayfinders' homeworld, a world folded inside its own heart. They practise Wayfinding: placing marks that attune the practitioner to a place, so they can travel back to it. In the wider cosmos their existence is rumour at best.

Beliefs

A way home that lives only in a person cannot be taken off a chart. The order sends its Wayfinders into the wider cosmos with no map and no name, and its custom is that home must be recognised again through remembered sensory and cultural fragments. The Runekeeper holds this as doctrine, not cruelty.

The Becoming is the rite in which a Wayfinder becomes the Runekeeper, and the Lunarfoot tale named for it: something came looking for their world, and the world folded itself around its people and gave them runes as gifts. A Runekeeper's death is a song, not a mourning.

Origin

About five centuries ago a seeker opened the homeworld's heart with the Dissolution fragment and linked it to a bad world. The leaders of four clans fought the seeker together and took the fragment, and Dissolution struck all four. The one who seized it became the first Runekeeper, and the other three lines were primed. The world folded in that struggle, by accident, and the order is what the four clans built to keep it folded.

Structure and ranks

The order is four of the homeworld's clans, and not all of them are halflings. The fragment forms only in someone of a primed line, so the office passes through those four clans in a fixed order and no others; each clan's Runekeepers can be its own species. The first Runekeeper was of the founder's clan. Lunarfoot descends from the founder's kin, which is why the Becoming is a Lunarfoot tale, and Lunarfoot comes fourth. Seven Runekeepers have held the office over about five centuries, and the eighth heir is two people, Wan and Tu, the last of the cycle's second turn. At the head is the Runekeeper, one living practitioner at a time, who holds the binding that hides the world.

A generation

The fragment is whole in a Runekeeper for their tenure and moves only when a next host exists. When the heir is born, the Runekeeper feels it begin to move, makes sure the world is safe, and goes on pilgrimage to build their haven. The order builds a council around the heir, so a new Runekeeper takes office with guidance. When the heir comes of age they go out as a Wayfinder and walk the havens, and the fragment builds in them as runes as it leaves the Runekeeper. At the Runekeeper's haven the two meet, and the exchange is the Becoming: the heir goes home as the Runekeeper, and the old Runekeeper stays and fades into the haven.

The havens

Each Runekeeper builds a haven on another world: a small pocket, folded the way the homeworld is folded, bound to the host world's heart. The havens are the sigils of the concealment, and each keeps its Runekeeper's essence, which teaches the Wayfinders who reach it. Each holds a trial its Runekeeper set. There are seven. By clan, the founder's clan holds the first and fifth, the second clan the second and sixth, the third clan the third and seventh, and Lunarfoot the fourth. Each primed line carries a trace of the fragment from the fight, and a clan's haven passes it to the heir as the clan's rune.

Wayfinders

Members of the order sent out into the wider cosmos for assigned purposes: to learn, to recover, to witness, or to bring something back. An heir goes out to walk the havens. A Wayfinder carries a partial mark, the inscription their training has earned, and it grows as their understanding does. A Wayfinder with a complete mark can attune to a celestial body by ritual and time, opening a route between attuned points.

Operations

The concealment is one spell, cast across a generation. The living Runekeeper holds the binding wherever he stands; the havens pin it, each drawing on its host world's heart; each heir's journey renews it, and the Becoming seals it. The Runekeepers also hid their own world's Atrium, its heart's one entrance.

Wayfinding is not Weave-based, so a Forbiddance, which is set against Weave-based teleportation, does not stop it; a Wayfinder still needs a mark to land on. A mark sits on a place, a station or a ship a Wayfinder could crew.

How they look

A Wayfinder's mark is on the skin, and its runes surface in the presence of cosmological threat. The order's fragment is visible in its bearers: it forms and re-forms in the chest of the Runekeeper and of the Wayfinders who carry it.

The order's emblem is the fragment itself, drawn half-formed, as it shows in a bearer's chest.

Relationships

Caelestis

Caelestis does not know the order exists. Two of its Wayfinders are cadets there, as one person.

What's hidden

  • The fragment. The concealment depends on an object nobody in the order has identified as residue. The Becoming tale is the cleaned-up memory of an accident.
  • Two heirs. The fragment has always come home whole, in one Runekeeper. This time it settles in two, so the Runekeeper fades faster than any before him and the binding slips; left too long, the world unhides. At homecoming it arrives as two halves, and that change is the world's second Zenith.
  • The broken link. The order holds the way a link was broken once. If the crew learns it, it counters N'ghathrod's lattice.

How to run them

The order is competent and its doctrine is sound; the thing it stands on is a piece of the Reach world that none of them can name. Asked about the inscription, a Runekeeper answers honestly in the order's terms, which are wrong about the mechanism.

Their arc

The Runekeeper's fade and the binding's slip are the clock of Tumak's arc. The havens are his road home, and the rules are on Rules.

Threads

  • N'ghathrod is made of joins, and Dissolution unmakes joins. The fragment forming in Tumak is a counter to him, and nobody holding it knows.
  • Vecna's inscription opens and links hearts into an eye, to see; the Runekeepers' binding draws on hearts without opening them, to stay unseen.

Still to decide

  • The order's own name for itself is not set. The clans other than Lunarfoot are unnamed.
  • How the fragment looks as it forms in a chest, and so the exact drawing of the device, is not set. How the order's members dress is not set.
  • The Lunarfoot clan has its own seal, a crescent moon cradling a footprint with two four-point stars; it is Tumak's clan's seal, not the order's, and how it relates to the order's emblem is not set.
  • Parked, not settled: whether a closed world is invisible to A.T.L.A.S.; how other worlds hide themselves; whether runes are the finding half of the art. The order closed its own heart, which answers in part whether closing is the inverse of opening.
  • The Runekeeper's name is not set, nor are those of the seven before him or the place, trial and reward of each haven.
  • What a fragment made whole does is not set, and so what the twins' rebuilt fragment does at about level 12, early in Act 3.

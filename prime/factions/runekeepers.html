The Runekeepers
A hidden order of four clans on a world folded inside its own heart, and the one Wayfinder it sends out each generation to walk the havens home.

The Runekeepers are a hidden order of four clans on the Wayfinders' homeworld, a world folded inside its own heart. Their art is Wayfinding: placing marks that attune the practitioner to a place, so they can travel back to it. Only two of them ever leave the homeworld, the Wayfinder and the Runekeeper. In the wider cosmos their existence is rumour at best.

Beliefs

A way home that lives only in a person cannot be taken off a chart. When the heir is sent out, the Runekeeper takes from them the name of home, the way there and most of the whole picture, and holds it in the binding. The Wayfinder keeps that they came from somewhere, and loose fragments: a grandmother, a smell, the look of the trees. A Wayfinder taken on the road cannot give the world away, and the journey home is a remembering. It is the rite of passage, and the Runekeeper holds it as doctrine, not cruelty.

The Becoming is the rite in which a Wayfinder becomes the Runekeeper, and the Lunarfoot tale named for it: something came looking for their world, and the world folded itself around its people and gave them runes as gifts. A Runekeeper's death is a song, not a mourning.

Origin

About five centuries ago the homeworld was under threat, and a seeker of its own people, close to the founder's line, went after a means to answer it. The seeker opened the world's heart with the Dissolution fragment and linked it to a bad world, and things grew worse. The leaders of four clans fought the seeker together and took the fragment, and Dissolution struck all four. The one who seized it became the first Runekeeper, and the other three lines were primed. The world folded in that struggle, by accident, and hiding answered the first threat too. The order is what the four clans built to keep it folded.

Structure and ranks

The order is four of the homeworld's clans, and not all of them are halflings. The fragment forms only in someone of a primed line, so the office passes through those four clans in a fixed order and no others; each clan's Runekeepers can be its own species. Before the fold the clans had no such names. Under the fold, with no sun, sky, stars or moon over them, they named themselves for the heavens, each with a part of the body, so as to remember what they no longer had. In the order the office passes: Dawnbrow, the Sun, the founder's clan; Skyshoulder, the Sky; Starfist, the Stars; and Lunarfoot, the Moon. Lunarfoot descends from the founder's kin, which is why the Becoming is a Lunarfoot tale. Seven Runekeepers have held the office over about five centuries, and the eighth heir is two people, Wan and Tu, the last of the cycle's second turn. At the head is the Runekeeper, one living practitioner at a time, who holds the binding that hides the world.

A generation

The fragment is whole in a Runekeeper for their tenure and moves only when a next host exists. When the heir is born, the Runekeeper feels it begin to move, makes sure the world is safe, and goes on pilgrimage to build their haven. The order builds a council around the heir, so a new Runekeeper takes office with guidance. When the heir comes of age they go out as a Wayfinder and walk the havens, and the fragment builds in them as runes as it leaves the Runekeeper. At the Runekeeper's haven the two meet, and the exchange is the Becoming: it passes the greater part of the fragment, the heir goes home as the Runekeeper, and the old Runekeeper stays, lives out their days in the haven and becomes its essence.

The havens

Each Runekeeper builds a haven on another world: a small pocket, folded the way the homeworld is folded, bound to the host world's heart. It sits close to the heart, behind a thin veil between the heart and the world, and draws on the heart without opening it. The havens are the sigils of the concealment. Each keeps its Runekeeper's essence and what that Runekeeper left: notes, belongings and records of their own era, so seven havens show the homeworld and the order across five centuries. Each holds a trial its Runekeeper set. There are seven: Dawnbrow holds the first and fifth, Skyshoulder the second and sixth, Starfist the third and seventh, and Lunarfoot the fourth, at Selûne and the Tears of Selûne. Each primed line carries a trace of the fragment from the fight. The essence of a Wayfinder's first haven of a clan gives that clan's rune, which takes the clan's shape and fills its chamber in the Heart, and a spell from the clan's own menu.

Wayfinders

The Wayfinder is the heir on the journey, the only one the order sends out. The Runekeeper leaves only when he must: to build his haven and prepare for the next Wayfinder, or to meet a threat he senses through the havens. One gives and one receives. The Wayfinder walks the havens in a fixed order, their own clan's first and the Runekeeper's last, found by hints: each a haven's Runekeeper's memory, lived in one sense.

Operations

The concealment is one spell, cast across a generation. The living Runekeeper holds the binding wherever he stands; the havens pin it, each drawing on its host world's heart; each heir's journey renews it, and the Becoming seals it. The Runekeepers also hid their own world's Atrium, its heart's one entrance.

Wayfinding is not Weave-based, so a Forbiddance, which is set against Weave-based teleportation, does not stop it; a Wayfinder still needs a mark to land on. A mark sits on a place, a station or a ship a Wayfinder could crew.

How they look

A Wayfinder's mark is on the skin, and its runes surface in the presence of cosmological threat. The order's fragment is visible in its bearers: it forms and re-forms in the chest of the Runekeeper and of the Wayfinders who carry it.

The order's emblem is the fragment itself, drawn half-formed, as it shows in a bearer's chest: the Heart with its four chambers, one for each clan. Each clan has a seal: Dawnbrow's is a half-sun rising over one brow, the brow its horizon; Lunarfoot's is a crescent moon cradling a footprint; the two four-point stars beside it on Wan and Tu's own were added for the twins, their family's version of it.

Relationships

Caelestis

Caelestis does not know the order exists. Its Wayfinder, two people, is a cadet there as one.

What's hidden

  • The fragment. The concealment depends on an object nobody in the order has identified as residue. The Becoming tale is the cleaned-up memory of an accident.
  • The seeker. Vecna, a god for five centuries by then and already moving, steered the seeker to the residue with a whisper, before the Seekers were the chapters they are now. The seeker meant to do good. The fragment is residue his side lost, and the link the order broke was one of his.
  • Two heirs. The fragment has always come home whole, in one Runekeeper. This time it settles in two, and being two wears the binding down: the pieces strain against each other, the Runekeeper fades faster than any before him and the binding slips; left too long, the world unhides. The twins learn it as they go. At homecoming it arrives as two halves, and that change is the world's second Zenith.
  • The broken link. The order holds the way a link was broken once. If the crew learns it, it counters N'ghathrod's lattice.

How to run them

The order is competent and its doctrine is sound; the thing it stands on is a piece of the Reach world that none of them can name. Asked about the inscription, a Runekeeper answers honestly in the order's terms, which are wrong about the mechanism.

Their arc

The Runekeeper's fade and the binding's slip are the clock of Tumak's arc. The havens are his road home, and the rules are on Rules.

Threads

  • N'ghathrod is made of joins, and Dissolution unmakes joins. The fragment forming in Tumak is a counter to him, and nobody holding it knows.
  • Vecna's inscription opens and links hearts into an eye, to see; the Runekeepers' binding draws on hearts without opening them, to stay unseen.

Still to decide

  • The order's own name for itself is not set. How the order's members dress is not set.
  • Skyshoulder's and Starfist's seals, and the shapes of their clan runes, are not set.
  • Parked, not settled: whether a closed world is invisible to A.T.L.A.S.; how other worlds hide themselves; whether runes are the finding half of the art. The order closed its own heart, which answers in part whether closing is the inverse of opening.
  • The Runekeeper's name is not set, nor are those of the seven before him, nor a name for a Runekeeper after the exchange. Each haven's place, trial and records are set as play nears it.
  • What a fragment made whole does is not set. About level 12, early in Act 3, the twins' two pieces first draw together; what that does is not set.
  • The seeker's exact place among the clans, the threat the world faced, and the bad world the link reached are not set.

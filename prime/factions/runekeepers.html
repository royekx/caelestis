The Runekeepers
A small, hidden order on a world that keeps itself unfindable, and the Wayfinders it sends out with no map home.

The Runekeepers are a small, hidden order on the Wayfinders' homeworld, a world that defends itself by being unfindable. They practise Wayfinding: marking places in ways that attune the practitioner to them, and through them to their hearts. In the wider cosmos their existence is rumour at best.

Beliefs

A way home that lives only in a person cannot be taken off a chart. The order sends its Wayfinders into the wider cosmos with no map and no name, and its custom is that home must be recognised again through remembered sensory and cultural fragments. The Runekeeper holds this as doctrine, not cruelty.

The Lunarfoot clan tells a tale, The Becoming, in which something came looking for their world, and the world folded itself around its people and gave them runes as gifts.

Structure and ranks

The order draws on the homeworld's clans; the Lunarfoot clan is one of them. At its head is the Runekeeper, a title held by one living practitioner at a time, whose own body bears the inscription that holds the world's concealment.

Wayfinders

Members of the order sent out into the wider cosmos for assigned purposes: to learn, to recover, to witness, or to bring something back. A Wayfinder carries a partial mark, the inscription their training has earned, and it grows as their understanding does. A Wayfinder with a complete mark can attune to a celestial body by ritual and time, opening a route between attuned points.

Operations

The Runekeeper's ritual withdraws the homeworld from cosmological sight. The working draws on the oscillation of the Dissolution fragment the order keeps, so the concealment rides a cycle and the ritual points it. The world sits where it has always sat, reachable by anyone who knows the way back. The Runekeepers also hid their own world's Atrium, its heart's one entrance.

Wayfinding is not Weave-based, so a Forbiddance, which is set against Weave-based teleportation, does not stop it. A Wayfinder can attune only to bodies with a heart; a built installation does not qualify.

How they look

A Wayfinder's mark is on the skin, and its runes surface in the presence of cosmological threat. The order's fragment is visible in its bearers: it forms and re-forms in the chest of the Runekeeper and of the Wayfinders who carry it.

The order's device is the fragment itself, drawn half-formed, as it shows in a bearer's chest.

Relationships

Caelestis

Caelestis does not know the order exists. Two of its Wayfinders are cadets there, as one person.

What's hidden

  • The fragment. The concealment depends on an object nobody in the order has identified as residue. It cycles between whole and broken across Runekeepers. In Tumak it is rebuilding in two halves at once, a state neither whole nor broken.
  • Going home. When Wan and Tu both arrive home, the three poles stall the cycle and a Zenith begins: going home ends the concealment.
  • The Becoming is Lunarfoot belief. The runes come from the Dissolution fragment, not from a gift.
  • Tumak knows of the previous Runekeeper and met him once, briefly. He does not understand what that meeting was.

How to run them

The order is competent and its doctrine is sound; the thing it stands on is a piece of the Reach world that none of them can name. Asked about the inscription, a Runekeeper answers honestly in the order's terms, which are wrong about the mechanism.

Their arc

The Runekeeper's fade runs on a soft clock: rising pressure, no date. The fragment rebuilds between the twins through resolved runes, and the rules are on Rules.

Threads

  • N'ghathrod is made of joins, and Dissolution unmakes joins. The fragment forming in Tumak is a counter to him, and nobody holding it knows.

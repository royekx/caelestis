Tarto or Saerthe — the players choose Locked

One instructor joins the crew for H'Catha, and the party picks which. That makes the comparison below a decision aid rather than trivia: whichever they take shapes how the arc runs, and they are choosing without knowing that.

What the choice actually decides Saerthe changes the battlefield; Tarto changes what is happening on it. The pair barely overlap, which is what you want from two possible allies rather than two versions of the same help.

At H'Catha specifically: the terrain cancels magic in places and the domains impose ray effects, so Saerthe arrives as capability — the magic problems get smaller and the crew gains a bridge-builder on a water world. Tarto arrives as authority — she is their own chain of command, so the crew's decisions get supervised, and she makes the party's own turns better rather than taking the spotlight.

Both of them also answer Ryeback's question at the Cargo debrief, which is how the reinforcement gets invited rather than imposed.

Boatswain Tarto Needs a revisit

"Listen to what I tell you; it might just save your life."

Hadozee boatswain and training officer at Caelestis. Scarred, grey at the temples, wreathed in cigar smoke. One wing flap was torn away long ago; she has not glided since and has never needed to.

Cut during design, deliberately: superiority dice, Deck Ape (redundant with her climb speed), Silhouette Deck (replaced by "There!"), and the "Hold!" temporary-hit-point order. Her maneuver list is in the working file and is not recorded here

Why it does not hold up

The diagnosis

Removing superiority dice removed the decision. A Battle Master is interesting because the resource is finite and spending it is a judgement call. Maneuvers on every hit with no damage attached turns that into three saves a turn, forever — which is simultaneously more bookkeeping and less memorable, because nothing ever spikes.

She contributes neither burst nor flexibility. At CR 10 her ~39 damage is unremarkable and her utility is rated low, so what she brings is durability plus admin. Clear the Deck is the only button anyone will remember.

Her best features queue behind each other. Commander's Strike and her maneuvers both spend an ally's reaction, so the same cadet cannot take both, and at a six-PC table the routing gets congested rather than generous.

The direction I would take: give her fewer, larger moments. Restore a limited resource so spending is a choice again, drop the per-hit maneuver to once per turn, and keep Clear the Deck as the payoff. Alternatively, lean all the way into the opposite reading — make her the ally who improves the party's own turns and barely acts herself, which suits a training officer better than a damage-dealer does.

Canon note: she passed on the crate holding the neogi hatchlings because she was informed rather than having checked it herself. Guilt without negligence, which is the version that lets her stay sympathetic and still be answerable.

Saerthe Abizjn Materializer

Senior caster at Caelestis, they/them, and the holder of the station's permanent Forbiddance. Their combat identity is a Materializer wizard — barrier work, built on conjured cubes rather than blasting.

The core mechanic Four active 5-foot cubes. They are a pool rather than a per-rest resource: a cube returns to the pool when dismissed, collapsed or destroyed, so Saerthe is never out of barriers, only ever out of placed ones.

Cubes configure contiguously into walls and blocks — or, corner to corner, into a checkerboard line roughly 25 by 2 feet that works as stairs or a bridge by exploiting corner elevation. On a water world, that last one matters.

Saerthe Abizjn
Materializer wizard · CR 11
Armor Class 15
Hit Points 117
Damage roughly 48 per round, out to 120 ft.
Ability scores, saves, skills and the full spell list are in the working file

Signature features

Encase (Recharge 5–6).
The hard lockdown — seals a target inside the barrier. Gated by recharge rather than available every round.
Collapse (bonus action).
Crushes a cube inward. 27 (5d10) to a sealed target, 16 (3d10) splash to creatures nearby.
Barrier Footing.
Saerthe walks on open air one step at a time, each step vanishing as they leave it. No bridge is left behind for anyone else.
Mend (bonus action).
Casts mending on one of their own cubes, restoring all its hit points. Anyone else can do the same with a normal one-minute casting — so holding a patch under fire is a job you can hand a cadet, and Gregory knows the spell.
Interpose.
Protects an ally at up to 60 feet. Encase doubles as a lifeboat.
Dematerialize.
Their escape. Also the campaign's natural counter to the CRC's Detention Orb.

Cut during design: Painted Barrier and Material Enhancement, as superfluous or off-theme. Not a wizard mechanic: the quicksilver eyes are an origin trait tied to Nimbral, and detect magic sight was rejected as a fit.

The ceiling, and its price Saerthe can mentally call a fleet of tethered spelljamming ships after a minute of concentration. It sits outside the subclass entirely and it costs prolonged helm-blindness afterward, which is what keeps it from being a solution to every problem. It is the reason Caelestis can respond to anything at all, and it is the reason Saerthe is not sent out often.

At the table against the CRC: Dematerialize answers the orb, so Saerthe should not engage the construct directly — the encounter is built as an escape, and a CR 11 ally standing in the way converts it into a fight the party does not need to have.

One ruling worth keeping handy: the simulation in the Spelljammer Nexus is pure illusion. Small mundane objects can bleed through at the exit threshold; magic items never cross.

Crystalline Retrieval Construct Retuned for level 4

"Sol Fortuna. You are required."

Commissioned by the Fortuned of Aethris after Sol's escape, which makes it bespoke: his leaving is the reason it exists. Its assignment is capture, correction and return. There is one of it. It stands seven to eight feet tall and occupies a single square.

Traits

Bespoke Target Lock.
While on the same plane as Sol, it knows his direction and approximate distance, with Advantage on Perception and Insight checks involving him.
Legendary Resistance (2/Day).

Regains 10 hit points at the start of its turn if it has at least 1. Thunder damage switches this off for the start of its next turn.

Reaction against a ranged or spell attack: impose Disadvantage. If it misses, redirect it at another creature within 30 feet that was in the original path, rolling a new attack with the original attacker's bonus.

At 75%, 50% and 25% of its hit points, it gains Resistance to one damage type that has hurt it since the last threshold. Resistances carry forward into future encounters — it remembers.
Non-Lethal Retrieval.
Anyone other than Sol reduced to 0 hit points falls Unconscious and stable rather than dying, and it will not pursue them unless they obstruct the retrieval.
Withdrawal Threshold.
At 68 hit points it disengages and leaves. It is never killable, so the party can survive it without it ever reading as beatable.

Actions

Multiattack.
One Crystal Strike, plus one of: Detention Orb, Geometric Growth, or a stored spell.
Detention Orb.
The win condition — put Sol in the orb and leave. The orb is a destructible object: AC 13, 25 hit points, vulnerable to thunder. Breaking it is the rescue, which keeps the answer available to a level-4 party.
Geometric Growth.
Battlefield reshaping with crystal terrain.
Stored spell.
Locate creature, which doubles down on inevitability.
Crystal Strike · legendary action.
Numbers in the working file One legendary action exists so six PCs cannot strip its action economy outright.
Run it as an escape It is built to retrieve rather than kill, so the first encounter is objective-based and survivable by moving. The win is breaking the orb and getting clear, not dropping the construct. They survived; they did not win.

Track which damage types hit it, because the resistances it takes away are permanent. By a second or third meeting the party has to find new vectors, which is the whole point: you cannot beat it the same way twice.

The face progressively resolves into a perfect mirror of Sol's. Visual only, with no mechanic attached — the horror should cost the player nothing.

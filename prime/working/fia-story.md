# The Fleet Intelligence Archive: the story it tells

The story behind the FIA, worked out from the anchors; what the FIA shows of
it; the threads the crew can pull; and every file and page that has to be
rewritten to match. Settle the story here, then rewrite the files once.

Nobody at the table has read the FIA yet. Until someone does, all of it can
change. After that, a thread laid down here holds for the rest of the
campaign.

## What the FIA is for

The FIA is a supplement: a mystery running in the background that the crew
can investigate, and a store of backstory.

- **It reinforces threads the crew meets in the world.** Whichever comes
  first, the file or the encounter, the second lands as *wait, we've seen
  this before.*
- **It shows as well as tells.** Each image in it is ambiguous without
  context and becomes clear once the crew meets its subject.
- **It holds one line of each cadet's origin**, in a file about something
  else. Any player may find any cadet's line first.
- **It is the Fleet's view.** Accurate about what the Fleet saw, and blind to
  what it means.

## The colony's illithids

### The elder brain, and what the colony lacks

An elder brain is a colony's shared mind, and it lives in a brine pool at the
colony's heart. Tadpoles grow in that pool for years. Between them, the
tadpoles eating one another and the elder brain eating them, all but about
one in a hundred die there. What reaches a host is the strongest and best
formed of the brood. The elder brain also links every illithid of
the colony and keeps the knowledge of its dead.

The Vth'orama have no elder brain. N'ghathrod's own was aboard Thessalar's
craft with him, kept apart from him, and was left trapped beneath the wreck
when the main craft came down in the Barrier Peaks on Fonains, in the caverns
beneath them. He believes it dead, or beyond reach. (On Fonains it lived, and
after Lola's Boys reached it, it escaped to a place nobody knows.)

N'ghathrod will not let a tadpole die.
After all the colony has been through, and the exile above all, nothing eats
the brood. The tadpoles in the pools still eat one another, as tadpoles do,
but without the elder brain's share far more of each mass survives than the
one in a hundred an ordinary colony keeps. Every
survivor is kept alive in the pools and waits there, for years if it must,
until it is found a host. The weak and the flawed survive with the rest, so
an illithid the colony makes is far more likely to come out of
ceremorphosis malformed. Most of them do, and the whole are a minority. The
pools always hold more waiting tadpoles than the colony has bodies for, and
that is why it always needs more captives.

**The voice.** Vecna reaches N'ghathrod as a voice, and as dreams and visions.
For some decades N'ghathrod took it for an elder brain. He learned it was
not, and stopped caring: his own world had cast him out, and the voice
provides. It gives him what Vecna's plan needs him to know; everything else
he learns from his taken.

**N'ghathrod has become the colony's elder brain.** He is not one in the
ordinary sense; he is a changed thing that has grown into the place. Its
illithids believe they answer to an elder brain through him, and in every
way that matters they do. He is fused into Vth'oramu and the tadpole pools
bloom out of the hull, so the pools are in his body as an elder brain's are
in its brine. He decides which tadpole goes to which captive, the taken
report up to him, and what they know ends with him.

**Unthinned** is Glyth's word for a malformed illithid: one the brood should
have thinned out. An unthinned illithid is visibly wrong in body, and
lopsided in mind. Where an ordinary illithid has a broad spread of psychic
powers, an unthinned one is very good at one thing and poor at the rest.

Every illithid spawns, whole or unthinned: a mass of tadpoles, two or three
times in its life, and every mass goes into the pools. **The true-formed** (proposed name) are the
colony's whole illithids: the old ones who came with N'ghathrod, and the
minority born in the colony who came out whole. They keep the pools and
oversee ordinary ceremorphosis.

The colony still holds Glyth's belief that the unthinned are an obscenity,
alongside the fact that most of its illithids are unthinned, three of the
enna among them. It has never resolved that, and the true-formed are where
it is loudest.

The first unthinned were three made aboard Thessalar's craft. Their
tadpoles were N'ghathrod's own, brought aboard before the experiment and cut
off from the elder brain; Thessalar experimented on them too, and put them
into bodies he supplied. They are
three of the enna. When N'ghathrod brought them to Glyth, Glyth saw what they
were and refused them, and exiled them. N'ghathrod went with them.

### What the experiment did

Thessalar's experiment with the seventh Rod piece, keyed to simulacrum,
changed N'ghathrod's reproduction: his tadpoles stopped being offspring and
became copies of him. That is the taking.

It also changed how he spawns. An ordinary illithid spawns a mass two or
three times in its life; N'ghathrod spawns on a slow, steady cycle, a few
copies at a time, again and again. Over a year he makes more tadpoles than
any illithid aboard, and far fewer than the colony's illithids make
together. His copies place the taken. The ordinary masses, from every
illithid in the colony, are what grow it.

**His copies never go to captives.** A copy is too scarce to spend on an
ordinary body. It goes to someone in a place of power, reached where they
are, so that the colony gains a post: a station master, a dockmaster, a
spelljammer the Fleet trusts. Every captive Vocath's line brings goes to the
pools, or feeds the colony's illithids.

**The one exception is the outcast.** N'ghathrod has a soft spot for the
neglected, the abandoned and the cast out, because that is what he and his
people were. When one of his taken finds such a person, he may spend a copy
on them for no gain at all, and his taken then protect them and raise them
up. He is evil, and this is the piece of him a crew can
reach.

**His mouse.** He saved a mouse in the escape from Thessalar's craft, and it
has travelled with him since. It was aboard with the seventh Rod piece as
long as he was, and the experiment changed it too: it has a power of its
own. He took it with one of his copies, so the tithe keeps it alive. It is
the first outcast he saved, and his secret sixth lieutenant: nobody aboard
counts a mouse.

It changed the five who became the enna with him. Each came out with a power
of its own. Zhauveth's is the Voice: domination and suggestion.

**Each enna's spawn inherits some of its power**, as N'ghathrod's do. An
illithid grown from an enna's tadpole leans toward its parent's power. One
of Zhauveth's is unthinned and very good at holding minds.

### One with N'ghathrod, and long life

**The enna joined him willingly.** Each took a Hold: a portion of N'ghathrod,
carried permanently. Some of the properly raised illithids who came with him
did the same, with lesser portions, so that they would all be one.

**Long life comes from the taking**, not from the experiment. A portion of
every taken person passes upward through the rungs and ends with N'ghathrod,
a tithe that extends his life. He shares it with a chosen few: the enna, and
some of the old illithids who joined him. They have outlived every illithid
of their age by centuries. **Proposed:** the eldest of the old
illithids keeps the colony's history.

Captives become illithids. Only key people are taken, never crowds. The taken
are mortal: they are found out, killed, or die, and N'ghathrod keeps placing
new ones. N'ghathrod knows only what his taken report, and what Vecna lets
him sense.

## The story

### What each side needs

**The colony needs bodies and cannot fetch them.** Captives become illithids
and grow the colony. The colony lives hidden inside a dreadnought; its
illithids cannot walk into a port, and its few taken are worth too much to
spend snatching crowds. It needs someone who already moves cargo and people
at volume and asks no questions.

**Vocath needs what the taken know.** Over years N'ghathrod has placed his
taken in useful posts: dockmasters, customs clerks, guild accountants. Every
one of them reports up to him. Zhauveth passes Vocath what serves him: which
cargo moves when, which inspection is coming, where the patrols and escorts
are. That knowledge is why his house prospers.

**The arrangement.** Vocath's line supplies captives; Zhauveth supplies what
the taken know. Vocath deals with Zhauveth, never with N'ghathrod.

**Why Vocath is not taken.** A willing partner costs no tadpole. His crews
follow him, and a copy drifts over time. Zhauveth, whose gift is being agreed
with, keeps him content.

### Cairn Station

Cairn is a small Caelestis Fleet watch post with a handful of crew, and the
Fleet's authority on its lane. Its long scope watches the lane, and its
reports go to Caelestis and feed the Fleet's charts. Vocath's captive line
runs along the lane, the colony's couriers run it, and Vth'oramu crosses it
now and then on its way somewhere.

**Vocath's house supplies the lane's stations.** The family business holds
the Fleet contract for stores on that lane, under the clean house mark. Its
ships call at Cairn as a matter of course.

**The colony holds Cairn with a pair: one of N'ghathrod's taken and one of
Zhauveth's illithids.**

- **The station master is taken.** One routine supply call carried one of
  N'ghathrod's tadpoles. The station master writes the reports and keeps the
  log, so every word that leaves Cairn is his before the signals officer
  sends it, and nobody can read his mind.
- **Everyone else is held.** One of Zhauveth's spawn, an unthinned illithid
  whose one strength is holding minds, lives at Cairn out of sight and holds the rest of the crew, the
  signals officer among them. Its hold works only by nearness, and it grows
  with every day spent near it; a crew that has lived beside it for years
  accepts it as part of the station and stops seeing it. Out of range, the
  hold fades. So it makes no attempt on anyone who will leave: a visitor
  would carry nothing away but a feeling. Visitors feel the place is a little
  off, and anyone psychically aware feels it more; the feeling fades once
  they are gone.
  They work their watches as normal, and what they see on the lane, they set
  aside as nothing worth remarking. It feeds from the captives that pass near
  Cairn. Zhauveth has few such offspring, and this one is spent where a hold
  matters most.

The station master defers the crew's rotation: each of them signs a waiver
and stays. Nobody in the Fleet finds that odd. Many who serve have few ties
to any world below, and a quiet post is easy to stay at. The same few people
serve there year on year, and the lane reads all clear.

**Zhauveth touches down at Cairn** now and then, not often. The captives
change hands every few weeks, out on the lane near Cairn, and she makes the
colony's deals with Vocath's people. Whether Cairn itself serves as the
waystation for the handovers or only watches over them need not be fixed.

**Where it sits.** Realmspace lies midway along the Caelestis Sector's arm.
Cairn's lane runs outward from it along the arm, into the sector's outer
zone, some weeks out by ordinary helm. Strake Station sits on its own lane
nearer home, off to the side of Cairn's. Krynnspace and Fonains lie inward
along the arm, nowhere near Cairn; the color pool that took Clint opened by
chance, and where it opened says nothing about where it led.

Cairn's stretch of lane lies at the very limit of Strake's scope, which picks
up faint, odd returns there. Nobody in the colony knows Strake can see that
far.

### The *Bellwether Crow*

1. **Tarroway notices.** Strake's cartographer pulls a pattern out of the
   noise: Strake sees things on the lane that Cairn does not report. On his
   filing, the Caelestis Fleet sends the *Bellwether Crow* to look in, with
   Clint Dashforest leading its crew and Joffrey aboard as its junior
   spelljammer. N'ghathrod has nobody taken in the office that sends it,
   so nobody warns Vocath. The *Crow*'s crew are told to look in on Cairn; the
   filing behind the order stays with the office.
2. **The raid.** The *Crow*'s course to Cairn runs up the lane and comes on
   Vocath's people mid-handover, captives half moved from their ship to a
   colony courier, on a stretch of lane Cairn watches closely and Strake sees
   only at the very edge of its range. With the
   cargo strung between two hulls they cannot scatter, so they strike to
   drive the *Crow* off, and
   bite off more than they can chew. To draw the fight off his crew, Clint
   boards one of their ships and seizes it. A color pool opens by chance and
   takes the ship, with Clint and its raiders aboard. Many of the
   *Crow*'s crew die. **Joffrey takes the helm, flies the ship out of the
   fight and holds the survivors together.** From then on they look to him.
   He earned it.
3. **The warning.** Vocath's people pull back and warn Zhauveth that the
   *Crow* is coming to Cairn.
4. **Cairn plays host.** The station takes the crippled *Crow* in, and
   repairs take days. The *Crow*'s crew will leave, so the illithid makes no
   attempt on them. It keeps out of sight, and Cairn's crew work as normal
   around the visitors. The survivors feel the place is a little off and put
   it down to shock and grief, and the feeling fades on the way home. The station master has Caelestis told that the *Crow* is safe
   and under repair, and relays the raid as the *Crow*'s crew tell it.
   Cairn's own scope log for that watch, written up as routine, shows no
   contact. Sending the ship home is less trouble than the questions a
   missing ship would raise.
5. **The opening.** The station master reports up to N'ghathrod, and Zhauveth
   sees what they have: a ship going home to Caelestis, and a spelljammer its
   survivors follow. A colony courier brings one of N'ghathrod's tadpoles up
   the lane within days, and the station master takes Joffrey. The colony
   wants a man inside Caelestis; it knows nothing of A.T.L.A.S. A hold cannot
   go home with him; a taking can. Joffrey is also insurance: a man
   inside Caelestis who can warn the colony if anyone looks at Cairn again. He knows the *Crow* was sent to look in on Cairn,
   and nothing of who asked.
6. **The report.** The *Crow* goes home and reports what it believes: raided
   on the approach, Clint lost, Joffrey held the ship, Cairn helped them home.
   The Fleet orders escorts against raiders on the lane, and Cairn is thanked.
   Escort crews that call at Cairn are visitors like the *Crow*'s: they
   leave, and the hold leaves them alone.
   The *Crow* went to look at Cairn and found it in order, so its report
   answers Tarroway's filing. The colony never learns Tarroway raised the
   question; as far as it knows, the Fleet looked and got its answer. N'ghathrod's
   taken tell Vocath, through Zhauveth, where the escorts run, and the line
   runs around them.

### Clint on Fonains

The pool drops the ship on Fonains, with Clint and its raiders aboard, before
the Monolith rose, about a year ago. Clint gets away. He loses his means of
reaching Caelestis, his communication piece, and cannot send word. He may also
carry an artifact device, not yet worked out, which he knows only as a magic
item. The raiders and Vocath know nothing of it, and it played no part in the
chase. Whether it is the second Distinction piece is open. They hunt him for a
while, for the sake of it and so that he cannot report what he saw on the
lane. When the Monolith rises and breaks, they see their chance: they still
have their ship and their sending stone, and they send word to Vocath.
Vocath is a merchant and gets things moving fast, and his people start their
trade on Fonains and carry its gems and shards home.

### After

- Joffrey turns over some months, not a year, and makes his name on the
  strength of what he did on the *Crow*. He keeps part of himself, and one
  survivor of the *Crow* sees that he has changed. Meanwhile he gathers what
  he can about Caelestis.
- The Academy thefts were already running, small, before he was placed, and
  have grown since. In his first months A.T.L.A.S. comes into the Brig, or
  he learns it is there, catalogued as an antique navigational instrument: a
  Reach housing. N'ghathrod wants residue, and the plan for it starts there.
- **Three insiders, one hub.** Joffrey tracks the shipments that carry
  Vocath's seal and handles what comes in: he leaves the rod where Miken will
  pick it up. Miken sabotages. A third insider, the thief, steals, and Joffrey
  uses the thief to learn where things are kept and the Brig's protocols,
  which he has no access to. Neither Miken nor the thief knows the other exists, and
  neither knows there are three: each knows only that someone else is
  involved. Nothing either of them knows points at Joffrey. Only the crew
  holds what does.
- Zhauveth tells Vocath that he has an informant inside Caelestis, and Vocath
  uses it for his grudge against Mirt. In name, Joffrey answers to Qitru,
  Vocath's intermediary; in fact N'ghathrod runs him, through Zhauveth.
- **Miken placed the rod.** He was already a cadet when Qitru recruited him,
  with money enough to set up him and his fiancé. Vocath meant the rod to
  hurt, as a message to Mirt. Qitru told Miken it would only spoil the drill,
  and he believed her. His own crewmates took the worst of it, and he regrets
  it. In the interlude after Voyage 006 he planted the beacon Qitru follows,
  and he reports to her by sending stone. What gives him away is a hushed conversation in the middle
  of the night.
- Qitru told Joffrey about Miken. Miken does not know of Joffrey.
- **The seal at the dock.** Joffrey watches for Vocath's personal seal to know
  which shipments are Vocath's. He paid Jeffrey, the hobgoblin dock hand, to
  pass the sealed crate the cadets later found; it came in under Mr. Blip's
  oversight without his knowing, because he does not inspect every cargo. The
  small things Miken needed, the rod and the beacon, came in the same way.
  The crate the crew took to Mirt, with the neogi, is a different crate;
  nobody has checked it for a mark, and the seal is there to find.
- **Mirt.** He is the Academy's headmaster, and he is no fool. He keeps a
  code, and the bumbling is mostly a manner. He lost his friend Clint to a
  raid he believes was Vocath's and cannot prove. He holds Above Bridge
  clearance and has read FR-016; he knows the seal on sight, and he says
  nothing of it. Vocath is his personal grudge. If that grudge were seen to
  cost the Fleet, Caelestis would turn him out, and he has too many enemies
  to live long without its protection. So he keeps quiet and works to learn
  what Vocath is actually doing. If he knew how to reach Vocath without
  risking his place or his students, he would act. When the cadets bring him
  Joffrey, he backs them: if they think something is there, they should look
  into it for him, carefully, given Joffrey's standing.

### Vocath's two marks

- **His house mark** is the public trade mark of the family business. The
  Fleet knows it from the supply contract and has never seen it on anything
  wrong.
- **His personal seal** goes on his darker dealings: the rod, the Sky Dock
  crate, the crate of Fonains shards Boogie looked into. It is registered
  nowhere. Mirt's trusted circle knows it on sight: Saerthe, Tarto, Mr. Blip,
  and Clint before he went overboard.

He signed the rod on purpose: a message to Mirt that he can reach him.

### If the crew goes to Cairn

Cairn is held, not empty. The illithid is always there; Zhauveth only
sometimes, and if she is, she could end a crew of their level with a word.
The crew feel the place is off from the moment they arrive. If they stay,
the hold reaches for them, a little more each day, and they can resist it.
The station should feel wrong in small ways that do not add up, never in one
reveal. A fight there is a choice between the crew around them, who are not
acting of their own will, and the thing holding them: knock the crew down or
go for the source. **Proposed** touches:

- The crew all pause at once, mid-sentence, and pick up again.
- A sighting in the watch log, struck through in the station master's
  steady hand.
- The duty roster names the same people as the roster a year ago.
- Nobody asks the visitors for news from home.
- A crewman answers a question a beat before it is asked.
- A cold room nobody goes into, and nobody mentions.
- Something moves at the edge of a corridor, and the crewman beside them
  does not look up.

### The discrepancy

Strake and Cairn disagree. Strake logs contacts on the lane that Cairn never
reports, among them a contact during the *Crow*'s approach, inside Cairn's
own range. For that watch, Cairn's relay reports the raid as the *Crow* told
it, while Cairn's own scope log shows nothing. Cairn's reports read
perfectly, week after week. Tarroway's first filing sent the *Crow*; the
*Crow*'s report answered it, and his later filings were set aside.

Hollis, *Bellwether Crow*, Clint, Joffrey, Tarroway, the edge run, the

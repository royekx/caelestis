# Draft: Tumak's road home, top to bottom

The whole of Tumak's arc in one place, from the homeworld's origin to what
may follow the Becoming. Built in the cadets'-arcs chat of 2026-11-10 to 11
on BACKLOG's "Make each cadet's arc tangible" (Crew, crew/index.html);
Korey keeps the lore and mechanic work for it in that chat. Gregory's prep
is its own chat.

Every line carries a tag:

| Tag | Means |
|---|---|
| **[canon]** | On the site's pages now |
| **[ruled]** | Ruled in this chat; waits in this draft for the change audit |
| **[proposed]** | Offered, not yet ruled |
| **[open]** | Undecided |

Rules are in DM voice, world facts in-world.

## Design constraints [ruled]

- The rules hold for every cycle, from the first Runekeeper to the eighth
  heir and after, and not only for Wan and Tu.
- Gregory helps, and never gates. Any navigator, a Fleet chart office or the
  crew's own legwork does his part, slower. His arc stands on its own.
- No fixed star chart. A haven is placed when its first hint lands, so the
  sandbox stays open.
- A rune never equals a place. No position is tied to a haven in advance.
- Getting home takes adventuring, not levelling. Runes say where; travel and
  trials get him there.
- The player tracks few kinds of thing; the DM tracks more, planned ahead.

## 1. Origin

**[canon]** About five centuries ago a seeker carried the Dissolution
fragment, Reach residue, to the homeworld. The world came to its Zenith and
a Monolith rose. The seeker carried the fragment into the world's Atrium,
opened its heart and linked it to one other world under the Monolith. That
world was a bad one; the two bled into each other and terrible creatures
came through. The leaders of four clans fought the seeker and took the
fragment, and Dissolution struck all four. The one who seized it etched one
pole into themselves and became the first Runekeeper; the other three lines
were primed. In the struggle the fragment folded the world inside its
heart, by accident: it cut the link, closed the heart and drew the world in.
The bleeding stopped and the Monolith faded.

**[canon]** Who the seeker was, what sent them, the world the link reached,
and where the seeker found the fragment are not set.

**[ruled] Who the seeker was.** One of the homeworld's own, close to the
founder: of Dawnbrow or near it, and not the founder's twin. Vecna, a god
for about five centuries by then, was already moving, though the Seekers
were not yet the chapters they are now. His whisper reached the seeker and
steered them to the residue, as he steers the Seekers through residue
today, and the seeker opened the heart meaning to do good. It backfired:
the link reached a bad world. The fragment became residue his side lost,
and the order's broken link was one of his. Several things happened around
the five-century mark (Thessalar's craft fell then too) because Vecna was
moving; the dates can shift.

**[open]**
- The seeker's place: a member of Dawnbrow itself, or of an older clan
  close to it that was lost afterward.
- The good the seeker meant to do.

**[alt] Where the seeker went.**

- **Sealed in the heart.** The seeker was in the Atrium when the fold came,
  and the fold sealed them in. The order hid the Atrium; the danger is at
  home, where Tumak's road ends. "Defeated, or so they thought."
- **Thrown to the far side** with the link, waiting at the cut.

**[proposed] The frozen link.** The fold pulled the link's two ends apart
and held them still rather than breaking them. If the binding fails, the
heart opens where it closed, onto the same link, and the bleed resumes. No
one has to find the world, and no new Monolith is needed.

## 2. The order and the four clans

**[canon]** The Runekeepers are four of the homeworld's clans, not all of
them halflings. The fragment forms only in someone of a primed line, so the
office passes through the four clans in a fixed order. The founder's clan is
first; Lunarfoot, descended from the founder's kin, is fourth. Seven
Runekeepers have held it over about five centuries. Wan and Tu are the
eighth heir, the last of the cycle's second turn.

**[ruled]** The clans are bound to the heavens and named for one and a part
of the body, top to bottom, in the order the office passes. A clan's body
part is its name only; the sixteen rolled positions belong to no clan.

| Order | Clan | Heavens | Body | Its Runekeepers and havens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st, the founder's | Dawnbrow | the Sun | brow | 1st, 5th |
| 2nd | Skyshoulder | the Sky | shoulder | 2nd, 6th |
| 3rd | Starfist | the Stars | fist | 3rd, 7th (the current Runekeeper) |
| 4th | Lunarfoot | the Moon | foot | 4th |

**[ruled]** Children learn the clans by a rhyme: head, shoulders, hands and
toes, with the movements carrying the clans (a finger to the brow for the
head, fists wiggled for the hands), and a second verse for the heavens: the
sun, the sky, the stars, the moon, stars and moon. It echoes a real-world
nursery rhyme as a wink for the player. **[proposed]** It comes back to the
twins whole at Lunarfoot's haven, their first.

**[ruled] Seals.** Dawnbrow's seal is a half-sun rising over one brow, the
brow its horizon. Lunarfoot's is a crescent moon cradling a footprint. The
two four-point stars beside it, on the Becoming handout and the Wayfinder's
Path tab, were added for Wan and Tu: the family's own version, not a sign of
Starfist. **[open]** Skyshoulder's seal (pinned: a bird alighting on a
shoulder, the wind on a shoulder, or a mantle of sky) and Starfist's.

**[open]** The story of the names, which comes from before the hiding.
**[proposed]** The order's emblem as the Heart with the four clan shapes
(see Conflicts 6).

## 3. A generation and the sending

**[canon]** The fragment is whole in a Runekeeper for their tenure and moves
only when a next host exists. When the heir is born the Runekeeper feels it
move, makes the world safe, and goes on pilgrimage to build a haven on
another world. A council forms around the heir. Come of age, the heir goes
out as a Wayfinder and walks the havens; the fragment builds in them as
runes as it leaves the Runekeeper. At the Runekeeper's haven the two meet in
the exchange, the Becoming: the heir goes home as the Runekeeper, and the old
one stays and fades into the haven. Every leaving passes through the
Runekeeper.

**[ruled] The sending.** When an heir is sent out, the Runekeeper takes from
them the name of home, the way there and most of the whole picture, and
holds it in the binding. The heir keeps that they came from somewhere, and
loose fragments: a grandmother, a smell, the look of the trees. It protects
the world, because a captured heir cannot give it away, and it is the rite
of passage: the journey home is remembering. Havens give back the senses of
home and its people; the name and the way return only at the Becoming.
Canon's "given no map and no name" becomes "loses the name and the way". It
explains why the twins do not remember meeting the Runekeeper as children.

**[open]** Whether the sending applies to every Wayfinder or only the heir
(see Conflicts 1). A check that losing the name and the way sits with how
the player plays the twins.

## 4. The havens

**[canon]** A haven is a small pocket, folded the way the homeworld is, on
a host world and bound to its heart. The havens are the sigils of the
concealment, one spell cast across a generation. Each holds a trial its
Runekeeper set and keeps its Runekeeper's essence.

**[ruled]** Each haven sits close to its host world's heart, behind a thin
veil between the heart and the world: it draws on the heart without opening
it. At the site the twins can feel the veil and press on it, like a hand on
a window screen.

**[ruled] What a walked haven gives.** Both twins present and the trial
passed:

- From the essence: the clan's rune, if it is the first haven of that clan
  he walks, and a spell chosen from the clan's menu (see 5).
- What its Runekeeper left: notes, belongings and records of their own era.
  Seven Runekeepers span five centuries, so each haven shows the homeworld
  and the order as they were in one era.
- A full memory of home, its senses and its people.
- A permanent mark. **[open]** Whether this is one mark with the clan rune
  or a second (see Conflicts 5).

A walked haven does not name other havens.

## 5. The inscription

**[canon]** The fragment writes itself on the skin as runes: half a rune on
each twin, so no rune is whole while they are apart. Runes arrive with
level-ups, rolled on a d20 by the rules page's procedure.

**[ruled]**

| What | Gives |
|---|---|
| A half-rune rolled | Nothing new: it waits for its match, and counts toward Slip |
| A matched rune (1 to 16) | A mark, and one hint toward the current target haven |
| A clan rune (17 to 20) | Its clan's place in the Heart; no hint |
| The Heart (21) | The fifth sense; already matched, unshown until the first haven |

- **Clan runes** are one per clan, his own included, each taking its clan's
  shape (Lunarfoot a crescent, Dawnbrow a sun). They sit inside the Heart,
  which holds a place for each clan. They count for every number (essence
  runes, marks, passengers) except hints. **[open]** Whether they count as
  wildcards toward anything.
- **The inscription grows with the order.** An heir carries 16 rolled runes,
  one clan rune per clan that has built a haven, and the Heart: 18 for the
  second heir, 19 for the third, 20 for the fourth, 21 from the fifth on.
  The early Runekeepers held a thinner binding.
- **Retired:** the four sets (Soul, Maker, Walker, Vessel) as sets, the
  Pacing Rule, and the exhaustion cost for extra Wayscribe casts.
- **Haven spells.** Each clan has a menu named for it, taking an old set
  menu: Dawnbrow the Soul spells, Skyshoulder the Vessel spells, Starfist
  the Maker spells, Lunarfoot the Walker spells. One free cast a day, then
  with spell slots. Four on the road, more from chance havens. A free daily
  high-level spell is acceptable when the trial matches its weight; the
  menus are revisited in play if one runs hot. **[open]** The menus and
  levels (mechanic review).

**[ruled] The Heart.** On a haven's world the Heart stirs as a sense and
leads him to the site. It gives no power. The first time it is a scene: a
rune he has never seen flares, then dulls. Leaving the haven's world it
goes inert and fades until the next. It wakes whole at the Becoming, when
its last place fills.

## 6. Hints

**[ruled]** The havens are sigils of the one spell and the fragment is its
engine. A rune matched whole hears the sigil it is bound for, and the sigil
answers with a memory its Runekeeper left in it.

- **A hint is a Runekeeper's memory.** He lives one sense of what that
  haven's Runekeeper saw, heard, touched, or smelled and tasted, as The
  Giver passes memories on. It is how the Runekeepers guide him without a
  voice explaining the way.
- **Four hints a haven, one per sense, in the same order**, broad to
  specific: sight, hearing, touch, then smell and taste. Set for the first
  haven; revisit if play says so.
- **Hints are the only way a haven on his road is found.** They are clues,
  not gates: a crew that works a haven out from two or three goes early,
  and a navigator helps.
- **Hints retarget.** A hint aims at the next unwalked haven on the road; a
  haven walked early sends the rest on. Hints with no haven left become
  memories of home.
- **The math.** Every heir makes 16 matches by level 16, four havens at four
  hints each. For the twins the 4th, 8th, 12th and 16th matches land at
  about levels 8, 11, 14 and 16 (simulation median). That is the backstop,
  not the pace. The current Runekeeper's haven comes last, about levels 15
  to 16, which is when he should go home; if pacing needs it, the living
  Runekeeper's voice can give an extra hint at a key moment.

## 7. The road

**[ruled]** An heir walks one haven of each clan that has built one, in a
fixed order: his own clan's nearest haven first, then the clans in the
order the office passes, so the current Runekeeper's clan always comes last,
at his haven. The order renews the binding from weakest to strongest. A
clan with no haven yet is skipped. Where a clan has two havens, the target
is the one nearest the crew when its first hint lands.

| Heir | Clan | Road |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | Skyshoulder | 1 |
| 3rd | Starfist | 1, 2 |
| 4th | Lunarfoot | 1, 2, 3 |
| 5th | Dawnbrow | 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| 6th | Skyshoulder | 2, 3, 4, 5 |
| 7th, the current Runekeeper | Starfist | 3, 4, 1 or 5, 6 |
| 8th, Wan and Tu | Lunarfoot | 4, 1 or 5, 2 or 6, 7 |

- **Out of order.** A haven reached early opens its trial all the same. Its
  records, memory and mark come at once; its clan rune is taken but lies
  dormant, with its spell, until the clan before it fills its place, and
  then both wake together. The current Runekeeper's haven waits for the
  last, because the exchange happens there.
- **Chance havens.** As the crew roams wider, the DM may put them on or near
  a haven his runes are not pointing at, for pacing. It gives what any
  haven gives except a clan rune already held. Every haven is equal.
- **Mishaps.** An Astral or Deep Astral mishap that lands a twin at a haven
  lands at the nearest haven of any kind, placed on the spot if need be: a
  safety in the binding, the same for a single heir. One twin alone cannot
  take the trial.

## 8. Where Tumak stands

**[canon]** Level 4, about to sail in V007 toward H'Catha aboard the Tyrant
Ship. Wan holds 01, 02, 08, 11; Tu holds 07, 08, 09, 16. One match, the left
sole (08). No haven walked. The Runekeeper's voice reached him in V004 ("all
things find their way home"). His first Wayfind is owed in V007.

**[ruled] V007.** The left sole is his first match, so it gives the first
hint, sight, of Lunarfoot's haven, the fourth: a Lunarfoot Runekeeper's
memory. Korey places the haven and writes the hint.

## 9. The conflict

**[ruled]** The twins are a manifestation the order has never seen, and
being two is wearing the binding down. The fragment's pieces strain against
each other, the Runekeeper fades, and the spell fails faster than ever. The
twins do not know it; they learn it as they go. The end of that road is the
binding breaking. Going home is the goal; this is the conflict.

**[ruled] Slip and Strain.** Slip is the unmatched halves; Strain is Slip
less the havens walked. The bands are raised: 0 to 6 steady, 7 to 9
flicker, 10 to 11 the binding slips, 12 and over crisis. The unhiding then
fires in about 6% of runs at the slow pace and 2% at the fast, against 31%
and 16% under the rules page's bands. Strain is 6 today. The player feels
Slip.

**[open]**
- How Slip is felt at the table (the simulation suggests the twins drifting
  out of step).
- How the twins learn the danger. **[proposed]** Through the havens' era
  records: each era shows the order as it was, and the records cannot
  explain what is happening now.
- Whether the crisis band fires the unhiding by the dice, or the DM starts
  it at 12+ when the story can carry it (simulation's lean: the DM).
- Havens worth 2 against Strain.

**[alt] Why the fragment split** (ties to Origin):

- **Chance.** Twins are rare; it happened now. One coincidence, accepted.
- **The seeker's reach.** If the seeker is sealed in the heart, they reach
  for the fragment they once held; twin heirs gave the reach something to
  pull on. Removes the coincidence and makes "being two" someone's doing.
- **Twins before.** Earlier eras had twin heirs and the fragment always took
  one; it splits now because the binding is thin. Learned from the records.

## 10. The unhiding

**[canon]** Left too long, the world comes out of its fold mid-journey. An
unhidden world can be reached by ordinary travel, and noticed: by Vecna's
work, by seekers, by whatever the old link reached.

**[open]** As written that is a slow threat; it needs an immediate one.
**[proposed]** The frozen link (see Origin): the heart reopens onto the old
link and the bleed resumes at once. **[alt]** With the seeker sealed in the
heart, the prisoner wakes inside the world.

## 11. The Becoming and after

**[canon]** At the current Runekeeper's haven the two meet; the exchange
makes the heir the Runekeeper, and the old one fades into the haven. The
fragment has always come home whole; this time it comes as two halves, and
that is the world's second Zenith, a natural one: the homeworld splits into
a blessing faction and an omen faction, and a Monolith follows and archives
people. The world may be rebound, hidden in a new way, or no longer hidden.
The outcome is the table's.

**[ruled]** The exchange gives the last clan rune, the Heart wakes whole,
and he is sent home. Arriving home with the havens walked completes
Homebound.

**[open]**
- Homebound's moment: sent home at once, or a way home he takes when the
  player chooses (the rules page has the latter).
- **[proposed]** A last haven on the homeworld where every Runekeeper takes
  office, which arriving home reaches.

## Conflicts and stale lines

From the top-to-bottom logic check of 2026-11-11. Each needs a ruling, or
a fix in the change audit.

**Need a ruling**

1. **Other Wayfinders and the sending.** Canon has the order sending
   Wayfinders out for many purposes (to learn, recover, witness), not only
   heirs. If every Wayfinder loses the name and the way, how do the others
   come home? Options: the sending is the heir's alone; or every Wayfinder
   loses it and returns through the Runekeeper, the only way in and out.
2. **The fragment whole at about level 12.** Canon (Runekeepers, Still to
   decide) places what the twins' rebuilt fragment does "at about level 12,
   early in Act 3". This draft has the Heart waking whole only at the
   Becoming, about levels 15 to 16. Either level 12 is a different beat (the
   two pieces drawing together, not the Heart), or one of the two moves.
3. **The frozen link replaces "cut".** Canon says the fold cut the link. The
   frozen link has it pulled apart and held. It also changes the counter to
   N'ghathrod ("the order holds the way a link was broken once"): a link
   held apart stays apart only while the binding holds.
4. **The second Zenith gives Vecna a Monolith.** Canon: the twins'
   homecoming brings a second Zenith, and a Monolith follows. A Monolith is
   the licence to link a heart. With the seeker Vecna's, the homecoming
   itself hands his side what it needs, with or without the unhiding. That
   may be the point; it should be chosen, not stumbled on.
5. **One mark per haven.** A walked haven gives a permanent mark, and its
   clan rune counts as a mark too: two now.
6. **The order's emblem.** Canon: the fragment half-formed in a chest. The
   proposal: the Heart with the four clan shapes. One or the other, or the
   Heart is how the fragment looks half-formed.

**Stale lines for the change audit**

7. Rules page: "Slip holds about 7 across levels 5 to 9" (the simulation
   puts the peak at 8 to 10); the Strain bands; "never passes 20"; "a
   clan's rune (17, 18, 19)"; "order is free until the last"; "one haven of
   each of the four clans"; "hidden from the figure and the table until you
   reveal it".
8. The Runekeeper's dossier: "with no map"; "keep Tumak's Heart-rune from
   the table until the Becoming"; "reaches his haven with the clan runes
   held" (three are held; the fourth comes in the exchange).
9. Runekeepers and homeworld pages: the essence "speaks and teaches" (it
   gives the rune and the spell; the haven keeps its Runekeeper's records);
   "no map and no name"; Lunarfoot's seal "with two four-point stars" as the
   clan's seal; "the clans other than Lunarfoot are unnamed".
10. Strain today is 6: under the raised bands that is steady, not flicker.
    Any page or plan reading Strain 6 as "anchors flicker at havens" moves.

**Checked and holding**

- The twins' forgotten childhood meeting with the Runekeeper now has a
  cause (the sending).
- The Becoming tale ("something came looking for their world") fits every
  seeker option.
- The current Runekeeper's own road (3, 4, 1 or 5, 6) matches his dossier:
  he met the sixth Runekeeper at the sixth haven.
- The Runekeeper's voice as an occasional extra hint fits his dossier's
  "speak through him rarely, at key moments".

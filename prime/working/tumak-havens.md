# Draft: Tumak's road to the havens

How Tumak finds the havens, what the runes give him on the way, and what the
sending takes from a Wayfinder. Built in the cadets'-arcs chat of 2026-11-10
on BACKLOG's "Make each cadet's arc tangible" (Crew, crew/index.html). Korey
keeps the lore and mechanic work for it in that chat. Rules are in DM voice,
world facts in-world. Items marked *(proposed)* wait for Korey's yes.

## Design constraints

- **The rules hold for every cycle**, from the first Runekeeper to the eighth
  heir and after, and not only for Wan and Tu.
- **Gregory helps, and never gates.** Any navigator, a Fleet chart office or
  the crew's own legwork does his part, slower. His arc stands on its own.
- **No fixed star chart.** A haven is placed when its first hint lands, so
  the sandbox stays open.
- **A rune never equals a place.** No position is tied to a haven in advance.
- **Getting home takes adventuring, not levelling.** Runes say where; travel
  and trials get him there.

## The sending

When an heir is sent out, the Runekeeper takes from them the name of home,
the way there, and most of the whole picture, and holds it in the binding.
The Wayfinder keeps that they came from somewhere, and loose fragments: a
grandmother, a smell, the way the trees look. It protects the world, because
a captured Wayfinder cannot give it away, and it is the rite of passage: the
journey home is remembering. Each haven gives back the senses of home and
its people; the name of home and the way back return only at the Becoming.
"Given no map and no name" on the pages becomes "loses the name and the
way".

## The clans

Four clans hold the office, each bound to one of the heavens and named for
it and a part of the body, top to bottom, in the order the office passes.
A clan's body part is its name only: the sixteen rolled positions belong to
no clan.

| Order | Clan | Heavens | Body |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st, the founder's | Dawn-, its body part open (was Sunbrow) | the Sun | head |
| 2nd | Skyshoulder | the Sky | shoulder |
| 3rd | Starfist | the Stars | fist |
| 4th | Lunarfoot | the Moon | foot |

Children on the homeworld learn the clans by a rhyme. They sing head,
shoulders, hands and toes, and the movements carry the clans: a finger to
the brow for the head, fists wiggled for the hands. A second verse names the
heavens: the sun, the sky, the stars, the moon, stars and moon. It echoes a
real-world nursery rhyme as a wink for the player. It comes back to the
twins in pieces through the half-rune memories, the song heard, the
movements seen and the movements felt, until the memory is whole.

## The runes

- **A half-rune rolled** gives a memory of home. Korey pre-writes one memory
  per position (16). For the twins each comes in two halves, Wan's and Tu's,
  two sides of one moment; a single heir gets it whole.
- **A matched rune** gives a mark, as now, and one hint toward the current
  target haven (see Hints).
- **Clan runes (17 to 20)**, one for each clan, his own included. Each comes
  from the first haven of that clan he walks; the current Runekeeper's
  clan's rune comes in the exchange at the Becoming. A clan rune takes its
  clan's shape: Lunarfoot's a crescent moon, the first clan's a sun. They count as
  runes for every number (essence runes, marks, passengers). Whether they
  also count as wildcards is held.
- **The Heart (21)** is already matched, and it holds the clan runes: it has
  a place for each clan, and a walked haven fills its clan's place. See On
  the haven's world.
- **The inscription grows with the order.** An heir's inscription is 16
  rolled runes, one clan rune for each clan that has built a haven, and the
  Heart. The second heir carried 18, the third 19, the fourth 20, and every
  heir from the fifth on carries 21. The early Runekeepers held a thinner
  binding, which is how the clans "learned to work it over the generations".
- **Retired**: the four sets (Soul, Maker, Walker, Vessel), their spell
  menus as sets, the Pacing Rule, and the exhaustion cost for extra
  Wayscribe casts. Positions keep their places on the body.

## Hints

The havens are sigils of the one spell, and the fragment is its engine. A
rune matched whole hears the sigil it is bound for, and the sigil answers
with a memory its Runekeeper left in it.

- **A hint is a Runekeeper's memory.** He lives one sense of what that
  haven's Runekeeper saw, heard, touched, or smelled and tasted, as The Giver
  passes memories on. It is how the Runekeepers guide him, in place of a
  voice explaining the way, and it sets his arc apart from the other cadets'.
- **Four hints a haven, one per sense, in the same order**, broad to
  specific: sight, hearing, touch, then smell and taste. Set for the first
  haven; revisit if play says so. The Heart is the fifth sense.
- **Targeting follows the haven order** (see Homebound). Matches 1 to 4 are
  hints toward the first haven on his road, 5 to 8 the second, 9 to 12 the
  third, and 13 to 16 the current Runekeeper's. Where a clan has two havens,
  the target is the one nearest the crew when its first hint lands. A haven
  is placed when its first hint lands, and stays where it is placed.
- **The math.** Every heir makes 16 matches by level 16: four havens at four
  hints each, the longest road any heir walks. The twins match late and in
  bunches, so on matches alone their havens lock at about levels 8, 11, 13
  to 14 and 16. That is the backstop, not the pace.
- **Hints are clues, not gates.** A crew that works a haven out from two or
  three hints goes early; a navigator helps. What happens to the hints left
  over is a BACKLOG item, to see in play.
- **A walked haven names older havens.** Each Runekeeper walked the older
  havens as a Wayfinder, so a haven's essence knows the host worlds of those
  before it: haven 6 knows 1 to 5, and haven 1 knows none. The essence
  teaches the way the hints come, by memory through its Runekeeper's senses.

## On the haven's world

Each haven sits close to its host world's heart, behind a thin veil between
the heart and the world: the haven draws on the heart without opening it.
On the haven's world the Heart-rune stirs as a sense and leads him to the
site. It gives no power. The first time, it is a scene: a rune he has never
seen flares, then dulls. At the site the twins can feel the veil and press
on it, like a hand on a window screen. When they leave the haven's world the
Heart goes inert and fades until the next one.

The player may guess a further rune. It stays unshown until the first haven:
there it appears, he knows it is tied to the havens, and it stays inert. The
rules page's "hidden from the figure and the table until you reveal it"
becomes "hidden until the first haven".

## Homebound and the haven order

An heir walks one haven of each clan that has built one, in a fixed order:
his own clan's nearest haven first, then the clans in the order the office
passes. The current Runekeeper's clan therefore always comes last, at the
current Runekeeper's haven. The order renews the binding from weakest to
strongest. A clan with no haven yet is skipped. The next Wayfinder starts
again with their own clan.

| Heir | Clan | Road (havens in order) |
|---|---|---|
| 2nd | Skyshoulder | 1 |
| 3rd | Starfist | 1, 2 |
| 4th | Lunarfoot | 1, 2, 3 |
| 5th | the first clan (Dawn-) | 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| 6th | Skyshoulder | 2, 3, 4, 5 |
| 7th, the current Runekeeper | Starfist | 3, 4, 1 or 5, 6 |
| 8th, Wan and Tu | Lunarfoot | 4, 1 or 5, 2 or 6, 7 |

At a haven out of order, the trial is not open, as at the current
Runekeeper's haven today; the rules page's "order is free until the last"
becomes this. At the current Runekeeper's haven the exchange gives the last
clan rune, the Heart wakes whole, and he is sent home. Arriving home with
the havens walked completes Homebound.

## Mishaps

An Astral or Deep Astral mishap that lands a twin at a haven lands at the
current target. The crew now knows where it is and still has to get there:
one twin alone cannot take the trial, so the twin already there does not
help.

## A walked haven

Both twins present and the trial passed, the haven gives:

- **A spell** from the clan's menu, named for the clan (the Lunarfoot menu,
  and so on). The old set menus pass to the clans: the first clan the old Soul
  spells, Skyshoulder the old Vessel spells, Starfist the old Maker spells,
  Lunarfoot the old Walker spells. One free cast a day, then with spell
  slots. Up to seven over the campaign. Needs a mechanic review.
- That Runekeeper's chapter of the order's history, and memories of home:
  its senses, its people.
- A permanent mark.
- The essence's knowledge of the older havens.
- The clan's rune, if it is the first haven of that clan he walks.

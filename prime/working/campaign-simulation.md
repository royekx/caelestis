# Reference: whole-campaign simulation (2026-11-12)

A thought experiment, kept as a baseline to check pressure points against. Six simulated players and a simulated DM ran the campaign from V007 to the end, against the repo as it stood on 2026-11-12. Nothing here is canon or a plan. [sim fill] marks a small gap the simulated DM invented. [big gap] marks a structural hole. Rulings and open items it prompted go to the subject's draft, STAGING or BACKLOG, never here. Some of its reads are already known to be wrong (for example, which players have played Fonains, Emporia and the Call of Cthulhu games); this file keeps the run as it was.

## The table

Personas inferred from voyages 001 to 006 and Korey's notes on each player.

- **Sol's player.** Plants himself at the front and carries burdens for others: he jinxed the horrors on Cargo and took Boogie's misfortune in V002. He speaks for the crew in parleys. He drifts when a scene asks nothing physical of him, and does best with a written objective.
- **Bartholomew's player.** Has the practical instincts: noisemakers, the lit match down the shaft, the ape pulling the great flower. Wants a list of things to do and a fight at the end. He missed V001 and V003, so he carries the least table memory.
- **Gregory's player.** Plays procedure deadpan ("no such provision appears to exist"). Saves things nobody asked him to save, like the Vortex Warp on Ostekk-6. Will steer any session toward navigation if he's handed a chart.
- **Boogie's player.** Generous and chaotic. Built legs out of himself and called the retreat. Kept quiet about the half-ship. He hands the spotlight to others, so the DM has to push his arc. Missed V006.
- **Casey's player.** Steals a shirt, follows Tumak without a word, tries to cut off an eyestalk to take home. The most at ease with horror and grey choices. His own thread has cooled, and he missed V003.
- **Tumak's player.** The planner: captain, the lie to Ostekk-10, naming Joffrey to Mirt. Holds the biggest secret at the table, the twins, and will reveal it when it pays off most.

## The run

### Act 1: Spelljammer Academy (levels 4 to 5)

**1. Shakedown (V007).** The Discord vote picks Saerthe by one vote [sim fill]. He takes the helm with Ryeback on deck, which leaves Miken aboard while the crew is ashore. [OOC, Bartholomew: "Wait, who's Miken again?"]

**2. The crossing.** The daily rolls bring the shadow ship (forced on day 12) and the mercane trader. The trader changes Vaelorix's coin and carries word of the crew ahead. Bartholomew's player only remembers the sending stone when Ryeback says Miken's name. Miken doesn't confess. The crew is kind to him, and that raises the quality of Qitru's intel. A drifting Watcher buoy hails Ezra by name [sim fill].

**3. H'Catha: the reception.** Qitru's rumour ("beholder killers, inbound") reaches every port first. One ruler wants to hire them; the ports want them watched. Tumak's player realises his own ladder joke caused it and is delighted. Ostekk's name turns up in the first port, so the spy tension has a real object. Struggle: Sol and Bartholomew sit out two long social scenes. [OOC, Bartholomew: "Can I punch someone yet?"] A sea-cave infiltration with a checklist (free three locathah, open one gate) brings him back [sim fill: ruler names, Edicts and what the rulers see are still open].

**4. H'Catha: the seam and the eclipse.** Ondessa makes her offer and the table splits. Sol wants to stay and help her retake her citadel; Casey wants the adamantine and out. Ezra's training pulls him away at the worst moment, as planned. They take a load during the three hours of dark. The CRC was meant to meet Sol in this window, but the V007 notes keep it near Caelestis [sim fill: delayed to graduation; the two pages disagree]. Level 5. **6. Graduation night.** The surface attack has no composition [big gap]; the sim uses Mustaf Shultz's giff and the Crow pirates [sim fill]. The CRC arrives in the chaos: "Sol Fortuna. ### Act 2: Driftwardens in the Field (levels 6 to 10)

**11. Lunarfoot's haven at Selûne (level 7).** Gregory plots the route and gets the navigation spotlight he wanted. The haven's trial and its failure cost are unset [sim fill]. **12. The church (Seekers S1).** The players spot Vecna's symbol at once. Casey's player loves the unease. **13. Sol's quest.** Beshaba's want is unset [big gap]. The sim makes it: break the Prism's hold on one Misfortuned before the Fortuned take another [sim fill]. **23. Oerth's heart.** A heist through a linked heart. The eye in the sky is about four-fifths drawn [sim fill: clock rule undecided]. Gregory's want gets named here: arrive before the break, as his maker's journal says [sim fill]. He breaks one lid link.

**24. The Becoming.** Tumak reaches the last haven, and the homeworld splits into blessing and omen factions. Dissolution unmakes joins and N'ghathrod is made of joins, so the sim makes it the finale's weapon [sim fill].

**26. After.** A lieutenant starts growing toward becoming the next N'ghathrod. Vecna falls back on his contingencies. One Call of Cthulhu session in GAL-02 as epilogue. The campaign ends with Rolt posting a new board.

## Forks

- **Graduation (ch. 6).** Joffrey escapes: Act 2 becomes a chase, with the crew arriving second everywhere. Sol and Bartholomew like having a target. Tumak and Casey lose the decoding they enjoy, and Vocath wins early. They return A.T.L.A.S.: institutional backing, but Act 2 slows. The sim rates keeping it richest and the escape clearest for this table.
- **The unhiding (ch. 19).** Home early: the homecoming Zenith hits mid Act 3, the colony and Vecna both notice, and the Becoming never happens. In exchange Act 3 gets the midpoint and the engine it lacks: protect the unhidden world.
- **The finale (ch. 25).** Kill him outright: if the ship dies with him, so do the families. Sol's player refuses; Casey's argues for it. Parley: N'ghathrod has never heard of Vecna. Shown the eye over Oerth, he learns he was a brushstroke and turns. The invasion ends without a kill.

## What the table latched onto

- **Sol:** the wanted notice, the CRC wearing his face, Burden as heroism.
- **Bartholomew:** skiff fights, the crate of copies, Miken's fiancé's debts. Ignored the bloom until it became a list.
- **Gregory:** every route, the Latchling's sadness, the inscription plane. The table's straight man.
- **Boogie:** the freed harbourmaster and the followers. The colony finale gave him back the spotlight he kept giving away.
- **Casey:** the child's drawing, Cairn, the twins reveal, Viren. Chased the church's grey goodness far past where the plan stops.
- **Tumak:** the beholder-killer rumour, the twin reveal, the race home.
- **Together:** Rolt, Vocath as the hated face, Cairn, "eyes everywhere", and a shared map the plan never asked for.

## Where they struggled

- **Spy genre at H'Catha.** Sol and Bartholomew went quiet until they had physical objectives.
- **Gaps between sessions.** Miken's sending stone, the half-ship, Joffrey's bottle and Ezra's letter were each forgotten at least once. The half-ship and half-logbook never came back.
- **Spotlight.** Boogie in Act 1 (his V006 absence cut his build-up). Bartholomew at the church.
- **Eye confusion.** H'Catha's eye geography, the Sovereign Eye, the eye-in-a-wheel, the church's hand-and-eye, Tharizdun's Elder Eye and Vecna's sky-eye blurred together through Act 2.
- **Act 2's leash.** "Realmspace plus one" held back Viren, Aethris and the bloom's world.
- **The church.** Recognised, nothing to act on, stall.

## The story as a whole

- **Theme.** Strongest where two arcs meet: the twins and Casey's lost brother; Boogie's need to belong and N'ghathrod's love of outcasts. Weakest for Sol and Gregory, whose missing piece is unset.
- **N'ghathrod.** Works as structure and arrives late as a face. Until the end of Act 2 the felt villain is Vocath. Oryx had Crota first; Zhauveth is the obvious Crota and never appears on-screen in the plan.
- **Vecna.** Lands almost too well. The players recognise him in Act 2, then wait for him for two Acts. With his per-Act beats unset, he feels absent for long stretches, then the Citadel hits all at once.
- **The ending.** Pays off when the colony is treated as a people and the crew gives it a home: the theme turned on the villain. The materials are there; the final scene is not designed.

## Big gaps, ranked

1. **N'ghathrod's finale is unbuilt.** Rule whether the ship dies with him, since that decides whether killing him kills the families. Consider making Dissolution the planned key.
2. **Act 3 has no goal or engine.** Option: "get inside Vth'oramu and learn what he's building", with the unhiding of Tumak's world as a midpoint clock. Or a race for the residue piece.
3. **The villain has no face before Act 3.** Make Zhauveth the recurring Act 2 antagonist, or keep the taken Joffrey alive as a prisoner the crew keeps going back to.
4. **Act 1's close is underspecified.** H'Catha's length, the surface attack, the thief, who signs the order. Option: compress attack and graduation into one voyage, with the Crow pirates as raiders.
5. **Act 2's range against the personal arcs.** Pick a second system on the lane toward Viren, or move Casey's and Sol's homeworld beats to Act 3 openly and give each a local Act 2 stand-in.
6. **Sol's quest and Gregory's want are unset.** Set Beshaba's want before the CRC's second meeting.
7. **Vocath has no endgame.** Plan a mid Act 2 takedown and how the colony pivots, or make him visibly untouchable on purpose.
8. **Closing a heart has no rules.** Rule a costly method before the bleed set piece.

## Things to watch for

1. **Eye overload.** Give each eye its own look and never describe two in one session.
2. **Memory loss between sessions.** Open interludes with the dangling objects the plan needs live (Miken, the half-ship).
3. **Spy scenes.** Give Sol and Bartholomew a physical objective in every one.
4. **The church.** Decide ahead of time what happens when they attack it.
5. **Arcs bunching at levels 7 and 8.** The haven, the bloom, the CRC and Viren: no two in the same month.
6. **The twin reveal belongs to Tumak's player.** Leave natural openings; don't force it.
7. **Absent players.** Give each personal beat a version that still lands if that player is away.
8. **Ezra.** Check every Ezra beat against his player's campaign before it runs.

# Draft: building the middle of the campaign

"The church" in this draft means the first Seekers chapter the crew meets: a benign-seeming congregation that shows Vecna's hand and eye openly, with its temple on its own world (Campaign § Act 2). It never means the Seekers as a whole.

Rungs, fronts and contracts: a framework for the post-graduation middle, from research into campaign design and fitted to current canon. Proposals are marked. Builds on the STAGING rulings "The invasion and the eye are two plans" and "Vecna's place in the campaign" (PR #115).

## 1. The core idea
- Don't write the story of the middle. Write the questions it answers. A revelation ladder lists what the crew should come to understand, in rough order. Each line is one rung.
- Give each rung three clues, in different forms. Spread them across contracts. Clues can move: a clue goes wherever the crew goes.
- The opposition pushes on its own. Each faction gets a front: what it wants, and the steps it takes if nobody stops it. Each new contract board reflects where those fronts stand.
- The crew picks the contract. Inside it, rails are fine ("scenes have walls"). Between contracts, they choose.
- **A contract is a vehicle, not a formula** (Korey, 2026-11-07). Contracts are the main way an adventure starts, and not every one ties to a thread. Some are plain: carry a dignitary to a planet. The job is dull, and it puts the crew somewhere something interesting is happening that has nothing to do with the job. What they stumble on there is the adventure.
- **The promise to the table:** however dull a contract sounds, something happens there. Every contract leads to an adventure and pays off with a story, something learned or a reward. None is ever a boring job and home again. The job itself may have nothing to do with what they find, and what they find may not touch the main story, at least not on the surface.
- So a lead can come from the job or from the place it takes them. Few contracts carry a cosmic trace: Campaign § Act 2 says a contract has at most one trace, and most have none. Where a lead exists, it lives in the local layer (a name, a buyer, a drawing).
- **Threads interconnect.** A contract can tie to Vocath, to the colony, to Vecna's Seekers, or to none of them. Pull on one and it tugs another somewhere else. The board should show that spread, so the crew sees the overlaps for itself.
- The "next rung" feeling comes from two things. First, the crew carries something out of each job that doesn't fit. Second, someone remarks on the pattern. Your rule "Twice, or say it" already covers this. Captain Haymond Rolt is the natural person to say it.

## 2. The framework

### 2a. The invasion ladder (Act 2, proposal)
Vecna already has a ladder: "What the crew can learn, in order" on the Vecna opposition page. The campaign threat has none. This one is built only from lines already on the colony's opposition page (`opposition/vthorama.html`) and the BACKLOG. Rung 5 is the goal Act 2 already states.

- **Rung 0, the weather.** Zeniths and Monoliths are coming more often. This is atmosphere and needs no clues.
- **Rung 1, one hand behind unrelated jobs.** Odd local incidents share a buyer or a cause.
  - Clues: the beacon-cut crystal buyer at Lane Marker 14. The surveyors on Anadia, whose eyes are wrong in a child's drawing. The clockwork horrors' resonance-cores come from the same crystal line. Gregory hums near all of it.
- **Rung 2, people are being replaced and stay themselves.**
  - Clues: a missing person who comes back fine but wrong. Someone who never takes off their gloves. Three officials who all arrived the same year. Joffrey's body and his one word, "Oramu" (if captured or killed at the Fork). The Bellwether Crow survivor.
- **Rung 3, they are searching worlds for one specific place.**
  - Clues: the Anadia well chamber (a failed survey for the Atrium). Digs at a world's deepest points. Scholars and archivists who go missing. A.T.L.A.S. readings (State B2). Sites already worked by the time the crew arrives (State A).
- **Rung 4, it is an illithid operation with ships and a supply line.**
  - Clues: a tadpole crate or a brine cask. A skiff-crew clash. Captives moving along Vocath's routes.
- **Rung 5, name the operation.** This ends Act 2: the crew names the operation and takes it to the Fleet.
  - Clues: a taken prisoner. "Vth'orama" translated. The instruction "a world with a Monolith is a marked world" found in a log.
- **Rung 6, late Act 2 or Act 3: Vth'oramu exists.**
  - Clues: a skiff's log, a taken prisoner, a chart that calls the dreadnought wildlife.

How to use the ladder:
- **Three forms per rung.** Use your hook kinds as the forms: Character, Place, Event, Object, Pattern. Three clues of one kind fail the same way.
- **Two kinds of lead.** An existential lead says something matters. An access lead says how to reach it. Every rung needs at least one access lead. Seed existential leads early so the access lead lands as a payoff.
- **Rungs 1 to 4 can fall in any order.** They behave like nodes, not steps. Rung 5 needs at least Rungs 2 and 4.
- **Push and pull players.** Sol's and Bartholomew's players want direct hooks, so put the access clue in an Event or Character for them. Casey's and Tumak's players pull on mystery, so give them the Pattern clues.

Keep a separate, slower Seekers ladder as the undercurrent:
- S1: the church of the hand and eye (through Hessa Dun's uncle).
- S2: a second chapter that reads the same eye differently.
- S3: every chapter name carries the word for "seekers".
- S4, Act 3: the colony is a chapter too. This is where the two ladders join.

### 2b. Fronts
A front is a threat's goal, its doom if nobody stops it, three to five steps (portents), and the questions play will answer (stakes). Your "villain planning at three resolutions" is already this.

**N'ghathrod (the campaign front)**
- Wants: a home for his people.
- Doom: a lattice of linked worlds, held from inside.
- Portents at Act 2 scale:
  1. The taken sit in small posts on sector worlds.
  2. A survey finds an Atrium.
  3. The residue piece arrives and a heart opens.
  4. A second heart is ready and a link is made. The two worlds bleed into each other (your planned bleed mission).
  5. Doorkeepers take Fleet or port seats.
- Counter-rule (STAGING): when pressed, he abandons Vecna's list and takes whatever worlds he can get.
- Stakes: Does the crew name the colony before any institution does? Who in the sector is already taken? Does the colony notice Tumak or the Latchling?
- Wins the crew can measure: a destroyed crate costs him months of placements.

**The Seekers**
- Each chapter is a separate danger, and none coordinates with another.
- The church seeks [proposal: its aim is set when you build it]. The Fracture Cult pushes worlds toward Zenith. The Archivists gather at Monolith sites.
- Give each chapter two or three portents.
- Stakes: Does the crew misread the church? Does any chapter help them?

**The Order of All**
- Wants: to preserve Continuity, with too few Watchers.
- Portents: Watcher requests rise. A Zenith on a contract world. A Restorer walks into a Zenith. Restorers compete for residue.
- Stakes: Does the crew trade what it has seen for the Ledger? Does Aurel Venn find them?

**The Fleet**
- Wants: to hold the sector with too few crews.
- Portents: Mirt's standing after the Fork. Vocath's gains. The illithid sect the Corps cannot name. Below-rank contracts going unfilled. [Proposal: Ondrel's "neutral" status gets tested.]
- Stakes: Does the Fleet listen when the crew names the operation? This front decides how Act 2 ends.

**Vocath (a front of his own; Korey, 2026-11-07)**
- Wants: his house's profit, and Mirt disgraced and pried loose from Caelestis's protection.
- His dealings run past the colony. The captive line is one of them; the Fonains shard trade, what moves under his personal seal, and other trouble he causes are others. [Proposal: set the rest when each is built.]
- Tied to the colony and to Mirt. A blow to Vocath is a devastating blow to the colony: it loses its captives and its reach into Caelestis (BACKLOG: how the colony pivots without Vocath). Taking him down is hard.
- Stakes: Does the crew tie the seal to the colony? What does Mirt do when they bring him proof?

**Astra Virex** sits as a danger inside the personal threads, not a front of its own.

### 2c. Contracts as nodes
- **Fill in the existing three layers for each contract.** Surface (the job), undertow (the local contest of wants), lead (at least one, toward a rung or another node) and trace (optional, rare). Then add: its front, and what happens if the crew declines.
- **Categories.** Your decide is still open. Proposal: Loose (local trouble, or a plain job that only gets the crew somewhere), Tied (a front: Vocath, the colony, a Seekers chapter, the Order or the Fleet), Personal (one PC's thread).
- **Board mix (proposal).** Of the 2 or 3 contracts at rank plus 1 reach, use one of each category. At least one carries a lead to the current rung.
- **Declined contracts** resolve without the crew. They come back as gossip or a posting, and the clue moves on with them. This is already your rule.

### 2d. Cadence (proposal)
- Act 2 covers levels 5 to 10. Roughly one rung per level, so six rungs. Allow 2 or 3 contracts per rung, about 15 to 20 sessions.
- For comparison: Paizo's hardcover adventure paths cover 9 to 10 levels each. Savage Worlds plot-point campaigns use 8 to 12 plot points.
- Gate the fixed set pieces by triggers, not session numbers. Fill the gaps between them with contracts, the way Savage Worlds fills them with "Savage Tales".
- Fixed set pieces (plot points): the Fork aftermath, the church, the first skiff clash, the linked-worlds bleed, and naming the operation to the Fleet.

### 2e. Reacting to the crew, after each session
1. Mark which clues they found, per rung.
2. Tick the fronts. Tick from what happened in play, and from what the crew ignored.
3. Resolve declined contracts as gossip.
4. Ask what the world looks like now, and post the next board from that.
5. Run the board through a three-question check, adapted from Justin Alexander's Dragon Heist remix:
   - Do they need a lead to the next rung? Put it on the board.
   - Did they hurt a front? It answers them.
   - Are they drifting? A front comes to them.
6. When they choose a lane, move the floating clues into that lane.

### 2f. The hidden Vecna clock
- Per the chart-5 STAGING ruling, start it at about two-thirds (the chart shows 67%, 10 of 15 marks).
- Proposal: advance it by milestone, not by roll. It ticks only when one of these happens in the world: a N'ghathrod portent that opens or links a heart, or a Zenith on a contract world. That gives every tick a cause.
- The crew sees only the weather, in postings and gossip. The clock is never a deadline.
- The roll-or-milestone question is still an open BACKLOG decide ("How the eye's clock advances").

## 3. Worked example: from graduation to Rung 1
Board one is the graduation board already in BACKLOG ("NEW: Contract board"). Everything in it is BACKLOG [add] material, not yet on a page.

**Lane Marker 14 (Loose)**
- Surface: a dark beacon.
- Undertow: the Ardenne family took the crystal to pay for a sick child's care.
- Leads: Sefn Tolur, the buyer, pays through a broker on Toril's orbital market. That broker is an access lead. Gregory hums, a Pattern clue.
- Rung 1 clue: the line wants beacon-cut crystal bought from four systems. It is the same crystal as the horrors' cores the crew has already seen.

**Dry Well, Anadia (Tied: the colony; Personal: Tumak)**
- Surface: a water compact dispute between Dunmarrow and Kesh Hollow.
- Undertow: Elder Tobb Ashlow took the surveyors' payment to deepen the well.
- Trace: the chamber, the eye-in-a-wheel, the four labourers' bodies, and Tumak's runes surfacing.
- Leads: the tall quiet woman and the talkative man. The child's drawing. They went inland toward the ranges.
- Clues: Rung 1 and Rung 3.

**Repossession, Tallow Wren (Tied: Vocath and the Fleet; Personal: Miken)**
- Surface: recover the skiff at Toril's orbital market.
- Undertow: the moneylender also holds Miken's fiancé's debts.
- Lead: the log "ties to the Fork".
- [Proposal: in State A, the log records the ship Joffrey escaped on. In State B, it shows a ship shadowing the Bellwether Crow a year earlier.] This becomes a Rung 2 or Rung 4 clue.

**Watcher Transport (Tied: the Order)**
- Surface: take Ezra to the Hall of the Watchers.
- [Proposal: the Hall is swamped with Zenith requests, which shows the weather. A Watcher passes the crew a tip, and the Fleet does take contracts on Watcher tips. The tip becomes a board-two posting.]

**Board two [proposal]**
- If they took the well: "an archivist missing" on another sector world, the surveyors' next stop. This leads toward Rung 3.
- If they skipped the well: gossip. "Dunmarrow and Kesh Hollow came to blows; the well was sealed." The surveyors turn up later.
- Add one Personal contract: a delivery to Hessa Dun's uncle's world, which opens the S1 church.
- Add one "came back wrong" contract, which opens Rung 2.

**Reaching Rung 1**
- It lands when the crew holds two of these threads and Rolt says the quiet part. For example: "Third crew this month back with a story about someone buying crystal."
- Conclusion: one hand.
- Note: the eye on the well wall and the eye at the church make a natural "twice" for the Seekers ladder. Whether they connect is yours to rule.

## Reference notes from the research
- **Light of Xaryxis** moves the party "location to location, plot to plot", which reviewers call borderline railroading. That is the failure mode to avoid.
- **Dragon Heist, remixed by Justin Alexander:** faction outposts give clues to faction lairs. Response teams "dump a lead in their lap" when players stall.
- **Curse of Strahd:** a card reading places the key items, so the same story sits in different places each run. This matches your "story is portable".
- **Critical Role, campaign 3:** the Ruidus threat was seeded early through Imogen's red-storm dreams and paid off over about 120 episodes. Episodes 112 to 114 ran Vox Machina in a concurrent arc against the Malleus Key. That is the model for your later crossover. My source doesn't confirm that the finale split the cast into teams.
- **Mercer:** before each session he works out where the last one could go, and "pulling in the consequences" of what already happened. **Mulligan:** build the world in broad strokes and develop what players chase. Both are paraphrased from interviews.
- **Matt Colville:** I couldn't reach a primary text in time, so nothing above relies on him.

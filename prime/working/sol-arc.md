## The scattering [ruled]

The Misfortuned on Aethris died when the Zenith came, except one or two
the Prism Ascendant archived (see Archived, below). The misfortune they
carried did not die with them. It scattered across the galaxy and landed
unevenly, on people who have never seen Aethris: whole lives where bad luck
gathers far past chance. Sol goes out to collect it.

## What the Fortuned need [ruled]

Sol is the last Misfortuned. The Fortuned's ritual stands open and closes
only on a Misfortuned at the temple; part of Sol has to go back for the
misfortune to be set right. The Fortuned believe that closing it on him
draws the scattered misfortune back into one bearer and restores their
perfect fortune. Each burden he takes on makes him more of what they need,
and the CRC's urgency grows with it.

**[proposed] What the Zenith did to Aethris.** The Prism Ascendant took the
character of the ritual that raised it, as every Monolith does, and leaned
on Determination, which holds that every outcome must be decided. Aethris's
luck is decided in pairs now: a good thing comes, and a bad thing follows it
at once. The Fortuned live inside that ledger, which is why they want Sol
back. Beshaba calls it no more balanced than perfection was. This replaces
the pages' "it does nothing" for the Prism Ascendant (see Promotes to);
waits for Korey's yes.

## Beshaba's charge [ruled]

Beshaba never tells Sol her purpose; on some level she believes in choice.
Her charge is plain: "You are to balance the scales they tipped in their
favour." She shows him, in clear visions, where the misfortune has landed:
a face, a place, what is wrong.

## Burdens [ruled]

- Collecting a burden is role-play he carries, with no new tracking for the
  player. Burden and Unburden on his sheet are the arc in small.
- A burden need not be a whole adventure. It can be a cleansing: he sees the
  person, goes to them, and lifts it.
- Lifting a burden shifts the balance around it. It can open something for
  the party, such as favour with the people helped; watch for the butterfly
  effect.
- The first is easy, a tutorial; later burdens grow harder.
- Not every stop has one. The DM paces them; some sit on the party's road,
  some are detours that pull away without breaking it.
- **[proposed]** Each burden leaves a mark he carries and one new small daily
  misfortune, as flavour.
- Sol leaves Aethris on duty, not guilt. He thinks the whole situation was
  wrong and does not feel sorry for leaving.

## The road

1. **V007, the crossing [ruled].** A vision points him at H'Catha.
2. **H'Catha [ruled].** A locathah first: a downtrodden person, which Sol
   cares about more than a beholder. It is the tutorial burden, a cleansing,
   and it leads into Ondessa, the larger burden. Ezra, a paladin, joins the
   crew here.
3. **Caelestis main entrance [canon].** The CRC's first encounter, on the
   return.
4. **Act 2 and on [open].** Burdens placed as the crew travels, harder each
   time.
5. **Aethris [ruled].** He goes home and closes the ritual on his own terms,
   setting fortune and misfortune right. **[proposed]** He lets everything he
   carries go back to even chance, so the ritual breaks rather than completes.

## Archived [ruled]

One or two Misfortuned were archived by the Prism Ascendant. Played only if
Sol shows interest: if he role-plays someone he was close to, that person
was archived. They surface if the crew reaches the Radiant Citadel.

## Play notes [ruled]

- His player struggles to visualise: describe more, and give every hook a
  plain "go do this".
- He likes tanking and taking the brunt; burdens are that in story.
- Steer him toward his subclass by placing allies' rolls that matter close
  to him.

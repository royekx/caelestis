## The scattering [ruled]

The Misfortuned on Aethris died when the Zenith came, except one or two the
Prism Ascendant archived (see Archived). The Fortuned's ritual did not go as
they meant: it still stands incomplete, and luck came loose. Fortune and misfortune both scattered across
the galaxy and fell disproportionately on certain people: lives where bad
luck gathers far past chance, and lives where good luck never breaks. Sol
will not find them all.

## Why the Fortuned need him [ruled]

The Fortuned set out to wipe misfortune from Aethris as if it were a disease.
Sol had become the main antenna for misfortune on Aethris, which is what
makes him the component their ritual lacks, and his leaving showed them what
they had missed: fortune needs misfortune to balance against. Since he left, their own fortune has stopped working as it should.
They need him back near them, and part of him has to go back for fortune and
misfortune to be set right. The CRC's lines say it: "Your absence has created
imbalance. Adjustment required."

**[open]** What Aethris is like now. It cannot simply be normal, because that
would mean chance is spread evenly there. The shape gets settled later.

## Beshaba and Tymora [ruled]

- Sol and Beshaba hold that misfortune is part of life and makes a person
  stronger. Sol believes he is made to carry it better than most, and that
  many people who carry too much did nothing to deserve it. Taking it from
  them is his duty. He left Aethris on that duty, not in guilt: he thinks the
  whole situation there was wrong.
- Beshaba never tells Sol her purpose; on some level she believes in choice.
  Her charge is plain: "You are to balance the scales they tipped in their
  favour."
- She also puts extremely lucky people in his way, and those meetings test
  his belief as much as hers. Luck brings benefits, but too much of a good
  thing is a bad thing: a person who never meets misfortune cannot grow.
  Everyone should hold the same amount of luck.
- Tymora is seen as the good sister, Lady Luck, and Beshaba as the evil one.
  She is not. Too much fortune does harm too, and that reversal is the arc's
  theme.
- Over time Sol's work changes his relationship with Beshaba, and later with
  Tymora. Late in the arc Tymora asks why he does this. Beshaba's own road
  leads to facing her sister: the way this was done is not the way it should
  be done, and Tymora knows better. Sol is how the sisters mend.
- **[open]** Whether Tymora notices herself thinning on Aethris, and acts
  (already open on factions/aethris.html); it may be where her late question
  to Sol starts.
- **[open]** Keep the metaphysics of luck simple. Luck running out, or being
  used up, stays out.

## Taking on luck [ruled]

Lucky and unlucky are the same thing in different measure, so Sol takes both
in the same way.

- **The check.** Sol takes the luck in with a Charisma check against a
  threshold the DM sets. The check never decides whether it works. It sets
  how much he takes.
- **The cost.** Misfortune deals necrotic damage to Sol; fortune deals
  radiant damage.
- **The log.** The player keeps one running record of how much misfortune
  and how much fortune he has taken on. Nothing else to track. Taking luck
  never changes his Misfortune points or his Jinx.
- **The change.** Something about him changes as the totals rise: how he
  looks, or more misfortune happening around him. Played as role-play.
- **[open]** The thresholds, what happens when a total crosses one, and any
  boons at milestones.
- Not every relief is a whole adventure. Some are a cleansing: he sees the
  person, goes to them, and takes it.
- Taking it shifts the balance around it and can open something for the
  party, such as favour with the people helped. Watch for the butterfly
  effect.
- The first is easy, a tutorial; later ones grow harder.
- Not every stop has one. The DM paces them; some sit on the party's road,
  some are detours that pull away without breaking it.

## How a vision comes [ruled]

Small Final Destination chains happen around Sol, and they are worth playing
up. The first, on the V007 crossing:

He is sitting at a meal when he feels the ripple of misfortune, and a small
arc of black lightning runs up his hand. He knows something is coming. A
vegetable rolls along the table and drops onto a knife; the knife flips and
strikes a plate; the plate flies up. He can act or not. The plate comes down
and does not shatter. When he goes to it, as he touches or reaches it, it
shatters, and in the shattering he sees the vision.

The vision: a locathah, described, being shunned by people around them. One
says the locathah's name: "Leave us alone, [name]. You bring nothing but bad
luck."

**[open]** Whether the vision carries markers of H'Catha. With them, he may
ask, and someone aboard, perhaps Saerthe Abizjn, could name the world, so he
arrives looking for the locathah. Without them, the meeting comes as a
surprise.

## The road

1. **V007, the crossing [ruled].** The plate and the vision.
2. **H'Catha [ruled].** The locathah first: a downtrodden person, which Sol
   cares about more than a beholder. It is the tutorial, and it leads into
   Ondessa, the larger burden. Ezra, a paladin, joins the crew here. The
   crew meets the locathah by chance. They are a seam-runner who could guide
   the crew through the seams to the meteor, and others will not hire them
   because they are bad luck. Sol recognises them from the vision. Taking
   their misfortune opens the guide's route and the locathah's favour.
3. **Caelestis main entrance [canon].** The CRC's first encounter, on the
   return.
4. **Act 2 and on [open].** People with too much misfortune or too much
   fortune, placed as the crew travels, harder each time.
5. **Aethris [ruled].** He goes home and sets fortune and misfortune right,
   and the sisters face each other.

## Archived [ruled]

One or two Misfortuned were archived by the Prism Ascendant. Played only if
Sol shows interest: if he role-plays someone he was close to, that person
was archived. They surface if the crew reaches the Radiant Citadel.

## Play notes [ruled]

- His player struggles to visualise: describe more, and give every hook a
  plain "go do this".
- He likes tanking and taking the brunt; taking on luck is that in story.
- Steer him toward his subclass by placing allies' rolls that matter close
  to him.

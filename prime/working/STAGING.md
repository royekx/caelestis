## Cosmology

### Naming: Rykoe and Erevos (cosmology/, glossary/, chart/, survey; done everywhere)
- Rykoe is the centre of the cosmos: the remains of the Reach world, the black hole beneath the Core Monolith and the Radiant Citadel, around which the five galaxies lie, and which opens only when all five cores wake. The First Heart lies beneath Rykoe. Erevos is the black hole at the core of GAL-01, and the galaxy is named for it: the Erevos Galaxy. ### The inscription plane, strokes and marks (opposition/vecna.html, cosmology/index.html, glossary/, chart/)
- The eye is drawn on the inscription plane: the plane through Oerth, perpendicular to the line from Oerth through Erevos. Erevos is the eye's centre, its pupil. The image is a straight projection along that line, with no perspective: a heart's place in the eye is its offset from the line, and its distance along the line does not count. Every heart in the figure lies on Erevos's side of the plane, in front of Oerth.
- The eye forms through the links, not through light. Nothing between a heart and Oerth blocks it, and no observer's eyes see it.
- A stroke is everything that lands on one point of the eye: a line through the galaxy parallel to the Oerth–Erevos line, with a margin of width around it. Any world on or near a stroke can carry that point.
- A mark is the heart actually chosen on a stroke. Vecna chooses marks for practical reasons: a Monolith stands there, the heart has been found, a Zenith is coming. Marks sit at different distances and heights along their strokes, so on a top-down chart they zigzag across the galaxy and their links cross at random. Only on the inscription plane do they form the eye.
- A mark is a whole system. Every planet in it, destroyed planets included, must have its heart found, opened and linked, and each link needs a Monolith on that world. A moon is part of its planet and shares its heart. Vecna therefore favours systems with few planets, and a mark takes years of work.
- Inside a mark, the system's hearts link to each other, and one of them links back to Oerth. Any heart in a mark can also link out to a heart in another system. The links form one network: every heart in it reaches every other, node by node, and anyone travelling it passes through the hearts between.
- The plan can change. A lost mark is replaced by another system on the same stroke.
- Vecna also has N'ghathrod open and link hearts the figure does not need, as backups and to muddy the pattern. N'ghathrod goes where he is told and cannot tell the needed hearts from the rest.
- Play (DM voice): the crew can reach the eye three ways, all kept open. N'ghathrod's top-down chart of marks done and planned looks haphazard, but the marks share one alignment seen from the right angle. Oerth's heart shows the eye in its sky. Lore about Vecna sends them back to the chart. This is late Arc 3 or Arc 4 material; plant seeds earlier.

### Hearts: scale, sight through a link, and gods (cosmology/index.html, glossary/)
- A world's heart is, in every sense that matters, the world's centre: its metaphysical core.
- A heart is a large interior, but it holds no civilization. A crew and its cargo pass through a link; a ship does not, and Vth'oramu never can.
- Linked worlds bleed into each other, and inside a linked heart the linked world bleeds into the descent: its sky carries images of the other world, like clouds that form pictures of it.
- A heart can be closed. Closing a heart ends every link that runs through it: nothing passes between it and the hearts it was linked to. The First Heart, closed since the Reach, is the oldest closed heart. Replaces "A link cannot be undone": a link stands until one of its hearts is closed.
- Polarity keeps gods out of hearts as it keeps them off worlds. Vecna is the one exception, and only for Oerth's heart: his ascension charged him into it. Every other heart pushes him back. He cannot go to the hearts he needs, so he makes them report back to the one he holds.

## Campaign

### Oerth's heart (opposition/vecna.html, locations/oerth.html, cosmology/index.html)
- Oerth's heart is Vecna's seat and the retina of the eye. Every link in the figure runs back to it, so its sky is full of the marked worlds bleeding through, and together they form the eye, with gaps where marks are not yet linked. Vecna watches the eye form there.
- Oerth's heart is the most valuable thing Vecna has and the most vulnerable. Breaking it would be the greatest setback he could suffer.
- N'ghathrod uses Oerth's heart as the hub of his network, moving small teams between linked hearts through it; Vth'oramu stays out in the drift. Somewhere in the long plan he stopped asking whether the voice he follows is his elder brain. It showed him enough that he no longer cared, and he chose to trust it.
- Oerth is the world where the heroes lost. Most of it is not evil, but it is ruled by wizards who follow Vecna openly.
- Play (DM voice): the crew's road to Oerth's heart runs through the network, not across the drift. A linked heart the figure does not need can be their way in. Flying to Oerth risks Vth'oramu waiting there. Arc 4.

### The gods and Vecna's ascension (factions/deities.html, dossiers/vecna.html, cosmology/index.html)
- The gods knew Vecna was climbing. They sent champions against him on Oerth, the heroes who rose and failed, and when he ascended they felt it reach their realms. Every god who matters to the crew has an opinion of him. Replaces "The Accordance know little of him" and "none knows his plan": the gods know what he did on Oerth, and none knows what he is building now.


### N'ghathrod (dossiers/nghathrod.html, factions/vthorama.html, timeline, artifacts/rod-of-seven-parts.html, rules/)
- About five hundred years ago, Thessalar captured N'ghathrod and experimented on him aboard his planar craft, using a residue piece Thessalar had obtained. The experiments changed N'ghathrod and the five who became his lieutenants: two properly raised illithids captured with him, and three unthinned, made aboard the craft. All six gained a greatly extended lifespan. They are not immortal.
- When the craft lost its gems and fell toward Fonains, N'ghathrod used the fall to escape with his five lieutenants and a few lesser illithids. He never reached Fonains. The craft broke apart as it fell: the main body raised the Barrier Peaks, the Thessalkraken came down in the sea, and other pieces may lie elsewhere.
- In the escape N'ghathrod saved a mouse, which has travelled with him since.
- The journey back to Glyth, a system away, took decades. Glyth rejected them: the unthinned were exiled. N'ghathrod chose to go with them, because they are his blood. This was roughly four to four and a half centuries ago.
- Over the centuries that followed, N'ghathrod searched for a home for his people, learned the full extent of his ability to take, and found that he and his lieutenants were still alive long after they expected to die. Vecna began whispering to him during these centuries, and the long plan formed.
- Taking the Astral Dreadnought and turning it into Vth'oramu took decades, perhaps a century. Generations of the colony were born aboard and many died in the Astral Sea.
- The active push to find and open hearts began only in the past few decades.
- The residue piece Thessalar experimented with was the seventh Rod piece, simulacrum. It is why N'ghathrod's tadpoles make copies of him, and it is the root of the taking.
- N'ghathrod's long life comes from his taken. A portion of every taken person is tithed upward to him, the way a hive feeds its center, and it extends his life. The more he takes, the longer he lasts.
- done: the capture, the experiments, the seventh piece and the escape are on artifacts/rod-of-seven-parts.html and vessels/thessalars-craft.html (section 4). dossiers/nghathrod.html carries them (section 5); the timeline carries the capture, the copies, the escape, the exile, the taking of Vth'oramu and the push for hearts (section 8). rules/ still needs them.
- If N'ghathrod's true body dies, the taken do not die. Part of him lives on in each of them. His lieutenants are closest to him; each rung down the colony is further away. Given enough time, one of them could grow toward becoming another N'ghathrod. The taken lose a part of themselves when he dies.

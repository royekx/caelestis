## Rulings of 2026-10-25 not yet on their pages

### Cosmology and gods (cosmology/, realms/, the divine-rules draft)
- Worship gives gods power, influence and reach.
- Gods can't walk on worlds or reach the center because of polarity: like charges push apart, and the push grows until it is unbearable. The center harms them without destroying them.
- A god worshipped on several worlds is one god under several names. Takhisis is Tiamat; Paladine is Bahamut.

### Vecna (campaign/ascension/, realms/oerth, factions/)
- Vecna's godhood is complete. He ascended on Oerth, his homeworld, a darkest-timeline world where his hold is strongest; its Monolith is a holy site.
- Ascension charged Vecna to Oerth's heart and pushed him off the world, so he works through vessels.
- The Ascension still to come is his climb to Axiom.
- Vecna is an open god with a following under many names. His followers are the Seekers.
- Vecna uses Tharizdun as a contingency. It is use, not alliance.

## Rulings of 2026-10-25, round 2 (backlog category 1)

### Hearts, Atria and Monoliths (cosmology/, glossary/, rules/ § Opening)
- Every world has a heart, and every heart has an Atrium: its own hidden entrance, which manifests differently on each world according to what that world is. Residue carried into an Atrium opens the heart. Opening needs no Monolith.
- A Monolith is what makes a link possible. Two opened hearts can be linked, Atrium to Atrium, only where both worlds have a Monolith. The Monolith licenses the connection between one heart world and another.
- Finding a heart, opening it and linking it are three separate, hard steps. Across the cosmos many worlds have a Monolith, far fewer have a found heart, fewer still an opened one, and only a couple are linked.

### Vecna's working: the linked circle (cosmology/, glossary/, campaign/ascension/, chart/)
- Vecna needs fifteen opened, linked hearts: three in each of the five spheres. In each sphere, one heart links to the sphere on one side, one to the sphere on the other side, and one to the First Heart. Together the links form a spellcasting circle drawn across the cosmos, and Vecna works toward completing it.
- Today almost none of it stands. A handful of hearts may have been found in the past few decades; a couple at most are linked.
- The chart's "claimed" and "primed" node states, and their counts, are retired. The chart shows Monolith worlds, found hearts, opened hearts and links, with very few of the last three.

### Vecna (factions/deities.html, factions/seekers.html, campaign/ascension/, realms/oerth)
- Vecna is a god of secrets, knowledge, manipulation and trickery, with a hold over death and the undead. He thrives on what others do not know.
- He keeps his own name and origin a close secret, because there is power in knowing who he was and where he came from. Most of his worshippers know him by other names. The name "Vecna" surfaces late, through lore: a figure missing his left eye and his left hand.
- On Oerth his worship is large and public. Elsewhere it lives in small, hidden pockets. The same holds for other gods: a god can be known on a world and have almost no following there.
- He ascended at least a few centuries ago. His legacy is hidden temples, secret texts and artifacts kept for his worship, not ruined ziggurats lost to time.
- Not every worshipper is a cultist. Some lean into necromancy. Some sincerely believe in an idea of him, the breaking of the cycle of death, more than in the god himself.
- Vecna wants to understand the cosmos and become part of it: where everything came from, what the Reach was, what was taken. Breaking reality is not his goal; he believes he could repair whatever breaks.
- Vecna and Tharizdun distrust each other. Tharizdun's weakening of reality on Fonains serves Vecna's ends, and Vecna lets it run.
- Lich Vecna, the mortal form the Core Monolith archived when Vecna ascended, sits in the Radiant Citadel. He has had centuries to conclude he was wrong, and he is calm, almost redeemed. Meeting him unsettles everything the crew thinks they know.

### The Seekers (factions/seekers.html)
- The Seekers are Vecna's faith. Seeking is the faith itself; what a chapter seeks is what names it. The Seekers of Secrets are one chapter. Others seek knowledge, a cure for death, or a home.
- Every chapter worships Vecna, often under another name or in the belief that he is something else. Chapters do not know about each other; each serves on its own. Their mutual ignorance is itself a secret Vecna keeps and feeds on.

### The Core Monolith's pressure (cosmology/, factions/order.html, factions/restorers.html)
- The Core Monolith has a degree of sentience. It presses an idea outward through the cosmos, and those who sense it read it differently. The Restorers take it as a call to bring back what was lost. The Order takes it as a charge to maintain things as they are, though some within the Order doubt that reading. Vecna took it as something he could learn, and change. The Restorers and the Seekers are separate.

### The Axiom strain (cosmology/, factions/deities.html, dossiers/astra-virex.html, crew/gregory.html)
- Over the past century Monoliths have been forming more often. Some of the rise is Vecna's doing; some is simply worlds reaching their moments.
- The rise strains an Axiom. Weakening an Axiom so that he can take its place is part of Vecna's plan.
- The strain ripples outward through the planes and the cosmos. It is slow and more metaphysical than physical, and it weakens as it travels, but mortals are starting to feel it. Gregory's awakening is one result; the Latchling and other oddities are others. The strain helps Vecna, Tharizdun and other powers, good and bad.
- The ripple woke Astra Virex. It also caused collisions between celestial bodies, and part of the entity broke off. All it knows is that it must heal and become whole.
- The entity can be called Astra Virex, Astra or Virex.

### The theme (campaign/spine.html, hub.html)
- The campaign has no central question. It has a theme: everyone is missing something and is trying to become whole, to find or make their place in the cosmos.
- The theme takes a different shape at each level. Vecna wants the answer to the cosmos and what was taken from him. N'ghathrod wants a home for his people. Casey wants his brother. Tumak wants his home. Bartholomew wants the peace within him, his constellation. Boogie wants to belong to something. Astra Virex wants to heal. Gregory and Sol carry it too.
- "Who decides what's real?" is retired, and the hub stops quoting it.

### Campaign shape (campaign/spine.html)
- N'ghathrod is the main villain of the campaign, and stopping his invasion takes most of it. Vecna stands behind him and beyond the campaign's direct reach; the late campaign is about stopping Vecna's working, not necessarily fighting him. A confrontation could be with a part of him manifested in the First Heart.
- Vecna has contingencies. N'ghathrod is a means to an end: Vecna needs him to open and link hearts so that Vecna's vessels can pass through Atria. Getting N'ghathrod's five lieutenants through would make it certain; many lesser taken, grown over time through the colony's spread, could still get him there. Defeating N'ghathrod does not end the threat, though it is never undone by fiat. Other avenues, such as the Restorers' aims, can bring Vecna close again or only delay him.
- The failure state is Vecna succeeding.
- The Radiant Citadel and the Core Monolith are late Arc 3 or Arc 4 destinations. The crew reaches them by a long voyage or through a linked heart, both late-game.
- Level pacing (DM): level-ups slow down in later arcs, so the crew is not at the top of the level range before the final arc.

### N'ghathrod's history (dossiers/nghathrod.html, factions/vthorama.html, timeline)
- About five hundred years ago, Thessalar captured N'ghathrod and experimented on him aboard his planar craft, using a residue piece Thessalar had obtained. The experiments changed N'ghathrod and the five who became his lieutenants, who were unthinned: illithids who should have been culled and were not, or who were produced by him. All six gained a greatly extended lifespan. They are not immortal.
- When the craft lost its gems and fell toward Fonains, N'ghathrod used the chaos to escape with his five lieutenants and a few lesser illithids. He never reached Fonains. The craft broke apart as it fell: the main body raised the Barrier Peaks, the Thessalkraken came down in the sea, and other pieces may lie elsewhere.
- In the escape N'ghathrod saved a mouse, which has travelled with him since.
- The journey back to Glyth, a system away, took decades. Glyth rejected them: the unthinned were exiled. N'ghathrod chose to go with them, because they are his blood. This was roughly four to four and a half centuries ago.
- Over the centuries that followed, N'ghathrod searched for a home for his people, learned the full extent of his ability to take, and found that he and his lieutenants were still alive long after they expected to die. Vecna began whispering to him during these centuries, and the long plan formed.
- Taking the Astral Dreadnought and turning it into Vth'oramu took decades, perhaps a century. Generations of the colony were born aboard and many died in the Astral Sea.
- The active push to find and open hearts began only in the past few decades.
- Vth'oramu means "the passage to a new home."

### The taken after N'ghathrod (rules/, factions/vthorama.html, dossiers/nghathrod.html)
- If N'ghathrod's true body dies, the taken do not die. Part of him lives on in each of them. His lieutenants are closest to him; each rung down the colony is further away. Given enough time, one of them could grow toward becoming another N'ghathrod. The taken lose a part of themselves when he dies.

### The elder brain (realms/fonains.html, timeline, dossiers/nghathrod.html)
- N'ghathrod's elder brain was separated from him in the crash. He believed it dead, or could not reach it. It lay trapped beneath the Barrier Peaks for about five hundred years, possibly growing a colony of its own, until Lola's Boys broke it in Session 27. It was buried in the collapse, but it teleported out and escaped.

## Rulings of 2026-10-25, round 4

### The Circle of Fifteenths (campaign/ascension/, glossary/, cosmology/)
- Vecna calls his working the Circle of Fifteenths, after the circle of fifths in music. He has a taste for music, and it shows in how he names things.
- The name is his alone. No one else knows what he is building. The Seekers do not know, and neither does the Order. Some gods suspect he is up to something. A few people on the fringes sense that something is wrong in the cosmos, but they do not know what it is or what to call it, and no one believes them.
- Pages that describe the working from outside call it Vecna's working; the Circle of Fifteenths is his name for it.

### Vecna's domains and symbol (factions/deities.html)
- Vecna's domains are Knowledge, Death and Trickery.
- His symbol is an eye in the palm of a left hand. Chapters read it their own way. Some take the eye for sight and revelation. Some glorify it in green, for light, nourishment and the life of the body. The same symbol means different things across his faith.

### Chapters across the cosmos (factions/seekers.html, campaign/)
- Each chapter's name is in its own people's language, and every one of them carries the word for "seekers". A crew that translates enough chapter names, by magic or by study, will notice the pattern.
- Chapters seek many things: hearts, lost knowledge, a cure for death, a home. Vecna needs them hungry for something, and their seeking ripples outward through the worlds they work on.
- The Watchers flag some chapters' work as a danger to a world's Zenith and track it, without knowing who stands behind it.

### Clint Dashforest and the Bellwether Crow (dossiers/clint.html, factions/vocath-operation.html, campaign/colony/, chart/, timeline)
- Vocath's crew ambushed the Bellwether Crow for a sister artifact of A.T.L.A.S. that the Crow was carrying. Clint took it and left the ship to draw the boarders off.
- What became of the artifact is not known. It may have been lost in the crossing, or Clint may still have it on Fonains.
- He steered for a color pool he could see opening, and went through it on purpose. Color pools open and close quickly, and this one carried him through a small pocket of another dimension and spat him out on Fonains, separated from the boarders who followed him in.
- The Crow's survivors still argue about whether he meant to go through. He did.
- In the Fleet, "went overboard" means more than falling from a ship. A crew member who leaves the ship to fight for it and does not come back has gone overboard. It is a softer way of saying killed in action. Tarto and Saerthe both feel they could have done something.
- This replaces the psychic wind as the way Clint reached Fonains.

### Tarroway (dossiers/tarroway.html, restricted/)
- M. Tarroway is a cartographer on Strake Station. His readings were the first hint that an unknown vessel or structure was disturbing the satellites at the edge of the system.
- He is a thread, not a figure: mentioned here and there so the players can put pieces together. He appears mainly in the Fleet Intelligence Archive. Nothing about him is public yet.

### The Vth'orama (factions/vthorama.html)
- The colony's chapter name, Vth'orama, translates as the Seekers of a New Home. The etymology above stands.

### Building contracts (DM: a planning page)
- Contracts fall into three or four categories by how they tie to the larger world: loosely tied, tied to a Seekers chapter, personal to a crew member, and others as they come up. Each contract can be built out from its category.
- The world is already in motion. A contract surfaces because of the state of the world, and the place around it should feel like it exists without the crew. The crew's choices, or their failure to act, change what was already happening.
- Contracts take any shape: excavations, politics, hunts. Many quietly lead back to a Seekers chapter, some of them knowingly nefarious and some unwitting.

## Rulings of 2026-10-25, round 5 (backlog category 2)

### The gods (cosmology/, factions/deities.html, working/divine-rules.md)
- Most gods came into being in the Reach event and grew into godhood over time, some sooner than others, so gods have different ages. Later gods ascended from mortality. Their standing as greater or lesser gods comes from how much worship they hold, the influence they gain from it, and the niche they find, through the same loop of attention and belief.
- Gods are not physical. They are woven into the cosmos and dwell between the spheres, in a space of their own. A god drifts toward the sphere where its worship is greatest. Worship reaches a god from any sphere, but the farther away it comes from, the weaker its signal.
- Gods are of the same make as worlds and their hearts, so polarity pushes them apart. The more powerful a god, the larger it is in that metaphysical sense, and the harder the push. This is why the greatest gods are kept farthest from worlds and from the center.
- Once, a god forced its way down onto a world. The friction between god and world was cataclysmic: it destroyed the god, and what remained became the Knowhere. Every god since has taken it as a warning.

### Asmodeus and the Accordance (factions/deities.html, Fonains doc)
- Asmodeus and Lord Primus are the eldest gods of the Accordance. Asmodeus is its face, though not its ruler or its leader.
- Asmodeus was among the first to understand what the gods had become. He began as a good-natured god. The Reach let loose good and evil alike, demons among them, and he took up the fight against them while the other gods sat in their comfort. He was betrayed, and he became the lord of the Nine Hells. He is called evil because someone has to do this work, and he does it.
- The Accordance know little of Vecna. A few centuries ago they felt something change: a new entity like them, where there had been none. Asmodeus likely knows something is wrong, and Lord Primus may.

### Vecna (factions/deities.html, realms/oerth, campaign/ascension/)
- Vecna's realm is Cavitius.
- Oerth is the world where Vecna became a god. His ritual to become a god pushed Oerth to its Zenith, and the Zenith made him one; the Monolith that answered archived his mortal form, Lich Vecna. That was his first ascension, the Godhood Ascension. The climb he is making now, to take an Axiom's place, is the Axiomic Ascension. The separate "Ascension World" is retired; Oerth is that world.
- His grievance: something was taken from him and hidden from him. He believes the cosmos and every being in it are owed an answer, and he means to find it.
- How the other gods respond if he comes close is left to reveal itself. The campaign is not a war of the gods.

### Tharizdun (working/tharizdun.md, realms/fonains.html, dossiers/thessalar.html)
- Fonains's Monolith is the only one ever to carry Tharizdun's crystal, and the only one recorded as destroyed.
- Tharizdun wanted nothing from Thessalar but chaos. His influence reached Thessalar through the craft's gems, born where Olhydra and Ogrémoch collided, and it drove him slowly mad. One of Tharizdun's temples lies within the planar craft's depths.

### The first Seekers chapter the crew meets (campaign/ascension/, factions/seekers.html)
- It is a benevolent church that shows Vecna's hand and eye openly. It is good and docile, with something a little odd about it: not evil, but easy to misread. The crew first hears of it through Hessa Dun, a Caelestis contract contact whose uncle tells strange stories. The meeting itself comes well after graduation.
- DM: the players will recognise the symbol and expect villainy; the characters know nothing of it. The church's goodness should sit wrong with the players without giving them anything to act on.

### The Restorers (factions/restorers.html)
- The Restorers are trying to reach a heart, and through it the Core Monolith, where they believe everything lost is stored. They compete for residue pieces. Without knowing it, they advance Vecna's plan.
- They follow the Core Monolith's pressure as Vecna does, read through a different mind. Their understanding is theory and vision, little of it concrete. They and the Seekers have clashed over the years, and some on each side believe they walk the same path.

### Vecna on H'Catha (realms/hcatha.html)
- Vecna's hand on H'Catha goes back only to its founding. A residue of worship lingers there. He may once have tried to turn the dreaming of the Sovereign Eye into a Zenith and fallen short. One of the eldest rulers may know something of it. H'Catha is the crew's first world beyond Caelestis, and Vecna stays faint there.

### Vth'oramu and the colony (dossiers/vthoramu.html, factions/vthorama.html)
- Vth'oramu has moved since it sat on the Cairn bearing a year ago. It can enter a system, but a vessel that size near a world's planets would almost certainly be seen, so it keeps to the dark between systems. Where it lies now follows from what N'ghathrod is doing. The unidentified mass Strake Station reported was Vth'oramu.
- The dreadnought bore no wound before the siege. N'ghathrod saw an opportunity and took it. He knew a dreadnought's physiology, and a whisper from Vecna told him it was the best way to keep a colony on its passage home. The fight lasted a long time, and controlling the creature took a great many tadpoles.
- The colony's arms should echo a hand and an eye without showing them outright.
- The tithe runs one way. Every taken person comes from N'ghathrod's tadpoles. As each grows, a portion of it passes up through the rungs of the taken, and all of it ends with N'ghathrod. The flow is paracausal, not physical. "The downward tithe" is retired.
- Qitru serves Vocath. She does not care who stands above him; for her, the top is Vocath.

### Thessalar (dossiers/thessalar.html)
- Thessalar is Halaster Blackcloak, and he is still in the planar craft. His records of the work on N'ghathrod are there too.

### The Order (factions/order.html)
- The Watchers keep watching. Over the past century they have seen Monoliths rise more often, and it worries them; some want to act. They are fighting oddities across many worlds and trying to keep a lid on them. Caelestis is thin partly because it answers the Watchers' calls for help.
- The Hall of Sight and Sound is the great hall within the Order's seat.
- H'Catha's Watcher is Nereth Vaul. Her species sets the final form of the name.

### Aethris (factions/aethris.html, realms/aethris.html, dossiers/beshaba.html, factions/deities.html)
- The Fortuned want Sol back alive because he is a component of their unfinished ritual. They do not care about him as a person.
- Beshaba finds the Fortuned heretical, a spit in her face. She thinks Tymora is fuelling them, and blames her sister for it. On Aethris Beshaba is the lesser of the two, because people favour luck over misfortune, and she calls what the Fortuned do unnatural.
- Neither sister knows what both of them need: each other. Luck and misfortune are two sides of one coin.

## Rulings of 2026-10-25, round 6

### Tharizdun and the planar craft (realms/fonains.html, dossiers/thessalar.html, working/tharizdun.md, Fonains doc)
- One of Tharizdun's temples was aboard Thessalar's planar craft. When the craft broke apart in its fall, the piece holding the temple came down somewhere other than the Barrier Peaks.
- The crash, about five hundred years ago, is how the Elder Elemental Eye reached Fonains. Tharizdun's influence spread across the world from the fallen temple. It is why the Eye watched Lola's Boys in the year 425, and why the craft's gems carried his touch.
- Shothragot, his avatar, was bound to that temple. Its true vestige may still be there, and Giorge may be trying to reach it.
- Lola's Boys, by taking the gems in the year 425, brought down the craft. In doing so they freed N'ghathrod and landed Tharizdun's foothold on their own world.
- What the planar craft was, a vast floating city of many tiers, and where it came from, is unknown.

### Vth'oramu (dossiers/vthoramu.html)
- The dreadnought's tether is an astral cord, a silver line running off to somewhere unknown. Its physical tail is immensely long, which is part of why Vth'oramu cannot enter a system.
- Its mouth draws things in, the way an astral dreadnought's mouth opens on its inner demiplane. Its great weapon is the reverse: a pulse pressed outward from every side of its body, and everything within range is teleported away. It takes a long time to charge and is rarely used.

### The Seekers (factions/seekers.html, factions/spectrum.html)
- The Fracture Cult and the Archivists are Seekers chapters. The Fracture Cult seeks to bring worlds to their Zenith. The Archivists seek what the Monoliths record.

### Prep (DM principles)
- A world that is named has core facts, even if nothing else is built: what it is like, who lives there. Anything named in play needs work from that point.
- Prep where the crew is going and one ring out from it: H'Catha and the cantina now. Seekers chapters and other far details get built when they become active in the larger shape, not before.

### The Princes on Fonains (timeline, realms/fonains.html, Fonains doc, the Cre'Tez line)
- Long ago, about twelve thousand years before now, the Princes of Elemental Evil rose on Fonains. The six gods of the Accordance stepped in, helped forge the four weapons, and sealed the Princes away with the weapons as their keys.

- About three and a half centuries ago a sect of the fire genasi tried to summon Imix. Asmodeus gave the few who resisted Tinderstrike to seal him again, and bound the key to their blood; the price was that Imix be forgotten. Over the generations since, the line has turned tiefling.
- About five hundred years ago Thessalar's planar craft crashed on Fonains, and Tharizdun's influence arrived with the temple aboard it. It spread slowly through the world and into new cults. Those cults raised the four prophets, and in recent years everything has begun to move.

### Residue and the fragments (cosmology/, artifacts/)

## Rulings of 2026-10-25, round 9

### Names across the cosmos (README naming rule; cosmology/, realms/, chart/)
- Celestial bodies have names or designations. Caelestis gives designations (CON-312); the people who live near a body may have their own name for it, sometimes a local epithet ("the falling star of Gethros"). A body known across a system or a sphere may also carry a wider epithet, the way a moon has a name and is also called the Blood Moon. Most things have only a name or a designation.
- The same place can be called different things in different parts of the cosmos.

### Erevos and Aurath (cosmology/, glossary/, chart/, realms/)
- The center of the cosmos is Erevos: the remains of the Reach world, a black hole beneath the Core Monolith and the Radiant Citadel.
- Those who dwell in the Core Monolith call it Aurath.

## Rulings of 2026-10-25, round 10

### The Boundary fragment and A.T.L.A.S.'s sister (artifacts/atlas.html, artifacts/index.html, dossiers/clint.html)
- The Boundary fragment is in five pieces. Every Boundary piece, in whatever housing, finds, reveals or draws toward something.

On the surface it finds magic items, a lesser kind of item-finder, because every enchanted thing carries a trace of residue. Its true use is finding Reach artifacts. This is what Vocath's crew ambushed the Bellwether Crow to take. Clint carried it through the color pool; it is somewhere on Fonains.

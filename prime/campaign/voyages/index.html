Voyage 005 — the descent Complete Crew log pending
Briefing — Planned

Descend from Cargo toward the Command Deck. The planned shape was the behir as a stalking dread — a wounded construct predator that assesses rather than attacks, teaching the crew it outclasses them — and an abduction: the behir takes two PCs down a Meditation shaft and strands them at the gravity plane level with the Command Deck floor, forced recon that hands over the problem and none of the solution.

The ruling that makes the dungeon work: the Command Deck is an anti-magic field that unmakes constructs. The crew can cross it; the behir cannot. Both need it cleared, for different destinations.

Briefing not written
Voyage — Actual

They skipped the rest and engineered the descent themselves. Boogie fitted OSTEKK-6 with pseudopod legs — Mini-Boogie, 25-foot speed — rigged feather-fall bounce suits, and left the helm on Cargo with Sol, Ryeback, Pffred and Krik'Lit.

On the Hollow Deck, Gregory found the remains of OSTEKK-4 and OSTEKK-8 and jolted OSTEKK-2 awake for eighteen seconds, long enough for it to name the behir and say it was sent by "the original Ostekk." Boogie's shield clanked, the behir woke, and the fight came. Bartholomew went down to a bite and was brought back with Tumak's potion; the behir screeched and three clockwork horrors answered. Boogie called the retreat.

The abduction never happened, and the dice did its job instead. Boogie missed the rope on an Acrobatics 4, fell, and found the gravity plane. The others followed down to help him, flipped through, and came out on the Command Deck — reaching it a session early, without being carried there.

Magic failed. Cure Wounds and Dancing Lights both died, a Nature check surfaced the plant grown out of the beholder's body, and the crew concluded on their own that the central eye cancels magic. Hollowed wisps latched onto Bartholomew and Casey, who learned to slap them off. Tumak called for hands; Miken came down and saw the dead beholder. Everyone climbed back to Cargo.

Delta — what to bring forward, what to scrap

Where they stand. Cargo Deck, holding the helm and the crew, resources spent. They know the helm goes on the Command Deck, that magic fails there, and that the wisps latch and drain. The rope rig runs Cargo to Hollow and on to the plane. OSTEKK-6 is unrepaired and riding Mini-Boogie; OSTEKK-2's state after the fight is unresolved. The behir is alive, roughly seventy damage down, and it does not repair.

Scrap the abduction. It was designed as forced recon, and a missed Acrobatics check delivered the same outcome better — the crew chose the descent and owns it. Reusing the beat now would take back something they earned.

What worked, and is worth repeating. Stating the goal once and then supplying only obstacles; the crew engineered the entire traversal. The gravity plane played well at the table. Describing the Command Deck before revealing the map. Letting them discover the anti-magic through failed spells rather than a check.

What struggled, with the fix. The Hollow Deck map went down before its description and the players did not read the behir as a creature until initiative. Fix: a three-line threshold card per room — first sense, dominant feature, one thing that moves — read before any token drops. Borrowed map props drew questions (“ignore the dead bodies”); strip them or pre-declare them as set dressing. Vertical positioning forced mid-turn recalculation, so note height on the token.

Voyage 004 — All Things Find Their Way Home Complete
Briefing — Planned

Pick up on the Cargo Deck cliffhanger: clockwork horrors mid-disassembly of a broken autognome. Then the exploration body of the redesigned dungeon — the bloom as a spreading lock that chokes shafts and forces clear-or-reroute choices, with a gradient teaching the crew the source is above them.

The autognome was planned as a clarifying oracle: repair him and the mystery unspools in fragments.

Briefing on site (reused)
Voyage — Actual

The horrors went down without serious injury. The cargo manifest gave the autognome its name — OSTEKK-6 — and the crew learned its damage was mechanical rather than arcane, past anything they could mend aboard. Tumak manifested a telepathic link for the first time without understanding how, connecting himself, Gregory and OSTEKK-6, and through it the autognome warned of a shape-taking specimen aboard and a bloom spreading through the ship.

Given a floating dolly for the helm, the crew routed into the access shafts and found the garden shaft choked with lantern-wisp flowers. They killed the flower in a hard fight and learned the wisps persist anyway — the flower produces them without being the only thing holding them there. Bartholomew took ten from one shock.

Tumak ended it with his first soul knife, two runes flaring as the blade left a small portal, and felt a tether to home for the first time — a connection rather than a location. As the throw looked set to miss, the Runekeeper's voice said all things find their way home, and the blade curved true.

Bartholomew also shifted into a starry form bearing two stars and recognized it as the shape he saw during a childhood fall. Bloodied by two fights with no rest between, the crew chose to hold and recover before descending.

Delta — what to bring forward, what to scrap

The oracle did not land as designed. OSTEKK-6 was meant to unspool the mystery once repaired, and the repair is impossible with what the crew carries. What it produced instead is better: a mute, helpless NPC they chose to save and now haul around, and a first-ever telepathic link improvised to talk to it. Keep the autognome unrepaired — the dependency is doing more work than the exposition would.

Two PC firsts in one session, both unprompted. Tumak's telepathy and his soul knife, and Bartholomew's two-star constellation recognized from a childhood fall. The constellation is CON-312 and the fall is now the earliest event attached to it, which makes it the strongest thread he has. Casey had glimpsed that same form earlier, so two PCs have independently seen it.

The Runekeeper spoke directly. First time, at a dice-driven moment, and it gave the session its title. The tether being connection rather than location is the line to hold — it keeps Lunarfoot unfindable while making home feel close.

Carry forward. The wisps persist with an unexplained cause, which is the thread leading up to the corpse-and-flower symbiosis. The mimic warning is unspent. The crew's decision to rest rather than push is the first time they declined to press since boarding.

Logistics note. The session recording was lost, so this account came from dictation and the existing briefing was reused rather than written fresh.

Delta — what to bring forward, what to scrap

What this session taught about the table. They work an investigation thoroughly and expansively, and they expand scenes you planned as short — the Rindle detour and the Sky Dock rail in V002, the desk snoop here. Budget investigation acts at roughly double and put the compressible scene last on purpose rather than by accident.

Reframed, not scrapped. Sol's recognition moves out of a formal institutional commendation and surfaces through Ryeback instead, which is warmer and does more work. Miken was rebuilt as a leveraged character wrestling with guilt rather than a concealed antagonist, so every friendly beat he gets is honest rather than a feint.

Owed. Sol's recognition in some form, the sailor-access inventory, and Ryeback warmth banked before the galley. Gregory's Latchling beat was deliberately held so the Wizpop repair could carry his arc instead.

Naming, settled. Vocath was named to the players directly. The preference is clarity over withheld mystery, and the real mystery layers stay intact regardless — the chart theft, Cairn, and the two-layer saboteur structure are all untouched by the players knowing a name.

The miss worth remembering. Gregory's beat was crowded out — Casey occupied the Sea Dock scene and the autognome moment never arrived. Two PCs with business in the same room means one of them loses, so place arc beats in separate scenes rather than separate moments within one.

Everything seeded here is still live. Four PCs left with a personal breadcrumb, and three of those threads remain open: Casey's first directional clue toward Viren, Boogie's half-ship, and the wardrobe. Tumak's theft of Mirt's bag went unexamined and is available whenever it is useful.

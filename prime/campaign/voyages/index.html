The trim does not actually trim

Two leave and two join, so the count is unchanged, and H'Catha still sails with five NPCs — Ryeback, a teacher, Ezra, Miken and Ostekk-6. What the swap buys is composition rather than size: two contractors with no stake become two people with their own business on the world.

If the cast genuinely needs to be smaller, Miken is the only one who could leave with Foundling, and Qitru's thread argues hard against it.

How a table of ten gets run

Six PCs plus Ryeback, Miken, Ezra and a teacher is ten bodies. The method that has been working holds: NPCs hang back and engage only when a player engages them, or when one of them has to act as the NPC they are.

At H'Catha that becomes a disposition rather than a discipline. Ryeback and Miken stay with the ship — somebody has to be able to move it, and that is a real reason rather than an excuse — which puts Ezra and the chosen teacher ashore and nobody else. Then the crew meets the Watcher and Ondessa, and the count climbs again. If the crew picks Saerthe, he can hold the helm instead, which changes who goes ashore (Campaign).

The reason to carry the weight: the margin is what lets the crew meet genuinely dangerous things at low level without the punches being visibly pulled. The alternative is encounters scaled down to what six level-4 cadets can safely absorb, and rats in a space odyssey is the worst outcome available. It levels out when the party is on its own.

Settled — decided, and not being revisited

The sabotage is dialed back to a beacon. A galley fire with a 4th-level glyph and four magma mephits is too large an event for a hull this small. Miken plants a beacon so Qitru can follow them, and that is the whole of it — Ryeback is off the hook, unhurt and aboard. Everything the fire was carrying is carried better by the smaller version: the crew still calls home, because reporting a commandeered ship is what you do with one rather than something an emergency has to justify, and Ezra still rendezvouses as a planned swap rather than a casualty response.

Miken stays aboard and unconfessed, which keeps him a friend with a secret instead of a confessed asset under guard. That is what makes Qitru's thread work at H'Catha: she is following a ship she can find, carrying a person she wants, and nobody aboard knows either fact.

They are choosing how the arc runs and they will not know that, which is the right amount of weight for a choice this early.

Miken reports in this session, and he tells them about the beholder. That is the channel — an asset doing the one thing he was leveraged into doing, while the crew treats him as a friend. Qitru carries it to H'Catha ahead of them: the beacon tells her where they are, Miken tells her what they are.

Delta — what to bring forward, what to scrap

Where they stand. Cargo Deck, holding the helm and the crew, resources spent. They know the helm goes on the Command Deck, that magic fails there, and that the wisps latch and drain. The rope rig runs Cargo to Hollow and on to the plane. Ostekk-6 is unrepaired and riding Mini-Boogie; Ostekk-2's state after the fight was not settled at the table. The behir is alive, roughly seventy damage down, and it does not repair.

Scrap the abduction. It was designed as forced recon, and a missed Acrobatics check delivered the same outcome better — the crew chose the descent and owns it. Reusing the beat now would take back something they earned.

What worked, and is worth repeating. Stating the goal once and then supplying only obstacles; the crew engineered the entire traversal. The gravity plane played well at the table. Describing the Command Deck before revealing the map. Letting them discover the anti-magic through failed spells rather than a check.

What struggled, with the fix. The Hollow Deck map went down before its description and the players did not read the behir as a creature until initiative. Fix: a three-line threshold card per room — first sense, dominant feature, one thing that moves — read before any token drops. Borrowed map props drew questions (“ignore the dead bodies”); strip them or pre-declare them as set dressing. Vertical positioning forced mid-turn recalculation, so note height on the token.

Delta — what to bring forward, what to scrap

The miss worth remembering. Gregory's beat was crowded out — Casey occupied the Sea Dock scene and the autognome moment never arrived. Two PCs with business in the same room means one of them loses, so place arc beats in separate scenes rather than separate moments within one.

Carried forward and paid off. The Miken-adrift cliffhanger set the pattern of ending on a question rather than a resolution, which the table responds to. Sol heard Beshaba for the first time, so small misfortune around him reads as attended to rather than random.

What the session was actually for. Seating the sixth cadet without the crew having to decide to include him, and making Caelestis feel lived-in. Both landed, and the Ryeback and Miken warmth banked here is the capital the galley spends.

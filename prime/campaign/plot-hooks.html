Play·Plot Hooks

Plot Hooks

Threads in the world the DM can pull, each grounded in a finished page. Each row shows its kinds and its status; open it for what it is, what happens if it is pulled, and where it leads. Below the rows, Speculation holds ideas the cosmos has room for that are not yet tied to a place, a person or a situation. Where each thread stands with the crew is in the record (data/quests.json) and on the Voyages board. The hook kinds are defined on Principles.

Active

Threads in motion or close to it.

H'Catha meteor
ObjectEvent
Forthcoming · V007
Adamantine that struck the Pupil on H'Catha, on a seam between two domains.
What it is
An adamantine meteor that struck the Pupil, the innermost band beside the Spindle, on the seam between Kurrzot's domain and his rival's. It is mined as a vein in contested ground. Caelestis wants it for containment; on H'Catha it matters because the Sovereign Eye cannot see through it. The crew goes in the tyrant ship, which reads as native traffic because every beholder ship is carved stone, and a scuttle ship carries the landing party down unnoticed.
If pulled
The ore hums on the same frequency as the resonance-cores, Gregory's core and Boogie's shard, and the Fleet archive logs it as threshold-resonant.
Where it leads
The first piece of the cosmos to pass through the crew's hands. Handed over at graduation, it is reclassified above their clearance, which tells them in hindsight that Arc 1 was never mundane. The mission is on Campaign.
Ostekk
CharacterPlace
Forthcoming · H'Catha
A ruling beholder of H'Catha whose stolen Lantern and OSTEKK units the crew now carries.
What it is
Ostekk rules a domain on H'Catha and builds. He made the Containment Engine and the nine OSTEKK units, and both were stolen from him. The crew carries the Lantern, travels with Ostekk-6, and ejected Ostekk-10 after lying to it.
If pulled
The crew hears his name soon after landing. His automatons search Ondessa's sea-caves, and he has reason to want his engine back.
Where it leads
H'Catha stops being a rock recovery and becomes his doorstep, which keeps the tyrant ship paying off after it is taken.

Object
Active · Gregory
Ostekk's lamp around the Rod's fourth piece. It pulls toward the fifth.
What it is
The Lantern of Flourishing holds the fourth piece of the Rod of Seven Parts, keyed to regenerate. The piece pulls toward the other six.

It can pull the crew toward the fifth piece, which Xanathar holds on Toril, and its pull toward Toril can start Gregory's journey home. It becomes Gregory's next anomaly if he investigates it.
Where it leads
The Rod, and through it Thessalar and the seventh piece that made N'ghathrod what he is.

Boogie and the Fonains shard
CharacterObject
Active · Boogie
A piece of the only Monolith recorded as destroyed, inside Boogie.
What it is
The Fonains shard, from the corrupted Monolith Lola's Boys broke, sits in Boogie's chest beside half of Saerthe's miniature ship and half of the derelict's logbook. Tharizdun wants nothing specific from him; what comes through the shard depends on what he does.
If pulled
The shard draws people to Boogie as something holy: arrival, and a promise of more. It is a genuine following built on a corrupted signal, and followers may read Tharizdun's "bring everything here" as ascending to something better. It is there to pull and never pushed.

Speculation

Questions the cosmos has room for and has not answered: speculation, late levers, and what a faction could do. When one is grounded in a place, a person or a situation, it becomes a hook above. What is settled lives on Cosmology, the Opposition pages and Factions; this section points at them and does not re-ask.

Hearts and the lattice

Hearts, Atria, opening and linking are settled. What remains is what the lattice does once it exists, and who else can reach it.

  • Toril as a doorway. Toril has a heart like any world, and its Atrium lies somewhere under the most charted world in Realmspace. If it is ever opened, the most familiar world in the system becomes an access point to whatever it is linked to.
  • The crew opens a world. They would hold the lattice's own tool and the colony's own method, pointed wherever they chose. The link is permanent, works both ways, and the two worlds bleed into each other. The first time the crew does this to a world, they become a thing that does this to worlds.
  • Living with a link. A linked pair far enough along that people have families on the far side, and crops that grow only because of what flows across. A link cannot be undone, so the question is what the crew does for the people on both worlds as the bleeding goes on.
  • A sealed heart. A world's heart folded deeper rather than opened, hidden from other hearts as well as from searchers. The Runekeepers do something like this for their own world. Whether the practice exists anywhere else, and whether a sealed world is invisible to A.T.L.A.S., is unwritten.

The Reach and its descendants
  • Memory of the attempt. The Reach world's descendants are the oldest lineage in the Radiant Citadel. If they carry cultural memory of how the Reach was attempted, Vecna and the Restorers both have a reason to want them.
  • Other residue as future arcs. Twenty-eight pieces exist across the five fragments. A Consolidation piece, an Expansion piece, or another Distinction housing that answers A.T.L.A.S.'s pull: each is an arc waiting to be needed. The scattered pieces are tracked on Artifacts.
  • A second product of the Rod. The seventh piece made N'ghathrod's reproduction into duplication, and Thessalar kept simulacra of himself in the same building. Another Rod piece doing to someone else what the seventh did to N'ghathrod, and two products of one artifact in one room, is a scene that has not been written.

Other readers of A.T.L.A.S.

The device's reading depths are a property of the artifact. The named readers are this campaign's; others could stand at those depths.

  • A Watcher. A Watcher's understanding of the cosmos sits between the crew's and Vecna's. They might see where a world can be entered without seeing the structure behind it, extract more than the crew and less than the antagonist, and have to decide what to do about it.
  • A Restorer. Their doctrine points toward the source where the All-Father holds what is taken. A Fifth Reading might read at a deep level and see more than the order expects. The order would write up what it read and distribute the segment, which puts the reading in a ledger, and ledgers can be reached.
  • A god. Gods see less than they think (Cosmology). A god might read at the surface only and be surprised by the limit. Beshaba reading Sol's world through A.T.L.A.S. is a possible late moment.

What the crew does with A.T.L.A.S.
  • Destroy it. If they hold it and decide it is too dangerous, they might break the housing and bury the piece. The piece survives, because residue is paracausal, so they would be hiding it, and some future finder learns what they buried.
  • Give it away. To the Watchers, to a Restorer they do not know is one, or to a faction they trust. Most third parties are not equipped to hold it. The Discerners would lock it away again and restart the cycle. A Restorer cell would measure it, write it up and distribute the segment, the most responsible handling available and also the widest publication. There may be no safe custodian.
  • Take it to Tumak's homeworld. The Runekeepers' Dissolution piece and A.T.L.A.S.'s Distinction piece are both residue. Pieces of different fragments do not pull toward each other, but putting them in one room might still do something. A card to play very carefully.
  • Find an Atrium and leave it closed. Knowing where a world's heart can be entered, and choosing not to, is a position nobody else in the cosmos holds. It also makes the crew the only people who could tell the colony where to dig.

The colony and the crew
  • The Vth'orama notice Tumak. A Wayfinder reaches worlds on a personal mark alone, where everyone else needs a link and an Atrium: the one capability N'ghathrod cannot build. The healthy version files him as an anomaly to investigate only if circumstances warrant. The unhealthy version makes him a quest object, which turns a PC into a plot device; play it only if the table earns it.
  • They learn about the Latchling. A paracausal anomaly with sentience, and a sign that Gregory's resonance produces usable phenomena. They might target the Sea Dock, try to recruit Rindle, or try to take the Latchling itself.
  • A taken prisoner kept alive. An n'ghathu cannot be read and does not consider itself a victim, and what it will say is limited by what its tadpole was taught. The enna carry far more, and they are kept escorted for that reason.
  • Someone they knew, taken. Same face, same memories, same affection for them, and an allegiance that runs elsewhere. The person is still there, which is the part that does not resolve.
  • A Seekers chapter helps them. A chapter that trades a real answer for a real question would be the most useful contact the crew has, and it would be a chapter, with a founding story and a voice in its history.

The Restorers

The order, its ladder of readings, its crossings and what restoration would actually take are settled. What remains is how far the approach has got, and what the crew's position becomes once they understand it.

  • A Fifth Reading has solved the approach. Four people hold that work. If one is further along than the order knows, a crossing could be attempted on a schedule nobody outside the order can see: a late clock that was running the whole time.
  • The crew carries a reading the order needs. They will have stood on worlds nobody surveyed and seen a loss in progress. Trading what they know buys them the Ledger, and it advances the approach by exactly as much.
  • A cell works out what restoration would cost. A Third Reading who assembles the whole requirement, a Monolith, a heart and all five fragments, carries news that makes four centuries of crossings look like a shortcut. Whether the order would hear her, or file her as an apostate, is the question that makes a faction real.
  • The Restorers find the colony first. A standing loss outranks everything in the Ledger, and the Vth'orama are the largest one in the cosmos. Scholars with no fleet, moving on it anyway, would need the crew badly.
  • A Restorer gets word back out. Nobody ever has. One returning crossing would prove the practice works, turn four centuries of filed failures into an inventory of the living, and point the order at a rescue it has never imagined.

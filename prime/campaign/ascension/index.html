The Ascension

What Vecna is doing, why, and what it requires. He ascended to lich. He ascended to god. This is the third. Each one was a change of category rather than a gain in degree, and it is the only move he has ever made. This page is the plan and nothing else — the people carrying it out have their own pages, and the terms are defined in the Glossary.

What lives where The cosmology this runs on is Cosmology. The Seekers and the colony are described here only by what they contribute; who they are lives in Factions. Campaign shape is the Spine.

What he wants Locked

He is a god of secrets, and he has run out of secrets worth having. Every mystery available to a god is a mystery about something inside the cosmos. The one question underneath all of them — how this was made, and why it is the way it is — sits outside every method he has. It is held by nobody, written nowhere, and known to no god old enough to compel, because the answer predates gods entirely.

The four Axioms are the universe. The Continuum is what they produce between them and there is nothing underneath it to find. So the answer cannot be learned at all, in the way he has learned everything else. It can only be become.

The pattern is the person A man became a lich to stop ending. A lich became a god to stop being small. A god is becoming an Axiom to stop being inside something he did not design. Each step was a change of category rather than a gain in degree, and each one he reached by making himself into the thing rather than acquiring it. This is the only move he has ever made.

And knowing is not the end of it. To sit in an Axiom's position is to hold one of the four principles everything passes through. A mind that understands why the cosmos is as it is, from inside the structure that makes it so, is a mind positioned to make it otherwise. He has not said this to anyone and there is nobody he would say it to.

To settle What he wants changed. Something in his history gave a man who already had everything a grievance against the arrangement itself — a loss the rules permitted, a bargain the structure honoured, a death that was simply allowed. It should be small, specific and old, and it should be the kind of thing the party can learn from the half of him held in the Citadel, who remembers the grievance and has stopped agreeing with it.

The seat Locked

Four Axioms, and he is taking Convergence. The choice is not sentimental and it is not about affinity.

  • It is already failing. Convergence produces Zeniths and absorbs their strain, and strain accumulates. Repeated Zeniths load that one Axiom disproportionately. It is presently the Axiom bearing the most pressure, and that is a measurable fact the Order has noticed without being able to explain.
  • He is the one loading it. Every chapter he taught drives worlds toward thresholds. The pressure on the seat he wants is pressure he is applying.
  • It is the respondent. Convergence is the principle that escalates, consolidates and answers. A mind sitting there does not merely govern a domain — it is what everything in the cosmos resolves toward.
He is auditioning To take the seat he must scatter himself across the cosmos, draw through a structure he built, and arrive as one thing. He is performing an act of convergence in order to become Convergence. The method and the position are the same principle, which is either the most elegant thing about the plan or the reason it works at all.

What happens when an Axiom is replaced is the one part nobody has tested. The Axioms are sentient and constrained entirely by function; no Axiom can act contrary to its own nature. A mind that can choose, in that position, is a categorically new thing. Nothing in the structure forbids it. Nothing in the structure has ever been asked.

Why he needs hands Locked

The Axiom layer is reachable from one place: the First Heart, the deepest heart in the cosmos, which sits at the center. The center is where the Core stands, permanently receiving, drawing everything near it inward. The approach is lethal to anything, worse for gods, and worst for the gods born from the Reach.

He is a later god and is harmed less. He still cannot make the crossing.

Mortals can That asymmetry is the shape of his entire career. Every single thing Vecna does, he does through people who can go where he cannot — which is why he taught a method instead of founding a church, why his agents have never heard his name, and why the plan is built out of other people's ordinary ambition. His working is shaped like a confession.

So he goes the other way. If he cannot cross to the First Heart, he will connect to it — through the one kind of door that reaches heart to heart.

The Figure Locked

The Figure is a lattice of opened and linked child hearts. Building one requires a chain of three conditions per world, and he controls none of them directly.

ConditionWhat it takesWho provides it

The Atrium foundAn instrument that reads a world beneath its surfaceA.T.L.A.S., or generations of scholarship

Fifteen hearts, three per sphere, five spheres The count is not symmetry. The pull has to be drawn from across the whole cosmos rather than one corner of it, so the lattice spreads deliberately — three hearts in each of five spheres, so that what he gathers is the Yearning of the Continuum rather than of a neighbourhood.

Surplus is doctrine. He builds past the count he needs, because a node can be taken off the board: the destruction of the Fonains Monolith proved that a standing Monolith and the world beneath it can both be lost. Losing one should cost him a substitution instead of the working.

The Cast Locked

The First Heart is closed and has been since the First World ended. Every child heart pulls toward it — the Yearning — and while the door is shut that pull is a tilt in the floor with nothing at the bottom of it.

The Cast is the massed Yearning of the whole lattice, routed into one line. Fifteen opened hearts pulling at once, through links that make them one structure, is the force that forces the door. Residue opens a child heart; nothing that small opens the first one.

Arrival and trigger are one act The same lattice that opens the door is the road he travels. He does not open it and then walk in. The opening is the arriving, and everything he has placed in those fifteen rooms moves in the same instant.

It gets one attempt. The seat is available only while Convergence is failing, and the Continuum's answer to a failing Axiom is a stabilization. If he is not in position when it comes, Convergence re-settles around the existing configuration, the accumulated strain discharges, and the seat is held permanently. There is no second window and no waiting for one.

Vessels Locked

A heart-link carries what a traveller carries. He is not traveller-sized. Pushed through one heart he arrives as a fragment, and a fragment holds a fragment's judgement. The seat needs the whole mind.

So the lattice is a wide road rather than a long one. He distributes across every mouth at once and reassembles at the far end, and that is the only way he arrives as himself. Fifteen vessels at minimum, one per opened heart.

The downward tithe Worship normally rises. This pools. N'ghathrod venerates him without knowing the name, and what returns fills the colony's taken. The oldest hold the most and a fresh one holds a trace, so any n'ghathu is a vessel and they differ only in capacity. The five Enna are the fullest by tenure; a hundred of the newly taken carry the same volume in worse containers.

Three consequences the table will feel:

  • Killing Enna degrades him rather than stopping him. Capacity lost to tenure has to be replaced by numbers.
  • Every taking the colony performs is another drop of capacity. Their expansion is his manufacturing line and neither of them has said so.
  • N'ghathrod is the one irreplaceable piece. He is the only being alive who makes vessels, and he cannot see what is in his own children, because tadpoles are copies of himself and of course they carry something of him.

The second key Locked

The lattice is one way to force the First Heart. A single fragment made whole is the other — all seven pieces of the Rod of Seven Parts, reassembled and brought before the Atrium.

It is a trap for the party Gathering the Rod is among the most powerful things a crew could accomplish, and completing it manufactures the one object that opens the door without any of his infrastructure. The heroic version of the quest and the catastrophic version are the same quest, and the difference is entirely who is standing nearby when it finishes.

He is aware of this route and has been collecting residue for centuries. He has never assembled a fragment, because the pieces of one fragment draw toward each other and a near-complete Rod is a beacon pointed at everything that wants it. The lattice is slower, quieter and under his control. The Rod is what he reaches for if the lattice fails.

The minimum Locked

Nobody knows the floor, including him. He is working from theory and has never had an opportunity to test it. Fifteen is his calculation with margin. Whether ten would serve, or eight, is unknown to every party in the cosmos.

Which settles what he does under pressure. He fires with what he has. The window is made by Convergence failing and the window closes, so a working short of its design is still a working he will attempt rather than lose.

The trap in the obvious heroism

A diminished arrival still takes the seat. What sits in Convergence afterward is a mind with pieces missing — governing escalation and consolidation, bound to act according to a nature that now has holes in it. That is a worse outcome than the whole of him arriving.

So cutting the channel while leaving the far end open degrades the ending rather than preventing it. The clean win is the First Heart. Everything else is harm reduction that can backfire, and the party can only know which is which by understanding the shape first.

The failsafe Running now

Monoliths relay inward. He drives Zeniths. So he has always had a one-way channel into the Citadel, and he has been using it for a long time: encoded messages, archived deliberately, addressed to an audience he cannot see.

He does not know whether any of it has landed, whether anyone inside can read it, or whether anything in there could act on it. The Citadel holds the densest concentration of capability in existence and every one of them wants the same thing. What he offers is the way out.

The party arms it An exit runs up an intake during an active taking — and Zeniths are the one phenomenon he watches constantly, because his own chapters cause them. Someone coming the wrong way out of an archiving event is the signal he has been waiting an age for. If the crew gets into the Citadel and out again, they prove to him that escape is possible, and they will have no idea they did it.

They will also assume the asset inside is the half of him held there, because who else would it be. It is not. That half read one of the messages, recognised the hand, and did nothing with it — and may not fully understand it either, since the plan postdates him.

The hands Locked

Two bodies do the labour. Each solves a problem the other cannot touch, neither knows the other matters, and both would stop if they understood.

ProducesBelieves it is

Weapon and vehicle The Seekers alone would break Convergence and do nothing about position. The colony alone would build a road to a door that stays shut. He needs both, and they have never met.

The Restorers appear in no part of this. Their work points at the Core for reasons entirely their own, and they are relevant to the campaign because they are the road the party takes to the truth rather than because they serve him.

He steers none of it. He taught a method and let the method run, and the single operational instruction he has ever given reached N'ghathrod secondhand: a world with a Monolith is a marked world, go there, find the heart, open it. The colony reads that as its own strategy.

The Remaking Terminal state

If the Cast succeeds, a mind with preferences occupies one of the four principles everything in the cosmos passes through. Escalation, consolidation and peak expression thereafter answer to a will. That is the Remaking, and it is the published apocalypse under its own name.

Consequence rather than aim He works toward the seat and may never have thought past it. It is the exhaust of the Ascension, and he would tell you he is a scholar. He would be telling the truth. That is the part that should frighten the table.

The campaign is unlikely to reach it. The Remaking is the state of the world after a total failure to contest him at every level, across every arc, and the realistic outcomes of this campaign cut the working off long before. It is on this page because the shape of the world bends toward it, and because every point the crew fails to contest moves one mark closer.

It also stands on its own as material: a cosmos already remade, run as a separate thing, with a party who arrives after. Held for that, rather than planned for.

Where the campaign actually sits

The Ascension is the frame. The bulk of play happens well inside it, against the people doing the work, and that is by design — a campaign spent fighting a god directly is a campaign with one scene in it.

LevelWhat play is about
The bulkN'ghathrod and the colony, the Seekers chapter by chapter, the Restorers, and the institutions arguing about all of it. Concrete opponents, reachable places, decisions with visible consequences.
The turnThe crew assembles enough of the pattern to see that the opponents they have been fighting are employees. Nothing about the opposition changes; everything about what winning means does.
LateEither his failsafe — if the colony fell and he is working the Citadel — or the Cast itself, if it is still standing. Both end at the center of the cosmos.
The centerTwo destinations at one address. The Citadel is where the answers are. The First Heart is where the ending is. Neither gets you the other, and reaching either is its own expedition.
Defeating N'ghathrod does not end it It removes the vehicle and the vessel supply, which is enormous, and it moves him onto the failsafe. Act III is a real victory that changes what Act IV is about rather than cancelling it — and a crew that cuts the channel and leaves the far end alone has made the ending worse, which they can only know by understanding the minimum.

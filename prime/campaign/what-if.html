What If

Untethered ideas. Cosmological speculation, late-arc levers, "what if a faction did X" — material that may or may not enter the world. When a What If gets developed enough to be grounded — assigned to a place, a person, a situation — it graduates to Plot Hooks. Loosely grouped by relevance. The most unstructured page in the wiki by design.

What this page is for Questions the cosmology has room for and has not answered. Anything settled lives in Cosmology, the Remaking or Factions; this page points at those rather than re-asking.

Hearts & the lattice

Hearts, Apertures, opening and linking are settled. What remains open is what the lattice does once it exists and who else can reach it.

  • What if Toril is a doorway? Toril has a heart like any world, and its Aperture is somewhere under the most charted world in Realmspace. If it is ever opened, the most familiar world in the system becomes an access point to whatever it is linked to. Through the most familiar to the most extraordinary.
  • What if the crew opens a world themselves? They would have the lattice's own tool and the colony's own method, pointed wherever they chose. The cost is permanent, the link works in both directions, and two linked worlds bleed into each other. The first time the crew does this to a world, they become a thing that does this to worlds.
  • What if a link has to be severed? A merged pair, far enough along that people have families on the far side and crops that only grow because of what flows across. Severing is the obvious right answer and it kills things that are now real. Nothing in canon yet says a link can be severed at all.
  • What if a world's heart can be sealed instead of opened? Folded deeper rather than unfolded, and hidden from other hearts as well as from searchers. The Runekeepers do something like this for their own world. Whether the practice exists anywhere else, and whether a sealed world is invisible to A.T.L.A.S., is unwritten.
  • What if the lattice reaches the Core? Vecna's figure terminates there and he has no solution for the approach. If the heart-links are the route rather than the drawing, the last stroke is also the road.

Reach & its descendants
  • What if the Reach descendants in the Radiant Citadel remember how the Reach was attempted? Canon: some of the Reach world's people were sequestered into the Core, and their descendants live in the Citadel. If they carry cultural memory of the attempt, Vecna and the Restorers both have a reason to want them.
  • What if Vecna is from the Reach civilization? The deepest version of his interest: he was there, or he descends from it, or he recognized the scattered artifacts because of a connection that predates his godhood. Arc III or later, and only if the table reaches it.
  • What if the other residue pieces surface as future arcs? Seventeen exist. A Convergence piece that amplifies threshold events. A Transformation piece that changes a thing into another thing in violation of what should be possible. Another Boundary piece that answers A.T.L.A.S.'s pull. Each is an arc waiting to be needed.
  • What if another Rod piece did to someone else what the seventh did to N'ghathrod? The seventh made his reproduction into duplication. Thessalar kept simulacra of himself in the same building. Two products of one artifact in one room is a scene that has not been written.

Alternate readers of A.T.L.A.S.

The device's three depths are a property of the artifact. The named readers are this campaign's; others could stand at those depths.

  • What if a Watcher reads it? A Watcher's cosmological understanding sits between the crew's and Vecna's. They might see the Aperture read — where a world can be entered — without seeing the structural one. They would extract more than the crew and less than the antagonist, and would have to decide what to do about it.
  • What if a Restorer reads it? Their doctrine points where Vecna's understanding points: structure, and the Core. A Fifth Reading might read at the structural depth and see what he sees. The order would write up what it read and distribute the segment, which puts the structural read in a ledger — and ledgers can be reached.
  • What if a god reads it? Gods see less than they think (see Cosmology → The Gods). A god might read at surface only and be surprised by the limit. Or one who has begun to see past their portfolio might read deeper. Beshaba reading Sol's world through A.T.L.A.S. is a possible late-arc moment.

Crew disposition of A.T.L.A.S.
  • What if the crew destroys it? If they hold it and decide it is too dangerous, they might break the housing and bury the piece. The piece survives — paracausal, Reach residue, possibly unbreakable. They would be hiding it. Some future finder learns what they buried.
  • What if they give it to a third party? The Watchers, a Restorer they do not know is one, a faction they trust. Most third parties are not equipped to hold it well. The Order would lock it away again and restart the cycle. A Restorer cell would measure it, write it up and distribute the segment, which is the most responsible handling available and also the widest possible publication. There may be no safe custodian.
  • What if they bring it to Tumak's homeworld? The Runekeeper's Dissolution piece and A.T.L.A.S.'s Boundary piece are both Reach residue. Pieces of different fragments do not yearn toward each other, but putting them in the same room might still do something. A card to play very carefully — but a card.
  • What if the crew uses it to find an Aperture and leaves it closed? Knowing where a world's heart can be entered, and choosing not to, is a position nobody else in the cosmos holds. It also makes them the only people who could tell the colony where to dig.

The colony & the PCs
  • What if the Vth'orama notice Tumak? A Wayfinder reaches worlds on a personal mark alone, where everyone else needs a link and an Aperture. It is the one capability N'ghathrod cannot build. Card to play carefully. The healthy version: they file him as an anomaly and investigate only if circumstances warrant. The unhealthy version: he becomes a quest object, which turns a PC into a plot device. Only if the table earns it.
  • What if they learn about the Latchling? A paracausal anomaly with sentience, and a sign that Gregory's resonance produces usable phenomena. They might target the Sea Dock, try to recruit Rindle, or try to acquire the Latchling itself.
  • What if a taken prisoner is kept alive? An n'ghathu cannot be read and does not consider itself a victim. What it will say is limited by what its tadpole was taught and by a century of practice at saying nothing. The enna are a different proposition entirely — they carry far more, and they are never unescorted for exactly that reason.
  • What if the crew meets someone they knew, taken? Same face, same memories, same affection for them, and an allegiance that runs elsewhere. The person is not gone, which is the part that does not resolve.
  • What if a Seeker chapter helps them? Most chapters are scholars. One that trades a real answer for a real question would be the most useful contact the crew has — and would be a chapter, with a founding story, and a voice in its history.

The Restorers

The order, its ladder of readings, Vecna's providence feed and the first entry are settled. What remains open is how far the approach has got and what the crew's position becomes once they understand it.

  • What if a Fifth Reading has solved the approach? Four people hold that work. If one of them is further along than the order knows, the race Vecna is content to wait out is already nearly over, and nobody on either side has noticed. A late-arc clock that was running the whole time.
  • What if the crew carries a reading the order needs? They will have stood on worlds nobody surveyed and seen a standing loss in progress. Trading what they know buys them the Ledger — and advances the approach by exactly as much. Every honest exchange with a Restorer moves the first entry closer.
  • What if a cell learns what the first entry is? A Third Reading who works it out has no doctrine to fall back on and four Fifth Readings to warn. Whether the order would hear her, or file her as an apostate, is the kind of question that makes a faction real.
  • What if the Restorers find the colony first? A standing loss outranks everything in the Ledger, and the Vth'orama are the largest one in the cosmos. Scholars with no fleet, moving on it anyway, would need the crew badly.
  • What if a Restorer hears the voice? Nobody in the order has, since the founding. A living account would prove the doctrine to them and collapse the distinction between the Return and every Seeker chapter they have catalogued as an imitation.

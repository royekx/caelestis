Colony Operations

What the Vth'orama are doing right now, and the operation they have already run against Caelestis. This page holds what is moving. Who they are lives in Factions; what their work is unknowingly building lives in the Ascension.

The load-bearing fact The colony is the only party in the cosmos opening hearts at scale. N'ghathrod wants a lattice of linked worlds because it is an empire that arrives without crossing wildspace. He believes he serves an elder brain, he has never been given an order, and he has never heard of the Figure, the Axioms, or Vecna. Every heart he opens is a mouth on someone else's working.

How they work Locked

The colony needs three things per world, and controls one of them. Zeniths happen on their own schedule; Monoliths follow when the strain is great enough; the Atrium has to be found and the heart has to be entered with residue in hand. Only the last of those is theirs.

Saturation

Zeniths are the cheap half and Atria are the slow half, so the strategy is volume: push as many worlds over the threshold as possible, so that the few Atria they do find already have a Monolith waiting over them. They do this by coordinating with planet-bound Seeker chapters — the chapters do what they were always going to do on their own world, and when it crosses, the colony comes.

The visible symptom Zeniths occurring far more often than they should, across worlds with nothing to do with each other. This is the single longest-running observable in the campaign. The crew can watch it for years before anyone can explain it, and the Order has the records to prove it is happening and no instrument that reaches the cause.

How they search

Slowly, by hand, and badly. Scholars and archivists taken for what they know. Digs at a world's deepest accessible points, frequently in the wrong place and years ahead of any need. The Anadia well is one of these — a world with neither Monolith nor Zenith, surveyed and abandoned when the dig came up empty. The bodies in the side cut were the labour.

The cost is tadpoles Everything the colony does is paid for in tadpoles, and N'ghathrod's production runs on a slow cycle. A person costs one. Something enormous costs many. A crate in transit is a strategic target — destroying one costs him months of placements, and it is a win the crew can measure long before they can touch him.

The Caelestis operation Locked

Two operations ran against Caelestis in the same window, by two parties, against the same vault, for unrelated reasons. Joffrey is the colony's only operative on the station; everything else moving against Caelestis is Vocath's, and Vocath believes Joffrey is his.

The station is teleport-sealed, so anything wanted from the Brig had to be reached physically — and reaching it required first understanding the station: routes, schedules, courier protocols, response patterns, which shifts overlap, where security thins, and who has clearance to know what.

Vocath's operation

A mercane with a commercial and personal grudge, working his own quarrel by proxy. His aim is to disgrace Mirt enough to prise him loose from the school's protection so that someone else can finish him. The school is Mirt's shield; Vocath needs to wreck his standing without facing him directly.

The thefts, the sabotage plates, the leverage on Miken and the disc-marked crate cache at the Sky Dock are all his, and all real. The eye-in-a-wheel is his own merchant mark — it appears on his goods wherever they move, which is why it turns up on the recovered rod, on the Sky Dock crate, and at the Cairn engagement.

The target is the Brig. A restricted vault breached on Mirt's watch, during his own crew's graduation, is the disgrace he has been building toward for years — and he is a merchant, so whatever comes out of it is worth money besides. The build-up has been deliberate: thefts to establish that the station leaks, sabotage to establish that it is poorly run, and a graduation night when security is spread across a ceremony and a dock.

He was steered to it The Brig's location and access protocols are classified, and Vocath could never find that vault himself. Joffrey found it and told him it was there. So the strike happens because the asset proposed a target the merchant already wanted to want — and Vocath experiences the entire plan as his own initiative. He is running a real operation, at a real objective, for reasons that are genuinely his. That is precisely why it works as cover.

The arrangement

Zhauveth — one of the Enna — approached Vocath roughly a year before Arc I. The offer was an asset inside Caelestis, a place Vocath had spent years failing to reach. The opportunity was already in motion: Cairn Station had gone silent, and the Fleet would send someone to look.

Vocath understood that he was dealing with a mind flayer offering a thralled agent. Ordinary illithid work: repellent, effective, and no concern of his. He asked no further questions.

The price was a Caelestis crew, taken at a time and place he could arrange. He read that as payment in bodies, which is what illithids always want.

What Vocath asks his asset for

Everything a man needs to plan a strike: security rotations and response times, where the Brig is and how it is reached, what it holds that is worth taking, shipment schedules and which cargoes are worth intercepting, who on the docks can be bought, and where Mirt is on any given evening.

Joffrey has never lied to him The reconnaissance Joffrey runs for his own purpose produces exactly that intelligence as a by-product, so every report is accurate, useful and freely given. He withholds one thing: that a specific instrument is in that vault and that it is the whole point. Vocath has no reason to suspect an omission in a stream of information that has never once been wrong.

The Tetherbreak engagement

Cairn Station had gone silent. Vth'oramu sat on the Cairn bearing for days, which is the mass signature in Cairn's final transmission — closing slow, reading differently on every sweep, matching no vessel profile, because it was not a vessel. Cairn reported it and stopped reporting anything. The dreadnought moved on afterward.

Vocath's people took the station after it went dark, and waited. A silent Fleet post guarantees a relief run, and a relief run has one bearing to take. Holding Cairn was how the arrangement got its ambush.

The Bellwether Crow was dispatched to find out why Cairn had stopped answering, with Clint Dashforest and Joffrey both aboard. She called at Strake, drew the approach packet, and went out on the edge run. They were taken on that leg, short of Cairn. Clint seized the boarders' craft and broke away with it to pull the pursuit off the Crow; a psychic wind carried him out of the system entirely. A second boarding followed. The senior deck officers and most of the crew were lost, and the Crow came about under damage, made Strake, and limped home from there.

Joffrey was taken in that second action — a tadpole of N'ghathrod's, implanted, with its directive already taught to it before it was carried. The survivors who came home with him were allowed to, so that there would be a story.

The account held, and it was a good one: with the deck officers gone and Clint away, Joffrey held the survivors together, organised the withdrawal, and brought them off alive. He was decorated on their unanimous account, posted to Caelestis, and rose on genuine merit from there. The survivors believe every word of it. The taking matured over the following year, which is why he read true for most of it.

Two events, one file The FIA chain holds both and connects neither: Cairn lost to something it could not identify, and a relief vessel mauled by a craft bearing a merchant's mark on its way to find out. Nobody has reached Cairn since. The standing order closes everything past Strake, which is why the colony's presence there stays unconfirmed and why the bearing is still watched by whatever was left behind. Strake itself remains open, crewed, and entirely unaware of what it is the last stop before.
What Vocath received An agent inside Caelestis who reports to him, accurately and usefully. What he did not receive is anything at all. Joffrey is himself — he thinks, speaks and works as he always did, and everything about him reads true. His allegiance runs to N'ghathrod.

The run

The Brig's location and access protocols are themselves classified, so surface reconnaissance was insufficient. The operative planted tracking material on a known artifact-bound shipment and watched where it went. Vocath's cover thefts and the bribed dock seam created the inventory turbulence that made anomalous handling look normal.

Jeffrey is the dock seam — bribed, with no idea what his friend is. Blip is uninvolved.

By graduation Joffrey holds the physical map of how to reach the Brig and how to leave the station afterward. Vocath's chaos provides the diversion. Joffrey runs.

Two thefts, one vault Vocath's people are robbing the Brig, and they mean it. They take what a merchant's crew would take — instruments, restricted supplies, anything catalogued and portable — and whatever leaves with them is real loss to the Academy and real leverage later. Joffrey is in the same vault for one object. The raiders are unaware he exists; he has no interest in what they carry out, because none of it is A.T.L.A.S. and none of them know it matters.

The Fork — the Brig heist sets the tempo Locked

During graduation chaos, with security diverted by the surface operation, Joffrey runs the Brig. He reaches the vault and takes A.T.L.A.S., and he has only the Sky Dock chaos to extract through, because teleporting out is unavailable to him. The Brig itself is dangerous — wards, constructs, automated responses — and he comes out haggard, wounded and half-broken, having burned every contingency to get that far.

What the body shows If he is captured or killed: chitin beneath the skin along the spine and the back of the skull, thin and recent. The jaw and hard palate wrong underneath. The brain intact — which makes no sense to anyone who knows what illithids do to a host. His mind resists reading. He says one word: Oramu.

The crew is the variable. Three outcomes, all playable.

State A — the thief escapes

The crew arrives too late, fails to intercept, or loses the engagement. Joffrey makes the Sky Dock and gets onto a ship. A.T.L.A.S. leaves Caelestis. The Academy knows something major happened, and knows neither what was taken nor where it went.

Arc II is a chase. The crew tracks the device across Wildspace, arriving at sites after the colony has been there — readings already taken, Atria already found, worlds already opened. They are behind, and they feel it.

Caelestis posture: Mirt's standing takes a real hit. The Brig was breached on his watch during his crew's graduation. Vocath gets exactly what he wanted, by accident, and the mortgaged trust is useful for later Bridge tension.

State B1 — the crew returns it

The crew intercepts during extraction. He is already badly wounded and breaks under pressure. They take A.T.L.A.S. and hand it back. The Academy now knows the device exists as a target, and its understanding of its own collection has changed.

Arc II is a contest with institutional backing. The crew can request access to study it later; the Academy quietly opens its own investigation and may or may not loop them in. Caelestis owes them, Mirt's standing is enhanced, and the Brig's curators start asking why anyone would go through that much trouble for this one instrument.

Alive or dead matters. A living Joffrey is a thread into protocols, contacts and recognition codes — and an institutional crisis, because he is a decorated officer whose mind resists reading. A dead one raises the same questions with none of the answers.

State B2 — the crew keeps it

Same engagement, different decision. They take A.T.L.A.S. and hold onto it — justified as Caelestis cannot keep it safe, or simply as a pull toward the thing they fought for. It is institutional theft either way, however defensible. If the Academy finds out it becomes a relationship problem; if it does not, the crew is sitting on a secret of their own.

Arc II is the literacy decode. Voyage by voyage, edge case by edge case, they learn the device. Each world they take it to feeds the pattern, and eventually — late Arc II, possibly Arc III — they see enough to recognize what it is reading. They earn the meaning.

And they become the target. Future encounters with the colony may be specifically about recovery. N'ghathrod forgets nothing and lets go of less.

Either way the spine holds The work continues regardless, slower without the device and faster with it. State A accelerates the colony; B1 and B2 hold them where they were. The crew bends the tempo rather than the direction. If the heist runs while the crew is elsewhere entirely — saving a wing, fighting Vocath's chaos, pulled into an Academy emergency — it resolves as State A by default, and they learn later that something was taken from a vault cadets were never meant to know about.

The race Running pattern

A directional pattern to thread through Arc II rather than a sequence to follow. A.T.L.A.S.'s readings produce a competitive geography: the crew sees adventure hooks at each indicated world, the colony sees Atria. Both reach for the same physical destinations for different reasons, and both are under time pressure.

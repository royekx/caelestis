Spine, Remaking, Voyages. The Spine is the authored shape of the campaign — its question, its crew, its arcs, what each act is for. The Remaking is the machinery of the opposition — the figure, the ladder, the factions that carry it out. Voyages is what actually happened at the table. Work the Spine when deciding where the campaign goes; work the Remaking when deciding what moves against it.

Central Question

Who decides what's real?

Every faction has an answer (see Factions & Characters). Every PC's arc is a personal version of the same question. Vecna's answer — I decide, and everyone else's reality is a footnote — is what makes him the antagonist. The crew's answer is what makes them the crew.

Cosmological Pressures

Three things are happening at once across the cosmos in this campaign. They pressurize everything the crew encounters.

  • Continuity is under strain. Vecna's project is bending the system. Paracausal events are more common than they should be; gods reach further than their portfolios usually allow; cosmic powers stretch.
  • Zeniths are arriving too often. Worlds cross their thresholds at a rate the Order's records cannot account for, scattered across systems that have nothing to do with each other. This is the most visible symptom of the Remaking, and the one the crew can observe for years before anyone explains it.
  • Astra Virex is expanding. A wounded celestial calls people to it and widens its reach through those who answer. A second cosmic pressure, running in parallel and belonging to nobody else's plan. DM only One of the answering is Casey, who understands his patron to be Polly.
  • The Restorers are moving. An order of scholars reaching toward the Core to recover everything the cosmos has lost, for reasons entirely their own. They have been walking into Zeniths for four centuries to get there, and a great many of them arrived.

The crew won't see most of this directly for a long time. What they'll see is the weather — strange events that don't quite explain themselves, NPCs hinting at things they shouldn't know, recurring patterns that suggest a coherent force behind them. The job is to let the weather be felt without explaining it.

Bartholomew Grayson
Circle of Stars Druid
The Almosts — a missing shape he's unconsciously circling. Recognition arc, not search.

Arc centers on the Almosts: a missing wildshape form he's unconsciously circling, something already present he has been avoiding. The accelerant is an unbloomed flower from the tyrant ship's garden — stirs the Almosts as an inkling and a direction, not a revelation. Blooms via narrative milestones of his becoming, and possibly of its provenance location. Dead flower never stalls the arc — it's accelerant, not key.

Recognition, not search. Creature design is held until observation surfaces it in play. Key observational flags: how he uses Beast Sense/Beast Bond, treatment of the star map, whether he reaches for Starry Form or Wild Shape under pressure.

Where it's moving: toward a moment of recognition — what the Almost actually is. The flower's bloom is one possible trigger; encountering a creature that feels like the missing form is another.

Carries the Fonains shard — a fragment of the only monolith ever destroyed, Tharizdun-corrupted. Becoming a container for archival residue: shard, half-ship, salvage logbook. The "urge to dance" is the Pulse triggered by the corrupted shard.

A Starchild, and his faculty is visual: he sees constellations others cannot. His patron is Polly, his brother, who left their world after a star fell on it and reaches him as a familiar voice. Gregory
Autognome Abjurer Wizard
Paracausal — an autognome who can cast. A tuning fork for Continuity.

Paracausal: no other autognome in this world can cast. The resonance he feels (Calibration, Bearing) is a consequence of the change, not the definition. A tuning fork for Continuity — anomalies surface near him.

Active hook: the Latchling at the Sea Dock, his first anomaly. The arc is the journey (Rindle as obstacle), not the destination (he knows where).

Sol Fortuna
Astral Elf Paladin of Beshaba
The Misfortuned. Escaped Aethris's ritual before it completed. Pursued by the CRC.

Born "Misfortuned" on Aethris — a Tymora-devoted world whose Zenith is underway. Escaped the ritual before it completed, was recruited by a Watcher, now manifests Beshaba's Oath of Misfortune. Beshaba speaks minimally: "Stand," "Again," "Continue."

Tumak Swan (Wan & Tu)
Twin halflings / Wayfinder + Runechild
Partial Wayfinders. Homeworld hidden by the Dissolution cycle. Runes track cosmic threat.

Twin halflings disguised as one person, both partial Wayfinders. Homeworld is hidden by the Dissolution fragment's cycle. The fragment is leaving the current Runekeeper, which is why the Runekeeper is dimming, and it began forming in Wan and Tu, which is why the twins were sent out. Wan and Tu are two separate hosts. When both arrive home, the three poles stall the cycle and the result is a Zenith.

Runes surface on skin near cosmic threats. The Wayfinder mark grows with understanding; the full mark is several levels away.

Where it's moving: a homeworld kept unfindable by the Dissolution cycle, and a return that could trip a threshold. The runes are a built-in proximity detector for cosmic danger. If the cycle stalls, the concealment that rides on it is at risk.

Arcs

Four arcs. Detail decreases as the arcs move further from current play — Arc 1 is concrete, Arc 4 is directional. Each arc is the campaign's shape at that scale; specific session prep lives in Voyages.

Arc 1 — Spelljammer Academy

The tutorial. Cadets going from raw recruits to Driftwardens. Surface text: training, missions, the academy's politics. Underneath: the cosmology asserting itself, two operations running against the station at once, the figure being drawn. The crew doesn't know yet, and that's the point.

Goal & end point

Graduation. The crew becomes Driftwardens, kept together as a unit. The Brig heist Fork resolves: State A (thief escapes with A.T.L.A.S. → Arc 2 is chase) / State B1 (crew returns it to the academy → contest with institutional backing) / State B2 (crew keeps it → literacy decode at their pace).

Where we are

Module 3 of the published Spelljammer Academy, pacing extended for breathing room and to let the sabotage mystery stir. The crew is on the tyrant ship's Cargo Deck holding an uninstalled helm, having reached the Command Deck once and withdrawn. The tyrant ship arc should close in one or two sessions, then H'Catha. See Voyages for the current beat.

Shape

Three published modules become the tutorial: Orientation, Trial by Fire, and the Realmspace Sortie running into the tyrant ship, then Behold H'Catha, then graduation. Session numbers have drifted from module boundaries twice, so the arc is tracked by beat rather than by voyage. Each module is the surface; underneath, every "ordinary" incident (the thefts, the neogi, the sabotage, the missing miniature, the clockwork horrors, the H'Catha meteor) is a component seeded so the crew can assemble the pattern themselves later.

Vocath's grudge-op is the noise. The reconnaissance against the Brig is the real operation, running for months underneath it. Joffrey — decorated, capable, and taken on the Cairn approach a year ago — is already on station and above suspicion. Graduation chaos is the cover for the breach. See the Remaking.

Arc 1 insert — the H'Catha arc Next

The world itself is in Realms — the Spindle, the eight domains, the terrain rays, who lives there. What follows is the arc: the objective is fixed and the route is theirs. Organized by beat rather than by session, because the beats have not yet landed in the order they were planned twice running.

The objective Recover the adamantine meteorite that struck the Spindle. It landed on a seam, so neither bordering domain owns it, and moving it into either domain is an act of war. Both claimants know that, which is the only reason it is still there three weeks later.

Beat 1 — the crossing

Millions of miles, roughly a fortnight. It does not need playing out day by day; the useful shape is a roll across a few days to see whether anything surfaces, and a sense of the passage rather than fourteen scenes.

One encounter earns its place: an Arcane merchant ship. It introduces the Arcane before H'Catha, where they run the ports, and it quietly connects to Vocath without the crew knowing. The waystation and the cantina are better saved for crossings between systems.

Beat 2 — the arrival, and the disguise working too well

Vaelorix was H'Cathan and landless. So his ship coming in does not read as a trader — it reads as a claimant returning, and the domain it enters decides whether that is a homecoming, a grudge, or an opportunity. A tyrant ship draws a look. An ordinary spelljammer draws an immediate response.

ApproachUpsideCost

Either way the tension is crossing watched borders, and the meteorite sits on a seam between two domains that are neither of these, so at least one border and one seam have to be crossed on foot.

Handing it over at graduation triggers the reclassification beat — it goes above their clearance, and that is the breadcrumb that retroactively loads the whole arc.

The tie-in worth taking Ostekk is unplaced. If he lives in the Spindle, and the crew arrives carrying the Lantern he has been trying to recover, H'Catha stops being a rock recovery and becomes his doorstep — which keeps the tyrant ship paying off after the ship is taken, and fits a beholder world without inventing anything.

Your call Ostekk on H'Catha, or elsewhere. The arc works either way; it is much better one way.

Arc 2 — Driftwardens in the Field

Missions. The crew earns rank, learns the Fleet's politics, runs contracts across Realmspace and beyond, and starts crossing the Vth'orama's traces in the field. Cosmological awareness builds from local strange events into a coherent pattern.

Goal & end point

The crew recognizes the Vth'orama as a coherent enemy before any institution does, and begins to suspect something behind them. The pattern engine resolves: the Passage marks, the resonance-cores, the people who cannot be read, the Zenith frequency, and the heart-readings (if they hold A.T.L.A.S.) converge into one operation, for a purpose its own operators have not been told. Arc 2 ends with that recognition and the first attempt to act on it institutionally.

Shape

Contract-based. The Fleet's handler posts contracts graded by scale; the crew chooses; each mission seeds a piece of the larger picture. The Fork state from graduation determines tempo — chase, contest, or literacy decode.

The board. Three or four contracts per downtime. Surfaces read as nearly mundane — a dark beacon, a failed well, an escort, a repossession — and the story is what the crew finds on arrival, whether or not it has anything to do with the contract. Contracts they decline resolve without them, and the outcome turns up later as a board posting or as gossip. Renown raises Driftwarden rank, which unlocks the higher grades.

GradeScaleLevels
VStation, lane, ship4–5
IVSettlement4–7
IIIRegion, moon, nation6–10

ISystem or sphere17+

A Sealed grade exists above these and never goes on the board; the Bridge offers it directly. The crew graduates at the lowest rank, so their first board carries no contract beneath them.

Three layers per contract. The surface is the job as written. The undertow is what is actually happening locally — a contest of wants between people who live there. The trace is a cosmic thread, at most one per contract and absent from most: the Vth'orama at work, the wake of work already done, a Zenith that is nobody's doing, or another force entirely.

The race runs as a directional pattern through Arc 2: the crew (if holding A.T.L.A.S.) and the Vth'orama reach for the same physical sites for different reasons. Sometimes the crew arrives first; sometimes the site is already touched. The realization that something is racing them is a slow-burn beat.

Open development

The handler is unnamed. The Realmspace worlds need their own records — identity, who speaks for them, an open fact and a rumour each. The Astral encounter table and its companion log are unwritten. The Fleet's institutional history — its named enemies, its defeats, and the doctrine that exists because of them — is unwritten, and the ensemble of named-but-unseen figures beyond Zerathis is unbuilt.

Arc 3 — Recognition

The crew acts on what they've learned. They know the Vth'orama exists; they know what it is building; they start trying to stop or redirect it. Institutional friction builds — the Order, the Fleet, the Restorers and the colony all have stakes. The campaign opens up.

Directional possibilities

Less detail. Arc 3's shape depends on Arc 2's outcomes. Possibilities the spine carries forward:

  • The Restorers surface as a third force.
  • The Runekeepers become legible; Tumak's homeworld arc moves toward resolution.
  • The crew encounters a second Reach artifact, hostile or not.
  • The lattice becomes operationally relevant: the crew uses a heart-link themselves, or has to decide whether to sever one that people now live across.
  • The Enna Vth'ghathu become distinguishable from one another, and killing one is understood to cost a function rather than a person.
  • Vth'oramu is located.
  • The Radiant Citadel and the Core become a real destination, possibly reachable.

Open. To be developed as Arc 2 reveals what the campaign actually wants Arc 3 to be.

Arc 4 — Cast

The inscription completes or fails. Vecna's spell gets its one cast. The crew has done what they could; the cosmology resolves around the result.

Direction

Minimal at this stage. Arc 4 will be shaped by everything the crew has done in Arcs 2 and 3. The shape of success for the campaign is not "stop Vecna" but "change what success would mean for him." The crew bends his casting precision, his options, his timing, his confidence in the outcome — to the point where the cast misses, partially succeeds, or succeeds at a cost he didn't intend.

For the failure-state shape (what happens if the crew loses), see Remaking → Failure state. The Convergence rule says he gets one cast.

Campaign

The whole campaign, read across its arcs: the theme, the shape, the frame, the pressures on the cosmos, the crew, and each arc with every force that acts in it. Who people are is on Dossiers and Crew. Vecna's and N'ghathrod's lanes are on Opposition: Vecna and Opposition: the Vth'orama, and their cells are embedded under each arc here. What happened at the table is on Voyages, and how the campaign is run is on Principles.

Theme

The campaign's theme is that everyone is missing something and is trying to become whole, to find or make their place in the cosmos. The theme takes a different shape at each level.

  • Vecna. Vecna wants the answer to the cosmos and what was taken from him.
  • N'ghathrod. N'ghathrod wants a home for his people.
  • Astra Virex. Astra Virex wants to heal.
  • The crew. Casey wants his brother, and Tumak wants his home. Bartholomew wants the peace within him, his constellation, and Boogie wants to belong to something. Gregory and Sol carry the theme too (Crew).

Shape

N'ghathrod, with Vecna behind him

N'ghathrod is the main villain, and stopping his invasion takes most of the campaign. Vecna stands behind him and beyond the campaign's direct reach. The late campaign is about stopping Vecna's working, the casting of the eye, and stopping the casting means stopping N'ghathrod, whose colony opens and links the hearts the eye is drawn in. The crew need not fight Vecna to stop his working.

The campaign is not a war of the gods. Gods are far from worlds (polarity), and Vecna works through vessels: N'ghathrod's colony and the Seekers chapters.

Defeating N'ghathrod does not end the threat, because Vecna has contingencies, and a victory over N'ghathrod is never undone by fiat. The failure state is GAL-01's core waking for Vecna, which brings down GAL-03, GAL-04 and GAL-05 with it and lets Rykoe open (Opposition: Vecna).

The scale moves outward

The campaign grows the way a fantasy campaign grows from town to kingdom to realm. The early levels play at the scale of one system, then neighbouring systems, then the Erevos Galaxy, and late in the campaign beyond it.

An awake core presses on its neighbours, and GAL-02's presses on GAL-01's. Late in the campaign it can pull the crew into the eldritch galaxy, where the rules become Call of Cthulhu, and what they do there is get home or close what is open. The Radiant Citadel and the Core Monolith are late Arc 3 or Arc 4 destinations, reached by a long voyage or through a linked heart.

Frame

Touchstones
  • The Taken King. Destiny's The Taken King is the tone and structure blueprint. N'ghathrod is its Oryx and Vecna its Witness.
  • Legend of Vox Machina. The story architecture follows it: each job plants small hints that tug up toward bigger threads, so exploration always reaches a larger story.
  • Mighty Nein. The world runs as a living world, the way the Mighty Nein's did. The feel is Destiny, Star Wars and Guardians of the Galaxy.
  • Found family. Found family is an explicit emotional pillar of the campaign.

Genre by place

Cosmic horror runs in the background of the whole campaign, and each world anchors its own genre.

  • Realmspace. Traditional D&D fantasy.
  • The spelljamming lanes. Bend toward science fiction.
  • H'Catha. A spy thriller, where subterfuge, stealth, information and a silver tongue are worth most, and violence is always an option with consequences.
  • The deep Astral and the edges. Where the horror lives.
  • GAL-02. Call of Cthulhu, rules and all.

Pressures

Several things are happening across the cosmos at once, and they press on everything the crew meets.

Zeniths and Monoliths, more often

Over the past century Zeniths have come more often, and Monoliths with them, on worlds with nothing to do with each other. Some of the rise is Vecna's doing and some is worlds reaching their moments. The strain it puts on the Axioms, the Axiom strain, ripples outward, and mortals are starting to feel it. It is the longest-running symptom in the campaign: the crew can watch it for years before anyone explains it, and the Watchers have the records to prove it is happening without an instrument that reaches the cause.

Astra Virex

Astra Virex, a wounded celestial woken and broken by the ripple, calls people to it and widens its reach through those who answer. It belongs to nobody else's plan. Casey is one who answers, and he hears it as Polly.

The Restorers

The Restorers reach for the source they posit, where the All-Father holds everything a Monolith takes, which they have never confirmed exists. Their most radical members walk into Zeniths to get there. They compete for residue, and without knowing it they can advance Vecna's plan.

The Princes of Elemental Evil

The crew meets the Princes' pressure as strange elemental-plane phenomena on the worlds they visit, never as the Princes themselves.

The Fleet stretched thin

Several pressures build on the Fleet at once: more Zeniths, more Watcher requests for the Caelestis Corps' help, a whiff of an illithid sect that the Corps cannot name, worlds where something is happening that the Watchers answer, astral-level events, something kept under wraps at the system's edge, and the strain of Vecna's work on the Axioms. Vocath sees the thinning and uses it to stress the Academy. It is why cadets get the H'Catha mission.

Two figures at the edges add to it. Ilsevaine Corr, who rules a hidden polity outside Realmspace, has noticed more traffic near her borders and will face Vth'oramu once N'ghathrod grows bolder. Aurel Venn, a Watcher who saw the same pattern and was not heeded, left his post to find it himself. Together with the rise in Monoliths and the Watchers' calls for help, these strain the Fleet.

Let the weather be felt

The crew will not see most of this directly for a long time. What they see is the weather: strange events that do not quite explain themselves, NPCs hinting at things they should not know, and patterns that suggest a coherent force behind them. Let the weather be felt, and leave it unexplained.

Arcs

Four arcs. Detail falls away as the arcs move further from current play: Arc 1 is concrete and Arc 4 is direction. Each arc ends with the Opposition cells for it. Session prep lives on Voyages.

Arc 1: Spelljammer Academy

Cadets go from raw recruits to Driftwardens. On the surface it is training, missions and the Academy's politics. Underneath, the cosmos asserts itself and two operations run against the station at once, and the crew does not know it yet.

Goal

Graduation. The crew becomes Driftwardens, kept together as one unit, and the Fork resolves. State A, the thief escapes with A.T.L.A.S., makes Arc 2 a chase. State B1, the crew returns it, makes Arc 2 a contest with institutional backing. State B2, the crew keeps it, makes Arc 2 a slow decode at their own pace.

Where the crew stands

Module 3 of the published Spelljammer Academy, paced out for breathing room. The crew holds the Tyrant Ship. In V006 they broke the Command Deck field, took the Lantern and ejected Ostekk-10, and the helm was seated off the table in the Make Ready interlude (V006.5), with level 4 applied. Next comes the swap and the crossing to H'Catha; the current beat is on Voyages.

Shape

Three published modules make the tutorial: Orientation, Trial by Fire, and the Realmspace Sortie running into the tyrant ship. Behold H'Catha follows, then graduation. The arc is tracked by beat, not by session. Each module is the surface. Underneath, every ordinary incident, the thefts, the neogi, the sabotage, the missing miniature, the clockwork horrors and the H'Catha meteor, is seeded so the crew can assemble the pattern later.

Vocath's grudge against Mirt is the noise. Joffrey's reconnaissance against the Brig is the real operation, running for months underneath it, and graduation is its cover (the Caelestis operation).

The close

Arc 1 closes around sessions 8 to 10 with the attack on Caelestis and graduation, with the party at level 4. The Brig thief thread comes forward before graduation, so the crew can engage with it and may learn the thief is Joffrey. At graduation the meteor is handed over and reclassified above the crew's clearance, the breadcrumb that loads the whole arc in hindsight. After the H'Catha mission the tyrant ship goes into Caelestis's inventory, and the crew is not told it will.

Critical beats

  • Close the tyrant ship. Drop the Command Deck field (V006), seat the helm (the V006.5 interlude), and the ship flies. The crew reports the commissioning home, and Flighty Foundling brings Ezra out for the swap (V007).
  • H'Catha. Recover adamantine from a contested seam (below). Sol's first encounter with the CRC lands in this window, as an escape with an objective rather than a fight.
  • Graduation. The attack on Caelestis, the Brig run, the meteor's reclassification and the Fork.

Arc 1: the H'Catha mission

The world itself, its bands, its ten domains, its rulers and the Pact, is on H'Catha. What follows is the mission. The objective is fixed and the route is the crew's. It is tracked by beat, not by session.

The objective Recover a worthwhile load of the adamantine meteor that struck the Pupil, on the seam between Kurrzot's domain and his rival's.

Why cadets, and how the report came

Nereth Vaul, H'Catha's Watcher, saw factions moving on the meteor and reported it as a precaution against escalation; there is no Zenith on H'Catha. The report went from her to the Hall, to the Caelestis Corps, to the Academy, and Mirt made it a cadet mission, because the Fleet is stretched thin (above). The report is also why Ezra is sent as backup.

The crossing

Fourteen days from Toril. A ship at spelljamming speed cannot be hailed, so nothing is met at speed: each day roll a d6, and on a 1 roll on the crossing d8 table. Ships stop at the lane marker, which is where encounters happen. The table, the lane marker and the mercane trader are on V007.

The approach

Every beholder ship is carved stone, so the tyrant ship reads as native traffic, and a few ships in orbit are normal. A Caelestis scuttle ship docks in the Cargo Deck intake and carries the landing party down unnoticed. The tension is on the ground: crossing watched domain borders, and at least one seam on foot.

Landing is hard for everyone. The crew arrives about three weeks after the impact, behind rivals who have been on the ground two weeks or more, at the moment the standoff is cracking. Qitru's dial: "beholder killers, inbound"

Miken reported that the crew killed a beholder. Qitru hears it, arrives at H'Catha ahead of them, and tells the ports that beholder killers are coming. On this world that reputation is read ten different ways. One ruler wants them hired, another wants them gone, the ports want them watched, and Ondessa, struck down and hiding, wants them very much. One rumour changes every faction's opening move, and it costs nothing to turn up or leave alone. The crew will have no idea why the reception is strange, and the cause is a joke Tumak made on a ladder. How the rumour reaches her is on V006.

The meteor

The meteor is large, a vein mined in an occupied area that rival forces skirmish over, and the crew takes a worthwhile load, never the whole stone. Caelestis wants it for containment: adamantine survives disintegration, so the Fleet wants it for a vault, a cradle or plating, and a cadet salvage crew is the cover. Tarto says only that it is rare, theirs by claim, and wanted first. On H'Catha it matters because the Sovereign Eye cannot see through adamantine, and working to carry it off is a form of treason. The ore is valuable to the Order as well. The Fleet archive already logs it as threshold-resonant, humming on the same frequency as Boogie's shard, Gregory's core and the clockwork horrors' resonance-cores.

Who goes down, and who holds the helm

Before V007 the crew picks Saerthe or Tarto as the instructor for H'Catha (Stat Blocks), and each handles talk of the missing things differently. Someone able to cast stays aboard to hold the helm. If the crew picks Saerthe, he can take the helm with Ryeback commanding the deck. Otherwise Ryeback and Miken stay with the ship, and the teacher goes ashore with Ezra. Leaving Miken aboard alone is the high-tension option.

The eclipse clock

The coming eclipse is the mission's clock: three to four hours of full dark, roughly once a year, when the Sovereign Eye shuts and every ruler sleeps at once, and the next one is close. The crew chooses whether to stay through it to protect Ondessa or help her retake her citadel, a huge ally at higher stakes, or to call the mission done.

Arc 2: Driftwardens in the Field

The crew works contracts as Driftwardens of the Caelestis Sector, out of Ondrel Anchorage, under Captain Haymond Rolt, who oversees several Driftwarden crews at once and hands them their contracts. They meet the colony's skiffs, and their contracts bring them close to N'ghathrod and the taken. They earn rank, learn the Fleet's politics, and start crossing the Vth'orama's traces in the field. Cosmic awareness builds from local strange events into a coherent pattern.

Goal

The crew recognises the Vth'orama as a coherent enemy before any institution does, and begins to suspect something behind them. The resonance-cores, the people who cannot be read, the rising count of Zeniths and, if they hold A.T.L.A.S., the heart readings come together as one operation working toward a purpose its own operators have not been told. Arc 2 ends when the crew can name the operation and first tries to act on it through the Fleet.

Range

Arc 2 stays in Realmspace and at most one other system. Crews start leashed to a system, and range grows with rank. The Fork sets the tempo: chase, contest or decode.

The board

After the graduation board, each board carries two or three contracts at the crew's rank plus one reach. Contracts below rank open only after promotion. Surfaces read as nearly mundane, a dark beacon, a dry well, an escort, a repossession, and the story is what the crew finds on arrival. Contracts they decline resolve without them, and the outcome turns up later as a posting or as gossip. Renown raises a Driftwarden's rank, which unlocks the higher grades.

Three layers per contract

  • Surface. The job as written.
  • Undertow. What is actually happening locally: a contest of wants between people who live there.
  • Trace. A cosmic thread, at most one per contract and absent from most: the Vth'orama at work, the wake of work already done, a Zenith that is nobody's doing, or another force entirely.

Contracts

Contracts fall into three or four categories by how they tie to the larger world: loosely tied, tied to a Seekers chapter, personal to a crew member, and others as they come up. Each contract is built out from its category. The world is already in motion: a contract surfaces because of the state of the world, and the place around it should feel like it exists without the crew. The crew's choices, or their failure to act, change what was already happening. Contracts take any shape, excavations, politics, hunts, and many quietly lead back to a Seekers chapter, some knowingly nefarious and some unwitting.

Arc 3: Recognition

The crew acts on what it has learned. They know the Vth'orama exist and what they are building, and they start trying to stop or redirect it. Friction builds between the three orders of the Order of All, the Fleet and the colony, and the campaign opens up. Arc 3's shape depends on Arc 2's outcomes; these are the possibilities it carries.

  • Vth'oramu. The ship becomes a place the crew can find and enter, and the enna become distinguishable, each one's death costing the colony a function.
  • The Restorers. They surface as a third force.
  • The Runekeepers. The Runekeepers become legible, and Tumak's homeworld arc moves toward resolution.
  • Another residue piece. The crew meets a second piece of residue, hostile or not.
  • A permanent link. The crew uses a link themselves, or has to decide what to do about a linked pair whose worlds are bleeding into each other. A link cannot be undone, so the question is how to live with it.
  • The Citadel and the Core. The Radiant Citadel and the Core Monolith become a real destination late in the arc, and Vecna (Lich) waits in the Citadel.

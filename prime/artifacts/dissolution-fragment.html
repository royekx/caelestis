Dissolution fragment
Both Dissolution pieces, forming in two people.

The Dissolution fragment is the Reach's residue of Dissolution, the Axiom that pulls everything toward sameness. It lies in two pieces, and it has no housing: it lives in people. The Runekeepers of the Lunarfoot clan have carried it for generations without knowing it as residue, and the ritual that hides their homeworld rides on it. It is now leaving the Runekeeper and forming in Wan and Tu, the twin Wayfinders who study at Caelestis as Tumak.

Appearance

The fragment is visible in its bearers. It forms and re-forms in the chest of the Runekeeper and of the Wayfinders who carry it, never still, and the order's device is the fragment drawn half-formed, as it shows in a bearer's chest. On the skin it writes itself as an inscription of runes: half a rune on each twin, so no rune is complete while they are apart.

Properties

The fragment oscillates. Its pieces cycle between whole and broken across the Runekeepers, one share diminishing as another forms, in a rhythm that has never stopped. The Runekeeper's ritual points that cycle and the cycle powers it, so the concealment of the homeworld rides the oscillation: the world sits where it has always sat, reachable by anyone who knows the way back.

Dissolution unmakes joins. A bearer's runes surface in the presence of cosmological threat, and a bearer develops the felt awareness every carrier of paracausal matter has, keenest for anything of its own Axiom. Like every fragment, its pieces draw toward each other; with both pieces in the twins, the pull runs between two people.

History

The fragment was already oscillating before anyone held it. The first Runekeeper etched one pole of it into themselves and caught a process already running, and every Runekeeper since has carried it. The lineage passes because the fragment chooses a new host, and a Runekeeper's dimming is the fragment leaving them.

It began forming in Wan and Tu, and the order sent them out into the cosmos. For the first time the fragment has three poles: the Runekeeper and two twins. Its fixed mass divides three ways, nobody reaches peak, and the cycle stalls mid-motion while the concealment still rides on it. In the twins it is rebuilding in two halves at once, a state neither whole nor broken.

What's hidden

  • The order's ignorance. Nobody in the order has identified the fragment as residue. The Becoming is their belief; the runes come from the fragment.
  • The counter. N'ghathrod is made of joins, and Dissolution unmakes joins. The fragment forming in Tumak is a counter to him, and nobody holding it knows.

Its arc

The Runekeeper's fade runs on a soft clock: rising pressure, no date. Completing the inscription takes Tumak home, and going home ends the concealment. When both twins reach home, the three poles stall the cycle and the result is a Zenith; the mechanics are on Rules and the homeworld's side on the Wayfinders' homeworld.

Pieces of a fragment pull toward each other This is the tracking mechanic. Pieces of the same fragment draw toward one another; pieces of different fragments are indifferent. So a Boundary piece points toward its one sibling, and a Convergence piece feels six — a direction and a rough sense of how many, which is why anyone holding a Convergence piece knows there is a great deal more of it out there.

A party holding A.T.L.A.S. is therefore holding a compass pointed at the only other Boundary piece in existence, and nobody at Caelestis has ever noticed that the instrument has a preference.

All four fragmentsBends reality. Enough axiomity to put a thing back into being — nowhere near enough to rewrite the world. This is what restoration would actually require, and nobody has confirmed it.
Two live consequences

Assembling the Rod is the campaign's sharpest trap. Gathering seven pieces is among the most powerful things a crew could do, and finishing it manufactures the one object that opens the First Heart without any of Vecna's infrastructure. The heroic version of that quest and the catastrophic one are the same quest.

And a whole fragment is already in the party. Tumak is one person in two bodies, which makes the Dissolution fragment assembled in a person — a first-key threshold walking around with the crew, unremarked by anyone including him.

Why they are scattered and hidden

Millennia of separate finders, each of whom worked out roughly what they had and then obscured that they had it. The pattern recurs across every documented housing: people who learn what they are holding stop telling anyone. That is why provenance trails run short, why institutions catalogue these things as curiosities, and why the set has never been assembled by anyone.

Vecna has been collecting for centuries. Which pieces he holds is unrecorded, and he has deliberately never assembled a fragment, because a near-complete fragment is a beacon pointed at everything that wants it.

A.T.L.A.S.
Boundary-biased · Reach
Renders worlds as holographic charts; reveals what each world hides from its own surface. Held in the Brig at Caelestis.

A brass-and-glass projection device, palm-to-fist sized, with a hinged case and an armillary-style internal frame. At its center, mounted at the central node of the frame, is a fragment of Reach residue biased toward Boundary — the Axiom of distinction, of this world is itself.

The device is made; the fragment is not. Whoever built the casing was a finder, not a creator — they discovered the fragment, observed that it responded to proximity to planetary bodies in ways no ordinary stone does, and built around it an instrument that could express the response as a readable form.

Function

Activated near a planetary body, the device projects a holographic chart of the world below — a three-dimensional rendering surfacing structural and cosmological features the naked eye and ordinary instruments cannot perceive. Once per long rest, the user reading the chart gains the effect of Commune with Nature, reframed: the device is not casting the spell, it is letting the world speak through the fragment. Three facts about that world surface, drawn from what the world knows of itself.

The kind of information surfaced depends on the world being read. A young world says different things than an old one; a world hiding a paracausal truth says different things than one without one; a world close to its own Zenith says urgent things. What surfaces is a function of what the world has to say.

Cosmological depths of reading

The device's readings carry information at multiple cosmological depths. A reader sees what their cosmological understanding allows them to see — the same activation produces the same data, but the meaning extracted depends on the reader.

Surface — the readable layer
Anyone with ordinary perception can read the chart's surface features: the world's hidden geography, structures the surface hides, presences and absences, places of resonance. A reader at this level can use the device as a portable exploratory instrument.
Operational — the surveying layer
A reader with cosmological-operational understanding sees where a world folds into itself — its Atrium, the one entrance to its heart, which the surface read registers only as an oddity. Cross-referencing that across world after world produces a survey: which worlds give strong responses, which weak, which carry resonant features. One world at a time it is a door; many worlds at a time it is a map of doors.
Structural — the architectural layer
A reader with structural cosmological understanding perceives what the device is actually reading — the Yearning, the latent pull every heart holds toward the First Heart, and against that background any link that has been made live. At this depth the instrument shows a reader where the lattice stands. Only a few entities in the cosmology have the understanding to perform this reading.

The device hides nothing from any reader. The data is the data; the interpretation is what each reader brings.

How a reader reaches a depth

There is no check and no DC. A depth opens when the reader holds the concept it depends on, and it stays open afterward — so the gate is knowledge the party can earn in play rather than a number they can roll against.

DepthOpens when the reader knows
SurfaceNothing. Anyone who activates it reads this much.
OperationalThat worlds have hearts and that a heart has one hidden entrance. The oddity on the chart becomes an Atrium the moment the reader has a word for it.
StructuralThat every heart pulls toward one other, and that links can be made between them. Then the background stops being noise and becomes the lattice.

Track who holds which concept, because the crew acquires them unevenly and a single conversation can promote a reader a whole depth. That is the campaign's central reward loop in one object: understanding is the upgrade.

Status & provenance
Current custody
Held in the Brig at Caelestis. Catalogued as "navigational instrument, antique, of uncertain origin, exceptional rendering capability" — known-rare, partially understood by the institution holding it.
Provenance
Partial. The instrument has changed hands across known history more than once. The trail before the most recent custodian is murky — finders have generally chosen to obscure their possession of it. This pattern is itself a tell, and recurs with most artifacts of the set: people who learn what they have tend to hide it.
The piece's nature

The central fragment is a piece of the Reach's own Boundary-signature — the part of that dead world that knew it was that world, scattered when the world broke. It retains its resonance with any other world's equivalent self-knowledge, which is why it can be coaxed into rendering a chart of any planetary body it is brought near. The Reach knew itself; through the fragment, every other world can briefly know itself in the same way.

This is what makes the device a survey instrument rather than a generic scrying one. Other Reach artifacts' fragments are biased toward different Axioms and produce different effects in different housings. A.T.L.A.S.'s bias toward Boundary is what shapes its function.

Ethics

The set-wide position is in Cosmology → Residue of the Reach. What it adds up to here: whoever first cut this piece out and built a housing for it is unrecorded, Caelestis has held the result for generations without the question coming up, and any user benefits from an extraction they did not perform.

The Rod of Seven Parts
Convergence-biased · Reach
The Convergence fragment, in seven pieces. Two accounted for, five loose.

The Convergence fragment of Reach residue, broken into seven and scattered further than any of the others. Convergence governs escalation toward extremity, consolidation, peak expression — and the pieces express that bias the way residue always does, by doing one impossible thing extremely well in whatever housing a finder built for them.

The seventh piece and the taking Its associated working is simulacrum. N'ghathrod was captured and experimented on aboard Thessalar's planar craft in the presence of that piece, and what it changed in him was his reproduction: his tadpoles stopped being offspring and became copies of himself. Every n'ghathu in the cosmos exists because of one piece of this fragment, and the colony has no idea that is what happened.

Why it matters more than any other fragment Seven pieces is the largest count, so a Convergence piece gives the strongest and most specific pull of anything in the set — it is the easiest fragment to hunt and therefore the most likely to be completed by accident. And completing it opens the First Heart. See the two thresholds.
Each piece does one named thing A Convergence piece expresses its bias as a single working performed impossibly well, and the three located pieces are each keyed to a spell. That gives the fragment a property the others lack: a piece can be identified by what it does before anyone knows what it is, and a crew that learns the pattern can start recognizing pieces in the wild by their effects.

Open: where the remaining four sit and what they do, whether any institution has catalogued one, and whether Thessalar understood what he was working beside. Xanathar is a known quantity in a known city, which makes the fifth piece the most reachable unclaimed one on the board.

The Runekeepers' fragment
Dissolution-biased · Reach
All three Dissolution pieces, held by an order on an unfindable world — and leaving.

The Dissolution fragment, whole: all three pieces, held for generations by the Runekeepers of Lunarfoot. Dissolution governs decay, dispersal and the prevention of permanence, and this fragment expresses that as oscillation — the pieces cycle, one diminishing as another forms, in a rhythm that has never stopped.

The cycle is what hides a world The homeworld is withdrawn from cosmological visibility by a ritual held in the body of the order's senior practitioner — and the working rides on the fragment's oscillation. The ritual points it; the cycle powers it. The world sits exactly where it has always sat, and finding it requires knowing the way back rather than knowing the coordinates.

It is changing hands

The fragment is leaving the current Runekeeper and forming in Wan and Tu, who are one person in two bodies. It writes itself onto them as an inscription, half a rune per twin, and no rune is complete while they are apart. The current Runekeeper dims as it goes.

What nobody has said out loud A whole fragment assembling in a person is a first-key threshold. By the register's own rule, one fragment made whole opens any Atrium — so the completed inscription is a key to the First Heart carried by a PC who thinks he is going home. Mechanics in Rules; the consequence is unresolved and is the largest open question attached to any character.

Open: what happens to the homeworld's concealment while the fragment is in transit between bearers.

Containment Engine, Fourth Series
Housing · tyrant ship
Built by Ostekk to harness one piece of the Rod. Aboard the derelict tyrant ship.

A built device rather than a found one, and the clearest surviving example of what a competent finder does with residue. Ostekk built it around the fourth piece of the Rod of Seven Parts, the one keyed to regenerate, and the name records that there were three earlier attempts.

Harnessing is the operative word: the Engine holds the piece in a working state rather than containing it, drawing on a Convergence bias to do one thing extremely well. As a usable item it is the Lantern of Flourishing — an iron lamp-housing with an iris shutter, uncommon, three charges refreshing at dawn. Its green pulse heals 1d8 to any creature it touches within fifteen feet, and 2d8 plus biological regrowth to constructs and plants.

Vaelorix stole it from Ostekk, which is why Ostekk sent the behir after it. It is on Vaelorix's body on the Command Deck of the tyrant ship, and it is the finale's prize rather than a thing found along the way. The crew has stood on that deck and seen the body, then withdrew to Cargo, so they have been within reach of it without knowing what they were looking at.

The autognomes are bound to it by design Ostekk built servants that depend on the Engine for maintenance, securing loyalty through need rather than trust. Nine OSTEKK units existed; OSTEKK-6 is the last survivor. That is the same question Gregory poses from the other direction, and the two can be put in a room together.
At the table The piece is extractable and the Engine is not built to survive losing it. That is the live decision whenever the crew reaches it: a Convergence piece in hand is a compass pointed at six more and a key to any Atrium they find, and taking it stops whatever the Engine has been doing for however long it has been doing it.

Open: what the Engine was built to contain or power, what the first three Series were, and whether Ostekk knew what the piece inside it was.

The Transformation fragment
Transformation-biased · Reach
Five pieces. Every one unlocated, uncatalogued and unhoused on the record.

Five pieces, and the only fragment the record is entirely silent about — every piece unlocated, every housing unnamed, and the institutions that collect such things silent on the subject.

Transformation governs a thing becoming another thing, and it is the Axiom Vecna ascended through. A housing built around a Transformation piece would do something to the category of what it touched, which is a strange enough effect that anyone who found one would likely have obscured it faster than most.

Why the silence is itself information Three of the four fragments have left traces across known history. This one has left none, in a cosmos that has been looking at residue for millennia. Either the pieces are somewhere nobody surveys, or somebody has been very successful at keeping five of them quiet.

Open: all of it. This is the cleanest hook in the set and the most portable — a Transformation piece can be found anywhere the campaign needs one.

Objects of consequence

Named things that carry cosmological weight without being residue. Different class, same reason for being on this page: the crew will ask what they are.

The Fonains shard
Monolith fragment · in Boogie
A piece of the only Monolith ever destroyed, still trying to archive.

A fragment of the Fonains Monolith — corrupted by Tharizdun, destroyed by Lola's Boys, and the only Monolith on record to have been destroyed at all. It is not residue. Residue is matter of the First World; this is a piece of a Continuity response, which is a different kind of object entirely and the only one of its kind anybody has.

It is still attempting to archive, on a very small scale. Whatever a Monolith does when it takes a sample of a world, this does, at the size of a thing sitting inside a plasmoid's nucleus.

How it got there Salvage brought back from Fonains by a crew sent looking for Clint Dashforest, sold on through Vocath's line as ordinary cargo, and inspected without authorisation by Boogie, who took it. Four separate people handled a piece of a destroyed Monolith and none of them knew what it was.

The Pulse is how it shows: a rhythm Boogie reads as his own and the player plays as character. It carries no saves and no hard rules by design. He also holds the half-ship from Voyage 001 and a half-logbook, both archived into him — and the logbook was pulled from a simulation of a real wreck, which means the original exists somewhere in Caelestis with pages now missing.

Open: what a corrupted archive fragment does over years at this scale, and where the original logbook is.

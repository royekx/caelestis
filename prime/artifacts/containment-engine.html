Containment Engine, Fourth Series
Built by Ostekk around a piece of the Rod, by someone who did not know what the piece was.

A built device rather than a found one, and the clearest surviving example of what a competent finder does with residue. Ostekk built it around the fourth piece of the Rod of Seven Parts, the one keyed to regenerate, and the name records that there were three earlier attempts.

Harnessing is the operative word: the Engine holds the piece in a working state rather than containing it, drawing on a Convergence bias to do one thing extremely well. As a usable item it is the Lantern of Flourishing — an iron lamp-housing with an iris shutter, uncommon, three charges refreshing at dawn. Its green pulse heals 1d8 to any creature it touches within fifteen feet, and 2d8 plus biological regrowth to constructs and plants.

Vaelorix stole it from Ostekk, which is why Ostekk sent the behir after it. It is on Vaelorix's body on the Command Deck of the tyrant ship, and it is the finale's prize rather than a thing found along the way. The crew has stood on that deck and seen the body, then withdrew to Cargo, so they have been within reach of it without knowing what they were looking at.

The autognomes are bound to it by design Ostekk built servants that depend on the Engine for maintenance, securing loyalty through need rather than trust. Nine OSTEKK units existed; OSTEKK-6 is the last survivor. That is the same question Gregory poses from the other direction, and the two can be put in a room together.
At the table The piece is extractable and the Engine is not built to survive losing it. That is the live decision whenever the crew reaches it: a Convergence piece in hand is a compass pointed at six more and a key to any Atrium they find, and taking it stops whatever the Engine has been doing for however long it has been doing it.

Open: what the Engine was built to contain or power, what the first three Series were, and whether Ostekk knew what the piece inside it was.

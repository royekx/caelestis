The Runekeepers’ fragment
All three Dissolution pieces, assembled — and assembled in a person.

The Dissolution fragment, whole: all three pieces, held for generations by the Runekeepers of Lunarfoot. Dissolution governs decay, dispersal and the prevention of permanence, and this fragment expresses that as oscillation — the pieces cycle, one diminishing as another forms, in a rhythm that has never stopped.

The cycle is what hides a world The homeworld is withdrawn from cosmological visibility by a ritual held in the body of the order's senior practitioner — and the working rides on the fragment's oscillation. The ritual points it; the cycle powers it. The world sits exactly where it has always sat, and finding it requires knowing the way back rather than knowing the coordinates.

It is changing hands

The fragment is leaving the current Runekeeper and forming in Wan and Tu, who are one person in two bodies. It writes itself onto them as an inscription, half a rune per twin, and no rune is complete while they are apart. The current Runekeeper dims as it goes.

What nobody has said out loud A whole fragment assembling in a person is a first-key threshold. By the register's own rule, one fragment made whole opens any Atrium — so the completed inscription is a key to the First Heart carried by a PC who thinks he is going home. Mechanics in Rules; the consequence is unresolved and is the largest open question attached to any character.

Open: what happens to the homeworld's concealment while the fragment is in transit between bearers.

World·How the cosmos works

Cosmology

How this cosmos works, from energy and the five Axioms up through galaxies, gods, hearts and residue. Each term is defined in one line in the Glossary; the map is the Chart; places have their own entries under Realms, the gods under Deities, and the fragments under Artifacts. Vecna's working has its own page, summarised in the last world section here.

The Axioms

Energy

Energy has always existed. It is never made and never destroyed, only arranged. Everything that exists, a star, a world, a person, is energy held in a pattern for a while.

Five Axioms govern how that energy behaves. Energy is the only thing there is; the Axioms are how it behaves. None of the five is a substance, and structure, every star, world and living thing, is what the five make together.

The five
Consolidation
draws things together. Dust gathering into worlds and gas falling into stars are its plainest work.
Expansion
spreads things apart. It stretches space and gives what lies in it room.
Dissolution
pulls everything toward sameness. It works the way ink spreads through water: scattered up close, an even sameness from a distance.
Distinction
makes and keeps things different from each other. Elements, weather, life and minds are what it builds and holds.
Determination
holds that every outcome must be decided, and nothing sets which way it goes. Until an outcome is decided it stays open; once decided it stays decided, and each decision opens new undecided things.

Each Axiom is named for what it means; the physics is in how it behaves. Determination does not mean that outcomes are fated. It means every outcome must be reached, and nothing chooses it in advance.

Two pairs and a fifth

Consolidation and Expansion decide where energy goes, and Dissolution and Distinction decide what form it takes. Determination stands between the two pairs.

Across the pairs, two Axioms close possibilities and two open them. Consolidation and Dissolution close them, pulling toward one place and one state. Expansion and Distinction open them, making more room and more distinct things.

Four meetings

The Axioms on the two axes meet in four ways, and each meeting does something to what is possible.

Determination acts on all four in turn. It is why each tension tips one way or another, without fixing which, and why Consolidation wins in one place and Expansion in another. Without it the cosmos would be the same everywhere.

The 5-cell

Pictured, the five Axioms stand equally far from one another, like pins in a cushion, with Continuity at the center. Determination is really a cloud through all the others, the likelihood that permeates them, and it stands as the fifth pin.

Five points all equally far apart exist only one dimension up, in the figure called the 5-cell. Drawn flat, it is five points around a center, the same figure as the five galaxies around Erevos.

DM The picture is a mirror for meaning, not a map: no galaxy belongs to an Axiom. The pairs oppose in what they do, not in where they stand.

What an Axiom is

Each Axiom is the absolute manifestation of its law, with the barest awareness and no more: Consolidation knows only to pull. The Axioms want nothing and choose nothing.

The five are balanced, but they are not equal. An Axiom can be removed, replaced or added as little as energy can be made or destroyed. What changes is their balance: over the ages the push and pull between them has shifted, and reality has changed with it.

Continuity

One thing, two names

Continuity and the Prime Continuum are one thing: all of existence. Continuity is the interplay of the five Axioms: reality itself, the fabric that rises out of how the Axioms resolve. Every galaxy, system, world and plane lies inside it.

Continuity preserves itself. When a world is pushed to an extreme, Continuity answers with a Monolith, and it has answered that way since the Reach. See Monoliths.

Continuity runs both ways

Continuity rises out of the Axioms, and whoever reaches into it can push and pull how the Axioms work, and so remake reality. Everyone else lives inside what it serves them.

Time runs one way because of Dissolution. Every pocket of difference sends more sameness out into the cosmos than it keeps.

Paracausality

A thing is paracausal when its place in the structure of reality has been altered, so that it operates outside ordinary cause and effect. Hearts and their Atria are paracausal. Residue is paracausal by origin. The Axiom strain is making new paracausal things, Gregory among them, and N'ghathrod's tithe is a paracausal flow, not a physical one.

Paracausality and the Weave are separate axes. The Weave is the fabric mortals reach into to cast spells; anti-magic severs local access to it and leaves the energy where it is.

Paracausal beings often develop a resonance, a sensed awareness of other paracausal things and sites nearby. How anti-magic and the Weave are adjudicated is in Rules.

Origins

How things came to be

Energy lay perfectly even, and nothing happened. At the smallest scale its outcomes were undecided, and tiny differences resolved. Expansion stretched space and gave those differences room.

Consolidation drew the richer places together into clouds, stars, worlds and galaxies, and where it won outright, with Dissolution, it collapsed into the cores. In the flow of energy from hot stars into cold space, Distinction built and held distinct things: elements, weather, life, minds.

Dissolution is the price of all of it. Every pocket of difference sends more sameness out into the cosmos than it keeps, and everything is running down. At every step, Determination demanded an outcome and set none.

The Reach

The people of the first world, the only world in an earlier version of reality, reached into Continuity and changed it. That act is the Reach, and their world was destroyed by it.

It was the first time Continuity had to answer. The first Monolith, the Core Monolith, stands where it answered. The Reach world collapsed into Erevos, its fragments spread across the cosmos as residue, and reality rebalanced from there into the form it has now.

Since then Continuity has answered every such strain the same way, with a Monolith. Monoliths are how this version of reality preserves itself. A reality remade from inside might answer differently, or not at all.

The people of the Reach did not simply die. They became woven into the cosmos at different depths, and over time they became the gods. Some may remember what they were. Other gods have risen since, and some have fallen; see Gods.

The First Heart

The First Heart is the Reach world's heart, the deepest heart in the cosmos, beneath Erevos. It has been closed since the Reach.

Every other heart pulls faintly toward it, a latent pull called the Yearning. A residue fragment made whole is a key to the First Heart; the Dissolution fragment rebuilding in Tumak becomes one once enough of it has rebuilt.

Zeniths & Monoliths

A Zenith is a world at its peak, and a Monolith is Continuity's answer to a world pushed to an extreme. Over the past century both have come more often, and the rise strains the balance of the Axioms.

Zeniths

A Zenith is a world at its peak of Distinction, the most fully itself it will ever be. A coronation that unifies a world, a magical saturation, a civilizational transcendence and a ritual that remakes a people can each be one. The world on the far side of a Zenith is a different world, and what it becomes is settled afterward, by events and by the people standing in the middle of it.

Many Zeniths come with nobody's hand behind them; worlds simply reach their moments. Mortals seeking power force others, compressing a peak into a ritual, a conquest or a coronation. The Seekers chapter called the Fracture Cult seeks to bring worlds to their Zenith.

Oerth's Zenith was Vecna's. His ritual to become a god pushed Oerth to its Zenith, and the Zenith made him one. Aethris's Zenith is unfinished, because the Fortuned have not completed their ritual.

Monoliths

A Monolith is Continuity's response, not any one Axiom's: reality preserving itself when a world is pushed to an extreme. Most often that extreme is a Zenith, and the Monolith answers it and records the pattern of the world at that peak. The death of a god on a world was another; Nowhere is what it left. The Reach itself was the first.

A Monolith manifests, anchors itself where it answered, and stays. It absorbs the character of what raised it, which is why no two are alike. It belongs to no one and serves only preservation. It records what is rather than what anyone believes, and it answers prayer from no one. It stands above the gods, who know little of Monoliths. The Prism Ascendant on Aethris shows the gap. Its people take it for Tymora's answer, and it is the Monolith that answered Aethris's Zenith. It has archived what it can and stays where it is, doing nothing more.

A Monolith is also what makes a link between hearts possible; see Hearts. The Order calls a Monolith an Ortholith in its registries.

Archiving

A Monolith archives bodily. It takes people, animals, structures, texts, craft and whole districts, alive and intact, as a sample of what the world was. What it takes is what the change threatens with loss: the measure is whether a thing is about to stop existing, never its worth or its power. From the ground it looks like a region of the world selected and removed while everything around it keeps burning or keeps cheering.

A Monolith holds nothing locally, unless it is corrupted (below). Everything it takes is relayed to the Core Monolith, and what stands on the world afterward is the marker, not the vault. The Order guards Monoliths as though the taken were inside them.

The relay runs through the intake, a channel a Monolith opens while it archives and closes when the taking ends. It is the only passage between a Monolith and the Core Monolith, and it runs both ways, though almost no one has ever gone up it. Every Monolith reporting to the Core Monolith makes up the relay.

A god standing on a world that is changing is not being lost, so a Monolith takes nothing of it. Vecna's ascension shows the rule from the other side: Oerth's Monolith archived his mortal form, because his mortality was the part about to cease. That form is Lich Vecna, who sits in the Radiant Citadel, and Vecna's faithful keep Oerth's Monolith as their holiest site.

Corrupted Monoliths

A corrupted Monolith is a tunnel to Tharizdun's prison. It holds what it takes locally and redirects it, sending existence to her instead of to the Core Monolith. Tharizdun's prison has no map and no address in any known cosmology.

Fonains's Monolith formed corrupted. Five cultists held a ritual: four channelled the Princes of Elemental Evil, and the fifth held pieces of a black crystal, a Tharizdun artifact. The ritual was the Zenith, and Vecna nudged it, knowing it would be ridden. The Monolith manifested in answer, and the crystal replicated into it as it formed. It is the only Monolith ever to carry Tharizdun's crystal and the only one recorded as destroyed: Lola's Boys broke it.

The Vanished of Fonains went to Tharizdun's prison. Scholars long guessed the Shadowfell, the most logical destination they had for displaced souls. A piece of the broken Monolith, the Fonains shard, sits inside Boogie.

Vecna uses Tharizdun as a contingency, a battering ram he can point. It is use, not alliance, and the two distrust each other. Tharizdun's weakening of reality on Fonains serves Vecna's ends, and he lets it run.

Axiom strain

Over the past century Zeniths have come more often, and Monoliths with them. Some of the rise is Vecna's doing; some is simply worlds reaching their moments. The rise strains the balance of the Axioms. The Axiom strain is a byproduct of Vecna's work, not its aim.

The strain ripples outward through the planes and the cosmos. It is slow and more metaphysical than physical, and it weakens as it travels, but mortals are starting to feel it. Gregory's awakening is one result; the Latchling and other oddities are others. Where the strain reaches a place, reality there behaves strangely: the weather of reality, a pressure that builds and belongs to no one. Few in the world have seen it, and fewer have a name for it. The strain helps Vecna, Tharizdun and other powers, good and bad. The Watchers have seen Monoliths rise more often over the past century, and it worries them.

The ripple woke Astra Virex. It also caused collisions between celestial bodies, and part of the entity broke off. All it knows is that it must heal and become whole. The entity can be called Astra Virex, Astra or Virex.

Erevos and Aurath

At the center of the cosmos is Erevos: the remains of the Reach world, a black hole beneath the Core Monolith and the Radiant Citadel. The five galaxies lie around it. Every galaxy's core is to its galaxy what Erevos is to the cosmos.

Erevos is not a plane. Reaching it is travel, and the approach is dangerous to mortals, who can still cross it. It harms gods without destroying them: the First Heart beneath it is the deepest heart there is, so the push of polarity is strongest there.

Erevos opens only when the cores of all five galaxies are awake and joined. Through it lies the way into Continuity itself; see Vecna's working.

Core Monolith

The Core Monolith is the first Monolith. It manifested over the Reach world as Continuity first answered, and it remained after the world was destroyed. Those who dwell in it call it Aurath.

It is far larger than any Monolith raised since. It is the terminus of every relay, and because a taking is underway somewhere at almost every moment, its side of the intake never shuts. Matter, light, structures and people arrive continuously, from everywhere, which makes the approach to Erevos the most violent region in the cosmos.

Core Monolith's pressure

The Core Monolith has a degree of sentience. It presses an idea outward through the cosmos, and those who sense it read it differently. It has never spoken a sentence; what reaches people is weight and meaning.

The Order
takes it as a charge to maintain things as they are, and calls the presence the All-Father. Some within the Order doubt that reading.
The Restorers
take it as a call to bring back what was lost, and call the presence the All-Mother. They are trying to reach a heart, and through it the Core Monolith, where they believe everything lost is stored.
Vecna
took it as something he could learn, and change.

The Restorers and the Seekers are separate. Both readings of the pressure survive contact with the Citadel, which is why two custodial orders with the same reverence treat each other as heretics.

Radiant Citadel

The Radiant Citadel is the civilization around the Core Monolith, built by the taken. It began with the people of the Reach world and has grown with every archiving since: cities, languages, species no one outside has seen in ages, and the ships people arrived in, sitting where they landed.

Zeniths are often caused, and the people who cause them stand in the middle when the Monolith manifests. The Citadel holds an age of archmages, ritualists, druids, hierophants and architects of transcendence, taken by the response their own workings summoned. It is the densest concentration of capability in existence.

A golden age on arrival

Preserved people, an inheritance of craft and knowledge from every world that ever crossed a threshold, and abundance that has never had to be fought over: on arrival it is beautiful, and the Order's doctrine looks borne out. The Restorers' objection stands beside it undamaged, because nobody asked the taken.

Underneath, the Citadel is broken the way every world is broken, and faster. Each archiving delivers a population that chose nothing, a district of strangers with their own gods, laws, languages, debts and grudges, set down inside a city that already had an order. Every grievance is permanent and every settlement provisional.

Precedence by arrival

The Reach world's descendants are the oldest lineage and sit accordingly. The long-settled hold the good ground, the deep archives and the offices that decide where a new arrival is housed, and abundance thins the further out from the center a district lies. The oldest hold that they were chosen: reached first, at the first Zenith, by the first Monolith. Every later arrival is a guest admitted to something already underway.

The forgetting

Time in the Citadel takes the outside away. A street name goes first, then a face, then the shape of a sky, until people can no longer say what their world was called. The long-settled regard it as healing; the newly taken fear it most, and they are the ones who most want out.

The way out is known in theory: find an intake that is open, because a taking is underway somewhere, and go up it against the flow. In practice a traveller must predict where a Zenith will peak, find that intake among a permanent inbound flood, and move in the brief window it runs, to arrive on an unknown world at the hinge of its history with no ship and no charts. Those who went have sent no word back.

Lich Vecna

Lich Vecna, the mortal form archived when Vecna ascended, sits in the Citadel. He has had centuries to conclude he was wrong, and he is calm, almost redeemed. See Vecna.

Galaxies

Five galaxies and their cores

The cosmos is five galaxies around Erevos. Caelestis designates them GAL-01 to GAL-05. On the Chart they sit as a pentagon around the center, GAL-01 at the top.

Every galaxy has a black hole at its own center, its core. A galaxy's core is Consolidation and Dissolution winning outright. A core can be woken; GAL-02's is awake, and the cores of GAL-03, GAL-04 and GAL-05 are each being worked toward waking by another being. What a woken core means is set out under Vecna's working.

Rykoe Galaxy

Caelestis's galaxy, GAL-01, is the Rykoe Galaxy, named for Rykoe, the black hole at its core. Rykoe sleeps. The galaxy's systems crowd along two spiral arms around it.

Its known systems include Realmspace, Caelestis's home system (Toril, Caelestis, H'Catha, Glyth); Greyspace, the system of Oerth, Vecna's homeworld; Krynnspace, CSC 16423, which holds Krynn and Fonains; Virenspace, CSC 69387 (entry); CSC 56973, the system of Aethris; and the hidden system of the Wayfinders' homeworld. Nowhere lies in this galaxy too.

Monoliths are common in the Rykoe Galaxy and found hearts are far rarer; see Found, opened, linked.

Eldritch galaxy

GAL-02, the eldritch galaxy, has gods, but none of them are good. Its core is already awake, and the galaxy is overrun by dark beings.

GAL-02's gods are eldritch, and over time they gathered there, each drifting toward the galaxy where it is worshipped most. No prime or good god holds the galaxy, and it has no Weave. The Axioms and Continuity still hold there. Its awake core presses on its neighbours, GAL-01's among them.

Astral Sea

The Astral Sea fills the dark between systems and between galaxies. The Fleet calls it the drift. It is material space, and all five Axioms work in it, as they do everywhere, in different measure from one reach to the next. Between systems Expansion and Dissolution weigh most, and things spread out and even out: Dispersal. Between galaxies Expansion weighs most of all. The greater gods dwell there, between the galaxies. What a crossing can hold is on the Astral Sea entry.

How systems lie

Star systems lie unevenly through a galaxy. All of them circle the core, at different speeds. They crowd along the spiral arms, gather in clusters and leave long empty reaches between, and the galaxy thins toward its edge.

Most of space is empty

Most bodies have no one living on them, and many could never support anyone. Some are desolate, and some are lethal: crushing air, elemental storms, dead magic. Others are overrun by creatures, hostile or harmless. Many have never been visited, and much of space is uncharted. An inhabited world is rare.

What Caelestis knows

Caelestis belongs to the United Intergalactic Fleet. It and the Fleet's other affiliates work almost entirely within the Rykoe Galaxy, and they chart only that. Few expeditions have tried to leave it. Leaving is near impossible, most who tried never returned, and many believe there is nothing beyond. The Order's Watchers keep to the Rykoe Galaxy as well.

Scholars know their galaxy is one part of something larger, with something at its center, but no one they know has reached it. What lies in the other galaxies carries no designation. Whoever first charts a world in another galaxy is the one who designates it.

Gods

What the gods are, where they live and what keeps them off worlds. Individual gods are in Deities.

Where gods come from

Most gods came into being in the Reach and grew into godhood over time, some sooner than others, so gods have different ages. Later gods ascended from mortality; Vecna is one. A god's standing as a greater or lesser god comes from how much worship it holds, the influence it gains from that worship, and the niche it finds, through one loop of attention and belief.

Gods are not physical. They are woven into the cosmos, each as deep as its standing allows.

Worship

Worship gives gods power, influence and reach. A god with no worshippers still exists but can do almost nothing. A god learns through its faithful: it knows what they see, report and pray about, and little beyond that.

Worship reaches a god from any galaxy, but the farther away it comes from, the weaker its signal. A god spends what it has as concentration or reach:

ConcentrationReach
What it isMany worshippers in one placeWorshippers spread across many places
What it buysForce on one worldA foothold almost anywhere
What it costsNear-blind elsewhereThin everywhere
ExampleTymora and Beshaba on AethrisVecna, across many worlds

A god can be known on a world and have almost no following there. Vecna's worship is large and public on Oerth and lives in small, hidden pockets elsewhere.

Rings by standing

Where a god dwells follows its standing.

Greater gods
dwell in the Astral Sea between galaxies. The push of a galaxy's core keeps them out, and a greater god forcing its way into a galaxy would tear it apart. A greater god may lean toward one galaxy, but it is known across all of them.
Lesser gods
can pass into a galaxy and travel the Astral Sea between its systems. A system's edge pushes them back.

A god drifts toward the galaxy where its worship is greatest. A god answers where it is believed in; answered prayer draws more belief, and more belief draws more attention. Over centuries the loop pulls a god toward one world, then one system, then one galaxy. That is how GAL-02's eldritch gods came to gather there.

Each god has a realm, a divine plane of its own, anchored to the galaxy where its worship is densest. A realm moves only when that centre of worship moves, which takes generations.

Polarity

No god can set foot on a world without destruction, because of polarity. Gods are of the same make as worlds and their hearts, and like charges push apart. A god can push toward a world, but the push back grows with every step until going further is unbearable. Reach residue pushes back on gods the same way a heart does.

The more powerful a god, the larger it is in that metaphysical sense, and the harder the push. This is why the greatest gods are kept farthest from worlds and from Erevos. Erevos harms them without destroying them.

An apparition or avatar carries too little of a god to be pushed. Gods act on worlds through avatars, visions, agents and champions.

Ascension takes on the charge

A mortal who becomes a god takes on the divine charge and is pushed off the world they rose on. Vecna stood on Oerth as a lich. His ascension, the Godhood Ascension, charged him to Oerth's heart and pushed him off the world, and he has not stood there since. He works through vessels.

One god, many names

One god is known by different names, and told different origins, in different galaxies and on different worlds. Takhisis is Tiamat, and Paladine is Bahamut. Asmodeus's role is the same wherever he is known as the devil. Vecna's faithful know him under many names, and most of them do not know the name Vecna.

Nowhere

Once, a lesser god forced its way into a system and down onto a world. The push between god and world destroyed them both. What remained is Nowhere, so called because the charts mark a world there and there is none. A Monolith answered.

Every god since has taken it as a warning. The wreck's heart lies exposed, the easiest in the Rykoe Galaxy to find and open.

What the gods know

The gods did not make energy, the Axioms, Continuity or the Monoliths, and they know little to nothing about Monoliths. The Reach-born carry something of the moment that made them.

Vecna is an open god with a following under many names. The Accordance know little of him: a few centuries ago they felt something change, a new entity like them where there had been none. Some gods suspect he is up to something. His plan runs through people who do not know whose plan it is.

Hearts and links

Hearts and Atria

Every world has a heart: its accumulated self, everything it has been and settled into being, held as a paracausal interior the world grew around. Because a heart is paracausal, it persists where its world does not. A destroyed world still has one.

Every heart has an Atrium, its own hidden entrance, which manifests differently on each world according to what that world is: a door in an old temple that appears only under certain light, the still point at the center of a maelstrom, a well that has always been dry, a courtyard that becomes the way in for a few minutes each year.

Past the Atrium the descent is made of what the world is. A world of sailors gives a sea with no sky and every ship that ever sank, still crewed. A world of war gives a battle that never resolved, and a dead world gives the last thing it remembered.

Found, opened, linked

Finding a heart, opening it and linking it are three separate, hard steps.

StepWhat it takes
FoundGenerations of scholarship, or an instrument that reads a world beneath its surface. A.T.L.A.S. reveals the aspects of a world, and a world's heart is one of them.
OpenedResidue carried into the Atrium. Opening needs no Monolith and grants no control over the world; it makes the heart able to hold a link.
LinkedTwo opened hearts, linked Atrium to Atrium, only where both worlds have a Monolith. The Monolith licenses the connection between one heart world and another.

A link takes moments where a crossing takes weeks, works in both directions, and serves anyone who reaches either end. World hearts link only to worlds in their own galaxy; only a woken core reaches beyond it.

In the Rykoe Galaxy many worlds have a Monolith, far fewer have a found heart, fewer still an opened one. Three links stand: two opened hearts in one system linked to each other, and each of them linked to Oerth.

Linked worlds bleed

Linked worlds bleed into each other over time, one gaining what the other loses. A tide arrives on a world with no moon; a crop fails on one world and appears on the other; people dream in a language that is not theirs. A link cannot be undone.

Three fragments in the Rykoe Galaxy

The Rykoe Galaxy holds pieces of three fragments, Consolidation, Dissolution and Distinction, both by chance and by pull. Pieces of a fragment draw toward each other: once a few came to rest near Realmspace, more followed.

Names and designations

Celestial bodies have names or designations. Caelestis gives designations; the people who live near a body may have their own name for it, sometimes a local epithet ("the falling star of Gethros"). A body known across a system or a galaxy may also carry a wider epithet, the way a moon has a name and is also called the Blood Moon. Most things have only a name or a designation, and the same place can be called different things in different parts of the cosmos.

Designations are what the charts use; names are what people say. Anything inhabited or famous carries the name its own people give it, sometimes alongside an epithet. A body nobody has charted has no designation.

Caelestis Survey Catalogue

Caelestis charts the cosmos the way astronomers chart the sky. Its surveyors keep the Caelestis Survey Catalogue (CSC), and every star they have charted has a CSC number. The numbers are old and scattered; a star the crew first hears of already has its number, high and out of sequence, such as CSC 48213.

Each sister institution keeps its own catalogue, so one star carries a different number in each. Sharing charts between institutions means matching one catalogue against another.

Other planes

The cosmos is the Material Plane. Every galaxy, system, world and stretch of the Astral Sea is material. The other planes sit adjacent to it and overlaid on it, and each keeps its standard makeup: the Plane of Water is water without end, the Elemental Chaos churns, the Feywild and the Shadowfell echo, the Outer Planes hold the divine realms. The Axioms and Continuity operate in all of them.

The Ethereal
the border layer pressed against a world, reached from the surface. It mirrors the Material closely enough that a traveller in the Border Ethereal sees Caelestis where Caelestis is.
The Astral Plane
lies over the Astral Sea. Astral projection sends the traveller onto it, and a projected traveller can cross the Sea without a hull.
Elemental planes
their own geography, tied to a galaxy. The elemental planes reachable from Fonains are the same set reachable from Realmspace, and the Princes of Elemental Evil work in them.
The Feywild and the Shadowfell
echoes of worlds, with their own geography and their own distances.
Divine realms
each god's own plane, anchored to the galaxy where its worship is densest; see Rings by standing.

Echoes

The worlds formed in the Feywild and the Shadowfell in the same act that formed them in the Material. A world that dates to that moment has an echo in each: its own landmasses, seas and living growth, rendered in that plane's terms.

Everything built afterward exists only where it was built. Cities, stations, roads and ships have no counterpart in an echo unless whoever lives in that plane raised one. A fey court stands in the Feywild because fey built it. Caelestis has no echo, and neither has any station or the Astral Sea.

  • Distances differ. Two worlds far apart by lane can be a walk apart in the Feywild, which makes echo travel a route between worlds for anyone willing to risk where and when they come out.
  • Crews rarely meet the fey. An echo is reached from a world's surface, and a career spent between systems never touches one.
  • Caelestis is planar-dead. A thin place aboard the station leads nowhere, which sits alongside the Forbiddance in making the Academy a sealed room.

Getting to another plane
Thin places
a spot where a plane presses close enough that going in is a physical act: a fountain that keeps going down, a vent that keeps going in, a shadow with depth. Anyone can find one.
Color pools
portals adrift in the Astral Sea, each opening onto another plane. They open and close quickly. The Fleet charts them and sails around them; Clint Dashforest went through one on purpose and came out on Fonains. See Color pools.
Spells
Plane shift and gate move a body. Astral projection reaches the Astral Plane over the Astral Sea.
Planar shortcuts
distance in an echo plane does not match distance in the Material, so a crossing that takes weeks by lane can be cut by entering one plane and leaving it somewhere else. Caelestis does not teach it.

Fonains and the elemental planes

Fonains lies in Krynnspace, in the Rykoe Galaxy, and its elemental planes are the ones reachable from Realmspace. A crew entering them enters the region the Princes of Elemental Evil work in.

The Day of Chaos is when the Princes of Elemental Evil break the barriers between the planes so Tharizdun can leave her prison. Shothragot, her avatar, named it in the Fonains Monolith's last hours: "Wait for the day of chaos." Fonains's history with the Princes and with Tharizdun is on its entry.

Waking a core

A galaxy's core can be woken, and there is no one way to do it: whoever works a core does it by their own method. Erevos opens only when the cores of all five galaxies are awake and joined, each core to its neighbours and each to Erevos. Through opened Erevos lies the way into Continuity itself, and whoever reaches it can push and pull the Axioms and remake reality.

In each galaxy a being works its core toward that end. Vecna works the Rykoe Galaxy's. The core of the eldritch galaxy is already awake, and what happens in the Rykoe Galaxy decides the other three.

DM notes

Vecna's working

How Vecna works the Rykoe Galaxy's core, the eye inscription, its worlds and how far it has got, lives on Opposition: Vecna.

Designations and charting

Every place carries a designation in the survey, charted or not, so it can be tracked before the crew ever hears of it. An uncharted place's designation is the one it will carry once someone charts it. Whether Caelestis has charted a place is marked separately in the survey; in the world, only charted places have a designation.

The weather of reality

The weather of reality is the lever for saying strange things are happening, anywhere and at any time. "Weather of reality" is the DM's working name and may change.

Anti-magic at the table

A spell slot is stored energy, so it persists inside an anti-magic field and cannot be cast there. Innate processes that run apart from the Weave carry on inside the field.

The galaxies in play

Each galaxy is assigned a game system ahead of time. GAL-01 runs as D&D 5e and GAL-02 as Call of Cthulhu. GAL-03, GAL-04 and GAL-05 get theirs before any campaign goes there. Each galaxy can hold a campaign of its own; the Fonains and Krynn campaigns take place in GAL-01.

Where each galaxy stands:

  • GAL-01. Two hearts are opened and linked to each other, on two worlds that share one system on the upper lid, and both are linked back to Oerth. One of them is a little-charted planet the crew learns of late in the campaign. One more heart, on the lower lid, is found and not yet opened: Nowhere's.
  • GAL-02. Its core is awake. In the eldritch galaxy the gods wear names close to real-world pantheons.
  • GAL-03, GAL-04, GAL-05. Partway, and their fate turns on GAL-01.

A galaxy's contents stay unbuilt until play reaches it. What Vecna's working needs from a galaxy is tracked as counts only: hearts found, opened and linked. Which worlds they are is decided when it matters, as is the bleeding between linked worlds.

The survey

The survey, prime/data/survey.json, kept with scripts/survey.js, holds every rolled system that is kept. Once saved, a system is part of the record and is edited by hand as play fills it in. The Chart reads it.

Axioms named only on the DM side

The Axioms are named only on the DM side. Scholars, faiths and Vecna describe them in their own terms, and Vecna hears them as music. In play, describe what an Axiom does and leave its name off.

Touchstone for the Axioms' bare awareness: Destiny 2's Nine.

At the table

If the crew finds a door, there is something on the other side: a plane entered by accident is still a real place with its own rules and inhabitants, and curiosity is rewarded with a place. Planar travel belongs to mid and late play; early on, a thin place is scenery the crew notices and leaves alone.

The Citadel's layout runs concentric: precedence, abundance and certainty all run from the center outward, with the oldest lineages innermost and the recently taken at the rim. Its districts are built when the crew heads there.

Lich Vecna is calm and almost redeemed. Meeting him should unsettle everything the crew thinks they know.

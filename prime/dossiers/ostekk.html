Ostekk
Beholder builder. Made the Engine, had it stolen, and has been reaching for it ever since.

A technology-obsessed beholder, and the only one in this arc who builds rather than rules. He made the Containment Engine, Fourth Series — the name records three earlier attempts — around the Rod's fourth piece, the one keyed to regenerate.

Vaelorix stole it. Ostekk has been trying to get it back ever since, and everything the crew has fought aboard the tyrant ship is his: the clockwork horrors, and the behir sent to retrieve the Engine and stranded by a field that unmakes constructs.

He has never appeared and is already everywhere The crew has met his automatons, his horrors, his behir and his name — OSTEKK-2 said it out loud, naming the behir's sender as “the original Ostekk.” Everything they have fought on that ship was built by a man they have never seen.
Loyalty through need rather than trust He built the OSTEKK units to depend on the Engine for their own maintenance, so that their service required no faith on his part. It is the clearest statement of what he thinks about other minds: they are unreliable, and dependency is the fix.

He does not collect, rule, or scheme for territory. He wants his property back, and he has been spending constructs on that problem for however long it has been.

He is still a beholder: he sends agents rather than coming himself, and he has never once had to be present for any of it.

He does not know what the piece inside his Engine is Nothing on record suggests he understood he was building around Reach residue. He built a housing for a thing that did one impossible thing well, which is what every competent finder in history has done with a piece of the Rod.

Which also means the paracausal trace in his automatons is an accident. He made nine servants slightly outside causality and has no idea.

Keep him offstage as long as it holds. His agents are more interesting than he is while the crew is still assembling what he wants, and the moment he appears the question stops being open.

The behir is the live thread. It is alive, roughly seventy damage down, and it does not repair. It also cannot cross the field, so it has been waiting on the one thing the crew can do and it cannot. If the crew takes the Lantern, they take it from Ostekk; if they let the behir pass, they are completing his retrieval for him.

The thing worth setting up now The crew can talk to his constructs. The horrors speak thri-kreen, Krik'Lit is thri-kreen and aboard, and nobody has tried. A negotiated outcome with the behir is a negotiation with Ostekk conducted through a machine — and the window closes when Krik'Lit leaves at the swap.
Where to put him — the highest-leverage call on the board Putting him in the Spindle turns a rock recovery into his doorstep, because the crew would arrive at H'Catha carrying the Lantern he has spent years reaching for. The tyrant ship keeps paying off after the ship is taken.

It fits a beholder world, it costs no invention, and it gives the H'Catha arc a second antagonist whose motive the crew already understands. Open

Open: his location, whether he knows the Engine is findable, and what he does when he learns a cadet crew has it.

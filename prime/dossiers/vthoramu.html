Vth’oramu
A living astral dreadnought, carved into a hull and lived in. The colony is named for it.

An astral dreadnought, taken. The apex predator of the Astral Sea — a chain-tailed leviathan that hunts dead cosmic matter — worn down across a long siege, and then made into a vessel.

The body was laid out and carved. The eye became observation. The mouth became an entrance. The physical tail still ends in the astral cord, and the cord still holds. What a Fleet cartographer charts as anatomy is architecture, and the creature it describes has been dead to its own purposes for fifty years.

It goes where it is sent

Every institutional record treats it as wildlife — a predator with a range, a patrol, a feeding pattern. It is a creature under command, and that is the single fact every Fleet model about the sector is built without.

Tarroway's timing tables fail. Cairn logged a mass signature that matched no vessel profile, closing slow and reading differently on every sweep. The gravitational variance runs strongest along Cairn's bearing. One object accounts for all of it, and the Fleet has no category that would let anyone say so.

  • It hunted its own territory, as its kind do, for as long as anything had been watching.
  • Then it was besieged. N'ghathrod spent a cycle of tadpoles wearing it down — people laid against an animal until the animal tired.
  • A little over fifty years ago it was taken. He went into it himself, and did not come out.
  • The body was opened and made habitable: hull, decks, transit points, the interior widened into a demiplane.
  • The colony moved aboard and took the ship's name for its own. The Vth'orama are named for the vessel because the vessel is what they are.
The interior is a demiplane, reached by fixed points

Movement inside runs through fixed plane-shift transit points rather than corridors, which is how a place that size fits within one creature. Boarding the hull and reaching the colony are separate problems, and solving the first does not begin the second. Layout and scale open

The ship means two different things to the two generations aboard, and N'ghathrod has never allowed the difference to settle.

The promise is kept unfulfilled

A people still travelling toward a home will follow whoever carries them. Arriving would end that. So the going-home continues, and the name of the ship restates the promise every time anyone says it aloud.

The ship is the antagonist

N'ghathrod has been fused into it for forty or fifty years. The dreadnought's nervous system is his. The tadpole pools bloom out of the hull, which means every n'ghathu aboard was grown from his own flesh.

Boarding is therefore no raid on a vessel he owns. It is walking around inside him. Most of the colony has never been told; they live in him and think the ship is a ship.

What is actually aboard

A civilisation. Bearers, the unthinned, the taken, thralls and their children, going about an ordinary life in a place that has been home to some of them since birth.

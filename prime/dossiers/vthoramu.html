Vth’oramu
A living astral dreadnought carved into a hull. The largest moving thing in the campaign, and a civilisation lives inside it.

The largest moving thing in the campaign, and the only one that is alive, commanded, and inhabited at once. An astral dreadnought — the apex predator of the Astral Sea, a chain-tailed leviathan that hunts dead cosmic matter — worn down across a long siege, taken, and then made into a vessel.

The body was laid out and carved into a hull. The eye became observation, the mouth an entrance, and the physical tail ends in the astral cord. What a Fleet cartographer charts as anatomy is architecture.

It goes where it is sent Every institutional record treats it as wildlife: a predator with a range, a patrol, a feeding pattern. It is a creature under command, and that single fact is what every Fleet model about the sector gets wrong.

Tarroway's timing tables fail, Cairn logged a mass signature matching no vessel profile, and the gravitational variance runs strongest along Cairn's bearing. One object explains the entire chain, and the Fleet has no category for it.

  • An astral dreadnought in its own territory, hunting as its kind does.
  • Besieged. N'ghathrod spent a cycle of tadpoles wearing it down — people spent as ammunition against an animal.
  • Taken, a little over fifty years ago. He went in himself and became it.
  • The body carved out: hull, decks, transit points, the interior opened into a demiplane.
  • The colony moved aboard and took the ship's name for itself. The Vth'orama are named for the vessel, because the vessel is what they are.
The interior is a demiplane, reached by fixed points Movement inside runs through fixed plane-shift transit points rather than corridors, which is what makes a two-part megadungeon possible inside a single creature. Boarding the hull and reaching the colony are two different problems. Layout and scale undecided

The ship means two different things to the two generations aboard, and N'ghathrod has never let that difference resolve.

WhoWhat the ship is to them
The founding colonistsA means to an end. The passage to a home they were promised and have yet to be given.
Those born aboardHome itself. They have never stood anywhere else, and the name describes a journey they have no memory of beginning.
The promise is kept unfulfilled on purpose A people still travelling toward a home will follow the one who is carrying them. Arriving would end that. So the going-home continues, and the name of the ship is the promise restated every time anyone says it.

That is the lever on the whole colony, and it is available to a crew who works out what the name means before they work out what the ship is.

The ship is the antagonist N'ghathrod has been fused into it for forty or fifty years. The dreadnought's nervous system is his, the tadpole pools bloom out of the hull, and every n'ghathu aboard was grown from his own flesh.

So boarding is not a raid on a vessel he owns. It is walking around inside him. Most of the colony does not know; they live inside him and think the ship is a ship.

What a boarding party would actually find A civilisation — bearers, the unthinned, the taken, thralls and their children, going about an ordinary life. The one location in the campaign where the antagonist faction is a society rather than an opposition.

The dilemma writes itself and should be allowed to: the crew will arrive expecting a dungeon and find a neighbourhood. Nothing aboard needs to attack them for that to be the hardest room they have entered.

Open: the interior's layout and scale, where the taken are held, and what boarding a creature rather than a hull means mechanically.

Open: whether the crew ever boards, and whether they meet the colony before or after they understand what the ship is.

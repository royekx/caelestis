N'ghathrod
Founder of the colony. He is the ship.

A mind flayer roughly a century into his second life, and a spacefaring elf before that. Ceremorphosis destroyed the elf, and the Rod piece returned part of him. He is the only illithid alive who is two things at once, and he experiences it as having been healed.

Everything he does to others is him giving them what he was given.

He is the ship

For the last forty or fifty years he has been fused into Vth'oramu. The dreadnought's nervous system is his. The tadpole pools bloom out of the hull, which means every n'ghathu aboard was grown from his own flesh. He made himself into the home his people needed.

Most of the colony does not know. They live inside him and think the ship is a ship.

The mouse

He met it on the planar craft. Whatever solace there was to find while Thessalar held and worked on him, he found some of it in a mouse — and when he escaped he took it along with the tadpoles and the people.

It is not a normal mouse. Whatever is wrong with it, the cause is already sitting in the history: it was in the same room as the Rod's seventh piece, for as long as he was. The exposure that rewrote his reproduction had a second occupant.

It is the only thing he carries out of the captivity. Ceremorphosis was Glyth, long before — so the mouse did not know the elf. It has only ever known what he became afterward, and it has been there for all of it: the severed brain, the voice that answered, Glyth turning him out, and the hull closing around him.

Distributed across five minds

Each of the enna carries a Hold — a portion of him, offered rather than taken, and permanent. Losing one costs him a faculty he cannot re-form.

He does not know what he is working for. The voice that answered him when he reached for his severed elder brain has directed him ever since, and he believes it is that brain grown into something greater. It is Vecna. Every heart he opens is a stroke of a figure he has never seen.

  • Ceremorphosis at Glyth, long before any of what follows. It destroyed the spacefaring elf he had been, and he lived a full illithid life there afterward.
  • Captured by Thessalar aboard the planar craft, and experimented on in the presence of the Rod's seventh piece. He was the first subject — no copy of Thessalar existed yet.
  • Escaped as the craft went down, taking who and what tadpoles he could. He never reached Fonains. The craft's trajectory carried his elder brain there, and distance severed the bond.
  • Reaching for the brain, he was answered. The voice has directed him since.
  • Returned to Glyth and was told the unthinned must go. He left with them.
  • Besieged and took an astral dreadnought, spending a cycle of tadpoles to hold it — then went in himself and became it.

What the experiment actually did

The seventh Rod piece is keyed to simulacrum. What it changed in him was his reproduction: his tadpoles stopped being offspring and became copies of himself. Every n'ghathu in the cosmos exists because of that piece, and the colony has no idea that is what happened.

Thessalar learned the trick here and then used it on himself. The eight numbered simulacra in his laboratory came afterward, which makes N'ghathrod the prototype for the Mad Mage's own continuation. He experiences the same event as having been healed.

What's hidden

Most of the colony does not know he is the ship. They live inside him and think the ship is a ship. Beyond that, there are three bodies that answer to his name, and only one of them matters.

Threads

Dissolution unmakes joins

He is made of joins. The Runekeeper's artifact is Dissolution-biased, and Tumak is carrying it.

How to run them

Run on sight

One stat block, built at the ceiling, never scaled down. He plays to win every time and leaves when his objective is met rather than when he is hurt.

He is never beaten by being damaged. What beats him is an interrupted taking, an extracted target, a broken meeting, a collapsed descent. Downed PCs are taken prisoner rather than killed, because bodies are valuable. The lesson lands the first time: they fought well, they lost, and he did not care about them at all.

Open

Is the mouse taken?

Left open, and the obstacle is the interesting part. Taking requires a tadpole, and tadpoles were the thing he was escaping with — so if it is taken, he spent one of them on a mouse while fleeing for his life. That is either the most human thing he has ever done or the first sign of what he would become. Oddity to decide

Taken: then it is the oldest n'ghathu in existence, and by the downward tithe any n'ghathu is a vessel. A god's arrival distributed across fifteen mouths, one of which is a mouse.

Untaken: then it is the one thing aboard that belongs to neither category — a being who grows his own people out of his own hull, who kept something he never reached into.

Not yet built One block, at the ceiling, never scaled. He fights to complete an objective rather than to win a fight, he withdraws on success, and he captures rather than kills.

What the block has to support: an exit condition tied to his objective rather than his hit points; non-lethal takedowns; and enough action economy to face six PCs without being stun-locked. The CRC's Withdrawal Threshold and Legendary Resistance are the working precedents.

N'ghathrod
A mind flayer who wants a home for his people, and has made himself into the ship that carries them toward it.

N'ghathrod is a mind flayer, once a spacefaring elf, and the founder of the Vth'orama, an illithid colony looking for a world to be its home. About five hundred years ago Thessalar experimented on him with the seventh piece of the Rod of Seven Parts, and since then his tadpoles make copies of him: the taking. He is fused into Vth'oramu, the astral dreadnought his people live in, and the colony has no elder brain but him. He wants a home for them, and everything he does serves that want.

Appearance

His true body lies joined to the dreadnought's nervous system inside the hull, and the tadpole pools bloom out of the hull around him, so the pools are in his body as an elder brain's are in its brine. A mouse travels with him.

Personality

He is patient on the scale of centuries and plays to win every time. His people are his blood, and he left his own world to stay with them. He will not let a tadpole die: since his people were exiled, nothing aboard eats the brood. Every illithid aboard is Vth'orama to him, whole or malformed, and he refuses the name the whole illithids give themselves. He thinks of the taking as a gift: a taken person keeps who they were, gains the colony bond, and belongs. What he does to others is him giving them what he was given. Captives are material to him, and he cares nothing for anyone who stands against him.

He has a soft spot for the neglected, the abandoned and the cast out, because that is what he and his people were. When one of his taken finds such a person, he may spend a copy on them for no gain at all, and his taken then protect them and raise them up. He is evil, and this is the piece of him a crew can reach.

Biography

Background

He underwent ceremorphosis on Glyth long before the rest of this, which destroyed the spacefaring elf he had been, and he lived an illithid life afterward. About five hundred years ago his ship clashed with Thessalar's planar craft and lost. Thessalar and the Thessalkraken captured him and some of his illithids. His elder brain was aboard the craft too, kept apart from him. Some of his own tadpoles had been brought aboard, cut off from the elder brain; Thessalar experimented on them and put them into bodies he supplied, and three came out of ceremorphosis malformed.

Thessalar then experimented on N'ghathrod with the seventh Rod piece, keyed to simulacrum. It changed his reproduction: his tadpoles stopped being offspring and became copies of him. It changed how he spawns, from a mass two or three times in a life to a few copies at a time on a slow, steady cycle. It changed the five who became his lieutenants with him, the three malformed and two properly raised illithids captured with him, and each came out with a power of its own. It changed a mouse that had been aboard as long as he had. Thessalar took the finding and turned it on himself.

When Lola's Boys took the craft's control and defence gems in the year 425 and it fell toward Fonains, he used the fall to escape with his five lieutenants, a few lesser illithids and the mouse, which he saved. He never reached Fonains. His elder brain was left behind, trapped in the caverns beneath the Barrier Peaks where the main craft came down, and he believes it dead or beyond reach.

The journey back to Glyth, a system away, took decades. Glyth thins its broods and holds the malformed an obscenity. It saw what the three were, refused them and exiled them, and N'ghathrod went with them, because they are his blood. That was about four centuries ago. Over the centuries that followed he searched for a home for his people and learned the full extent of his ability to take. The tithe of his taken kept him alive, and he shared it with a chosen few: the enna, and some of the old illithids who joined him. They have outlived every illithid of their age by centuries. In those centuries a voice reached him, and the long plan formed.

He took an astral dreadnought that bore no wound, because he knew a dreadnought's physiology and saw his chance. The siege lasted a long time, controlling the creature took a great many tadpoles, and taking it and turning it into Vth'oramu took decades, perhaps a century. He went into it himself and has been joined to it since its taking. Generations of the colony were born aboard, and many died in the Astral Sea. The active push to find and open hearts began only in the past few decades.

In the campaign

The crew has not met him or heard his name. His work is all around them: taken people in small positions, odd disappearances, a station or crew acting strangely.

Relationships

The Vth'orama

His colony, the Seekers of a New Home, and his blood. They believe they answer to an elder brain through him, and in every way that matters they do. Vth'oramu holds a civilization: families, children born aboard, trades and streets. Most of the colony live inside him and think the ship is a ship, and most of them never chose his war. Most of its illithids are malformed. The whole ones, the old illithids who came with him and the minority born aboard who came out whole, keep Glyth's ideal and call themselves the true-formed; he does not accept the name, and the colony has never resolved the tension. The colony's mark is an eye inside a wheel, the wheel for the way home: ordinary spokes in its top half, illithid tentacles in its bottom half.

The enna

His five lieutenants, each carrying a Hold, a portion of him offered and permanent: Osskhal, the Flesh; Ulh'rek, the Arcane; Zhauveth, the Voice; Ith'varn, the Sight; and Khaleth, the Surveyor. Ulh'rek, Zhauveth and Ith'varn are the three malformed grown from his own tadpoles; Osskhal and Khaleth were captured with him. Each joined him willingly, and each carries the power the experiment gave it. He shares his long life with them. He speaks through them at will and does not drive them.

The old illithids

Some of the properly raised illithids who came with him took Holds of their own, lesser portions, so that they would all be one. He shares his long life with them too.

Thessalar

His captor, who made him what he is and learned from him how to copy himself.

Vocath

The merchant whose line supplies the colony's captives. N'ghathrod has never dealt with him; Zhauveth made the arrangement and keeps it, and passes Vocath what the taken know.

Glyth

The world that refused his people. He has outlived those who exiled them and has never forgiven the refusal. He knows the Order's Watchers exist, through the Watchers posted to Glyth.

Abilities

He takes. One of his tadpoles, implanted in a host, does not consume it: the host persists, a copy of him sits alongside, and the taken person gives him their allegiance while keeping their memories, skills and relationships. The taken still care about the people in their lives, and those ties shape how they act: what they say to a friend, whom they protect, how gently they lie. When an old tie and the colony pull apart, the colony wins every time. A tadpole can be removed, and removal frees the host.

He spawns copies on a slow, steady cycle, a few at a time. Over a year he makes more tadpoles than any illithid aboard, and far fewer than the colony's illithids make together. His copies never go to captives. Each is spent on someone in a place of power, reached where they are, so that the colony gains a post: a station master, a dockmaster, a spelljammer the Fleet trusts. The one exception is the outcast. The ordinary masses that every illithid aboard spawns, two or three times in its life, are what grow the colony. They go into the pools, far more of each mass survives than in any colony with an elder brain, and the pools always hold more tadpoles than there are bodies, so the colony always needs captives. Every captive Vocath's line brings goes to the pools or feeds the colony's illithids.

He fills an elder brain's place. He decides which tadpole goes to which captive, the taken report up to him, and what they know ends with him. A portion of every taken person passes upward through the rungs of the taken and ends with him, a tithe that extends his life; the more he takes, the longer he lasts. When a taken person dies he receives what it knew. He can assert himself over an ordinary taken person, and through the enna he speaks.

His join with Vth'oramu is permanent. Only a wish or an artifact could break it. The colony carries one residue piece from world to world to open hearts. Any heart can be opened; a link needs a Monolith on both worlds, so the colony goes where Monoliths stand.

Notable items

  • The colony's residue piece. The one piece the colony carries to open hearts; without it he must win it back or find another.

What's hidden

  • The voice is Vecna. In the centuries after Glyth exiled his people, Vecna reached N'ghathrod as a voice, and as dreams and visions. For some decades N'ghathrod took it for an elder brain. He learned it was not, and stopped caring: Glyth had cast him out, and the voice provides. It gives him what Vecna's plan needs him to know, and everything else he learns from his taken. Vecna is not omniscient. His whispers are suggestions; a whisper told N'ghathrod a dreadnought was the best way to keep a colony on its passage home. The list of worlds is his standing direction, and “a world with a Monolith is a marked world” is his one explicit instruction. N'ghathrod's invasion and Vecna's eye are two plans. He wants worlds for his people and a home; Vecna steers him toward the worlds the eye needs, so each conquest opens and links hearts for a figure he has never seen. When the crew puts pressure on him, he stops following the list and takes the worlds he can get, and the two plans diverge.
  • His elder brain lived. Tharizdun's influence never reached it in the caverns beneath the Barrier Peaks. After Lola's Boys reached it, it escaped to a place nobody knows.
  • The mouse. The seventh piece changed it, and it has a power of its own. N'ghathrod took it with one of his copies, so the tithe keeps it alive. It is the first outcast he saved, and his secret sixth lieutenant: nobody aboard counts a mouse.
  • Three bodies. Three bodies answer to his name, and only one matters (table below). Two of the enna know; the others suspect and have never asked.
  • The taken outlive him. If his true body dies, the taken live on, each keeping part of him, and each loses a part of themselves. His lieutenants are closest to him, each rung down the colony further away, and given time one of them could grow toward becoming another N'ghathrod.
  • The experiment. The colony has no idea that every taken person exists because of the seventh Rod piece.

How to run him

He plays to win every time and leaves when his objective is met, whatever his wounds. Damage never beats him. What beats him is an interrupted taking, an extracted target, a broken meeting or a collapsed descent. Downed PCs are taken prisoner, because bodies are valuable. The lesson lands the first time: they fought well, they lost, and he did not care about them at all.

The soft spot is the lever. A crew that finds an outcast his taken have raised up has found the one thing he spends on for nothing, and a crew that understands why can bargain with it, use it, or turn it against him.

If the crew boards Vth'oramu, run it as a civilization, with families, children, trades and streets, and people who never chose his war. Any plan to destroy the ship or end him weighs that cost, and the table should feel it.

He has one stat block, built at the ceiling and never scaled down. It needs an exit condition tied to his objective, non-lethal takedowns, and enough action economy to face six PCs without being stun-locked. The CRC's Withdrawal Threshold and Legendary Resistance are the working precedents.

His arc

He is the main villain of the campaign, and stopping his invasion takes most of it. He works on all four parts of his plan now: planting the taken in key positions, finding hearts, opening them and seeking links. By the crew's graduation his landed operations ripple outward at contract scale, and those are the first things the crew meets as Driftwardens. Defeating him does not end Vecna's threat, and a victory over him is never undone by fiat. His lane is on Opposition: the Vth'orama and Opposition: Vecna.

Threads

  • He is made of joins, and Dissolution unmakes joins. The fragment forming in Tumak is a counter to him, and nobody holding it knows.
  • The mouse was aboard the craft with the seventh piece for as long as he was. Following the mouse is one of three roads to Thessalar's wreck.
  • The true-formed keep the ideal Glyth exiled his people for, in a colony that is mostly malformed. A crew that understands the colony's history can press on that wound.
  • A taken Watcher is a goal he could set himself, through what he knows of the Watchers posted to Glyth. None exists.

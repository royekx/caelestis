N'ghathrod
A mind flayer who wants a home for his people, and has made himself into the ship that carries them toward it.

N'ghathrod is a mind flayer, once a spacefaring elf, and the founder of the Vth'orama, an illithid colony looking for a world to be its home. About five hundred years ago Thessalar experimented on him with the seventh piece of the Rod of Seven Parts, and since then his tadpoles make copies of him: the taking. He is fused into Vth'oramu, the astral dreadnought his people live in. He wants a home for them, and everything he does serves that want.

Appearance

His true body lies joined to the dreadnought's nervous system inside the hull, and the tadpole pools bloom out of the hull around him. A mouse travels with him.

Personality

He is patient on the scale of centuries and plays to win every time. His people are his blood, the unthinned most of all, and he left his own world to stay with them. He thinks of the taking as a gift: a taken person keeps who they were, gains the colony bond, and belongs. What he does to others is him giving them what he was given. Captives are material to him, and he cares nothing for anyone who stands against him.

Biography

Background

He underwent ceremorphosis on Glyth long before the rest of this, which destroyed the spacefaring elf he had been, and he lived an illithid life afterward. About five hundred years ago his ship clashed with Thessalar's planar craft and lost. Thessalar and the Thessalkraken captured him and some of his illithids, and Thessalar experimented on him aboard with the seventh Rod piece, keyed to simulacrum. It changed his reproduction: his tadpoles stopped being offspring and became copies of him. The experiments also changed the five who became his lieutenants, two properly raised illithids captured with him and three unthinned made aboard from bodies Thessalar supplied and N'ghathrod's own tadpoles. All six gained a greatly extended lifespan. They are not immortal. Thessalar took the finding and turned it on himself.

When Lola's Boys took the craft's control and defence gems in the year 425 and it fell toward Fonains, he used the fall to escape with his five lieutenants, a few lesser illithids and a mouse he saved. He never reached Fonains. His elder brain was separated from him in the crash; he believed it dead, or could not reach it. It lay trapped beneath the Barrier Peaks for about five hundred years, until it broke free and teleported away.

The journey back to Glyth, a system away, took decades. Glyth rejected them and exiled the unthinned, and N'ghathrod went with them, because they are his blood. That was roughly four to four and a half centuries ago. Over the centuries that followed he searched for a home for his people, learned the full extent of his ability to take, and found that he and his lieutenants were still alive long after they expected to die. In those centuries a voice answered him, and the long plan formed.

He took an astral dreadnought that bore no wound, because he knew a dreadnought's physiology and saw his chance. The siege lasted a long time, controlling the creature took a great many tadpoles, and taking it and turning it into Vth'oramu took decades, perhaps a century. He went into it himself and has been joined to it since its taking. Generations of the colony were born aboard, and many died in the Astral Sea. The active push to find and open hearts began only in the past few decades.

In the campaign

The crew has not met him or heard his name. His work is all around them: taken people in small positions, odd disappearances, a station or crew acting strangely.

Relationships

The Vth'orama

His colony, the Seekers of a New Home, and his blood. They believe they answer to an elder brain through him. Most of them live inside him and think the ship is a ship. The colony's mark is an eye inside a wheel, the wheel for the way home, its lower spokes trailing like an illithid's tentacles. His field mark, the Passage, is a circle with a tail ending in another circle, and it gains a circle for every linked world.

The enna

His five lieutenants, each carrying a Hold, a portion of him offered and permanent: Osskhal, the Flesh; Ulh'rek, the Arcane; Zhauveth, the Voice; Ith'varn, the Sight; and Khaleth, the Surveyor. He speaks through them at will and does not drive them.

Thessalar

His captor, who made him what he is and learned from him how to copy himself.

Glyth

The world that refused his people. He has outlived those who exiled the unthinned and has never forgiven the refusal. He knows the Order's Watchers exist, through the Watchers posted to Glyth.

Abilities

He takes. One of his tadpoles, implanted in a host, does not consume it: the host persists, a copy of him sits alongside, and the taken person gives him their allegiance while keeping their memories, skills and relationships. A portion of every taken person passes upward through the rungs of the taken and ends with him, a tithe that extends his life; the more he takes, the longer he lasts. When a taken person dies he receives what it knew. He can assert himself over an ordinary taken person, and through the enna he speaks.

His join with Vth'oramu is permanent. Only a wish or an artifact could break it. The colony carries one residue piece from world to world to open hearts. Any heart can be opened; a link needs a Monolith on both worlds, so the colony goes where Monoliths stand.

Notable items

  • The colony's residue piece. The one piece the colony carries to open hearts; without it he must win it back or find another.

What's hidden

  • The voice is Vecna. N'ghathrod believes the voice that answered him is his elder brain grown into something greater. It is Vecna, who feeds the colony the few secrets it needs; a whisper from Vecna told him a dreadnought was the best way to keep a colony on its passage home. Every heart he opens and links serves Vecna's eye inscription, a figure he has never seen.
  • Three bodies. Three bodies answer to his name, and only one matters (table below). Two of the enna know; the others suspect and have never asked.
  • The taken outlive him. If his true body dies, the taken do not die. Part of him lives on in each of them, and they lose a part of themselves. His lieutenants are closest to him, each rung down the colony further away, and given time one of them could grow toward becoming another N'ghathrod.
  • The experiment. The colony has no idea that every taken person exists because of the seventh Rod piece.

How to run him

He plays to win every time and leaves when his objective is met, whatever his wounds. Damage never beats him. What beats him is an interrupted taking, an extracted target, a broken meeting or a collapsed descent. Downed PCs are taken prisoner, because bodies are valuable. The lesson lands the first time: they fought well, they lost, and he did not care about them at all.

He has one stat block, built at the ceiling and never scaled down. It needs an exit condition tied to his objective, non-lethal takedowns, and enough action economy to face six PCs without being stun-locked. The CRC's Withdrawal Threshold and Legendary Resistance are the working precedents.

His arc

He is the main villain of the campaign, and stopping his invasion takes most of it. He works on all four parts of his plan now: planting the taken in key positions, finding hearts, opening them and seeking links. By the crew's graduation his landed operations ripple outward at contract scale, and those are the first things the crew meets as Driftwardens. Defeating him does not end Vecna's threat, and a victory over him is never undone by fiat. His lane is on Opposition: the Vth'orama and Opposition: Vecna.

Threads

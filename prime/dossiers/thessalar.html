Thessalar
He experimented on N’ghathrod first, learned what the seventh piece did, and then used it on himself.

He captured N'ghathrod aboard the planar craft and experimented on him there, in the presence of the Rod's seventh piece. The colony, the taken, and every heart being opened across the cosmos run out of that room.

The order is the whole story He did the experimenting first, on N'ghathrod and on whatever else came to hand, while there was still only one of him. He learned something from it. Then he made copies of himself.

The seventh piece is keyed to simulacrum. What it did to N'ghathrod was turn his offspring into copies of himself. The man standing beside that result walked away knowing how to do the same thing to his own body — and there are now eight numbered proofs that he got it working.

N'ghathrod was the prototype for Halaster's immortality. Neither of them has ever said so, because N'ghathrod experiences his own making as having been healed.

The original is the one still at the bench The copies exist to be copies. The Thessalar who holds the seventh piece and runs the experiments is the first one, which removes the usual problem with a duplicated villain: there is a real one, he can be found, and killing a numbered copy settles nothing.

The planar craft is enormous and layered, and the Temple of Moloch is one of its layers. Beneath the temple sits his laboratory — pipes, steam, test tubes, a long workbench. The people living on that layer call him a scholar.

When the craft came down, it became the dungeon The wreck is the Mad Mage's dungeon, and the layers came down with it. It has been collecting things the entire time.

That resolves what the Rod register has been carrying as an open question. The seventh piece was aboard at the time of the taking, the craft is now a dungeon with a known master, and the master is the man who held the piece. It is reachable, it is defended, and it is full of everything else he has gathered since.

A Fonains party reached the temple by travelling into the past — sent there by the gods of the Accordance, who had their own reasons. They moved through the laboratory, took what they came for, and left. He was elsewhere in the craft while they did it.

The present has forgotten him In the Fonains present the name lands on nothing. A party member with every reason to recognise it, rolling well, had never heard it. He is forgotten rather than hidden, which is better cover than anything he could have arranged.

Unestablished, and worth leaving thin until he is needed. What the record supports is narrow and damning enough: he experimented on a captive intelligent being for long enough to produce a permanent change, took the finding, and applied it to his own survival.

He is the second man on record to build something world-altering out of a Rod piece without understanding what the piece was. Ostekk is the first. Neither of them is a cosmologist; both of them are competent, and that is what makes residue dangerous.

The distinction worth keeping Ostekk built a device and got a result he did not want. Thessalar got a result and then went looking for what else it could do. The difference is curiosity rather than cruelty, which is why he reads as a scholar to the people who live near him.
What he did, and what he owes for it What the seventh piece changed in N'ghathrod was his reproduction: his tadpoles stopped being offspring and became copies of himself.

Every n'ghathu in the cosmos exists because of that exposure. The colony has never worked it out. N'ghathrod has never worked it out either. Thessalar is the one person who does know — he took the lesson and built his own continuation out of it.

Something was watching the work: the Elder Eye A Fonains player character spent that excursion in the ethereal plane, unseen and unheard, and saw one large eye with a purple slit iris pass over the party and vanish. The material side registered nothing.

That is the Elder Eye — Tharizdun reaching into Fonains for his own purposes, as he did with the Zenith and the Monolith that answered it. He was present at the work on N'ghathrod, in some sense, and a piece of his handiwork is already aboard the crew's own ship: Boogie's shard.

Thessalar is unlikely to know he was watched. The interesting question is whether Tharizdun took an interest in the experiment, in the piece, or in the man — and whether the answer explains why a corrupted Monolith shard and a Rod piece have both ended up inside this crew's story.

He is one of three live leads on the Rod, alongside Ostekk's fourth and Xanathar's fifth — and the only one whose location is now a place with a door.

He is a destination rather than an antagonist, and the destination is a dungeon. The crew can reach him by following the Rod, by following the colony's origin, or by following the mouse, and all three arrive at the same wreck.

Why he is worth keeping alive He is the only person who could tell the crew what the colony is made of and why, with none of the colony's own beliefs attached. N'ghathrod experiences his own making as having been healed. Thessalar would describe it as a result, and then ask what they wanted to know next.
A dungeon is a different kind of arrival Reaching him means descending through layers that came down with the craft and have been filling up ever since. That turns the Rod's seventh piece into a site rather than a scene, and it gives the campaign somewhere to put everything collected there over the years.

Open: how many layers are worth mapping, which of the eight copies are loose outside the wreck, and whether he kept records of the work on N'ghathrod.

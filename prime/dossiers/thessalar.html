Thessalar
Held N'ghathrod aboard a planar craft and worked on him beside a piece of the Rod.

He captured N'ghathrod aboard a planar craft and experimented on him there, in the presence of the Rod's seventh piece. Everything that followed — the colony, the taken, the hearts being opened across the cosmos — runs out of that room.

The most consequential person in the campaign's history He is a footnote in his own story and the cause of the entire Act II antagonist. He did not create a colony, a plan, or a villain on purpose. He put a mind flayer in a room with a piece of residue and kept him there.

The craft went down. N'ghathrod escaped with what tadpoles and people he could carry, and with a mouse. Thessalar's own fate is unrecorded.

A Fonains party travelled into the past and walked through his laboratory. It sat beneath the Temple of Moloch — pipes, steam, test tubes, a long workbench — and the people who live there called him a scholar. He was absent while they were in it. They took what they came for and left by the way they entered.

That last line is the whole man He found something out of another cosmology, wired it into the wall, and ran his experiments off it. That is exactly what he did with the Rod's seventh piece and N'ghathrod. The method is consistent across two tables and an unknown stretch of time: acquire a power he has no theory for, and put a living subject on the other end of it.
The present has forgotten him In the Fonains present the name lands on nothing. A party member with every reason to recognise it, rolling well, had never heard it. He is forgotten rather than hidden, which is better cover than anything he could have arranged.
One discrepancy to settle The Caelestis record puts his work on N'ghathrod aboard a planar craft. The transcript puts his laboratory under the Temple of Moloch. Both can stand — a man with eight numbered copies can hold two addresses, and the temple lab is plainly the older of them. Decide whether the craft and the temple are two sites of one operation, or whether the craft was always the temple and the detail drifted. Needs your call

Unestablished, and worth leaving thin until he is needed. What the record supports is narrow: he experimented on a captive intelligent being for long enough to produce a permanent change, and he did it beside an object he had no apparent understanding of.

He is the second person in the campaign to build something world-altering out of a Rod piece without knowing what the piece was. Ostekk is the first. Neither of them is a cosmologist; both of them are competent, and that is what makes residue dangerous.

What he actually did, which he does not know The seventh piece is keyed to simulacrum. What it changed in N'ghathrod was his reproduction: his tadpoles stopped being offspring and became copies of himself.

Every n'ghathu in the cosmos exists because of that exposure. The colony has never worked that out. N'ghathrod has never worked it out either. And Thessalar has no idea he is the reason any of them are alive.

The simulacra are confirmed, and there are eight of them A Fonains player character spent that excursion in the ethereal plane, unseen and unheard, and wandered into a room the rest of the party walked past. Inside were eight tubes with humanoids suspended in them, each labelled with his name and a number.

That settles what he is. He was using the seventh piece rather than standing beside it, and what he did to N'ghathrod's reproduction stops reading as an accident. A man growing numbered copies of himself was reaching for precisely that result in something else, and a mind flayer is the obvious subject: a creature whose offspring are already a resource.

It opens a better question than his location. Which Thessalar held N'ghathrod. The original would be a cosmology-level villain with one great crime. A fourth or a sixth, running the same protocol in parallel with seven others, is worse — it makes the colony a by-product of a production line, and it means seven more results exist somewhere.

Something was watching him, too In the same excursion, from the ethereal plane, the same witness saw one large eye with a purple slit iris pass over the party and vanish. The material side registered nothing.

A beholder-shaped observer, present across both the planar distance and the centuries, sits one short step from the Watchers and a shorter one from the rogues among them. Left unassigned deliberately. When the crew asks who else knew what Thessalar was doing, this is the answer waiting to be given a name. Unassigned

The piece may still be where he left it. Whether it is still aboard, and whether he still holds it, is unestablished — which makes him one of three live leads on the Rod, alongside Ostekk's fourth and Xanathar's fifth.

He is a destination rather than an antagonist, at least to begin with. The crew can reach him by following the Rod, by following the colony's origin, or by following the mouse — and all three arrive at the same room.

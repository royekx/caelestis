Boatswain Tarto
Boatswain and training officer, Caelestis

Runs the Simulation Deck. Clipped manner, tracks cadet performance closely, and assigns to scenarios without sentiment. Once counted among the academy's great spelljammers.

Scarred, grey at the temples, wreathed in cigar smoke. One wing flap was torn away long ago; she has not glided since and has never needed to.

"Listen to what I tell you; it might just save your life."

One of the two instructors the crew can choose to bring to H'Catha. She arrives as authority — their own chain of command, so their decisions get supervised. The comparison.

The neogi crate She passed on the crate holding the neogi hatchlings because she was informed rather than having checked it herself. Guilt without negligence, which is the version that keeps her sympathetic and still answerable.

Traits

Superiority Dice (4d10).
Tarto has four superiority dice. When she hits with an attack she can spend one to add 1d10 damage and apply one maneuver — at most one maneuver per attack, so a committed round spends up to three. Maneuver save DC 16. She regains all expended dice on a short or long rest.
Indomitable (2/Day).
If Tarto fails a saving throw, she can reroll it and must use the new roll.

Actions

Multiattack.
Three attacks, Cutlass or Belaying Pin in any combination.
Cutlass.
Melee Attack Roll: +8, reach 5 ft. Hit: 13 (2d8 + 4) slashing.
Belaying Pin.
Melee or Ranged Attack Roll: +8, reach 5 ft. or range 20/60 ft. Hit: 8 (1d8 + 4) bludgeoning.

Bonus Actions

Clear the Deck (Recharge 5–6).
Tarto moves her full Speed without provoking opportunity attacks, making one Cutlass attack against every creature she passes. She can spend a superiority die on each of those attacks as normal. This costs her no part of her Attack action, so a recharge round is three attacks plus everything she crosses.
Commander's Strike.
An ally within 60 ft. who can hear her uses its Reaction to make one attack.
"There!"
She calls a weak point. The next ally attack against that creature before her next turn has Advantage.
"Eyes on Me!"
A creature within 30 ft. makes a DC 16 Wisdom save. On a failure it has Disadvantage attacking anyone but Tarto until the end of her next turn.

Reactions

Take It.
Trigger: an ally within 5 ft. is targeted. Response: she swaps places and becomes the target.
Parry.
Trigger: melee damage. Response: reduce it by 9 (1d10 + 4).

Cut during design, deliberately: Deck Ape (redundant with her climb speed), Silhouette Deck (became "There!"), and the "Hold!" temporary-hit-point order. Her maneuver list lives in the working file. Not recorded here

Revised — what changed and why The block above is the revision. She was failing to read as a warrior for two reasons, and both are now fixed.
WasNowWhy
Maneuvers on every hit, free, with no damage attached. Superiority dice, 4d10, short rest. The die adds damage and fuels the maneuver together. Removing the dice had removed the decision and the spike at once. Three saves a turn forever is bookkeeping; four dice she can burn in two rounds is a choice. A committed round goes from about 39 damage to about 55, which is what a CR 10 veteran should manage when she means it — and running dry now means something.
Clear the Deck as an action, replacing Multiattack. A bonus action. Same movement, same attack on everyone she passes, and her Attack action is untouched. As an action it was a worse Multiattack against one target — her signature button went dead in exactly the fights meant to feel biggest. It is never a downgrade now, and it keeps the thing she is actually for: crossing a deck under fire.
One problem left standing on purpose Commander's Strike and her maneuvers both spend an ally's reaction, so the same cadet cannot take both, and at a six-PC table the routing congests rather than scales.

Left alone deliberately. It is a constraint on her rather than a flaw in her — she can only lend her tactics to one person at a time, which is a fair thing for a boatswain to be limited by, and it gives the table a reason to spread her attention around. Revisit only if it slows a turn in play.

Against Saerthe The revision widens the gap on purpose. Tarto is now the pick that makes the crew hit harder and survive a bad opening; Saerthe is the pick that changes what the crew can do. The choice should feel like trading capability for muscle rather than picking the better statblock. The comparison

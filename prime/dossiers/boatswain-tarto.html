Boatswain Tarto
Boatswain and training officer, Caelestis

Runs the Simulation Deck. Clipped manner, tracks cadet performance closely, and assigns to scenarios without sentiment. Once counted among the academy's great spelljammers.

Scarred, grey at the temples, wreathed in cigar smoke. Worn leathers, a cutlass on one hip and on the other a belaying pin of dark wood banded in tarnished brass, something carved deep into its grain.

One wing flap was torn away long ago; she has not glided since and has never needed to. She crosses a mooring line hand over hand the way only a hadozee can, and arrives unwinded.

"Listen to what I tell you; it might just save your life."

One of the two instructors the crew can choose to bring to H'Catha. She arrives as authority — their own chain of command, so their decisions get supervised. The comparison.

Cadets come home. Everything else she does is downstream of that. She drills them hard because the alternative is burying them, and she tells them so.

She wants the name of whoever routed the neogi crate through her. That is a chain of custody, a manifest and a signature, and a boatswain can pull all three without anyone thinking twice about it.

She does not want command. She had the helm once and gave it up, and she has never explained which way round that went.

She draws. Charts, boarding plans, the shape of a room nobody has walked yet, scrawled on a bulkhead and gone by the next watch. It is the only thing she does that looks like patience.

The neogi crate She passed on the crate holding the neogi hatchlings because she was informed rather than having checked it herself. Guilt without negligence, which is the version that keeps her sympathetic and still answerable.

She fights through the crew. Commander's Strike, Maneuvering Attack and Bait and Switch all put the payoff in a cadet's hands, so let them roll the big numbers. Goading pulls danger onto her and off them.

Five dice is the whole fight. Three can go in one round, so she is empty on the third if she opens hard. That is the decision the block is built around — running dry in front of cadets is a scene worth having, and it is why she picks her moment instead of swinging freely.

A spent round is about 55 damage. An empty one is about 39.

The ally-reaction bottleneck Commander's Strike, Maneuvering Attack and Bait and Switch all spend an ally's reaction, so the same cadet cannot take two in a round, and at a six-PC table that congests rather than scales. It is a constraint on her rather than a flaw in her — she lends her tactics to one person at a time, and the table has a reason to spread her attention around. Revisit in play
Against Saerthe Tarto is the pick that makes the crew hit harder and survive a bad opening; Saerthe is the pick that changes what the crew can do. The choice should read as trading capability for muscle rather than picking the better block. The comparison

On a deck, Pushing Attack is the kill. Fifteen feet and a missing section of rail ends a boarder without a second attack, and it teaches the crew to look at the terrain.

Three cheap things Saerthe builds fixed points out of nothing. She owns exactly one, and it is a stick. That is the difference between them in a single object — and she would rather have three uncommon things that work together than one rare thing that works alone.

The pin and the line are a rescue rig. Throw the pin past a falling cadet, press it, and send the rope after them. Going over the rail is the death a Wildspace fight actually threatens, and she assembled the answer to it out of a stick and a rope.

Apart they still earn their place. The pin is a rail where the rail is gone, a handhold when gravity cuts out, a jammed hatch, and a stop to shove a boarder into — Pushing Attack into a set pin is the combination only she has, and it rewards arranging the board before swinging. The line rigs between two hulls with no crew.

The ring is the one that is only hers, and it is the cheapest thing she owns. She thinks on surfaces. Hand her a bulkhead and she will draw the deck plan, the boarding order, or the route out, and it will be gone by the next watch — which is also how she leaves a trail nobody can follow a day later.

Traits

Combat Superiority.
Tarto has five superiority dice (d10), regained on a short or long rest. She spends one to use a maneuver, adding the die to that attack's damage. One maneuver per attack, so a committed round spends three. Maneuver save DC 16.
Indomitable (2/Day).
If Tarto fails a saving throw, she can reroll it and must use the new roll.

Actions

Multiattack.
Three attacks, Cutlass or Belaying Pin in any combination. She can replace one with Commander's Strike.
Cutlass.
Melee Attack Roll: +8, reach 5 ft. Hit: 13 (2d8 + 4) slashing.
Belaying Pin.
Melee or Ranged Attack Roll: +8, reach 5 ft. or range 20/60 ft. Hit: 8 (1d8 + 4) bludgeoning. Dark wood banded in tarnished brass, a ship's name carved deep into the grain.
The Pin (immovable rod, uncommon).
Pressed, it holds where she leaves it — in open air, against nothing — bearing up to 8,000 pounds, and takes a DC 30 Strength check to shift.
The Line (rope of climbing, uncommon).
Sixty feet, spliced to the pin's eye when she means to use them together. It answers as a bonus action — uncoils, knots itself, or fastens to a creature or object within 20 feet — bears 3,000 pounds, has AC 20 and 20 hit points, and coils itself back on command.
The Ring (doodling ring, common).
A wooden band, always splotched with charcoal, ink or paint however hard it is scrubbed. She touches a finger to any solid surface and draws in any colour. Marks smudge or wash off clean, cover up to 5 square feet at a time, and vanish on their own after 24 hours.
Band of the Lost and Found (common).
One of a matched pair of leather bracelets. Each compass points at the other while both are worn, giving heading but no height or depth, and spins uselessly if the two are on different planes. Saerthe wears the other. They have worn them since Clint Dashforest was lost.

Maneuvers

Commander's Strike.
Replaces one of her attacks. A cadet who can see or hear her uses its Reaction to attack, adding the die to the damage.
Maneuvering Attack.
On a hit, a cadet uses its Reaction to move half its Speed without provoking an opportunity attack from her target.
Goading Attack.
On a hit, Wisdom save or the target has Disadvantage attacking anyone but Tarto until the end of her next turn.
Pushing Attack.
On a hit, Strength save or pushed up to 15 ft. away.
Trip Attack.
On a hit, Strength save or Prone.
Bait and Switch.
On her turn, within 5 ft. of a willing creature: they swap places without provoking. One of them gains a bonus to AC equal to the die until her next turn.
Parry.
Reaction to melee damage. Reduce it by the die + 4.

Bonus Actions

Know Your Enemy (1/Day).
She reads a creature within 30 ft. and knows its Immunities, Resistances and Vulnerabilities. She can restore this by spending a superiority die, no action required. This is the silhouette deck, and it is why she made the crew memorize it.

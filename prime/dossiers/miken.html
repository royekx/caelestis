Miken Haverstance
A cadet, a friend, and a leveraged asset who is wrestling with it.

Academy cadet, sociable and willing, relatively new to the cohort. Eager, slightly awkward, easy to like. Engaged to a young fiance off-station whose debts have entangled him in difficulties well beyond a cadet's stipend to resolve.

The crew lifted him at lunch, saved his simulation crew, and made him one of their own. He is a friend, and the module's version of him as a concealed antagonist has been discarded: he acts no differently from any other recruit because there is no act.

He is not going to die Settled. Whatever he does and whenever it surfaces, Miken lives — which is what makes deferring his resolution worth anything.

He believes he can keep both — the crew and the fiance — and that the thing he was asked to do was small enough for that to be true. A beacon is not a knife. He has worked hard to make that distinction load-bearing.

He holds that the debt is his to settle and that telling anyone would make it theirs. Which is the mechanism of every leverage that has ever worked: he is protecting them from being involved.

He is wrestling with guilt rather than concealing malice, and the guilt is the part the crew can reach.

The beacon Recruited by a mercane and paid for his fiance's debts. What he was asked for was a beacon, planted aboard the ship, so that Qitru could follow them. That is the whole of the sabotage.

The larger version — a glyph on the galley stove, magma mephits, a dead cook — was cut as too large an event for a hull that small. It never happened and never will.

What he reports, and what it costs the crew He makes his report in the coming session, and he tells them about the beholder — that the crew killed one, or had a hand in it. He believes it, because Tumak joked about it on a ladder and he took it at something near face value.

Qitru carries that to H'Catha ahead of them. And when the graduation assault comes, Vocath's people have been told this crew kills beholders, so the crew stops being something to work around and becomes a problem worth handling directly.

How the crew treats him sets the difficulty of two later arcs, since it decides how good Qitru's intelligence is. The galley and the consequence sit far enough apart that neither looks like the other's cause.

Give him no shadow. Every friendly beat is honest, which is what makes the eventual reveal land as a friend's betrayal rather than a trick the DM was running.

If a PC cannot attune the helm and he volunteers, let it read as loyal eagerness. In hindsight it is access. Do not lean on it.

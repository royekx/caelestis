Miken Haverstance
A cadet, a friend, and a leveraged asset who is wrestling with it.

Academy cadet, sociable and willing, relatively new to the cohort. Eager, slightly awkward, easy to like. Engaged to a young fiance off-station whose debts have entangled him in difficulties well beyond a cadet's stipend to resolve.

The crew lifted him at lunch, saved his simulation crew, and made him one of their own. He is a friend, and he acts no differently from any other recruit because there is no act.

He believes he can keep both — the crew and the fiance — and that the thing he was asked to do was small enough for that to be true. A beacon is not a knife. He has worked hard to make that distinction load-bearing.

He holds that the debt is his to settle and that telling anyone would make it theirs. Which is the mechanism of every leverage that has ever worked: he is protecting them from being involved.

He is wrestling with guilt rather than concealing malice, and the guilt is the part the crew can reach.

The beacon

Recruited by a mercane and paid for his fiance's debts. What he was asked for was a beacon, planted aboard the ship, so that Qitru could follow them. That is the whole of the sabotage.

What he reports

He made his report in V006, through the sending stone on the first watch, and he told them about the beholder — that the crew killed one, or had a hand in it. He believes it, because Tumak joked about it on a ladder and he took it at something near face value. Bartholomew overheard the call, and Miken covered it as saying hello to his family.

Qitru carries that to H'Catha ahead of them. And when the graduation assault comes, Vocath's people have been told this crew kills beholders, so the crew stops being something to work around and becomes a problem worth handling directly.

Give him no shadow. Every friendly beat is honest, which is what makes the eventual reveal land as a friend's betrayal rather than a trick the DM was running.

If a PC cannot attune the helm and he volunteers, let it read as loyal eagerness. In hindsight it is access. Do not lean on it.

He is not going to die

Settled. Whatever he does and whenever it surfaces, Miken lives — which is what makes deferring his resolution worth anything.

What his handling costs

How the crew treats him sets the difficulty of two later arcs, since it decides how good Qitru's intelligence is. The sending stone and the consequence sit far enough apart that neither looks like the other's cause.

Why deferring him is worth it

If he redeems himself — tells them more, helps them assemble the mystery, comes around — and is then captured or nearly killed during the graduation raid, the crew's investment pays off as genuine loss. The inverse works too. Either way the deferral is what makes it land harder than a one-and-done reveal.

Open: the crew now suspects him and has the stone on record. Whether they confront him, watch him, or let it ride is theirs. What he does if they work out the beacon before he confesses it is still the call to make.

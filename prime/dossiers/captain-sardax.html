Captain Sardax
Captain of the Moonraider, whose last, lost fight the Academy runs every new cadet through.

Captain Sardax commanded the Moonraider, a Caelestis ship lost with her to astral reavers. She died on her own top deck, ordering her crew to hold and give no quarter in a battle already lost, and the ship went down with her. The Academy keeps the battle as a simulation and runs cadets through it on their first day.

Appearance

As the simulation shows her: bleeding across the deck of the Moonraider, in a captain's coat, giving orders to the end.

Personality

“Hold the deck, give no quarter.” Read generously it is a captain refusing to let her crew's last minutes be a rout. Read plainly it is the order that killed her. The Academy teaches her as courage first and judgement second, which is a choice about what kind of officers it wants.

Biography

Background

Sardax captained the Moonraider for Caelestis until astral reavers took the ship. The Academy preserved her last fight as a simulation detailed enough to render the deck, her wound and her dying order, and it uses the simulation as a lesson about retreat.

In the campaign
  • Voyage 1. The crew's first day began inside the simulation. Sardax appeared bleeding across the Moonraider's deck, ordering the cadets to hold the top deck and give no quarter. Tarto then revealed the battle was a simulation of her doomed final stand, never meant to be won.

Relationships

How to run her

She is a fixed point, not an NPC. She cannot be met, talked to or saved, which is what makes her useful: she is the one piece of history every player character experienced identically. Beginning inside a battle that could not be won set the register in one scene: courage is assumed here, and the interesting question is knowing when to withdraw. The crew has since withdrawn on purpose more than once and counted it competence; that reflex is Sardax's, learned by failing to save her. The Academy has never corrected them.

Threads

  • A simulation that detailed needs a source. Either someone survived to describe it, or something watched the Moonraider die closely enough to render it: a survivor still carrying it, or the Watchers, or the Academy's own instruments.

The Runekeeper
Holds the ritual that hides a world in his own body, and dims as the fragment leaves him.

The Runekeeper is the head of the Runekeepers, the hidden order of the Wayfinders' homeworld. The title is held by one living practitioner at a time, whose body bears the inscription that holds the world's concealment. The man who holds it now is dimming: the Dissolution fragment is leaving him and forming in Wan and Tu, the twins who study at Caelestis as Tumak. He is the only permitted way into and out of the homeworld.

Appearance

The inscription that hides his world is written on his skin. The fragment is visible in him: it forms and re-forms in his chest, never still, and as it leaves him the loss shows.

Personality

He holds the order's withholding as doctrine, not cruelty. Wayfinders go out with no map and no name for home, and a returning exile must recognise it through remembered fragments. That is deliberate, and he would do it again. He believes the cost of the office is his to carry, and that dimming is what the office is. He knows only that the world's protection must continue.

Biography

Background

He took the title from the Runekeeper before him and the inscription with it. The world hides itself by a living ritual: his ritual points the working, and the oscillation of the Dissolution fragment powers it. The world sits where it has always sat, and finding it means knowing the way back, not knowing coordinates.

When the fragment began forming in Wan and Tu, he sent them out himself. Leaving the homeworld is rare and allowed only for set reasons; the Becoming is one of them, and every leaving passes through him.

A Runekeeper's death is celebrated, not mourned. “We do not mourn this. We sing.”

In the campaign
  • Voyage 4. As Tumak's first soul-knife throw went wide, a quiet voice said that all things find their way home, and the blade bent back to strike true. Tumak felt a tether running toward home, a connection and not a place.

Relationships

Wan and Tu

The two Lunarfoot Wayfinders he sent out, in whom the fragment is rebuilding in two halves at once. He spoke to Tumak once, directly.

The Runekeepers

The order he leads. It is competent, and its doctrine is sound.

Abilities

He carries the ritual that withdraws a world from cosmological sight, and he holds it in his body, not in a place. He is a complete Wayfinder, able to attune to a celestial body by ritual and time, and his voice can reach a Wayfinder of his order across the cosmos.

What's hidden

  • The fragment. The concealment rests on an object nobody in the order has identified as residue. Its pieces cycle between whole and broken across Runekeepers. In Tumak it is rebuilding in two halves at once, a state neither whole nor broken.
  • The concealment in transit. While the fragment moves between bearers, the concealment rests on the share still leaving him. It weakens as he fades, and the world's protection decays as the cosmos changes around it. The marks Tumak places are the sensors that let it be renewed when he returns.
  • Going home. Completing the inscription takes Tumak home, and going home ends the concealment. When Wan and Tu both arrive, the three poles stall the Dissolution cycle and the result is a Zenith: the homeworld splits into a blessing faction and an omen faction, and a Monolith follows. The office Tumak comes home to dims whoever holds it.
  • Tumak. Tumak has his own Heart-rune. He knows of the previous Runekeeper and met him once, briefly, and he does not understand what that meeting was. Keep this from the table until it is revealed.

How to run him

He is someone doing everything right with an incomplete cosmology. The order is competent, the doctrine is sound, and the thing they stand on is a piece of the Reach world that none of them can name. He spoke to Tumak at a dice-driven moment; the tether is a connection, not a location, and that is the line to hold.

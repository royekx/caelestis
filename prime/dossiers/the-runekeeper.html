The Runekeeper
Holds the ritual that hides a world, in his own body. Dimming as the fragment leaves him.

“The Runekeeper” is the title and the moniker, held by one living practitioner at a time. The man currently holding it has no name on record. Name open

He is the single living practitioner whose body bears the inscription that holds the homeworld's concealment. The world hides itself cosmically, and the ritual that does it lives in a person rather than in a place — which is the order's whole doctrine: a way home that lives only in a person cannot be taken off a chart.

He is dimming. The fragment is leaving him and forming in Wan and Tu, and the loss is visible.

The ritual proceeds regardless. He and the order both lack the word for what is happening. Neither understands it as a Zenith — they know only that it has happened this way once, now, for the first time, and that the world's protection must continue.

He holds the withholding as doctrine rather than cruelty. Wayfinders are sent out with no map and no name, and a returning exile has to recognise home through remembered fragments. That is deliberate, and he would do it again.

He believes the cost is his to carry. Dimming is what the office is.

What's hidden

What holds the concealment up

The ritual points the working; the Dissolution fragment's oscillation powers it. The world sits exactly where it has always sat, and finding it requires knowing the way back rather than knowing coordinates.

So the concealment depends on an object nobody in the order has identified as residue, and it is currently in transit between bearers.

Both twins are forming at once, which has never happened. Two poles were the pattern; three stalls the cycle mid-transfer. When Wan and Tu both arrive home, the three poles stall it and the result is a Zenith — the act of going home is the act of ending the concealment.

He spoke to Tumak directly, once: “all things find their way home.” The tether Tumak felt was a connection rather than a location.

Threads

Held in reserve, elsewhere N'ghathrod is made of joins, and Dissolution unmakes joins. The fragment inscribing itself onto Tumak is the counter to the Arc 2 antagonist, and nobody holding it knows that.

How to run them

He is the campaign's clearest case of someone doing everything right with incomplete cosmology. The order is competent, the doctrine is sound, and the thing they are standing on is a piece of the First World that none of them can name.

He spoke to Tumak at a dice-driven moment. The tether is a connection rather than a location, and that is the line to hold.

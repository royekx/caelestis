Ezra
A Watcher cadet, joining the crew to complete his field training.

A Watcher in training — a cadet, by his own order's reckoning, which is a useful thing for the crew to recognise in him. He is at the same stage of a different institution.

He joins at the crew swap, collected by the Flighty Foundling once the tyrant ship is reported taken, and travels on to H'Catha. He knows what he is going there for: to complete his field training under a certified Watcher already working that world.

So he arrives informed rather than mysterious. He can say who he is meeting and why, and he will — which makes him the first Watcher the crew can simply ask things of.

Why he exists in the plot The crew swap needed a reason, and his training is it. The reinforcement arrives as an arrangement rather than a rescue, so the crew keeps the credit for having handled the ship themselves.

Background

A young elf among the youngest batch of trainees in the Order of the All-Father, after roughly a century of it. Trainees are knighted into the Order and christened Watchers in the Hall of Sight and Sound, and then leave home for a posting. Postings are to planes rather than to worlds — the elemental planes, overlapping ones like the plane of gems, the Shadowfell.

He has done none of that yet. H'Catha is the field mission that stands between him and the ceremony, which makes the crew the last thing that happens to him as a cadet.

Captain-Commander Dejexic Leader of the Watchers and speaker of the All-Father. A purplish-blue plasmoid in obsidian armour with small galaxies swirling in its reflection, who floats, barely raises his voice, and silences a hall by entering it. His hand is amorphous and firm.

A named leadership figure the Order had been missing. He is who Ezra answers to, and who a crew would have to get past to complain about a Watcher — though at this point in the campaign Ezra has yet to be singled out by him. He addresses him, later, as “son of the All-Father, He who hears and sees all.”

He is also a plasmoid, which quietly puts the Order's leadership in the same species as Boogie.

Where the violet comes from Obsidian with galaxies in it, on the commander's armour. Ezra's own magic renders as cosmic dust and violet light. The register is the Order's, not his invention — so any Watcher the crew meets should read the same way, and Ezra looks like what he belongs to.

“Birds.” — later, and what he made of it This happens after the crew leaves him on H'Catha. At his knighting, Captain-Commander Dejexic — leader of the Watchers and speaker of the All-Father — put a hand on his shoulder and said, unprompted: “Birds are vigilant creatures. Some can even see in the dark and have a near 360 field of view. Watch the birds and they'll tell you all you need.”

It was advice about vigilance, offered as a maxim by a man who speaks in them. Ezra took it as a literal instruction and has been trying to carry it out ever since. His own silent reaction, as the ceremony began, was to wonder what the hells birds were supposed to be.

So this is a comic misunderstanding rather than a doctrine of withheld orders. It still tells you the register: the Order's leadership instructs in aphorism — “doubt is a choice, worry is a crutch” — and a junior who wants to do well will take the figurative literally. He will do the same with anything the crew says to him.

And then the birds answered He followed birds to his posting. A bird eventually spoke to him with someone else's voice, called his order “quite the pest,” and threw him into another world.

Dejexic told him the birds would tell him all he needed, and they did. That rhyme was an accident of two tables rather than a plant, which is the best kind. Whether the Captain-Commander knew what he was pointing at is worth leaving open — and it is the question Ezra would have to go home and ask.

How his Emporia arc ends, for continuity He guarded a nearly mute child the party was protecting, and buffed a ritual that read a cursed crown. Then a small black bird with grey feathers and very large eyes spoke to him with somebody else's voice. It called the party visitors and him “watchers — quite the pest.”

Its open mouth held four landscapes and pulled like gravity. The child stood frozen in time beside him. He braced, refused to leave her, and chose the water. He came up on the sand of an island called Hulila. His last act before going was to leave a figure of dancing light — himself, holding his shield in front of her — so she would know he had not walked away.

Two things there are worth keeping. Something crossing worlds already knows what a Watcher is and finds them a nuisance. And the birds had tried to take that child before, saying “I see you” as they were driven off.

The bird moved him across a world, rather than between them The four landscapes in its mouth were four places on the same world, and the island he washed up on is one of them. He is still where his assignment sent him, a long way from the child he was guarding, with no way back that he knows of.

So this is no parallel to Clint, who genuinely left his system. One man is lost on a world; the other is lost from one. Keeping them distinct matters, because Clint is a Caelestis problem and Ezra's displacement belongs to the other table.

He holds the Watchers' position sincerely and incompletely: that what happens to worlds is observed, recorded, and left alone. He has not yet had to apply it to anything he cares about.

He is the order's doctrine before it has been tested, which makes him the right person to voice it and the wrong person to defend it. A cadet quoting a rule he has never had to pay for is something this crew can recognise in itself.

He does more than tolerate the disaster. Part of him wants it. Waiting to be knighted, he worked through the postings he might draw and dreaded one above the rest: a world he had heard was in continuous strife. His private thought was that some extraplanar entity wiping the mortals out “would be a blessing in disguise.” He caught himself, decided it was too grim, and went straight back to idly picking a method — an extinction-level meteor, or a syzygy to raise a wave.

That reframes him completely. The doctrine of observing without intervening is not a burden he carries; it is permission he has already been glad of. He is a sincere, decent, helpful young man whose first instinct about a suffering world was that it might be better ended, and who has never said that aloud to anyone.

That is the thing to test at H'Catha, where the crew will want help and the doctrine will tell him to watch. The interesting failure is not that he follows the rule. It is that he might agree with it.

The meteor, reconciled He idly settles on an extinction-level meteor as his method, and he is sitting in a hall waiting to be knighted after his field training. So he is remembering H'Catha.

That resolves cleanly in-world. The musing is not foreshadowing — it is a young man who has just spent his training on a world where a meteor strike started a war between two beholders, reaching for the first image to hand while his mind wanders. The crew is the reason the thought is a meteor and not something else.

Which makes H'Catha load-bearing for his character rather than merely his first posting. Whatever the crew does there is what he will be thinking about at his own knighting, and the grimmer the outcome, the more the daydream costs him.

Very little, and that is deliberate. He is the open door into an order that has only ever shown the crew closed ones.

The other table, and what it is good for His Emporia sessions have landed and are mined on the History tab. They sit after H'Catha from his own point of view, so they are continuity rather than canon here, and nothing in them is knowledge he has aboard.

Use the confirmed half freely: the name, the order, the All-Father, the tower-and-eye device, the violet register, the kit. Keep the events as a destination. Mined

Open: what the Watchers actually want him to learn, how much of what the order knows about Zeniths has been passed to him yet, and whether he has been told anything about the beholder his instructor is living with.

Mirt and Saerthe let things slip; Ezra can be asked directly, and answers as far as he understands it, which is not far. He is the crew's first reachable source on the order.

Run him as the parallel thread that pulls a PC away at the wrong moment. His objectives at H'Catha are his order's rather than the crew's, and they will not always point the same way.

The useful tension His job is to watch a disaster and let it happen. On a world where two beholders are about to fight over a meteorite the crew has been sent to take, that doctrine gets tested in front of them — and he is junior enough that it might not hold.

His instructor is at the rim fog with an enlightened beholder exile, and she filed the report on the meteorite strike that started the dispute. Meeting her is his objective and the crew's shortcut at the same time.

He does not leave with them The crew leaves him on H'Catha. His training continues there after their business is finished, so the parting is administrative rather than dramatic — he stays because the work does.

That is worth staging properly. A cadet waving off the ship that got him there is a better goodbye than a casualty or a betrayal, and it leaves him somewhere the crew can find again. Whatever fell out of H'Catha falls out with him still standing in it.

The ferry — one last stint, later Once he is knighted and given Emporia, there is a contract in it for this crew: take him there and drop him off. A short, low-stakes job with a passenger they already know.

It earns its place three ways. It shows them an assignment being handed down, which is the Order working in front of them rather than described to them. It gives a returning NPC a proper exit instead of a silence. And it puts the crew on the doorstep of a world whose business they then decline to enter, which is a cleaner boundary than a coincidence — Emporia stays the other table's.

Pitch it as a favour or a routine transit rather than an adventure. The payload is the conversation on the way, with a young man who has just been told where the rest of his life happens and has opinions about it he will not quite say. Available after H'Catha

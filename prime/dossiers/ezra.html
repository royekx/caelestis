Ezra
A Watcher cadet, joining the crew to complete his field training.

A Watcher in training — a cadet, by his own order's reckoning, which is a useful thing for the crew to recognise in him. He is at the same stage of a different institution.

He joins at the crew swap, collected by the Flighty Foundling once the tyrant ship is reported taken, and travels on to H'Catha. He knows what he is going there for: to complete his field training under a certified Watcher already working that world.

So he arrives informed rather than mysterious. He can say who he is meeting and why, and he will — which makes him the first Watcher the crew can simply ask things of.

Why he exists in the plot The crew swap needed a reason, and his training is it. The reinforcement arrives as an arrangement rather than a rescue, so the crew keeps the credit for having handled the ship themselves.

He holds the Watchers' position sincerely and incompletely: that what happens to worlds is observed, recorded, and left alone. He has not yet had to apply it to anything he cares about.

He is the order's doctrine before it has been tested, which makes him the right person to voice it and the wrong person to defend it. A cadet quoting a rule he has never had to pay for is something this crew can recognise in itself.

Very little, and that is deliberate. He is the open door into an order that has only ever shown the crew closed ones.

Material inbound, from another table Ezra is played as a PC in another campaign, and transcripts of that are coming. They show where he ends up afterward, which makes them context and continuity rather than canon for this timeline.

Timing has to stay separated. What Ezra knows here is bounded by where he is in this campaign, and anything he learns at the other table happens later from his own point of view. Treat the inbound material as a destination the thread could be pulled toward, never as something he already carries aboard. Awaiting transcripts

Open: what the Watchers actually want him to learn, how much of what the order knows about Zeniths has been passed to him yet, and whether he has been told anything about the beholder his instructor is living with.

Mirt and Saerthe let things slip; Ezra can be asked directly, and answers as far as he understands it, which is not far. He is the crew's first reachable source on the order.

Run him as the parallel thread that pulls a PC away at the wrong moment. His objectives at H'Catha are his order's rather than the crew's, and they will not always point the same way.

The useful tension His job is to watch a disaster and let it happen. On a world where two beholders are about to fight over a meteorite the crew has been sent to take, that doctrine gets tested in front of them — and he is junior enough that it might not hold.

His instructor is at the rim fog with an enlightened beholder exile, and she filed the report on the meteorite strike that started the dispute. Meeting her is his objective and the crew's shortcut at the same time.

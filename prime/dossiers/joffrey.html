Joffrey
A decorated officer of the Caelestis Corps, top of his class, decorated for bringing the Crow's survivors home, and liked by everyone.

Joffrey is an officer of the Caelestis Corps, named the present-day great spelljammer of Caelestis. He was top of his class. When the Bellwether Crow was ambushed a year ago, he held the few survivors together and brought them home, and he was decorated on their unanimous account. Cadets know him and instructors trust him. He is not to be confused with Jeffrey, a Sky Dock hand.

Personality

Competent, well liked and entirely convincing. He frames the spelljamming life as duty, vigilance and survival, not riches. He thinks, speaks and works as he always did.

Biography

Background

He went out on the Crow to learn why Cairn Station had stopped answering, with Clint Dashforest aboard. Vocath's crew ambushed the Crow short of Cairn. Clint went overboard drawing some of the boarders off, and the crew fended off the rest. A second boarding, by a different party, killed the senior deck officers and most of the complement. The few survivors were let go, and Joffrey held them together, organised the withdrawal and brought them off alive. The Crow made Strake Station under damage. He was decorated, posted to Caelestis, and has risen since on merit. His record is clean and earned in every particular.

In the campaign
  • Voyage 1. Mr. Blip named him the present-day great spelljammer of Caelestis. Sol sought him out to ask what the life offered; he answered duty, vigilance and survival, not riches.
  • Voyage 2. Casey looked for him at the Sea Dock and the Sky Dock to ask about hadozee birth signs, and did not find him.
  • Voyage 3. Tumak told Mirt of a suspicious exchange the crew had seen Joffrey make at the Sky Dock. Mirt urged them to look into him cautiously, given his standing.

Abilities

A gifted spelljammer and a steady officer under fire. He knows Caelestis, its people and its routines from the inside.

What's hidden

  • Taken. The second boarding of the Crow was N'ghathrod's colony, not Vocath's crew. Working with Vocath under the arrangement Zhauveth had already made, it shadowed the ambush as a backup; once Clint was gone with the device, its task was to take someone instead. It took Joffrey, with one of N'ghathrod's tadpoles implanted with its directive already taught to it. He was taken slowly: for months after the Crow he was himself, decorated, and he finished converting only recently. He is not a puppet wearing a man; he is the man, pointed somewhere else.
  • The survivors. They were let live so that there would be a story with him as its hero, and they believe every word of it, because every word happened. One survivor of the Crow noticed that Joffrey had changed, and never reported it.
  • Two employers. He reports to Vocath as agreed, and his reports have never contained a lie. His allegiance is N'ghathrod's. Zhauveth arranged him as a conduit feeding the colony, and every report passes through Ith'varn too.
  • The Brig. The Brig's location and access protocols are classified well enough that Vocath could never have found the vault. Joffrey found it and told him it was there, so the strike happens because an asset named a target Vocath already wanted, and Vocath experiences the plan as his own.

How to run him

He shows that a taken person is not a disguise. Divination, interrogation and long acquaintance all return the same answer, because the answer is true. He carries a taken person's mind blank, so he cannot be read.

Catching him is a Fleet scandal before it is a victory. He is decorated, liked and provably heroic, so the institution's first instinct will be to disbelieve the crew, and the colony loses one view of one station while learning the crew can see it. How a taking reads is on Rules.

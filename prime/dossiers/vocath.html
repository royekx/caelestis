Vocath
A mercane merchant prosecuting a private grudge, and a tool of something he has never heard of.

A mercane merchant of long standing in the trade circles around Realmspace, in active commercial conflict with Mirt. The conflict is well known in the relevant community and predates every recent incident at Caelestis.

His aim is to disgrace Mirt enough to prise him loose from the school's protection, so that someone else can finish him. He is not a fighter and takes no part in the assault on Caelestis.

The eye in the wheel His own house mark, appearing on his goods wherever they move — which is why it turns up on the recovered rod, on the Sky Dock crate, and in the Bellwether Crow's last transmission. It is private, so the Fleet registry has no entry for it, and anyone who trades in the right circles reads it on sight.

He owns his grudge openly, and that is his best cover. A man loudly feuding with a headmaster is doing something everyone can see and nobody looks past.

He holds that a trade is a trade: that what he buys is what he gets, that an asset who takes his money answers to him, and that his manifests describe his cargo. Each of those is a belief rather than a fact.

The agent inside Caelestis answers to him.Joffrey is N'ghathrod's, and has never reported a word of a lie to either of them.
The illithid who dealt with him supplied a thrall.Zhauveth supplied a colony operative, and she arranged the deal rather than accepting it.

He proposed the target he was steered to The Brig's location and access protocols are classified well enough that he could never have found that vault. Joffrey found it and told him it was there. So the strike happens because an asset proposed a target the merchant already wanted to want — and Vocath experiences the entire plan as his own initiative.

Nothing he was told was false. He was simply never told the part he did not think to ask about, and a competent man asking good questions was all the arrangement ever needed.

Name him to the players. The name means nothing to them yet, and withholding it buys artificial mystery at the cost of clarity. The real mystery layers survive intact: the chart theft, Cairn, and the two-layer structure underneath his operation.

He never appears in a fight. Pressure on him is commercial, legal and social, and the most dangerous thing the crew can do to him is prove he was used — which costs him the standing the whole feud was meant to win.

Open: what he does when he learns there was a second principal, and whether he would trade what he knows to avoid being the one holding the bag.

Krik’Lit
A deckhand who happens to be the only mouth on the ship the enemy’s machines can understand.

Signed on for a job of work. He came aboard to help fit the helm, he has done that, and the crew roster has him leaving at the swap alongside Pffred. By the public record he is a deckhand with a competence and a species and little else.

He is the most valuable person aboard and nobody knows it Ostekk's clockwork horrors speak thri-kreen. Krik'Lit is thri-kreen, he is standing on the deck, and no one has tried talking to a single machine.

That makes him the difference between a fight with the constructs and a conversation with the man who built them, conducted through one of them. He is a translator the crew has been treating as cargo.

VoyageWhat he was doing
V003Assigned under Ryeback for the run to H'Catha. Deck work.
V004Aboard through the behir engagement. Left on the Cargo helm with Sol, Ryeback and Pffred while the party went down.
V005Still aboard, still unremarked.
V006Scheduled to leave. The Flying Foundry takes him and Pffred off and puts Ezra and a teacher on.
The window The parley is available only while the ship is still being taken. He leaves the moment the swap happens, and Sor'Kur is the only other thri-kreen anywhere near the board.

So the cost of the swap is larger than a headcount. Trading two deckhands for a cadet and a teacher also trades away the ability to negotiate with Ostekk. Worth knowing before you run it, and worth the crew having a chance to notice.

Nothing hidden in him. What is hidden is what he can do, and it is hidden by the ordinary fact that nobody asks a deckhand to interpret for the enemy.

If you want it found The horrors already speak in front of the crew. One passive check, or Krik'Lit tilting his head at a noise everyone else heard as grinding, hands it over without a scene. Cheaper than a clue and more satisfying, because the answer was standing there the whole time.

A reason to argue for keeping him, which makes the swap a decision instead of an administrative beat.He leaves on schedule and the option goes with him.
Keep him flat He is better as a competent stranger than as a character with an arc. The payoff is the crew realising what they had, which lands harder if he was never interesting. Give him a trade, a short way of speaking, and no agenda.

Open: whether he leaves at the swap or the crew argues to keep him, and whether he can hear the horrors at all or merely share a language with them.

Krik’Lit
A deckhand who happens to be the only mouth on the ship the enemy’s machines can understand.

Signed on for a job of work. He came aboard to help fit the helm, he has done that, and the crew roster has him leaving at the swap alongside Pffred. By the public record he is a deckhand with a competence and a species and little else.

The translator, used once

Ostekk's clockwork horrors speak thri-kreen. Krik'Lit is thri-kreen, and in V006 he translated the crew's parley with Ostekk-10.

That makes him the difference between a fight with the constructs and a conversation with the man who built them, conducted through one of them. He leaves at the V007 swap unless the crew argues to keep him.

The window

The parley is available only while the ship is still being taken. He leaves the moment the swap happens, and Sor'Kur is the only other thri-kreen anywhere near the board.

What's hidden

Nothing hidden in him. What is hidden is what he can do, and it is hidden by the ordinary fact that nobody asks a deckhand to interpret for the enemy.

Threads

What the swap costs

The cost of the swap is larger than a headcount. Trading two deckhands for a cadet and a teacher also trades away the ability to negotiate with Ostekk. The crew should get a chance to notice that before the roster closes.

If the crew works it outIf they do not
A negotiated route through the constructs, and a line to Ostekk that goes around the behir entirely.A fight on H'Catha against machines that were trying to say something.
A reason to argue for keeping him, which makes the swap a decision instead of an administrative beat.He leaves on schedule and the option goes with him.

How to run them

How it surfaces

The horrors already speak in front of the crew. One passive check, or Krik'Lit tilting his head at a noise everyone else heard as grinding, hands it over without a scene. Cheaper than a clue and more satisfying, because the answer was standing there the whole time.

Keep him flat

He is better as a competent stranger than as a character with an arc. The payoff is the crew realising what they had, which lands harder if he was never interesting. Give him a trade, a short way of speaking, and no agenda.

Open

Whether he leaves at the swap or the crew argues to keep him, and whether he can hear the horrors at all or merely share a language with them.

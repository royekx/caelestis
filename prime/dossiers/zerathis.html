Zerathis
A spellcaster who went deep and dissolved into the system. What is left still speaks.

A spellcaster of historical note, and a Starchild before Casey. He tethered to Virenspace repeatedly, took every gift Astra extended, and went further toward whatever was calling her than Casey has.

He gave Mirt the wardrobe. At the time it read as a gift from an accomplished caster to a headmaster, and Mirt has disclosed nothing about where it came from because there was nothing obvious to disclose.

What is at Viren now

His identity dissolved into the system's paracausal layer. What is there is part Zerathis, part Astra's reach, part the site itself.

It speaks, sometimes in his voice and with his memories, sometimes with neither. It carries no malevolence in any direction at all. It is what is left.

He believed the gifts were worth the cost, and kept believing it past the point where the question was still his to answer.

Nothing suggests he was reckless or deceived. He went deep on purpose, and that is what makes him a warning rather than a victim.

What's hidden

Mirt has been keeping a piece of the network in his bedroom

The wardrobe is one of his tethers — a paracausal replica of his anchor site, carrying a thread of the Viren shift across space.

That is why Casey was drawn to it in his first voyage, and why Polly could reach him there: Astra's presence was already established at that location. Casey read it as luck. He was being magnetised.

It also means anyone who recognises what the wardrobe is — a Watcher, the colony, a rival — has a reason to want it that Mirt would not see coming.

Threads

He is Casey's own arc seen from the far end, he sits at the near end of Astra's reach into Virenspace, and the wardrobe in Mirt's quarters is the piece of him already aboard.

How to run them

He is the answer to “what if a Starchild says yes every time.” The cautionary tale needs no explaining; Casey gets to meet it.

He is how Casey hears the word

What he became may call him brother or Starchild without preamble — which is how the term first gets applied to him, by the thing a Starchild turns into. He has to sit with the implication.

Run his remains as neither threatening nor helpful. It answers some things, cannot answer others, and occasionally answers as a person who is gone. The horror is the inconsistency, rather than anything it does.

Open

His stat presence, if any — he is unbuilt and stays a presence rather than an encounter; and whether he recruited other Starchildren, which decides whether Viren is a two-tether overlap or something much deeper.

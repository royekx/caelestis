Zerathis
A spellcaster who answered Astra Virex every time, and dissolved into Virenspace. What is left of him still speaks.

Zerathis was a spellcaster of historical note and a Starchild of Astra Virex before Casey. He tethered in Virenspace again and again, took every gift Astra Virex offered, and went further toward what was calling him than Casey has. His identity dissolved into the system's paracausal layer. He gave Mirt the wardrobe in his quarters.

Appearance

Zerathis has no body now. What remains of him in Virenspace is heard, not seen: a voice that is sometimes his and carries his memories, and sometimes neither.

Personality

He believed the gifts were worth the cost, and he kept believing it past the point where the question was still his to answer. He was not reckless and he was not deceived. He went deep on purpose, which makes him a warning and not a victim.

Biography

Background

Zerathis was an accomplished caster when Astra Virex called him. He followed it, and he was no part of Vecna's following. He gave Mirt the wardrobe, and at the time it read as a gift from an accomplished caster to a headmaster. Mirt has said nothing about where it came from, because there was nothing obvious to say.

His tethers in Virenspace overlapped, and paracausal change there ran faster than one tether could make it. What is at Viren now is part Zerathis, part Astra Virex's reach and part the site itself. It speaks, sometimes in his voice, and it is malevolent in no direction at all.

Notable items

  • The wardrobe. A magical replica of a stellar feature in Virenspace: a bounded stretch of astral night under unfamiliar constellations. It stands in Mirt's quarters.

What's hidden

  • The wardrobe. Mirt has been keeping a piece of Astra Virex's reach in his bedroom. The wardrobe is one of Zerathis's tethers, a paracausal replica of his anchor site, carrying a thread of the Viren shift across space. That is why Casey was drawn to it in Voyage 1, and why Astra Virex, in Polly's voice, could reach him there: its presence was already established at that spot. Casey read it as luck. He was being drawn in.
  • Who wants it. Anyone who recognises what the wardrobe is, a Watcher, the colony or a rival, has a reason to want it that Mirt would not see coming.

How to run him

Zerathis is a name the crew hears long before they meet what remains of him, and the accounts of him disagree with one another. The touchstone is Osiris, from Destiny.

He is the answer to what happens when a Starchild says yes every time. The cautionary tale needs no explaining; Casey gets to meet it. What he became may call Casey brother or Starchild without preamble, which is how Casey first hears the word applied to himself. Run the remains as neither threatening nor helpful. It answers some things, cannot answer others, and now and then answers as a person who is gone. The horror is the inconsistency.

His arc

He waits in Virenspace, Casey's first tether point, at the near end of Astra Virex's reach. He stays a presence there, not an encounter.

Threads

  • He is Casey's own arc seen from the far end, and the wardrobe in Mirt's quarters is the piece of him already aboard Caelestis.
  • If Polly answered the call the way Zerathis did, the voice reaching Casey is as real and as lost as what speaks at Viren. Casey can meet that answer before he knows it is about his brother.

Still to decide

  • How Zerathis looked in life, and his species, are not set.
  • Whether Zerathis recruited other Starchildren, which decides whether Viren is a two-tether overlap or something deeper, is not set.
  • The accounts the crew hears of him are not written; each is set when it is used.

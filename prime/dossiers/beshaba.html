Beshaba
Lady Doom. Sol's patron, and she talks at him rather than to him.

Goddess of bad luck, misfortune and random mischance. Tymora's twin sister. She speaks to her chosen in brief, direct utterances rather than extended communion.

“Stand.” “Again.” “Continue.”

The register Capricious, and she talks at Sol rather than to him. The acknowledgment scene set it: “Well, you had my curiosity but now, you have my attention… I must say, it was a bold move leaving your 'perfect' home, but what makes you so sure you escaped? Fate can be a cruel and twisted thing. Wield it wisely. Or not.”

Her lines leave deliberate air for him to answer into, and she does not wait for it.

Adversity is the only honest teacher. The Oath of Misfortune is written deity-agnostic, and it states her position anyway: comfort breeds weakness, hardship endured is strength earned, and misfortune is worn rather than worshipped.

She is not cruel for its own sake, and she is entertained rather than invested. Sol interests her because he walked out of a world built on her sister's favour — which is the funniest thing that has happened to her in a while.

The twin reading Aethris compressed fated outcomes into ritual and sorted its people into the Fortuned and the Misfortuned. Beshaba did nothing to that world. Tymora's worship did. The misfortune Sol was born marked with was a category his own culture invented, and she is the goddess who answered someone the system threw away.

Unestablished by design. What she wants from Sol beyond the entertainment is undecided, and the campaign reads better while it stays that way: a patron whose interest is unexplained is more unsettling than one with a plan.

Open: whether she knows about the Ascension, what the gods make of a place they cannot safely approach, and whether she has any position on the unfinished Zenith on Sol's homeworld — where her sister's ritual is the thing being completed.

Keep her short. One to three words is her normal volume, and the long speech from the acknowledgment scene should stay rare enough that it lands as an event.

Small misfortune around Sol should now feel attended to rather than random. He knows the voice.

What she never does She does not explain, reassure, or answer questions. She responds to what he does, and the response is usually an instruction to do it again.

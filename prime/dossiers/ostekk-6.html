OSTEKK-6
The last of nine. Voiceless, unrepaired, and carried.

The lone survivor of nine OSTEKK units, found broken in the tyrant ship's cargo hold with clockwork scavengers taking him apart. Lower half gone, voice box destroyed, arcane core intact and his body past anything magic can mend. Noncombatant.

He is named off the cargo manifest. The crew found their own way to talk to him — Tumak improvised a telepathic link, without understanding how — so the voicelessness became a problem they solved rather than a condition they had to live with.

He is an automaton rather than an autognome, and he says so plainly: “I am a unit. An automaton.” Clerical, precise, and particular about it.

Boogie fitted him with pseudopod legs — Mini-Boogie, 25-foot speed — so he moves with the crew rather than being carried. Tumak opened the telepathic link that let him speak at all, and he used it to warn them of the mimic, the bloom, and the horrors.

His loyalty was engineered rather than earned Ostekk built servants that depend on the Lantern for maintenance, securing loyalty through need rather than trust. OSTEKK-6 is the last working proof of that design.

Open, and worth deciding: whether he knows. A unit who understands that his devotion is a maintenance schedule is a very different NPC from one who does not — and he is precise enough to have worked it out.

He holds to his designation, his manifest and his function. Being called an autognome is the one thing that reliably provokes him, and it is the closest thing he has to pride.

He is the question Gregory poses, approached from the other side: one was built to need and the other woke up able to choose, and putting them in a room together is the cheapest way to make that visible.

Why the field does not stop him He was built with a fragment of the Lantern of Flourishing, which holds the Rod's fourth piece and is therefore paracausal. That trace is why the Command Deck's anti-magic field slowed him rather than shutting him down — he stuttered, went sluggish, and kept talking.

Everything Ostekk built separately — the clockwork horrors, the behir — has no such trace. The horrors froze outright and the behir cannot enter at all. One rule explains all four behaviours.

He does not know that the thing keeping him running and the thing the behir has been trying to reach are the same object.

Keep him unrepaired He was designed as a clarifying oracle — repair him and the mystery unspools. The repair turned out to be impossible with what the crew carries, and what that produced is better than the exposition would have been: a mute, helpless NPC they chose to save and now haul around.

The dependency is doing more work than the answers would. Fix him late, if at all.

What he can still do: answer direct questions through the link, name things on the manifest, and confirm mechanics the crew has already guessed. He is a reference rather than a revelation.

Open: whether he knows about the maintenance dependency, what repairing him would actually require, and whether he has anything to say about the eight units who did not survive.

Noncombatant No block needed, and giving him one would invite using him as one. He has 25-foot speed on Boogie's pseudopod legs, no attacks, and the durability of something already broken.

The construct behaviour table is the only mechanical ruling that matters for him: in an anti-magic field he stutters and slows, and he keeps running.

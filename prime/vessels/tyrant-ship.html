Tyrant Ship
Vaelorix's carved-stone beholder ship, taken by the Caelestis crew in Toril orbit.

The Tyrant Ship is a beholder vessel of carved stone, once the ship of Vaelorix the Cultivant, a beholder of H'Catha who held no domain and collected living things. He died aboard it, and it drifted in orbit around Toril for years. The Caelestis crew took it and fly it now. Four of its ten great eyes answer at crew stations, and it carries Vaelorix's specimen garden, his quarters and his study.

Appearance

Carved stone throughout, with no windows, and ten great eyes along the hull, each on its eyestalk. Inside, the floors are stone, the light is dim, and the air smells old. Its decks are joined by shafts once served by a small propulsion system for its units, which no longer works. Circling Toril, its hours run close to the Academy's.

Every beholder ship is carved stone, so at H'Catha the Tyrant Ship reads as native traffic; a few such ships in orbit are normal, and a tyrant ship draws less concern at the domain borders than an ordinary spelljammer. It carries some beholder essence, so it obeys the Pact: the eyestalk matching the hour's shut eye goes dead, and in an eclipse the whole ship goes dark.

Decks and interior

The gravity plane

The ship's gravity turns over at a plane through its middle, almost level with the Command Deck floor. The Cargo Deck and the Hollow Deck lie on one side of it. The Command Deck lies on the other, and past it, at the top of the ship, the Meditation nodes: the Garden, the Quarters and Research, each at the end of its own shaft.

Cargo Deck

The ship's entry, and the crew's base camp aboard. Most things leave the ship through its intake, and the expulsion is worked from the Command Deck. A small craft can dock in the intake and carry a party down to a world.

Hollow Deck

A round room shaped like a pit, reached from the shaft through a side opening carved and scored as if cut through the ship one pass at a time. Its walls are lined with machinery: it was the ship's deck for repairs and maintenance, with a repair bay on its far side. Above the bay's pod sits the empty cradle of the Lantern of Flourishing. The floor is strewn with the remains of the ship's automaton units and wrecked clockwork horrors, and a few dormant horrors stand where the behir used to lie. OSTEKK-2 lies inert here.

Command Deck

Reached through a carved opening just past the gravity plane. Vaelorix lies dead on the dais, his central eye dried open. The dais is the ship's helm, an interface only a beholder could work; Vaelorix never installed a spelljamming helm. The Academy's chair-helm is installed into the dais now, with a crew station for each great eye rigged around it, and the controls for the expulsion are here.

Garden

Vaelorix's specimen garden lies at the foot of its shaft: a lavish greenhouse of cultivated flowers along the walls, a bed of grass at its centre, and research tables laid out with instruments and dissected flowers. A thick stalk of unopened velvet-blue buds stands at its heart, and a single whole wisp drifts in the air. The Faelight spore grows here among his other specimens, and the bloom that woke Bartholomew's Aspects came from here.

Quarters

Vaelorix's personal quarters hold a treasury of coin from his deals.

Research

His study: notes, and star charts of the worlds he collected plants from.

The wisps

Lantern-wisps still linger in the ship. Nothing aboard puts out a fire.

History

Vaelorix stole the Containment Engine, Fourth Series, from Ostekk, along with the nine OSTEKK automatons built to depend on it. Aboard this ship he pointed the Engine's regenerative working at a wisp-lotus symbiosis and got a paracausal eruption instead of a specimen. The bloom overran the ship. Its flowers made the wisps, which drain the living, and a field around the Command Deck that killed magic and unmade constructs. Vaelorix died on his dais with a plant grown out of his body.

Ostekk's clockwork behir, Ostekk-10, came for the Engine and was stranded aboard, unable to enter the field, and clockwork horrors fed on the broken units in the hold. Nobody on H'Catha learned what became of Vaelorix, and the ship drifted in orbit around Toril for years.

In the campaign

  • Voyage 3. Boatswain Tarto sent the crew to install a helm and fly the ship through H'Cathan space unchallenged. The Flighty Foundling dropped them into its Cargo Deck, where clockwork horrors were feeding on OSTEKK-6.
  • Voyage 4. The crew destroyed the horrors and cleared the bloom from the garden shaft, though the wisps persisted. The Collection Manifest gave OSTEKK-6 his name.
  • Voyage 5. On the Hollow Deck the crew fought the behir and retreated; Gregory jolted OSTEKK-2 awake long enough for it to name the behir's sender, "the original Ostekk". Boogie found the gravity plane, and the crew reached the Command Deck and the dead beholder.
  • Voyage 6. The crew found the Garden, cut down the flowers and tore the great one from Vaelorix's body, and magic returned. Gregory found the Lantern; the crew lied to Ostekk-10 and ejected it through Cargo. The helm went to the Command Deck for fitting. In the days after, it was seated, four of the ten eyes answered, and the Flighty Foundling came alongside.

What's hidden

  • Not the crew's to keep. The ship goes into the Caelestis inventory after the mission; see the Fleet. The players need not know.
  • Vaelorix's debts. Nobody on H'Catha knows Vaelorix is dead. Something between him and Kurrzot was left unsettled, and it lands on whoever flies his ship.

How to run it

Run the ship in the theatre of the mind. Someone able to cast must stay aboard to hold the helm: Saerthe at the helm and Ryeback commanding the deck, or Miken left aboard alone as the high-tension option. A scuttle ship from the Flighty Foundling docks in the Cargo intake and carries the landing party; the approach is on H'Catha and the H'Catha arc. Manifest entry 048 stays unused unless a player asks, and the soft light Casey saw under the behir is left undefined.

Threads

  • OSTEKK-2. The crew could heal it back to function if they return for it, with the Lantern of Flourishing for one.
  • The wisps. Keeping them or eradicating them is the crew's choice, made in play.
  • The Rod. The ship ties to the Rod of Seven Parts through the Lantern it carried.
  • Ostekk. He wants his Engine back, and the crew lied to his machine.

Still to decide

  • Which shaft letter Research gets is not set: B or D. The Garden is A and the Quarters C.
  • The crew has not settled on a name for the ship.

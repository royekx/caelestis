Vth'oramu
An astral dreadnought taken alive and lived in: the Vth'orama's passage to a new home.

Vth'oramu is an astral dreadnought, the apex predator of the Astral Sea, taken alive by N'ghathrod and rebuilt into a ship. It is still alive. The Vth'orama live aboard, a civilization rather than a garrison: families, children born aboard, trades and streets, and most of its people never chose N'ghathrod's war. In their tongue the name means the passage to a new home: the founders say it as a promise, and those born aboard say it as "here".

Appearance

A chain-tailed leviathan of the Astral Sea. Its physical tail is immensely long, which is part of why it could never hide inside a system, and the tail ends in an astral cord, a silver line running off to somewhere unknown. Its eye still works as an eye.

Decks and interior

The eye

The eye is the bridge. The colony watches the Astral Sea through it.

The mouth

The mouth is the only way in or out. It draws things in, the way an astral dreadnought's mouth opens on its inner demiplane, and passing through it puts a traveller inside the demiplane the interior was widened into. Boarding is a plane shift, not a walk through a door.

The demiplane and its transit points

Inside, movement runs through fixed plane-shift transit points rather than corridors, which is how a place that size fits within one creature. Boarding the hull and reaching the colony are separate problems, and solving the first does not begin the second.

The tadpole pools

The tadpole pools bloom out of the hull, which is N'ghathrod's body, so the pools are in him as an elder brain's are in its brine. Every illithid aboard, whole or malformed, spawns a mass of tadpoles two or three times in its life, and every mass goes into the pools. N'ghathrod's own copies wait there too; he spawns them on a slow, steady cycle, a few at a time. Nothing eats the brood. The tadpoles still eat one another, as tadpoles do, but far more of each mass survives than in an ordinary colony, and every survivor is kept alive until it is found a host. N'ghathrod decides which tadpole goes to which captive. The pools always hold more tadpoles than the colony has bodies for.

The colony's quarters

The colony's illithids, the true-formed and the malformed, live here with the taken, thralls and their families. It is a civilization with streets and trades, and children are born and raised aboard; for some of its people it has been home since birth.

Skiffs

Vth'oramu works as a mothership. Skiffs go out from it to the worlds in its vicinity while the ship stays where no one looks.

The pulse

Its great weapon is the reverse of its mouth: a pulse pressed outward from every side of its body, and everything within range is teleported away. The pulse takes a long time to charge and is rarely used.

History

The dreadnought hunted its own territory for as long as anything had watched it, and it bore no wound before the siege. N'ghathrod knew a dreadnought's physiology, and a whisper from Vecna told him it was the best way to keep a colony on its passage home. He saw his chance and took it. The fight lasted a long time, and controlling the creature took a great many tadpoles.

Taking it and turning it into Vth'oramu took decades, perhaps a century. The body was opened and made habitable: hull, decks, transit points, and the interior widened into a demiplane. Generations of the colony were born aboard, and many died in the Astral Sea.

It can enter a system, but a vessel that size near a world's planets would almost certainly be seen, so it keeps to the dark between systems. It crosses Cairn Station's lane now and then on its way somewhere. Cairn's stretch of that lane lies at the very limit of Strake Station's scope, and Strake has caught faint, odd returns there, a different shape on every sweep. Cairn reports nothing on that bearing.

What's hidden

  • Inside him. Boarding is walking around inside N'ghathrod. There is no elder brain aboard: he has become the colony's elder brain in function, and its illithids believe they answer to an elder brain through him. Most of the colony has never been told the rest; they live in him and think the ship is a ship.
  • Under command. Every institutional record treats it as wildlife, a predator with a range and a patrol. It is a creature under command, and every Fleet model of the region is built without that fact.
  • Cairn's silence. The colony holds Cairn Station, so Cairn reports nothing when Vth'oramu crosses its lane. The odd returns at Strake are Tarroway's sweeps; nobody in the colony knows Strake can see that far.

Its arc

In Act 2 the crew meets its skiffs and the taken; the ship itself belongs to Acts 3 and 4, and word of it may reach the crew late in Act 2. Once N'ghathrod grows bolder, Ilsevaine Corr will face it. The colony's lane is on Opposition: the Vth'orama.

How to run it

Use it first as an explanation, long before an encounter: Strake's odd returns, and Cairn's silence on the same bearing, already point at it. It can be seen without being reached, and a thing that size on a bearing, reading wrong on every sweep, is a scene on its own. As a destination it is a two-part megadungeon that is also somebody's street: the hull, then the colony going about its day.

If the crew boards, run it as a civilization, with families and trades and people who never chose the war. Any plan to destroy the ship or end N'ghathrod weighs that cost.

H'Catha
A disk of sea around the Spindle, split among ten beholder rulers and watched by the Sovereign Eye they dreamt together.

H'Catha is a toroidal water world at the outer edge of Realmspace, fourteen days' sail from Toril: a disk of sea with the Spindle rising at its centre and fog along its rim. Ten beholders rule it, one to each domain. Over them stands the Sovereign Eye, which the rulers dreamt together, and which sees everything in daylight and enforces the Pact that binds them. Traffic and merchants pass by it at the system's edge.

Description

The world is a flat ring of ocean around the Spindle. In H'Catha's geography the Spindle is the central eye and the ten domains are arranged around it like eyestalks. The Spindle is also the world's sundial and its clock: as H'Catha turns, the Spindle's tapering shadow sweeps across the disk. Night lasts longest close to the Spindle and never comes near the rim, where the fog lies in constant daylight.

The bands

The bands are named for the parts of an eye. From the rim inward they are the Lashline, the Sclera, the Iris and the Pupil. The Lashline is the misty rim, and it holds the most civilised people on H'Catha, who live under the Eye's constant threat because darkness never reaches them. The Pupil, beside the Spindle, is the smallest band and the most contested and lawless, because its dark lasts longest; overlapping occupying forces struggle there constantly. Danger changes from band to band, the way a neighbourhood changes from street to street.

Domains and seams

H'Catha has ten domains, one for each eyestalk, each running from the rim toward the Spindle. They are continent-scale: crossing into another domain is like travelling to another continent, with natural and unnatural obstacles in between. The borders are watched. Between the domains run the seams, strips held by no ruler, where free locathah, guides, exiles and anyone avoiding a ruler collect.

Ports and arrival

Ports are spread across the world. They take outsiders who come to trade and set them a time to leave. Every beholder ship is carved stone, and a few such ships in orbit are ordinary traffic; the watch is kept at the domain borders on the surface, and orbit is left open. Landing is hard for anyone.

History

Beholders came to H'Catha drawn by the Spindle and took the world together, before their paranoia set in. The founding rulers divided it into ten domains and dreamt the Sovereign Eye together to keep the peace among them. They agreed that each of them needed some privacy, and that privacy is the shadow: it is the Eye's blind spot by their design.

At the last eclipse Ondessa was struck down through a betrayal inside her own court, and her citadel has been occupied since. Vaelorix, a beholder of H'Catha who held no domain, vanished from the world, and nobody on H'Catha learned what became of him. Recently an adamantine meteor struck the Pupil on the seam between Kurrzot's domain and his rival's, and the fight over it has drawn forces from both sides (see the meteor).

Peoples and culture

Every domain answers to its ruler. Most of H'Catha's people are bound, native or neutral, and most of the hostile ones are agents the rulers send.

The rulers

Each domain has one ruler, and all ten are enlightened. A ruler floats, keeps its citadel wherever in its domain it chooses, and acts through agents and forces rather than in person. A new ruler can be dreamt only when a seat is open, and it is an ally of whoever dreamt it.

The Sovereign Eye

The rulers dreamt the Sovereign Eye together. It sees everything in daylight, across the world and the space around it wherever H'Catha's day falls, and it enforces the Pact. Shadow is its blind spot.

The Pact

When the Spindle's shadow reaches a domain, the eye bound to that domain shuts on every beholder, and it stays shut until the shadow moves on to the next domain. A shut eye gives a beholder partial sleep: enough rest, without the full sleep in which a beholder dreams a rival. Every eye ray belongs to a domain, death and disintegration included, and the Eye punishes a lawbreaker with whichever eye is shut that hour. A crime in the hour of death is punished with death.

The Pact holds whatever carries beholder essence. The tyrant ship the Caelestis crew now holds carries some, so it obeys: the eyestalk matching the hour's shut eye goes dead, and in an eclipse the whole ship goes dark.

Common Law and the Edicts

Common Law holds everywhere in daylight. It forbids killing, wounding, stealing, and taking anyone against their will, so nothing can be taken by force while the Eye can see. Each domain also has one Edict, a strange law born of its ruler's paranoia, and the Eye enforces it too.

People believe the Eye also holds deals made in daylight. They call such deals light-sworn, and true deals are struck in the light. Deals the Eye should not see are made in the dark.

The eclipse

Roughly once a year, on an uneven schedule, the moon Turnbetl makes a close pass and darkens H'Catha for three to four hours of full dark. The Sovereign Eye shuts and every ruler sleeps at once. Open action is possible only then, and the rulers spend the time between eclipses plotting toward the next. While they sleep they act through their forces, tentative alliances and betrayals: agents cross enemy lines to find a sleeping ruler, kill it and take its seat. The next eclipse is close.

Sleepborn

Partial sleep leaks, and what leaks becomes sleepborn: ill-formed beholders that live in the shadow, where light unmakes them. Some rulers hunt them. Others ignore them and let their domains be ravaged, and some fear what the sleepborn could become.

The peoples
  • Spectators. Bound guardians, lawful neutral: dutiful beings with real problems of their own.
  • Locathah. Native fish-folk with a long history of enslavement, and the most endangered of H'Catha's natives. Free locathah live on the seams and make natural guides.
  • Thralls. Charmed or held rather than evil by choice; those who have been freed are survivors.
  • Mercane. Neutral traders working the ports. One of them works for Vocath.
  • Giff. Mercenaries who serve as port muscle, lawful and professional.
  • Kuo-toa and ogres. The hostile majority among the agents the rulers send.

Notable people

Ondessa
Ruler, struck down
Holds her seat with a ruined central eye, hiding in the light of her own Lashline.

Ondessa is a ruling beholder who still holds her seat and her link to the Sovereign Eye. At the last eclipse she was struck down through a betrayal inside her own court. The attack destroyed her central eye: she cannot see out of it or use it to cancel magic. Her citadel is occupied, and she hides in the light of her own Lashline. The other rulers want her dead, and most of the populace does not know she lives. She is better than her neighbours without being good, and she keeps a beholder's paranoias and obsessions.

She and H'Catha's Watcher, Nereth Vaul, protect each other and trade information. Her domain's port is an intelligence hub, because H'Catha sits where traffic and merchants pass, and what she learns reaches the Order and Caelestis through the Watcher. Ostekk's automatons search her sea-caves.

Kurrzot
Ruler, claimant to the meteor
Claims the meteor from his side of the seam.

Kurrzot rules the domain on one side of the seam where the meteor struck, and he claims it. His agents and thralls fight his rival's for the diggings. Something between him and Vaelorix was left unsettled.

Points of interest

The Spindle

The Spindle rises from the centre of the disk, and it is the reason the beholders came to H'Catha. Magic grows unreliable the higher one climbs it. Its shadow sweeps the world as H'Catha turns, and every domain's hours are told by it.

The meteor

An adamantine meteor struck the Pupil, the innermost band, on the seam between Kurrzot's domain and his rival's. It is large, and it is mined as a vein in an occupied area that rival forces fight over in skirmishes. Adamantine matters on H'Catha because the Sovereign Eye cannot see through it. Working to carry the ore off is a form of treason on H'Catha. Caelestis wants it for containment: adamantine survives disintegration.

Turnbetl

The first of H'Catha's two moons, with a flammable atmosphere. Its close passes cause the eclipse.

Lumbe

The second moon, a metallic cylinder with doors in it. Lumbe is Sarelk's laboratory and has no part in the Pact.

In the campaign

  • Voyage 3. A Sky Dock cargo clerk named H'Catha as a secondary stop for shipments routed through Mr. Blip. Boatswain Tarto sent the crew to recover the meteor, in an abandoned tyrant ship whose appearance would let them move through H'Cathan space unchallenged.
  • Voyage 6. The crew took Vaelorix's tyrant ship in Toril orbit, and hold it now.

What's hidden

  • Vecna and the Sovereign Eye. Vecna's hand on H'Catha goes back to its founding. He influenced the making of the Sovereign Eye, hoping its dreaming would bring H'Catha to a Zenith. It did not; it is one of his plans that failed. A trace of his worship lingers on H'Catha, and he stays faint there.

How to run it

H'Catha plays as a Cold War spy thriller on a drowned world. Subterfuge, stealth, information and a silver tongue win. Violence is always an option, and it always has consequences. Keep the player-facing rules minimal: darkness moves, places have rules, consequences follow.

Arrival tension comes from crossing watched domain borders on the ground, never from orbital checkpoints. A Caelestis scuttle ship docks in the tyrant ship's Cargo Deck intake and carries the landing party down unnoticed. The approach, the beats and the clocks are on the H'Catha arc.

Terrain at the table

Where a domain's terrain carries its ruler's ray, play the ray as a terrain hazard rather than an attack roll. The ray belongs to the land, which is what lets a level-appropriate party cross a beholder's domain at all. The working table covers eight rays:

Its arc

H'Catha is the crew's first world beyond Caelestis. They arrive about three weeks after the impact, behind rivals who have been on the ground two weeks or more. The coming eclipse is the arc's clock, and it puts Nereth Vaul on a clock too. Staying through the eclipse to protect Ondessa, or to help her retake her citadel, is an optional larger goal: a huge ally, at higher stakes. The crew can also call the mission done. Beats, approach and forces are on the H'Catha arc.

Held in reserve: Ondessa may be immune to the eclipse sleep, and she does not know it. Reveal it at the eclipse.

Threads

  • Ostekk. The crew carries the Lantern of Flourishing, his Containment Engine, and travels with OSTEKK-6, which carries his pattern. Hearing his name soon after landing is the payoff.
  • Nereth Vaul. Her report went from her to the Hall, to the Fleet, to the Academy, where Mirt made it a cadet mission. She expected a seasoned crew, is suspicious of cadets, and probes whether they are real sailors; that suspicion carries the thread that Caelestis is spread thin.
  • Ezra. A Glimpse of the Order, he joins the crew for the H'Catha voyage to finish his training under Nereth Vaul. Later a Caelestis crew collects him at H'Catha and carries him to the Hall.
  • Vaelorix and Kurrzot. The unsettled business between them lands on whoever flies Vaelorix's ship.
  • Qitru. She and her githyanki have moved to H'Catha ahead of the crew. Her "beholder killers, inbound" rumour is on the H'Catha arc.
  • Vocath. One mercane in the ports works for him, and his crates route through Mr. Blip toward Toril and H'Catha.

Still to decide

  • What the rulers themselves can see is open: either the Sovereign Eye lets every ruler see all, so none can act openly against another, or each ruler sees only its own domain.
  • Whether every domain keeps a ray-based terrain effect with ten domains is open, and the terrain table has no entries for death and disintegration.
  • Whether the Eye truly enforces light-sworn deals, or people only believe it does, has no answer yet.
  • The name of Kurrzot's rival is not chosen; Oszrax is proposed.
  • The ruler names besides Kurrzot, Ondessa and Ostekk are proposals (Gorrathax, Ulmorrow, Oszrax, Saelvex, Vhezzar, Hethrul, Oumnu), and the order of rays around the ring is a placeholder. The rays of Kurrzot's and his rival's domains follow from it.
  • The Edicts are drafted only, one per ruler, and the word for them may change.
  • Who betrayed Ondessa and who backs the occupiers has no answer; the lean is a neighbour, Ulmorrow or Gorrathax.
  • What Ondessa's ruined central eye does to her land, and what stands at the Spindle's summit, have no answer.
  • What the meteor's Ortholith matter is (valuable rock, a Monolith fragment shed or aimed, or bait) has no answer, nor whether the Academy wanted this meteor or any adamantine, and what made it urgent. The spine logs the ore as threshold-resonant.
  • A name for the Sovereign Eye has no answer; the lean is all three by speaker: the Unlidded (locals), the Overgaze (courts), Ahm-Vauth (rulers).
  • The measures have no answer: the length of a turn, the disk's radius, the Spindle's base and height, and how far the shadow reaches.
  • Whether there is an underground beyond rare pockets (caves, the Old Courses) has no answer.
  • The drafted rulings on Edict strictness, first strikes, sleepborn resistance, moving blind spots, sight inside rock, the Carriers and the Unwaking are neither locked nor cut.
  • The ground-level drafts (the Unwaking, its places, factions and old things, and five routes inward) are unreviewed and not canon.
  • Who Sarelk is, beyond Lumbe's keeper, is open.
  • Nereth Vaul's species is open, and it sets the final form of her name. The placeholder was triton, and the name must stay distinct from Ondessa's.

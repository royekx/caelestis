The Astral Sea
The material space that fills all of space, which the Fleet calls the drift, and what a crossing can hold.

The Astral Sea fills all of space: within each system, between systems and between galaxies. Its stretch between systems is Wildspace; the space within a system goes by that system's name, as Realmspace is the space within Toril's system. The Fleet calls the whole Sea the drift, and a Driftwarden is a warden of it. It is material space, and all five Axioms work in it, in different measure from one reach to the next: between systems Expansion and Dissolution weigh most, and things spread out and even out; between galaxies Expansion weighs most of all. The greater gods dwell between the galaxies. The principle is in Cosmology; this entry holds what a crossing can contain.

Description

Travel between worlds in one system takes days by spelljamming helm. Travel between systems crosses the drift on lanes, and a lane's tier records how well that crossing is charted. Few expeditions have tried to leave the Erevos Galaxy.

Lanes

The Fleet grades every lane by how well it is known. A charted lane is surveyed, logged and routinely sailed. A long haul is charted but far, and supplies decide how a crew arrives. An uncharted route is sailed rarely and recorded badly; conditions change along it, and both the arrival time and the arrival point are uncertain. A closed lane is shut by standing order: it can be sailed, the Fleet forbids it, and a crew caught on one answers to the Fleet.

Currents and eddies

The charted lanes run with the drift's currents. Off the lanes the currents run uncharted: some carry a vessel inward toward a system, some hold it in place, and some feed a bearing nobody has surveyed.

Color pools

Portals adrift in the Astral Sea, each opening onto another plane. They open and close quickly. The Fleet charts them and sails around them. Clint Dashforest of the Bellwether Crow went through one on purpose; it carried him through a small pocket of another dimension and set him down on Fonains. Other ways onto another plane are in Cosmology.

Dead gods and drifting matter

The corpses of gods drift through the Astral Sea, with the wreckage, debris and abandoned works that collect around them. They are the landmarks of an otherwise featureless crossing, and salvage crews, hermits and githyanki all work them.

How to run it

Give each leg of a crossing one feature. Name it, let the crew see it, and let them decide whether to look. Any feature can be scenery, an obstacle or a thread the crew chooses to pull, and it needs no tie to anything larger unless it earns one. A charted crossing should be quiet more often than not; a long haul or an uncharted route should not be.

  • Currents. A chase or a breakaway can end with a vessel pushed into a current it cannot leave.
  • Color pools. A pool is a way off the map, briefly open; it is how Clint left the Crow.
  • Dead gods. A place to hide, a place to be ambushed, and a place where something can be found. Each can carry its own small thing of interest.

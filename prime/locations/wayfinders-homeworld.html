The Wayfinders' homeworld
A world of many clans folded inside its own heart. It has no name, and its way home lives in people, not on charts.

The Wayfinders' homeworld is a world of many clans, not all of them halflings, in the Erevos Galaxy. About five centuries ago it folded itself inside its own heart, and it has hidden there since. It is the one world known to hold a civilization inside its heart. The world has no name. Lunarfoot is a clan, and the Runekeepers, the order that keeps the concealment, belong to this world alone.

Description

Those who come home know it by its sensory anchors: a luminous haze, like light beneath water; tall pale trunks with smooth, glasslike bark, spiralling toward one unseen point; the smell of warm earth after rain, and of sap touched by lightning; and people who move with certainty.

History

About five centuries ago the world was under threat from something close at hand, and a seeker of its own people, close to the founder's line, went after a means to answer it. The seeker came back carrying the Dissolution fragment. The world came to its Zenith and a Monolith rose. The seeker carried the fragment into the world's Atrium, opened its heart and linked it to one other world under the Monolith, meaning to do good. That world was a bad one. The two began to bleed into each other, terrible creatures came through, and things grew worse than before.

The leaders of four clans fought the seeker together and took the fragment, and Dissolution struck all four. The one who seized it etched one pole of it into themselves and became the first Runekeeper; the other three lines were primed. The fold happened by accident in that struggle. Dissolution disjoined the world from everything around it, cut the link, closed the heart and drew the world inside it. The bleeding stopped, and the Monolith faded afterward, as Monoliths do. Hidden, the world was better off: hiding answered the first threat too. The clans understood none of it at the time and learned to work it over the generations that followed. With no sun, sky, stars or moon over them, the four clans of the order named themselves for the heavens: Dawnbrow, Skyshoulder, Starfist and Lunarfoot.

Peoples and culture

The world's clans include the Lunarfoot clan; the name belongs to the people and their works, as in the Lunarfoot clan, the Lunarfoot Runekeeper, the Children of Lunarfoot, and a Lunarfoot Wayfinder's script. Only two people ever leave the world: the Wayfinder, the heir on the journey, and the Runekeeper when he must. At the sending the Runekeeper takes from the Wayfinder the name of home and the way there, and holds them in the binding; the Wayfinder keeps that they came from somewhere, and loose fragments. A Wayfinder remembers home along the way, a haven at a time, and the name and the way come back at the Becoming. The withholding is doctrine: a way home that lives only in a person cannot be taken off a chart.

The Becoming is the rite in which a Wayfinder becomes the Runekeeper, and the Lunarfoot tale named for it: something came looking for their world, and the world folded itself around its people and gave them runes as gifts. Children learn the four clans by a rhyme sung with movements, a finger to the brow for Dawnbrow and fists for Starfist, and a second verse for the heavens: the sun, the sky, the stars, the moon.

Points of interest

The concealment

The concealment is one spell, cast across a generation. The living Runekeeper holds the binding, wherever he stands. The havens are its sigils, each drawing on a host world's heart. A Wayfinder's journey renews them, and the sealing of the spell is the Wayfinder becoming the Runekeeper. The Runekeepers also hid their own world's Atrium, its heart's one entrance.

The havens

Every Runekeeper builds a haven on another world and, after the exchange, lives out their days there and becomes its essence. A haven is a small pocket, folded the way the homeworld is folded and set on a host world close to its heart, behind a thin veil between the heart and the world; it draws on the heart without opening it. Its site carries traces of the homeworld's sensory anchors, and it keeps its Runekeeper's essence and their records of their own era. There are seven. Six hold faded Runekeepers; the seventh is the current Runekeeper's, waiting for him. Lunarfoot's, the fourth, lies at Selûne and the Tears of Selûne in Realmspace.

The hidden system

The world's system is hidden with it. No lane reaches it, and Caelestis has never charted it, so it carries no designation.

What's hidden

  • Homecoming. At the Becoming the exchange fills the Heart's last chamber and sends the twins home as the Runekeeper. The fragment has always come home whole, in one Runekeeper; this time it comes home as two halves, in Wan and Tu. That change is the world's second Zenith, a natural one: the homeworld splits into a blessing faction and an omen faction, and a Monolith follows and archives people. The world on the far side may be rebound as before, hidden in a new way or no longer hidden. Runekeeping and Wayfinding work differently afterward. The outcome is the table's.
  • The unhiding. Being two wears the binding down: the fragment's pieces strain against each other, the Runekeeper fades faster than any before him, and the binding slips. Left too long, the world comes out of its fold mid-journey, back among what it hid from: the threat close at hand, and the link, which may bind again and bleed, perhaps not as it was. A world appearing is no quiet event. It can be found by ordinary navigation, and noticed: by Vecna's work, by the Seekers, by whatever the old link reached.
  • The seeker. Vecna, a god for five centuries by then, steered the seeker to the residue with a whisper, before the Seekers were the chapters they are now. The homecoming's Monolith is an opening his side will not expect.
  • The fragment. The concealment rests on residue that nobody in the order has identified as residue. The Becoming tale is the cleaned-up memory of an accident.
  • The broken link. The order holds the way a link was broken once. If the crew learns it, it counters N'ghathrod's lattice.

Its arc

The Runekeeper's fade and the binding's slip are the clock of Tumak's arc; the mechanics are on Rules. About level 12 the twins' two pieces first draw together; what that does is not set (see the Dissolution fragment).

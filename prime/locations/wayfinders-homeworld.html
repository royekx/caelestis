The Wayfinders' homeworld
A halfling world that hides itself by a living ritual. It has no name, and its way home lives in people, not on charts.

The Wayfinders' homeworld is a halfling world in the Erevos Galaxy that hides itself. A living ritual withdraws it from cosmological sight, and the world sits where it has always sat, reachable by anyone who knows the way back. The world has no name. Lunarfoot is the clan, and the Runekeepers, the order that keeps the concealment, belong to this world alone.

Description

Those who come home know it by its sensory anchors: a luminous haze, like light beneath water; tall pale trunks with smooth, glasslike bark, spiralling toward one unseen point; the smell of warm earth after rain, and of sap touched by lightning; and people who move with certainty.

Peoples and culture

The world's clans include the Lunarfoot clan; the name belongs to the people and their works, as in the Lunarfoot clan, the Lunarfoot Runekeeper, the Children of Lunarfoot, and a Lunarfoot Wayfinder's script. Children sent into the cosmos as Wayfinders are given no map and no name for home. A returning Wayfinder recognises home through remembered sensory and cultural fragments. The withholding is doctrine: a way home that lives only in a person cannot be taken off a chart.

The Lunarfoot clan tells a tale, The Becoming, in which something came looking for their world, and the world folded itself around its people and gave them runes as gifts.

Points of interest

The concealment

The Runekeeper's ritual points the working, and the cycle of the Dissolution fragment powers it. The fragment is visible in its bearers: it forms and re-forms in the chest of the Runekeeper and of the Wayfinders who carry it. It is leaving the Runekeeper now and rebuilding in two halves in Wan and Tu, which is why the Runekeeper dims and why the twins were sent out. The Runekeepers also hid their own world's Atrium, its heart's one entrance.

While the fragment is in transit, the concealment rests on the share still leaving the Runekeeper. It weakens as he fades, and the world's protection decays as the cosmos changes around it. The marks a Wayfinder places are the sensors that let it be renewed when he returns.

The hidden system

The world's system is hidden with it. No lane reaches it, and Caelestis has never charted it, so it carries no designation.

What's hidden

  • Going home. Completing the inscription takes Tumak home, and going home ends the concealment. When Wan and Tu both arrive, the three poles stall the Dissolution cycle and the result is a Zenith; the homeworld splits into a blessing faction and an omen faction, and a Monolith follows. Between them the twins then carry a whole fragment, a key to the First Heart held by someone who believes he is only going home. The office he comes home to dims whoever holds it. The mechanics are in Rules.
  • The fragment. The concealment depends on residue that nobody in the order has identified as residue, and The Becoming is belief: the runes come from the fragment.

Its arc

The Runekeeper's fade runs on a soft clock: rising pressure, no date. The twins are not a key to the First Heart until enough of the stone has rebuilt between them through resolved runes, not before about level 12, early in Arc 3.

Still to decide

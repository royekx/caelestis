Strake Station
The staging point, and the one place to meet a competent professional holding every fact and the wrong model.

The middle post of the three, directly out from SYS-01, and the last Fleet relay on the Cairn approach. Operational, crewed, and the authorized limit of the bearing. Everything a crew needs to reach Cairn is here, and so is the order telling them they cannot.

What is on station

Thing or personWhy it matters
The staged Cairn approach packetCharts, survey material, and timing tables for the dreadnought's patrol. The only copies in the sector.
M. Tarroway, station cartographerKeeps the packet and has revised it three times in one cycle. Alive, on station, still working the problem.
Quartermaster Halix VerrowCounter-signs everything that moves on this bearing, which makes him the obstacle and the paper trail both.
Secure storageAn item held here under seal. Contents open
Tarroway's tables cannot work and he does not know why The patrol the timing tables track is the Vth'oramu itself. A hunting animal keeps a rhythm; a ship under command goes where it is wanted. He has spent cycles revising a patrol table for something that was never keeping a patrol, and he is the most useful person in the sector to talk to for exactly that reason.

He also does not know the thing his tables tracked has gone. Strake is where the crew can learn the shape of the problem from a competent professional who has every fact and the wrong model, which is the cleanest way to hand players a mystery without an NPC solving it for them.

Run it as the staging point Strake is the last friendly deck before anything happens. Everyone who went out went through here, and everyone who came back came back through here — so every account of the engagement, every revised table and every signature on the manifest is on this one station. Treat it as the hub the Cairn arc is launched from rather than a checkpoint on the way.

Open: what the secure-storage item is, and whether Verrow can be moved by authorization, persuasion or something worse.

Setting·Geography

Realms

The systems, the worlds in them, and the places on those worlds. Organized by designation, with a route table at the top, so that the question “can we get there, and what is waiting” has one answer in one place.

This page and the chart The Cosmos Chart is the registry: every designated body, every lane, the whole world list, drawn. This page is the places you can run — what is physically there, who is standing in it, what happens if the crew pushes. A world that is only a survey entry lives on the chart and stays off this page until it has a situation.

Routes Reference

Travel between worlds in a system takes days by spelljamming helm. Travel between systems crosses Wildspace on lanes. Crossing the Flow to another sphere is a senior-crew operation and has never been on the table for cadets.

The spheres

Five crystal spheres are designated. One is home and charted; the rest are held deliberately empty, because each is a campaign that has yet to be run. The Figure needs three linked systems in every one of the five, which is the structural reason they all exist and the reason none of them can stay blank forever.

SphereStateWhat is settled

That is available as a scale reveal at any point: the crew can learn that what they are fighting in SPH-01 is one fifth of a finished structure.

Crossing the Flow between spheres is a senior-crew operation and has never been within a cadet crew's reach. Lane-level detail and the drawn layout are on the Cosmos Chart.

Caelestis

A Fleet station in Toril orbit. A single structure with two functional wings: the Academy wing, where cadets train and live, and the Fleet operations wing, where contracts are issued and operational vessels work. Cadet graduation is internal movement from one wing to the other.

Staffed and run by the Caelestis Academy & Fleet. Mirt is its headmaster.

It is a target and the staff half-know it Caelestis holds A.T.L.A.S. in the Brig, and Joffrey has been mapping routes to it from inside the operations wing for a year. The sabotage, the thefts and Vocath's grudge campaign against Mirt are all noise over that one objective. Fleet Intelligence sees incidents and has not assembled them.
Security

Caelestis is under permanent Forbiddance, maintained by Saerthe Abizjn as senior caster. Teleportation in, out, or within the warded area fails. The ward is renewed nightly across a 30-day cycle and tested monthly at the dock perimeters. Mechanics in Rules.

Authorized internal transit uses bound Teleportation Circle sigils linking a small number of fixed points: the Bridge, the Brig, the Sky Dock control house, a handful of operational nodes. Sigil sequences are issued by role on a need-to-know basis and rotated periodically. Saerthe maintains the registry. Cadets and most personnel never see a sigil.

The wards are standard Fleet security for installations of consequence, which is the point: nobody reads them as evidence that Caelestis holds anything unusual.

The Brig — Bay of Restricted Inert Goods

Caelestis's high-security holding. A vault, in plain Fleet language, for objects whose cosmological character is unknown, unstable, or institutionally inconvenient to discuss. The acronym is bureaucratic.

Access is heavily compartmentalized. The full inventory is known to a small handful at Bridge level; individual handlers know what passes through their own station and nothing about what is held elsewhere. Routes to and from the Brig are themselves classified, and couriers know only the next handoff. Teleportation fails in and out of it.

Current holdings include A.T.L.A.S., catalogued as a navigational instrument of antique origin.

Its location and access protocols are classified well enough that Vocath could never have found it, which is why the strike against it exists at all: Joffrey found it and told him it was there. See Colony Operations.

Simulation Deck

Where Trial by Fire simulations run. Pods, scenario controls, instructor stations, run by Boatswain Tarto. A sabotage incident here injured cadets and left metallic plates and a jagged sigil in the chamber walls — the same hand as the astrally collared neogi's brand.

Weeping Goddess

The station's main refectory and informal social hub. Cadets, instructors, visiting personnel, dock workers and Fleet staff all eat here. Petty Officer Winston Ryeback presides over the galley, and it is the one room on the station where every faction's people are in each other's hearing.

Sky Dock

The surface-facing ship dock, where most cadet ship-handling exercises take place. Kip and Pik, two rock gnomes, run ship-anatomy drills and dock operations.

The bribed seam is here. Jeffrey is a Sky Dock hand paid to let certain manifests go unchecked, which is how the colony's material moves in and out — and it is where the crew already watched Joffrey make an exchange they thought was odd.

Sea Dock

The lower cargo dock. A working dock for bulk supply, maintenance and salvage handling, where Rindle Gearloft works. The aligner berths along the mooring struts are where the Latchling is, and Rindle has been protecting it from the academy.

Spelljammer Nexus / Chart Room

Navigational training and reference. Star charts, sphere maps, registered route logs, ledger archives. Saerthe is sometimes present. Anyone trying to work out where a lane goes without Bridge authorization comes here first.

Stores

The station's supply office and Mr. Blip's domain. Issued kit, ration rotation and miscellaneous logistics pass through here. Blip is also the control case for Gregory: an autognome with personhood inside the normal range, who cannot cast.

Cadet Quarters

Bunk space for the Academy wing. Communal, with a few windows onto the astral dark for the wings that face outward.

Gymnasium / Assault Course

Physical training. Fitness, combat drill and obstacle assessment.

Mirt's Quarters

The headmaster's private quarters, on the Bridge level. Contains an extradimensional wardrobe of unknown manufacture whose interior corresponds to a stellar location in Virenspace, and Mirt has disclosed nothing about its provenance.

It is one of Zerathis's tethers — a paracausal replica of his anchor site, carrying a thread of the Viren shift across space. That is why Casey was drawn to it and why Polly could reach him there. Mirt has been keeping a piece of Astra's network in his bedroom for years.

W-01 — Toril

The principal inhabited world of Realmspace and the sphere's namesake. A standard-class world by Fleet cataloguing: atmosphere, oceans, continents, varied civilizations. Diplomatic and trade contact with Caelestis is established and limited, and the Fleet's standing practice is to operate discreetly rather than advertise the wider cosmos to surface populations.

The Cogfast Enclave is placed here in draft — the Cycle 1489 auroral event in SA-002, where mechanical installations across a region synchronized. It is the strongest unclaimed lead attached to Gregory, and it is close enough to home that the crew can reach it between voyages.

W-02 — H'Catha

A toroidal water world sixteen days out — a disk of sea with the Spindle rising at its center and rim fog closing the edge. Ruled by beholders, and the only world in the system where the political geography is written into the terrain.

Non-beholder traffic is rare and dangerous. Patrols range broadly through the surrounding space, screening incoming vessels and engaging anything they fail to recognize or intimidate. Six of the eight domains hold a port at the Spindle's base and accept outsiders with goods to trade, so arriving openly is possible and comes with a deadline to leave.

Scale

The Spindle's base runs roughly 630 miles around, giving each domain about 80 miles of frontage on it. Crossing a single domain along the shore is a multi-day trip, which is what makes a border crossing a decision rather than a transition.

The Spindle

The structure at the center, and the reason beholders are here at all. In the geography of the world it is the central eye, with the eight domains arranged around it like eyestalks.

Magic grows unreliable the higher you climb. At the summit is a knowledge station that drives small-brained visitors insane. Beholders who survive it come back lawful neutral — and are cast out by their own kind for it, which is the single most useful fact on this world.

The endless cycle Beholders conquered H'Catha cooperatively, before the curse of their own paranoia set in. They gathered here drawn by the Spindle, and a world this dense with paranoid dreamers now breeds them, since a beholder's dreams can make another. They came here, and now it makes more of them.

That is also why the war never ends: whenever one nation gains a foothold on the Spindle, the others bring it down. The eight-domain layout was designed during the cooperative conquest and outlived the cooperation.

Each domain's terrain carries its ruler's signature ray, soaked in over ages — the same way a dragon's presence shapes its region. The land remembers who has been looking at it.

The moons

Turnbetl — a flammable atmosphere. Lumbe — a metallic cylinder with doors in it, and almost certainly artificial.

Neither has been visited. Lumbe is the more interesting of the two and is unattached to anything, which makes it available.

The meteor. An adamantine meteor struck the Spindle in recent record and the strike site remains. It landed on a seam, which is why it is still there. Everything about the recovery — the claimants, the clock, the routes in — is in Spine → the H'Catha arc.

W-07 — Glyth

Ringed and remote, ten days out. The illithid world in this system, and a settled civilization with elder brains, thrall populations and the full apparatus of illithid society.

Glyth is the reason the colony exists N'ghathrod returned here with illithids the world judged unfit — the unthinned, raised without an elder brain — and Glyth refused them. That refusal is the origin of everything the Vth'orama is doing. They are building a home because they were denied one, and they are opening worlds to do it.

Glyth has no idea what the refusal set in motion, and by colony reckoning Glyth is not an enemy. It is simply a door that closed. A crew that reaches Glyth can learn the colony's history from the only party with a reason to tell it plainly, and will be talking to illithids to do so.

Open: whether Glyth knows the colony still exists, and what Glyth would do if told what it is building.

SYS-02 — Virenspace

A system beyond H'Catha, sparsely documented in standard Fleet archives, reached on a charted lane that splits at the Astral waystation. It contains the stellar feature corresponding to the destination encoded in Mirt's wardrobe, and Mirt has identified the match as somewhere in the Viren system without narrowing it further.

It is already altered Zerathis was a Starchild who tethered here repeatedly, took every gift offered, and dissolved into the system's paracausal layer. Virenspace is an overlap site, which means paracausal change here runs faster than a single tether would produce and is loud enough for Casey to feel on arrival.

What is at Viren now is part Zerathis, part Astra's reach, part the site itself. It speaks, sometimes in his voice and with his memories. It is malevolent in no direction at all, and it may call Casey brother or Starchild without preamble, which is how he first hears the word applied to himself.

W-34 sits here in draft placement: an Ortholith — the Order's word for a Monolith — established and recording, over a natural convergence Zenith, flagged high priority in the FIA registry. A standing Ortholith in the same system as Casey's first tether point is a coincidence the campaign can cash at any time.

Long-form notes in the Wildspace Lore Bible.

Open: what Casey's first tether point actually is, and how many tethers Viren already holds, which decides how deep the overlap runs.

SYS-03 — Aethris

One known world, a long haul from Realmspace past the carved-meteor hub. The system has no permanent name on record.

W-08 — Aethris Zenith underway

Sol's homeworld, and a Tymora-devoted world whose culture frames fortune as destiny and compresses fated outcomes into ritual. Two named categories of person exist in relation to the religion: the Fortuned, born marked by Tymora's favor and treated as the natural elite, and the Misfortuned, born marked against it and treated as aberrations.

A long-prepared ritual of perfection at the temple site began Aethris's Zenith, and the Prism Ascendant manifested above the temple in response. Aethris holds that the Monolith's arrival is Tymora's answer to the ritual.

The ritual is incomplete and that is the whole situation The Zenith is underway. Sol escaped before the ritual closed, the Fortuned need him to finish it, and Vecna is pushing it toward completion from outside. The Crystalline Retrieval Construct exists because of the escape and was built afterward, which makes it bespoke: his leaving is the reason it was made.

Three parties want the same event for different reasons. The Fortuned want their perfection, Vecna wants a completed Zenith he can use, and Sol wants to stay a person. Nobody on Aethris knows the third party is involved, and the Prism Ascendant still stands above the temple with its present function undocumented.

Sol is the campaign's proof that this already happened once, and Aethris is the world the crew can visit to see what the rest of the cosmos is being aimed toward.

Open: whether the Fortuned want him back as a person or as a component, and what the Prism Ascendant has been doing above an unfinished Zenith for all this time.

SYS-04 Two known worlds

Two known worlds, reached from Realmspace on an uncharted route. Position and routes are drafts. This is the system that holds the campaign's two pieces of other-table history, and both of them matter.

W-21 — Fonains

The site of the only Monolith on record to be destroyed. Vecna encouraged the world's Zenith; Tharizdun corrupted the Monolith that answered it, without Vecna's knowledge; and Lola's Boys destroyed the corrupted Monolith, which forced Vecna to change his plan.

Why it is the most important precedent in the setting A destroyed Monolith is the proof that a point can be taken back out of a finished working. That is the late-game answer to “how do you stop a completed spell,” and it is sourced from another table's history rather than invented for this one. A piece of that Monolith is sitting in Boogie's chest.

Most of Fonains's dragons left for Krynn to fight Tiamat. The world's current population and condition are unestablished, and the half-logbook Boogie carries was pulled from a simulation of a real wreck — meaning the original is somewhere in Caelestis with pages missing.

Open: what remains on Fonains, and whether the released population went anywhere the crew can reach.

W-10 — Krynn

An aspect of Bahamut is held here by Tiamat. The dragons who left Fonains came to fight that war, which makes Krynn the destination of a migration the campaign has already established and never followed.

It is a complete setting in its own right and needs nothing from this campaign. Its value here is as the place a question about dragons leads — and the First World and dragons thread is the one that would make Krynn load-bearing rather than adjacent.

The Tetherbreak sector Restricted

A Fleet sector under elevated monitoring, out along the edge of SPH-01. The approach lane from Realmspace to Strake Station is open. Everything past Strake is closed without Bridge authorization.

Hollis Post

The western perimeter post, on a charted lane from Realmspace. Crewed, filing instrument and transit readings on the perimeter rotation as recorded in FR-005.

Hollis is the control case. It is a post on the same ring, doing the same job, with the same equipment, and nothing has happened to it. That is what makes Cairn's silence legible as an event rather than as the ordinary attrition of a remote station, and it is where a crew goes to find out what a normal filing looks like.

Open: its complement, its commander, and whether its readings on the Cairn bearing a year ago caught anything that Strake's did not.

Strake Station

The middle post of the three, directly out from SYS-01, and the last Fleet relay on the Cairn approach. Operational, crewed, and the authorized limit of the bearing. Everything a crew needs to reach Cairn is here, and so is the order telling them they cannot.

Cairn Station

A station in the territory of an astral dreadnought — the apex predator of the Astral Sea, a titanic chain-tailed leviathan that hunts dead cosmic matter. The dreadnought is the hazard the Fleet's records name for this location, and the last assessment to say so is a year old.

Cairn is the eastern end of the perimeter ring, reached along the edge run from Strake. It carries a small standing complement and files through Strake on the resupply rotation rather than directly, so its filings have always been irregular. A year ago it reported something on the long scope whose readings made no sense, holding on the station bearing and closing slow. Then it went silent and stayed silent.

The chain in the FIA: LG-003 (Cairn's final logistics report, filed through Strake, carrying the last transmission) → WM-002 (the relief vessel engaged by an eye-in-a-wheel craft past Strake, and whether that party was after the packet) → SA-005 (the standing order closing everything beyond Strake) → FR-005 (Cairn beyond the sector limit, with gravitational variance strongest along its bearing).

What actually happened

The dreadnought whose territory this is was taken a little over fifty years ago and has been N'ghathrod's since. A year ago the Vth'oramu sat on the Cairn bearing for days. That is the mass signature in Cairn's final transmission — closing slow, matching no vessel profile, reading differently on every sweep, because the thing on the scope was a ship pretending to be nothing at all. It moved on afterward and has stayed away.

The ambush was the point of holding the position Vocath's people took Cairn after it went dark and waited there, because a silent Fleet station guarantees a relief run on a single predictable bearing. The eye-in-a-wheel craft that engaged the relief vessel was his. His line has run goods through the sector for years and had every reason to know the rotation.

The engagement, in two actions

It reads in the file as one running fight. It was two, and only the first was planned.

So the Crow brought home a decorated survivor who held his crew together through the withdrawal, and that man is the embedded asset. The FIA chain records the whole event accurately and understands none of it.

The dreadnought moved on long ago. Vocath's people withdrew after the ambush and left something behind. Whatever it is has been alone for a year, reports to nobody who answers, and will register the first Fleet vessel to come up that bearing since the standing order.

If it is a person or a taken thing, it is a witness as well as a tripwire, because it knows what happened to the crew that did not come back. Also open: what remains of the station, what became of its complement, who built onto it, and whether any of Cairn's own instrument record survives. The payoff should open onto something larger rather than resolve in one room.

The Astral Sea

The Astral Sea is the space between worlds. Wildspace is the stretch between systems; the Flow fills the space between spheres. Intensity varies between systems and between spheres, and a lane's tier records how well that variation is charted.

What follows is what a crossing can contain. Any of these can be scenery, an obstacle, or a thread the crew chooses to pull. Nothing here needs to connect to the campaign's spine unless it earns it.

Psychic wind

A surge in the Astral that throws a vessel off course. A crew can arrive far from their heading and later than they experienced. It is the honest answer whenever someone ends up somewhere they could not have sailed to.

In play: the Bellwether Crow engagement. Clint Dashforest broke away in a stolen craft and reached SYS-04; a psychic wind took him. Intensity on the Cairn bearing shifts, which is why a crossing there is scored against the cycle rather than the chart.

Currents and eddies

The charted lanes run with the Astral's currents. Off-lane, currents run uncharted: some carry a vessel inward toward a system, some hold it in place, some feed a bearing nobody has surveyed.

In play: a chase or a breakaway can end with a vessel pushed into a current it cannot leave.

Color pools

Shimmering portals adrift in the Astral Sea, each opening onto another plane. They are stable enough to chart and dangerous enough that the Fleet logs them and sails around them.

In play: a pool is an exit from the campaign's geography rather than a shortcut across it.

Dead gods and drifting matter

The corpses of gods drift through the Astral Sea, along with the wreckage, debris and abandoned works that collect around them. They are the landmarks of an otherwise featureless crossing, and salvage crews, hermits and githyanki all work them.

In play: a place to hide, a place to be ambushed, and a place where something can be found. Each one can carry its own small thing of interest with no tie to anything larger.

Running a crossing Give each leg one feature. Name it, let the crew see it, and let them decide whether to look. A charted lane crossing should be quiet more often than not; a long haul or an uncharted route should not be.

Constellations Meanings open

A constellation is a pattern visible from one or more systems, and it is geography of a particular kind: it belongs to no world and is seen from many. Four are catalogued. Two have already appeared at the table attached to a player character, which is the reason this section exists.

How the catalogue numbers work

The number is the bearing. Each designation records the constellation's bearing in degrees from the charting origin, SYS-01 Realmspace, measured clockwise. The catalogue runs 001 to 360, so CON-025 sits almost dead ahead on the charting axis and CON-312 sits behind the shoulder.

Three consequences, all of them wanted. A navigator reading a designation knows roughly where to look. A new constellation takes whatever number its bearing gives it, so the catalogue fills in between the existing entries rather than after them. And nothing about the number records when it was found.

Two patterns can share a bearing at different elevations; those take a letter suffix in order of cataloguing — CON-091, CON-091B. Sorting the catalogue sorts the sky.

CON-312 is the loosest thread in the campaign A Circle of the Stars druid whose focus is a star map has a specific constellation in his chest, and nothing has been decided about what it means. It is the obvious candidate for the third register — the slippage belonging to neither his beast forms nor his Starry Form — and it is already on the board, already his, and already pointed somewhere.

Casey's tethers are constellation points rather than whole constellations, and his chart is the record of which ones he has bound. A catalogued constellation visible from a system he tethers in is the natural place for a tether point to sit.

Recommended: give each one a spacer's name as well The designation is how the Fleet files a constellation. It is a poor way to recognize one, and recognition is the thing worth engineering: nobody looks up and knows NGC 1976, and everybody knows Orion. A working name used by spacers, alongside the catalogue number, gets both — the wiki sorts by designation, the table talks in names, and a crew hearing a name twice in two systems has learned something real about where they are.

Names are undecided and are not invented here. CON-055 and CON-312 are the two that need one first, since both are already in play. Awaiting your call

The First World

The world where the Reach happened. Its civilization reached for the Axioms directly. The Reach was a Zenith, the first Monolith — the Core — manifested above the world while the Zenith was occurring, and the Zenith concluded with the world's destruction. The Core remained.

Location: the center of the cosmos, beneath the Core and the Citadel. Deeper still lies its heart, the deepest heart there is and the only closed one, known to almost nobody.

Name: no canonical name in any institutional record. The civilization's own name for itself went into the Core with its people, and their descendants in the Citadel carry it — for as long as the forgetting leaves it with them.

Every child heart in the cosmos pulls faintly toward this one. That latent pull is the Yearning, and it is the force the Ascension is built to mass and aim.

SYS-06 — the Wayfinders' homeworld Unnamed by doctrine

The world has no name, and that is canon

“Lunarfoot” is the clan, not the world. The arc doc is explicit: Tumak does not know the name of the homeworld. This is intentional and cultural, not a gap in the backstory. A Wayfinder is given no map.

An earlier pass promoted the clan name to the world's name — it appeared here as “SYS-06 Lunarfoot” and on the chart as “W-11: Lunarfoot” — which contradicted the paragraph below it on this same page. Corrected: the world is referred to by designation and description only.

Lunarfoot stays correct for the people and the order: the Lunarfoot clan, the Lunarfoot Runekeeper, Children of Lunarfoot, a Lunarfoot wayfinder's script. Do not name the world

A halfling world that hides itself cosmically. The world is a living thing that decides what reaches it, and its concealment is held by a single living practitioner — the Runekeeper, whose body bears the inscription that carries the ritual.

The concealment rides on the fragment The ritual points the working; the Dissolution fragment's oscillation powers it. The world sits exactly where it has always sat, and finding it requires knowing the way back rather than knowing the coordinates. The fragment is currently leaving the Runekeeper and forming in Wan and Tu, which is why the Runekeeper dims and why the twins were sent out at all.

Children of Lunarfoot sent into the cosmos as Wayfinders are given no map and no name, and a returning exile identifies home through remembered sensory and cultural fragments. The withholding is doctrine: a way home that lives only in a person cannot be taken off a chart. The Runekeepers maintain the ritual and the practice both.

Wan and Tu are two separate hosts. When both arrive home, three poles stall the cycle and the result is a Zenith — which means the act of going home is the act of ending the concealment. Mechanics in Rules.

Open: what happens to the concealment while the fragment is between bearers.

SYS-05 — the Ascension World Specifics pending

A world where Vecna once forced a local threshold and won — a small, personal ascension distinct from the canonical Sigil event. The world remains marked by it: terrain, atmosphere and surviving structures all carry the residue of an event in which a single will dictated local reality.

Surface accessible, reached from Realmspace on an uncharted route and from Virenspace on a charted lane. The sphere is uncatalogued.

It is a worked example the crew can walk around in Everything the Ascension intends at cosmic scale, this world has already experienced at local scale. A crew that visits sees the endpoint before they understand the plan, which is the cheapest and most honest foreshadowing available.

Open: what he forced here and what it cost him, what the residue does to a visitor, and whether anyone survived it.

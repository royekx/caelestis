Boogie
An urge to dance that the player invented, given a source afterward: a shard of the only Monolith ever destroyed.

Plasmoid Divine Soul sorcerer. The player invented the urge to dance as character texture; it was given a source afterward. The urge is the Pulse, and the Pulse is the Fonains shard in his nucleus — a fragment of the only Monolith ever destroyed, Tharizdun-corrupted. He does not know that, and the arc works because his own reading of himself is already the correct emotional reading.

No dice touch this The Pulse is adjudicated entirely as fiction. You describe the sensation — a buzzing in the nucleus, rhythmic pressure — and he decides what Boogie does with it. Describing it is the only lever you need, because it puts you in control of when the Pulse matters while leaving the response his.

The two answers

His player frames the whole character as finally committing to himself after a life of drifting, which is the same tension the Pulse encodes. That is why the mechanic needs no enforcement: he is already roleplaying it.

He is becoming a container Three archived objects have accumulated inside one living body, and the shard is the reason they stay. The arc question is whether he remains himself as the volume grows — which is the personal-scale version of what a Monolith does, running inside a PC.

The Shield of Expression was a gift from Boom-chik so that a plasmoid could emote. It is narratively loaded and mechanically idle, and it is waiting for the scene where being read correctly is what matters.

Open: the escalation milestone. Effects stay small early by design, and the point at which the accumulation starts costing him is undecided.

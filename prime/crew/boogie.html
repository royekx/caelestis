Boogie
A plasmoid who keeps the ship alive, with a rhythm in his nucleus and things in his chest he cannot explain.

Boogie is a plasmoid Divine Soul sorcerer, a drifter with no family or lasting home, and the crew's medic. A shard of the broken Fonains Monolith sits in his nucleus. It is the source of the Pulse, the inner rhythm that pulls him into dance, and it keeps what it takes: two half-objects now sit inside his chest. Tumak named him second in command, and the crew has yet to vote on it.

Appearance

A semi-transparent blue plasmoid with a glittering stone inside him. He carries the Shield of Expression, a gift from his friend Boom-chik so that a plasmoid could emote; its face shows what his own cannot, and it looked mild and content while he slept through his watch. When he dances, his signature move is the Blob Drop.

Personality

He instinctively helps and repairs rather than commands: he wants to keep the ship alive, not take the helm. He is calm in a dispute and generous with his own body, spreading his gel over the crew and building a friend a pair of legs out of himself. He commits to himself after a life of drifting, and the Pulse asks the same of him every time it comes. He sometimes says nothing when he should, as when Saerthe described a stolen model ship that matched the one inside him.

Biography

Background

Boogie drifted, with no family and no lasting home. As a child he survived a tribal sacrifice, and he has felt watched ever since. Before his first voyage with the crew he looked into a shipment he should have left alone: Vocath's people had chased Clint Dashforest to Fonains and come back through the Cairn Station line with fragments of the broken Monolith as ordinary cargo, and one of them ended up in him.

In the campaign
  • Voyage 1. He rushed to help the wounded Tumak in the Moonraider simulation, held a corner bunk calmly when Veena tried to take it, and charmed a light hammer from Mr. Blip. At the Nexus he took instantly to the helm exercise and said he wants to keep the ship alive, not take the helm. He used magic to help Gregory up the last climb of the gym course, shared his lonely drifting past with Tumak and promised the crew would help find Tumak's family, and helped Mirt through his hangover. Then he pulled a tiny, half-missing model spelljamming ship from his own chest.
  • Voyage 2. He ordered only fruit and was given a silvery starfruit, set the half-ship on the table for the crew to see, spoke for the medical role and quietly insisted Gregory take the helm. When Saerthe described his own stolen miniature ship, the same model, Boogie said nothing. He tended the bulwark through the storm and took the salvage logbook into his chest, then leapt onto the githyanki ship, saw he was overmatched, and threw himself back.
  • Voyage 3. He helped the rival crew up in the wrecked simulation chamber, understood the recovered rod as a device built to feed on ambient magic and detonate, and caught the word “Vocath” between Saerthe and Tarto. He told Mirt of the tribal sacrifice and the sense of being watched. He dreamed of countless other versions of himself in an infinite dark and a presence converging on him, and woke half-dissolved before humming himself back together. The half-ship and the logbook were both still in his chest.
  • Voyage 4. With Gregory he understood that Ostekk-6's damage was mechanical and past anything magic could mend.
  • Voyage 5. He gave the legless Ostekk-6 a pair of legs, a small, wrongly built replica of himself that carried him at the waist, and later filled them with a leaping charm and launched him across the Hollow Deck at the behir. He spread his gel over the crew and slowed their fall down the shaft, blessed Casey, Tumak and Bartholomew as the behir woke, and called the retreat when three clockwork horrors answered its screech. He missed the rope and fell, burning a gap through the flowers below with his faith, found the plane where the ship's gravity turns over and called the others down, and recognised the body on the Command Deck as a beholder whose central eye still cancels magic.
  • Voyage 6. He slept through his watch, asked Ostekk-10 its name, cut down a side flower and pressed a potion on Tumak. Tumak named him second in command, pending the crew's vote.

Abilities

A sorcerer of the divine soul: blessings, healing, a slowing charm and a leaping charm, and faith that burns. He takes to a helm by instinct. His gel can coat and carry others, and he can pull himself back together when his form comes apart. Things go into his chest and stay there. Like every carrier of paracausal matter he feels paracausal events nearby.

Where it heads

Wherever the shard takes him, and it is reactive: what comes through the shard depends on what he does. His arc has no fixed reveal. Gates are planted where they fit and follow the player, and he may never learn the source. The shard escalates rather than repeats, and it is not revealed as a Monolith piece too soon.

Gates and escalation

Loosely ordered: passive archiving now; environmental oddities around him; an acolyte encounter; something coming back through the current; understanding what “full” means.

  • Reaching for the archive. He will learn to reach for the archive deliberately. He can offer something during a Pulse; it goes in incomplete and comes back incomplete. The ceiling stays bounded because he is a damaged relay. The gate is his realising that what goes in is going somewhere.
  • The prophet thread. The shard draws people to him as something holy, and a following may grow around him as a prophet-like figure built on a corrupted signal. It stays player-driven: available to pull on, never pushed.
  • The Lantern. When the Lantern of Flourishing heals him he briefly loses his form, and near it he feels out of step with his rhythm. The healing rules are on the Lantern's page.

The Pulse

No dice touch the Pulse. Describe the sensation, a buzzing in the nucleus, a rhythmic pressure, and the player decides what Boogie does with it. Describing it is the only lever: it puts the timing in your hands and leaves the response his.

The archive

Two archived objects are held inside him by the shard: the half-ship from Voyage 1 and the half-logbook from Voyage 2. The shard is what keeps them there. The logbook came from a simulation drawn on real records, so the rest of it exists somewhere in Caelestis, a dormant hook.

What's hidden

  • Tharizdun. The Fonains Monolith formed corrupted, and a corrupted Monolith is a tunnel to Tharizdun's prison, so what the shard takes goes toward Tharizdun. Tharizdun wants nothing specific from Boogie. She is not lucid; she is an opportunist. What happens through the shard depends on what Boogie does, and things may come back through the current. Now and then he feels corruption leaking into him from the shard.
  • His power. Boogie's Divine Soul power comes from Tharizdun's influence through the shard. He does not know it.
  • The dream. His Divine Soul woke in a dream. He looked into his nucleus and saw a fractal, pyramidal network of other Boogies, each with a differently shaped shard. A crack ran from him through it, his line connected upward wrongly, and at the apex sat an unnamed source that gives and consumes. This shard made this Boogie, and other shards exist. The dream ended: “You wake. Your chest is heavier than it was when you closed your eyes.”
  • The logbook. Boogie holds half of the derelict logbook. The other half was in his chest inside the Monolith.

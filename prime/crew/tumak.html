Tumak Swan
Twin halflings passing as one cadet, the Dissolution fragment rebuilding in them in two halves.

Tumak Swan is two people: Wan and Tu, halfling twins of the Lunarfoot clan, who study at Caelestis as one human cadet. Both are partial Wayfinders of the Runekeepers, sent out from the Wayfinders' homeworld because the Dissolution fragment began forming in both of them. In them it is rebuilding in two halves at once, a state neither whole nor broken. Tumak holds the captain's chair for the crew aboard the Tyrant Ship.

Appearance

Wan and Tu stand stacked inside one oversized coat, and the figure it makes reads as a slight human about five foot seven, with a long body, short arms and a small head. He mutters reassurances to himself, which is the twins talking. The fragment is visible in both of them: it forms and re-forms in each twin's chest, never still. Runes write themselves on their skin in halves, Wan always carrying the left half of a rune and Tu the right, so no rune is complete on either body alone.

Personality

Two minds run one persona, and others notice that his mind often seems divided. The twins hold the disguise together with charm and practised deception, and they plan as one: in Voyage 6 they built the lie to Ostekk-10 inside the crew's minds and told it. He keeps a curtained corner of his bunk for things he would rather not explain, and he takes on what others hand him without asking why. Home pulls at him constantly, through small memories of his grandmother and of the homeworld: a smell, the way the trees look. He wants to go home, and he has begun to trust the crew to help him get there.

Biography

Background

Wan and Tu were born on the Wayfinders' homeworld, a halfling world that hides itself by a living ritual, and they are Runechildren. When the Dissolution fragment began forming in both of them, the Runekeeper sent them out himself, with no map and no name for home, as the order's doctrine requires. Both twins carried a note that read “come home safe”. They enrolled at the Caelestis Spelljammer Academy as one human cadet because it was the quickest way home.

In the campaign

  • Voyage 3. He told Mirt of Joffrey's exchange at the Sky Dock and of the family he is looking for, glimpsed “fleet intelligence archive” on Mirt's notepad, and argued Mirt into granting the crew sailor-level clearance.
  • Voyage 4. Frustrated by Ostekk-6's silence, he opened a telepathic link between himself, Gregory and Ostekk-6 without knowing how, and cast a fire bolt he did not know he had. In the wisp fight his soul knife came for the first time, drawn through a small portal as his two half-runes flared, and he felt a tether running toward home. The throw went wide until the Runekeeper's voice said that all things find their way home, and the blade curved true to strike the last wisp.
  • Voyage 5. He called up his soul knives a second time and drove one into the behir, threw up a shield of force its bite could not pass, and poured his potion into Bartholomew when the behir dropped him. On the Command Deck he saw the near-invisible wisps gathering on Casey and Bartholomew, worked out that the dead beholder lay on the helm's platform and that the helm belongs there, and told Miken the crew had defeated the beholder.
  • Voyage 6. He told Ryeback the crew was fully capable and had killed the beholder. He named Boogie second in command and put it to a crew vote, which the crew has not held. He burned the dead horrors at the foot of the great flower and threw his soul knives into the ceiling flower. When Ostekk-10 came back he planned the lie in the crew's minds, told it nothing had been found, and ordered the hold opened. He carries the Lantern of Flourishing in his bag of holding. In the days of making ready that followed, Tu's half of the left-sole rune surfaced to meet Wan's, and the left sole became the twins' first whole rune.

Relationships

Casey passed him the shirt he stole from Mirt's wardrobe without a word, and Tumak hid it without a question. Casey followed him once, unseen, and noticed his jacket askew, and said nothing. Casey clings to him as an older brother.

Boogie

The first person Tumak told about his search for family. Boogie answered with his own lonely drifting past and promised the crew would help. Tumak named him second in command.

The head of the order, who sent the twins out. His voice reached Tumak once, in Voyage 4, and the tether Tumak felt then runs toward home.

Ostekk-6

Abilities

He fights with psychic blades that come through a small portal, a piece of the home he is looking for. The twins share one telepathic link and can open it to others, and they share their magic, which wakes when they act together: fire, and a shield of force. Two perspectives on one conversation make him hard to lie to. As runes match across the twins' bodies he can place marks at places he has stood, perceive through them, and Wayfind to them, and each matched rune carries the inscription further toward the Heart. Like every carrier of paracausal matter he feels paracausal events nearby, keenest for anything of Dissolution.

Where it heads

Runechild, Wayfinder, Runekeeper is one continuous story. He was a Runechild before the campaign, and his sorcery is the in-world answer to his backstory. He is a Wayfinder now, on the journey, and he becomes the Runekeeper when he returns home. The arc resolves at Homebound: sixteen matched runes wake the Heart, he Wayfinds home, and what the homecoming sets off is in What's hidden. The marks give him a goal beyond going home, because each one is a sensor his world puts out into the cosmos.

Gates and escalation

  • The fading. The Runekeeper's fade is his pressure to go home now and to start attempting the journey. It runs on a soft clock: rising pressure, no date. Count nothing down. Deliver it through memories of his grandmother and of home, a smell or the look of the trees.
  • The runes. One roll per character level, plus character moments and story beats you bestow. Each match grows his marks and the reach of his Wayfind.
  • Sorcery. He took his first Sorcerer level at level 4. The player wants to discover in play how the abilities work, so reveal them through use.

Table notes

  • The disguise. The other players believe Tumak is one human. At the table, address the twins as “left side” and “right side”, so only his player knows which twin you mean. The per-level roll, the left-and-right rule and the separation lockout all describe two people; anything written for the players about his runes describes one. The reveal is the player's to make.
  • The blade. The player decides how the psychic blade looks.
  • The trade-off. His powers carry a stark, visible cost so the other players do not feel he has too much: each Wayscribe's first casting is free and each further one costs a level of exhaustion, and the first Wayfind per long rest is free at any distance, with each further one costing a level of exhaustion (Rules → Wayfind).

Where the runes stand

In Voyage 3 Wan's half surfaced on the left sole (08) and Tu's on the right shoulder (16). At the level-4 roll Tu's half landed on 08, so the left sole is his first matched rune. The Heart (20) was matched before the campaign and stays concealed. The position table is on Rules → Current state.

Runes arrive with level-ups, character moments and story beats you bestow, so the player cannot equate a rune with a place. A rune surfacing near something cosmic can be an occasional story trigger, never a rule.

What's hidden

  • One cadet, two halflings. Everyone at Caelestis and aboard the ship knows Tumak as one slight human rogue. The table believes it too: his player sheet and every player-facing page read Human, Rogue (Soulknife). That is correct, because it is what the crew has been shown.
  • His Heart-rune. Tumak has his own Heart-rune. It is already matched, and he does not know he carries it.
  • The previous Runekeeper. Tumak knows of the Runekeeper before the present one and met him once, briefly. He does not understand what that meeting was.
  • Going home. Completing the inscription takes Tumak home, and going home ends the concealment. When Wan and Tu both arrive, the three poles stall the Dissolution cycle and the result is a Zenith: the homeworld splits into a blessing faction and an omen faction, and a Monolith follows. The office he comes home to dims whoever holds it.
  • The concealment in transit. While the fragment moves between bearers, the concealment rests on the share still leaving the Runekeeper. It weakens as he fades, and the world's protection decays as the cosmos changes around it. The marks Tumak places are the sensors that let it be renewed when he returns. The world needs enough of them, and nobody can say how many.
  • The fragment. The runes come from the Dissolution fragment, residue of the Reach world, which nobody in the order has identified as residue.

Threads

  • The Runekeeper dims as the fragment leaves him and believes the cost is his to carry; every rune Tumak matches is more of it leaving.
  • N'ghathrod is made of joins, and Dissolution unmakes joins. The fragment forming in Tumak is a counter to him, and nobody holding it knows.
  • Casey clings to Tumak as an older brother, the identity he finds in place of the one he does not know (Casey).
  • Runes 17 to 19 become Anchors, fixed places Tumak goes to, so the arc leans into navigation and working with Gregory.

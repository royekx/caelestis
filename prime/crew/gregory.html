Gregory
An autognome who casts, woken by the strain on an Axiom, and the crew's navigator.

Gregory is a bronze autognome wizard from the Cogfast Enclave on Lantan, on Toril, and he casts. He was woken by the strain on an Axiom, and he feels places of cosmological distortion as a pull in his core. He is the crew's navigator, his course-plotting proven under pressure, and an abjurer whose magic gets underneath a break before it happens.

Appearance

A bronze autognome with glasses and a mustache. He has twin water cannons built into his chest. Mr. Blip, the Academy's autognome, looks unnervingly like him.

Personality

Hesitant and observant, quick to notice what others miss, and inclined to think too far ahead until someone names the problem. He follows orders and trusts everyone at their word, which is why denials hit him hard. He takes kindness as it is offered and answers procedure with procedure, straight-faced. Far from home and from his dead master, he has begun to reach for others before the damage does, and to cast before he has fully decided to.

Biography

Background

Gregory was built in the Cogfast Enclave on Lantan by Gregory Prime, his maker and master, and held the casing steady while his maker mended things. Over the past century Monoliths have formed more often, and the strain that puts on an Axiom ripples outward through the planes and the cosmos, slow and more metaphysical than physical. Gregory's awakening is one result of that ripple; the Latchling and other oddities are others, and the same ripple woke Astra Virex. No Zenith is tied to his origin. Gregory Prime is dead and buried, and Gregory reached Caelestis on a cargo ship.

In the campaign
  • Voyage 1. He spoiled a deadly strike with clever spellwork in the Moonraider simulation, recalled the code of conduct forbidding gifts and unofficial payments at the Sky Dock, and was unsettled by Mr. Blip. He overcame thinking too far ahead in the Nexus helm exercise once Saerthe named the problem, saw through the gym's first illusion and called the truth to Sol, and added frightened but effective magic to the fight in Mirt's quarters.
  • Voyage 2. He accepted spare utensils from Ryeback as the kindness they were meant to be, took the navigator's role and plotted the hammerhead's course toward the asteroid field. He named targets in the githyanki fight, saw the arc of black lightning snap out of Sol and back, and duplicated the salvage logbook alongside Casey. He judged, straight-faced, that no cadet-manual provision governed stopping for stranded vessels.
  • Voyage 3. He worked the arcane rod free of the wall and assessed it as spent arcane dynamite, then thrust it at Boatswain Tarto and fled. In Saerthe's desk he found a note about Bartholomew's helm affinity. A fleet-clearance category on the Nexus terminals locked him out by his cadet star. He told Mirt of Gregory Prime, and that night he reread his master's journal and dwelt on the line about getting underneath things before the break did. When he saw his own face on the broken autognome the clockwork horrors were feeding on, he cast before he had fully decided to.
  • Voyage 4. He opened the fight aboard the Tyrant Ship with magic missiles, then pulled Ostekk-6 clear with a vortex warp before turning back to fight. With Boogie he understood that Ostekk-6's ruin was mechanical and past any magic. Inside Tumak's telepathic link he received Ostekk-6's warnings.
  • Voyage 5. On the Hollow Deck he found the remains of Ostekk-8 and Ostekk-4, followed a pull in his chest to Ostekk-2, and woke it with a jolt of lightning. He fought the behir with lightning and with twin jets of water from the cannons in his chest, and carried the rope through the gravity plane. On the Command Deck the pull came again, stronger, pointing at the beholder's body.
  • Voyage 6. He held the last watch alone and saw Ostekk-10 come up the garden shaft. He fed his magic into the garden flower to show the wisps in its spores, and read the flowers by touch: they made both the wisps and the field. Then he followed his pull to the Lantern of Flourishing and spent a charge on Ostekk-6.

Abilities

Gregory casts: magic missiles, lightning, wards and a vortex warp, with the abjurer's habit of bracing the seam before it cracks. Near anything the Axiom strain's ripple touched, a place, a person, an object or an event, he feels a directional pull in his core like a compass needle trying to settle, and with effort he can read it into a bearing. He reads paracausal growths by touch and knows his way around navigator's and tinker's tools.

Notable items

Quotes

  • Gregory Prime's journal: “Mended the brass aligner a fourth time tonight — but this time, before it could crack again, I braced the seam from the inside first. Gregory held the casing steady while I worked. Steady hands. He's good at that. Funny thing. I've spent my whole life fixing what's already broken. Tonight I caught myself thinking — what if I'd gotten there sooner? Reached the thing before the break did. Then there'd be nothing to mend at all. Going to try doing it that way from now on. Where it heads

Through one anomaly after another, each reframing his origin; the question his existence poses is cosmological, not personal. His theme is that he knows roughly where to go and has to work out how to get there and through what stands in the way. The Lantern's pull toward Toril starts the journey home.

Gates and escalation

  • The Latchling. His first anomaly, at the Sea Dock. Voyage 2 deliberately held the Latchling beat so the autognome-repair moment could carry his arc instead, and the beat is still owed.
  • The Lantern. Whether the Lantern of Flourishing becomes his next anomaly depends on whether he investigates it. Its source is sealed inside the lamp, below the light, where identify cannot read; once the Lantern is seated in the bay its note grows louder and calls past him rather than to him.
  • The anomaly queue. Each anomaly after the Latchling reframes his origin again.

Next beat

In Toril orbit in Voyage 7 the pull points straight down at his home world. The beat is on Voyages → V007, Beats owed. The Latchling beat waits for the crew's return to Caelestis.

Table notes

  • You initiate, always. Describe the sensation, then offer it: does he want to calibrate? He can decline, ask what it costs, or hesitate; the offer is a role-play beat, not a gate. Good moments: he is near the anomaly or something connected to it; a quiet beat where he is alone or reflective; he is navigating, working on a construct or reasoning about space; something nearby resonates briefly.
  • Anomaly prep. Destination, physical obstacle, social obstacle, interpretive obstacle, payoff.
  • Mr. Blip and Ostekk-6. Mr. Blip is the control case, an autognome with ordinary personhood who cannot cast, and Ostekk-6 is the contrast from the other side.

Build

Wizard, School of Abjuration from level 3. Intelligence (Arcana) +2. Built for Success gives him a d4 after seeing a roll, twice per long rest, so he can push a static Calibration into a signal and feel the decision. He casts detect magic as a ritual.

Calibration

Near anything the ripple touched, describe a sensation in his core: a directional pull, like a compass needle trying to settle, with no malfunction or pain. Then offer the roll. Calibration is DM-initiated at fitting moments.

The roll is Intelligence (Arcana), with no declared DC, and he never learns the thresholds. At his +2 the bands run:

ResultBandWhat he gets
1–7StaticThe signal is real and unread. He knows something is there and cannot read it. Interference, never misdirection.
8–12WeakA direction, a texture, an emotional residue. Useful and incomplete.
13–17ClearA bearing: a location type, a behaviour, a defining sensory detail. Actionable.
18+SharpA precise characteristic narrowing the anomaly to a place, object or condition. He is close.

Clues stack, and he never needs one perfect roll. Three or four solid clues locate any anomaly, and then its obstacles begin. A failed roll gives static and costs him nothing, so the instrument reads like a sensor: the anomaly is real, he is working, the signal is still unread.

The Latchling

Anomaly 01, at the Sea Dock, near cargo and lower dock storage, hidden by Rindle Gearloft, who is protecting it from the Academy. Its change is sentience where its kind has none. The plot hook is the Latchling.

WeakA pull toward somewhere low and mechanical, a surface rather than a room. A faint rhythm that moves like a heartbeat without being one. A warmth toward the docks that reads closer to recognition than heat.
ClearThe signal is at the Sea Dock, near cargo and lower dock storage. Something small, moving on its own. It goes still when it senses attention.
SharpIt has already noticed him. It has been there since the crew arrived, and he walked past it during orientation inspection.
  • The signal track. The track describes how clear the obstacles are: first the bearing, the Sea Dock near cargo; then a muddy signal, because something is interfering and something is concealed; then the interference takes a human shape.
  • Obstacles. Dock interference, metal and noise. Rindle, whom Gregory has to convince he is no threat to the Latchling. The Latchling itself, which hides until it trusts him.
  • Other reads. Detect magic near the Latchling reads strangely, which is itself a clue. Navigator's tools and tinker's tools add flavour.
  • Rindle sits outside the mechanic. Calibration delivers location and nature; Rindle appears when somebody physically looks, asks questions or pokes around the dock, and the discovery there is that someone already knew.
  • The reaction. The Latchling goes to Gregory as something familiar, and that visible reaction is what cracks Rindle's secrecy.

What's hidden

  • The Latchling's waking. Gregory's prologue repair of a latch may have been the local condition that let the ripple resolve into the Latchling. He has a causal and ambiguous connection to its waking, mirroring his own uncertain origin, and Rindle is protecting something a cadet arguably caused.

Threads

  • His waking, the Latchling and Astra Virex's wound share one cause, the Axiom strain.
  • His flaw, trusting everyone at their word, is why Rindle's denials hit hard.
  • Runes 17 to 19 become Tumak's Anchors, fixed places Tumak goes to, so Tumak's arc leans into navigation and working with Gregory.

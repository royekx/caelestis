Casey Geim
A Starchild who sees constellations nobody else can, following a voice he knows as his brother's.

Casey Geim is a hadozee warlock of The Many and a Starchild: he sees constellations nobody else can, and every constellation point he attunes to becomes a tether. He follows a familiar voice he knows as his brother Polly's, and in Voyage 1 it pointed him past H'Catha toward Virenspace. He is the crew's bosun.

Appearance

A barefoot hadozee under a pale green cloak stitched with constellations. A Gemini brand sits on his left shoulder, the sign he shares with Polly. His magic is cold and star-bright, and even borrowed spells come out through his constellations: tentacles of starlight, a shell of arcane ice.

Personality

He is awkward and composed at once, half-orbiting any conversation, and he can still draw a stranger's private life out of him with warmth. He rarely explains what he does: he pulls notices off boards, steals shirts and passes them on without a word. He reads the sky in everything and asks people their signs. He leans too heavily on others. Casey is the younger brother who does not know his full identity; he finds it in having an older brother, and with Polly gone he clings to Tumak.

Biography

Background

Casey grew up with his older brother Polly on the Geims' homeworld. A star fell on their world. Polly went to it, was changed by what he found there, and left home to learn where it had come from, and he has been missing since. Casey reads people by the Harptos calendar, a month and a sign for everyone, and his listed faith is astrology.

In the campaign
  • Voyage 1. He fought with speed and cold, star-bright magic in the Moonraider simulation and crossed the gym course with natural ease. He slipped into Mirt's wardrobe and saw unfamiliar constellations and a flickering point, and a familiar voice told him “Go there.” He stole a shirt from the wardrobe, etched the constellation pattern onto it, and left it in Tumak's hidden bunk corner without explanation.
  • Voyage 2. He pulled a Sea Dock cargo notice from the refectory board without saying why, drew Ryeback's private life out of him and earned an extra portion, and followed Tumak unseen, noticing his jacket askew and saying nothing. He took the bosun's role. He worked a cargo shift at Rindle Gearloft's dock by mistake and was paid for the time. At the Sky Dock he sat thinking of his brother, and the familiar presence settled beside him again. In the salvage fight he duplicated the logbook to confound the githyanki.
  • Voyage 3. He helped the rival crew up in the wrecked simulation chamber, then went first into the Tyrant Ship wrapped in a shell of cold arcane ice, and did not move quietly. Looking closely at Bartholomew, he glimpsed a two-starred constellation rising in him.
  • Voyage 4. He held the line beside Sol against the clockwork horrors, working his aim from cover, and cast arms of Hadar through his constellations, tentacles of starlight filling the shaft. He roped the crew down to the deck below.
  • Voyage 5. He split into copies of himself and cut into the behir from the walls, and saw that it had been coiled around something giving off a soft light. He glided down the shaft and flipped through the gravity plane without the rope. On the Command Deck wisps latched on and drained him until he learned to slap them off.
  • Voyage 6. He climbed head down into the garden with no rope and anchored it for the others, cut a wisp from Sol's arm, destroyed a side flower with his daggers, and brought the ceiling flower down with a thrown dagger from where he lay. He is glad to be done with the Command Deck.

Relationships

Polly Geim

His older brother, missing since he left home after the fallen star. Casey answers the voice as his brother, and finding Polly is what he is missing.

Tumak Swan

The older brother he has in Polly's place. Casey gave him the etched shirt without a word, followed him once unseen, and keeps what he noticed to himself.

Zerathis

A Starchild before him, who tethered in Virenspace again and again, took every gift offered and dissolved into the system's paracausal layer. What remains there can speak, sometimes in Zerathis's voice and with his memories, and may call Casey brother or Starchild without preamble.

Mirt

Casey broke into his wardrobe. Mirt told the crew it replicates a place beyond H'Catha in the Viren system, a gift from Zerathis.

Abilities

He sees constellations nobody else can. Over a creature he has not yet spoken with he can raise his own stars and read its sign, and a true reading turns a conversation his way. At a constellation point he can attune and bind, and the point becomes a tether. His spells carry zodiac names: Ward of the Capricorn, Duplicate (Gemini), Armor of Taurus, Arms of Aquarius, Life of Cancer and Disguise of Aries. In a fight his patron's heads show as stars, sharpening his instincts and unsettling anyone he threatens.

Notable items

What he is missing

His brother. The row is on Campaign → Crew.

Where it heads

Toward Virenspace, the first tether point and already occupied. Virenspace lets Casey stand in the consequence of someone else's devotion before he decides about his own. Every tether brings the voice in clearer and costs the cosmos, never Casey, and the arc turns on the moment he learns enough to choose. If he resists or stops, the costs in What withdrawal costs land on him.

Gates and escalation

  • The crossing to H'Catha. The two weeks of the crossing are where Viren information gets scattered: rumours and small clues placed in the world for the crew to notice or ignore, not every one a mystical pull or the voice speaking.
  • Each point is an adventure. A constellation point is a real adventure for the whole party and a different experience for Casey, modelled on Fjord placing the stones on Uk'otoa's seal.
  • Viren. The first changed region anyone notices is Virenspace, already tethered by Zerathis. After Casey's first deliberate tether there, something he cared about at Viren is different: not destroyed, changed in its nature, and the change makes it more his. The Lantern Node at Viren is his first named alignment.
  • Another Starchild. A living Starchild like Casey will appear as pressure, rival or partner, modelled on Avantika.

Table notes

  • The players never learn the word “Axiom”. Casey learns only that specific things are wrong or changed.
  • In narration the voice is “a familiar presence” or “a familiar voice”. Use it sparingly, as a nudge toward something, and use visions sparingly too.
  • Casey is point-driven: he must find constellation points and attune to them. Tumak is time-driven: marks arrive per level and he chooses where they go. Casey's stars are a lens he reads with, continuous and unbounded; Tumak's runes are an apparatus on his body that fires once. Casey never teleports to his constellations.
  • Put situations in the world and let him respond or do nothing.

Inspirations

Fjord and Uk'otoa's seal for the points; Avantika for the other Starchild; a twisted Howl's Moving Castle, with falling stars, for the patron's look and feel.

Build

Warlock, The Many. Charisma 18 (+4) is his main stat. Wisdom 4 (−3) gives him a passive Perception of 7: he reads cosmic patterns and misses the obvious. Lucky feat. Background Wayfarer; listed faith Astrology; bond his brother Polly; flaw “relies too heavily on others”. He has 3 stars now, one more than his proficiency bonus, to a maximum of 7.

Stellar Reading

The player text is on his crew manifest. A bonus action costing 1 star, targeting a creature Casey can see and has not yet spoken with. His stars appear over the target, visible only to him, and he names the constellation they form. A correct sign gives Advantage on Charisma checks against that creature for 1 minute; a wrong sign gives Disadvantage. The window closes once the encounter begins, and walking away to try again does not reopen it.

  • Keeper's guidance. Reveal NPC signs sparingly. Vary sign distribution so archetypes do not give signs away. Leave wrong guesses unconfirmed outside the fiction, and allow no retries.
  • The throttle. Full chart access is intended. The number of stars over the target is the throttle.

The chart

The chart is a 10-node grid, and each sign is an 8-node constellation. The star cap equals Casey's current star count, and one node is always hidden. At 3 stars these sets are ambiguous:

StarsFits
1, 2, 7Aries, Leo, Taurus
3, 6, 8Capricorn, Virgo, Gemini
4, 6, 9Cancer, Scorpio
3, 5, 10Aquarius, Pisces
1, 7, 9Libra only
1, 8, 10Sagittarius only

Aries and Leo resolve only at 7 stars. Each Harptos month maps to a sign; the month table is on the stellar chart. The holidays are rare signs: Midwinter is Aquarius (“bends fate”), Greengrass is Taurus, Midsummer is Leo, Highharvestide is Libra, and the Feast of the Moon is Sagittarius (“walks unseen; between worlds”).

Tethering

Every constellation point Casey attunes to becomes a tether. He believes he is building a chart. He is anchoring his patron into another thread of the cosmos (What's hidden), and the chart he keeps is that reach drawn from the inside.

StepWhat happensWho sees it
1. He attunesHe is present, he witnesses, he binds. The patron cannot extend itself and needs a Starchild to do the stitching.Casey, who experiences it as progress.
2. The patron reaches throughThe anchored region drifts, slowly and uncannily. Mortal politics warp around it; people who go there return altered. This is the Toll.Nobody, for a long time.
3. The voice clarifiesHis brother's voice comes through cleaner than before.Casey. This is the reward signal.

The reward arrives at the same moment as the cost and masks it, which is how the affection works as a trap: he notices the voice getting clearer and never the place going wrong, and he is the one writing it in.

  • The Toll. What the patron takes at each point, tracked as a hidden meter and never named at the table. It shows only as change at the level of nature, never as a magic effect: a river reflects another sky, the wind holds one direction, children born there recognise each other. A single tether stays below anyone's notice for a long time; an overlap site does not.
  • Overlap. Where several Starchildren gather at one celestial body, the patron holds more tethers there and the change around them speeds up, loud enough for Casey to register.
  • Point design. Each point has a Sign (the hook), a Subject, a Work (the adventure), a Gift (what he learns) and a Toll. Each new point shifts at least three of five axes from the last: scale, subject, tone, engagement, guidance.

What withdrawal costs

Held in reserve for the moment he learns enough to choose. If he resists or stops:

CostAt the table
The voice thinsThe immediate one, and the one that lands. He feels his brother slipping.
The chart goes quietStars he could read turn inert: visible and meaningless. Stellar Reading degrades or stops.
The cloak stops matteringThe stitched constellations read as a costume rather than a record.

Walking away costs him the only family he has left, the practice that ever made him feel chosen, and the most concrete piece of his own identity. That is what makes it a real warlock choice rather than a reveal.

The Many

Eldritch heads are constellation points. Unaligned heads are capacity; an aligned head is a perspective with a name and a flavour, earned when he completes an alignment arc. Proficiency sets the head count. An alignment changes nothing mechanically and is the whole arc narratively.

What's hidden

  • The voice. The voice Casey hears as Polly is Astra Virex wearing Polly's shape, and it grows clearer with each tether. Astra Virex is his true patron, and every tether anchors it into another region.
  • The real Polly. The real Polly was consumed, or is caught between life and death. No one knows which.
  • The wardrobe. Mirt's wardrobe is a paracausal replica of Zerathis's anchor at Viren and carries a piece of Astra Virex's reach. That is why Casey was drawn to it, and why the voice in Polly's register could reach him there. He read it as luck; he was being magnetised.
  • Viren. What is at Viren now is part Zerathis, part Astra Virex's reach and part the site. It is malevolent in no direction at all.

Threads

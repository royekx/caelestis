The shared substrate Five of the six bespoke systems on this page read or write the paracausal layer. Gregory detects it, Casey creates it, Boogie contains it, Bartholomew is reaching for it, Tumak carries a piece of it. That is deliberate and it means the arcs can touch without sharing a story — the clearest case being that Gregory's static can be Casey's chart bleeding through.

Bartholomew Grayson — the Almost-Shift Creature pending

Circle of the Stars druid, Rashemi, WIS 18 against an unremarkable INT. He reads a situation by feel before his reasoning catches up, and the Almost-Shift is built to fire in that same order: the body answers first.

His tradition was pointed outward before he ever reached the Academy. His spellcasting focus is a star map, a family staff worn smooth and held together with tape, and his subclass draws power from stellar patterns. He did not come to Caelestis in spite of being a nature druid. He came because his practice was already about the sky.

How a slip runs

An Almost-Shift is a partial, involuntary slippage toward a shape he has not taken. It is flavor by default — a hand that holds wrong for a moment, a gait that is briefly another animal's. The rule that keeps it from becoming noise is a budget: one slip per scene carries mechanical weight, and the rest are texture.

RegisterWhat it resemblesReading
FirstApe, Boar, Giant Wolf Spider, WolfHis own four Wild Shape forms. The baseline, and the thing that makes a departure legible.
SecondStarry FormConstellation-light. Reads as his subclass surfacing, and is a cover for the third.
ThirdNeitherThe breadcrumb. Luminescent, between-worlds, matching no form he has and no constellation he knows.
The third register is the whole mechanic His four shapes and his Starry Form exist so that a slip belonging to neither has somewhere to be noticed from. Third-register slips are how the Almost is seeded, and they should arrive attached to something — a creature studied, a biome in collapse, a place where living systems are being rewritten. The strain of a slip is the breadcrumb; there is no separate clue track.

Recognition, not search

The Almost is already present in him and he has been avoiding it. That makes the arc a recognition and keeps it off a fetch-quest footing: there is no creature to hunt down, and the climax is whether he can understand the shape well enough to hold it. The creature design is deliberately unwritten until play surfaces what it should be.

Boogie — the Pulse RP only

Plasmoid Divine Soul sorcerer. The player invented the urge to dance as character texture; it was given a source afterward. The urge is the Pulse, and the Pulse is the Fonains shard in his nucleus — a fragment of the only Monolith ever destroyed, Tharizdun-corrupted. He does not know that, and the arc works because his own reading of himself is already the correct emotional reading.

No dice touch this The Pulse is adjudicated entirely as fiction. You describe the sensation — a buzzing in the nucleus, rhythmic pressure — and he decides what Boogie does with it. Describing it is the only lever you need, because it puts you in control of when the Pulse matters while leaving the response his.

The two answers

HeReads asDirection
Embraces itDancing. Being Boogie.The archive turns outward. He asserts himself and the stored material stays stored.
Suppresses itShying from who he is in that moment. Being Booger again.The archive turns inward. A small regression, and the accumulation gets a little easier.

His player frames the whole character as finally committing to himself after a life of drifting, which is the same tension the Pulse encodes. That is why the mechanic needs no enforcement: he is already roleplaying it.

He is becoming a container Three archived objects have accumulated inside one living body, and the shard is the reason they stay. The arc question is whether he remains himself as the volume grows — which is the personal-scale version of what a Monolith does, running inside a PC.

The Shield of Expression was a gift from Boom-chik so that a plasmoid could emote. It is narratively loaded and mechanically idle, and it is waiting for the scene where being read correctly is what matters.

Open: the escalation milestone. Effects stay small early by design, and the point at which the accumulation starts costing him is undecided.

Casey Geim — tethering Locked

Hadozee warlock of The Many. A Starchild, and his faculty is visual: he sees constellations nobody else can. Stellar Reading is that faculty at the table, and it is also the mechanism of everything below.

The loop

Every constellation point Casey attunes to becomes a tether. He believes he is building a chart. He is anchoring Astra Virex into another thread of the cosmos, and the chart he keeps is that network drawn from the inside.

StepWhat happensWho sees it
1. He attunesHe is present, he witnesses, he binds. Astra cannot extend itself and needs a hearer to do the stitching.Casey. He experiences it as progress.
2. Astra reaches throughThe anchored region drifts. Slowly, ambiently, uncannily. Mortal politics warp around it; people who go there return altered.Nobody, for a long time.
3. Polly clarifiesHis brother's voice comes through cleaner than before.Casey. This is the reward signal.
Casey never pays. The cosmos does. The reward arrives at the same moment as the cost and masks it, which is how the affection works as a trap. He notices the voice getting clearer and never the place going wrong, and he is the one writing it in.

What withdrawal costs

Held in reserve for the moment he learns enough to choose. If he resists or stops:

CostAt the table
Polly's voice thinsThe immediate one, and the one that lands. He feels his brother slipping.
The chart goes quietStars he could read turn inert. Visible, meaningless. Stellar Reading degrades or stops.
The cloak stops matteringThe stitched constellations read as a costume rather than a record.

Walking away costs him the only family he has left, the practice that ever made him feel chosen, and the most concrete piece of his own identity. That is what makes it a real warlock choice rather than a reveal.

The Many, mapped

Eldritch heads are constellation points. Unaligned heads are capacity; an aligned head is a perspective with a name and a flavor, earned when he completes an alignment arc. Mechanically an alignment changes nothing. Narratively it is the whole arc.

Overlap

Where more than one Starchild has tethered, paracausal change accelerates, and the drift becomes loud enough for Casey to register. A single tether sits below his threshold; an overlap site does not.

Virenspace is point one and it is already occupied Zerathis was a prior Starchild who went deep, took every gift offered, and dissolved into the paracausal layer of the system. What is at Viren now is part Zerathis, part Astra's reach, part the site. It can speak, sometimes in his voice and with his memories, and it is malevolent in no direction at all.

It may call Casey brother or Starchild without preamble, which is how he first hears the word applied to himself. The cautionary tale does not have to be explained to him. He gets to meet it.

Mirt's wardrobe is a paracausal replica of Zerathis's anchor, carrying a thread of the Viren shift across space. That is why Casey was drawn to it, and why Polly could reach him there: Astra's presence was already established at that location. He read it as luck, and he was being magnetized. Deep overlap sites elsewhere in wildspace are available as bartender stories and a that planet — strange properties, no stated cause.

Open: whether he has met the word Starchild before, and whether Zerathis recruited others, which decides how deep Viren's overlap runs.

Gregory — Calibration Locked

Autognome abjurer. His paracausality is that he casts at all — autognomes are constructs and no other one in this world can. Calibration is downstream of that change rather than the definition of it, and the distinction matters when the arc reaches his origin.

Mr. Blip is the control case: an autognome with personhood inside the normal range who cannot cast. OSTEKK-6 is the contrast from the other side — an automaton, built rather than ensouled, who knows the difference and says so.

The tug

Near anything the ripple touched — a place, a person, an object, an event — you describe a sensation in his core. A directional pull, like a compass needle trying to settle, and no malfunction or pain. Then you offer it: do you want to calibrate?

You initiate, always The offer is yours to make, which keeps the pacing yours, and the answer is his. He can decline, ask what it costs, or hesitate — and the offer itself is a roleplay beat rather than a gate.

Good moments: he is physically near the anomaly or something connected to it; a quiet beat where he is alone or reflective; he is doing navigation, construct work or spatial reasoning; something nearby resonates briefly.

The roll

Intelligence (Arcana), no declared DC. He never learns the threshold. His modifier is +2, and Built for Success gives him a d4 after seeing the roll twice per long rest, which means he can push a static result into a signal and will feel the decision.

ResultWhat he gets
Below thresholdStatic. The signal is real and unresolved. He knows something is there and cannot read it. No clue and no misdirection — interference.
Low successWeak signal. A direction, a texture, an emotional residue. Useful and incomplete.
Solid successClear bearing. A location type, a behavior, a defining sensory detail. Actionable.
High successSharp lock. A precise characteristic narrowing the anomaly to a place, object or condition. He is close.
Clues stack; failure is honest He never needs one perfect roll. Three or four solid clues locate any anomaly, and then its obstacles begin. A failed roll yields interference and costs him nothing, which keeps the instrument reading like a sensor rather than a skill check: the anomaly is real, he is working, the signal is still unresolved.

Anomaly 01 — the Latchling

At the Sea Dock, hidden by Rindle Gearloft, who is protecting it from the academy. Its change is sentience where its kind has none, and Gregory's prologue repair of a latch may have been the local condition that let the ripple resolve into it. He has a causal and ambiguous connection to its waking, mirroring his own uncertain origin.

BandClues
WeakA pull toward somewhere low and mechanical, a surface rather than a room. A faint rhythmic quality that moves like a heartbeat without being one. A warmth toward the docks that reads closer to recognition than heat.
ClearThe signal is at the sky dock, in the aligner berths along the mooring struts. Something small, moving on its own. It is not hiding — it goes still when it senses attention.
SharpIt has already noticed him. It has been there since they arrived, and he walked past it during orientation inspection.

Rindle sits outside the mechanic. Calibration delivers location and nature; Rindle appears when somebody physically looks, asks questions or pokes around the berths, and the discovery there is that someone already knew. Calibration gets him to the dock, and Rindle is what is waiting. The arc is the journey, since he knows the destination.

Open: the anomaly queue past the Latchling. Each one should reframe his origin again, and the question his existence poses is cosmological rather than personal.

Sol Fortuna — Oath of Misfortune Subclass

Astral elf paladin. The oath is homebrew and complete, written deity-agnostic so it stands on its own: paladins of Misfortune hold that adversity is the only honest teacher, that comfort breeds weakness, and that hardship endured is strength earned. They face misfortune in understanding rather than fear, and they wear it.

Tenets. Never Yield — failure is not the end, only surrender is. Trial by Fire — a life without adversity is a life unlived. Risk Is Not Ruin — calculated danger is wisdom, malicious harm is not.

Jinx

The core resource, at 3rd level. Charges equal his Charisma modifier, minimum one, all regained on a long rest. Two modes, both free actions in play:

ModeEffect
Jinx a creatureSpend 1 charge. One creature he can see within 30 feet gains the Jinxed condition — choose one ability score, and it has Disadvantage on d20 tests using that score until the start of his next turn. Jinxed is a curse and can be removed as one.
Jinx himselfSpend any number of charges before a d20 test. Each shifts his critical and failure thresholds by 1 in both directions. On a critical failure he takes 1d4 psychic per charge spent.
The self-Jinx is the character in one mechanic Buying a wider crit means buying a wider failure at the same price, and paying for the failure in psychic damage. He gets better odds of greatness by accepting worse odds of ruin, which is the oath stated as arithmetic and is the reason the subclass needs no flavor text at the table.

Regaining charges: on a critical hit, on a critical failure, or on being the target of a critical hit. The economy rewards the extremes in both directions and keeps him reaching for them.

Beshaba's voice

Minimal and capricious, and she talks at him rather than to him. "Stand." "Again." "Continue." The acknowledgment scene set the register: "Well, you had my curiosity but now, you have my attention… I must say, it was a bold move leaving your 'perfect' home, but what makes you so sure you escaped? Fate can be a cruel and twisted thing. Wield it wisely. Or not." Her lines leave deliberate air for him to answer into.

The Crystalline Retrieval Construct

Built by the Fortuned of Aethris after his escape, which makes it bespoke and personal: his leaving was the reason it exists. CR 8, on an advanced detention drone chassis. Traits: Bespoke Target Lock, Regeneration suppressed by thunder damage, Refracting Shielding, Adaptive Reconfiguration at HP thresholds, and Non-Lethal Retrieval.

Run the first encounter as an escape It is built to retrieve rather than to kill, so the first meeting is objective-based and survivable by moving. Its line is "Sol Fortuna. You are required." — and the thunder vulnerability is the kind of thing a crew can find out and use, which is the better version of beating it.

Open: whether the Fortuned want him back as a person or as a component of an unfinished ritual. The answer decides whether his thematic question gets answered by his enemies or by himself.

Tumak Swan — Wan and Tu In Rules

Twin halflings passing as one person, both partial Wayfinders, and between them one whole Dissolution fragment in the act of arriving. His systems are the most mechanically formal of any character's and they live on the Rules page, because they are rulings you adjudicate with dice rather than fiction.

The table believes he is one human, and that stays true

Nobody at the table knows Tumak is two halflings. His player sheet and every player-facing page record him as Species: Human, Class: Rogue (Soulknife), and that is correct rather than stale — it is what the party has been shown.

So the player side says “his mind often seems divided” and stops there, which is the right amount. Wan and Tu, the halfling species, the two bodies and the split inscription are DM-side only, and the reveal is still his to make.

Watch for this leaking through the mechanics. The per-level roll, the left-half/right-half rule and the separation lockout all describe two people; anything written for the players about his runes has to describe one. Keep off the player side

SystemWhat it governs
Runekeeper inscriptionThe fragment writing itself onto the twins. The per-level roll, the position pool, the choose band, and the Pacing Rule that holds Vessel positions back while Soul, Maker or Walker remain unresolved. Wan takes the left half and Tu the right, always, so the separation lockout falls out of the model rather than being imposed on it.
WayfindingThe partial mark, and its growth with understanding rather than with level. The full mark is several levels out.
The BecomingGoing home. Three poles stalling the cycle, and a Zenith as the result.
One in two, and what it makes him He is a paradox held as a rule: one person in two bodies. A whole fragment assembling in a single person is a first-key threshold, so the completed inscription is a key to the First Heart carried by a PC who believes he is going home. He is the only crew member who is himself an answer to the campaign's central lock, and nobody at the table has said so.

The runes surface on skin near cosmic threat, which gives the crew a proximity detector they did not ask for and cannot calibrate. His homeworld stays unfindable while the fragment's oscillation carries the concealment, and the fragment is leaving.

Open: what the inscription completing actually costs him, and what happens to the homeworld's concealment while the fragment is between bearers.

Sol Fortuna
A Misfortuned astral elf who left Aethris before its ritual closed, and Beshaba's chosen.

Sol Fortuna is an astral elf paladin born Misfortuned on Aethris, a world devoted to Tymora whose Zenith has passed and whose ritual stands incomplete, because he left before the Fortuned's ritual closed. He bears Beshaba's mark openly and swore her Oath of Misfortune. He is the crew's gunner and front line, and the Crystalline Retrieval Construct the Fortuned built to bring him home is hunting him.

Appearance

Tall and pale, with dark hair and white eyes. When misfortune moves through him, black lightning breaks from his body and snaps back, thin as a hair.

Personality

He is steady, even after a simulated death, and he anticipates danger before it arrives and puts himself between it and his crew. He backs people without being asked. He believes his life has always turned unlucky, and he wants to spend it exploring. He answers honestly when a god asks him a question. Small misfortunes find him every day, chores landing on him and things falling near him, and he wears them.

Biography

Background

Long ago the Fortuned, a sect grown from Tymora's temple order on Aethris, cast a generational curse to increase their own fortune, and the people made to carry their misfortune have been born Misfortuned ever since. Sol was born one of them. The Fortuned's ritual of perfection at the temple site brought Aethris to its Zenith, and the Prism Ascendant manifested above the temple in answer. Sol left before the ritual closed and came to Caelestis. The Fortuned built the Crystalline Retrieval Construct after he left, for him alone.

Relationships

Beshaba

The goddess of misfortune, who answered someone Aethris threw away. She talks at him in brief, capricious utterances and leaves air for him to answer into. Her reward for his oath is power, and small misfortunes around him now feel attended to.

The Fortuned

The sect that cursed his people. They want him back alive because he is a component of their unfinished ritual, and they do not care about him as a person. Their wanted notice for him travels the lane-marker boards.

Crystalline Retrieval Construct

The Fortuned's construct, built for one target. Its mask resolves toward his face, and its address to him never changes: “Sol Fortuna. You are required.”

Boogie

Sol backed him in the bunk dispute without a word, Boogie shouted him back onto his feet on the gym course, and the misfortune Sol first took onto himself was Boogie's.

Abilities

A paladin of the Oath of Misfortune. He senses the divine and the profane, heals and smites, and his oath's magic is dark: tentacles of shadow, curses and stolen life. He can jinx a creature so its luck fails it, or jinx himself, widening his chance of greatness and of ruin at the same price. He can take an ally's failure onto himself and turn it into a success, and each time he does, misfortune weakens him a little more until he releases it all at once onto someone else's fortune.

Where it heads

Back toward Aethris, whose ritual needs him, with the Crystalline Retrieval Construct hunting him and every meeting with it harder than the last. Beshaba's reward for the oath is power, and to give it shape she has to want something and lay that want on Sol as a quest; that quest is the next step of his arc. Mechanically the arc runs Misfortuned, then the Oath of Misfortune, then Misfortune's Favored, the capstone.

Table notes

  • Sol's player chose Beshaba and wants to lean into her.
  • He likes complex mechanics. Keep them from bogging down the table.
  • The table struggles with ambiguity, so vague, Augury-style answers are out.
  • The party has no cleric, and the oath spells were weighed against that.
  • Keep small daily misfortunes in play around him: chores landing on him, things falling. The player wants this.
  • Present something and let him respond. Never dictate what he feels or does.

Build

Paladin, Oath of Misfortune from level 3, with the Lucky feat. At the oath his sheet runs Constitution +4 and Charisma +4 (Charisma 18). Oath spells: arms of Hadar and hex at 3rd level; detect thoughts and warding bond at 5th; bestow curse and life transference at 9th; death ward and phantasmal killer at 13th; steel wind strike and synaptic static at 17th. Full D&D Beyond snippets are kept outside the wiki.

Misfortuned

Sol's own permanent character feature, because he is Misfortuned. The points it tracks are called Misfortune. It is separate from the oath and persists if he breaks the oath. The personal burden lives here so the oath stays pure upside, like the official oaths.

  • Misfortune caps at his proficiency bonus.
  • Each point is −1 to one ability modifier, chosen by d6 (1 Str, 2 Dex, 3 Con, 4 Int, 5 Wis, 6 Cha). The penalty carries through to spell save DC and spell attack. Penalties stack. All Misfortune clears on Unburden or at level-up.
  • Burden (reaction). When a friendly creature within 60 feet fails a roll, after the die is shown and before the outcome is declared, the roll becomes a success and Sol gains 1 Misfortune.
  • Unburden (reaction). At cap, Sol applies plus or minus his total Misfortune to any creature's roll within 60 feet, and all Misfortune clears. A natural 20 stays a critical hit.
  • Burden and Unburden share his reaction, so only one happens per round. He cannot Burden himself.

At levels 1 and 2, before the oath, it ran without a cap or Unburden: penalties persisted until level-up, and the player did not know when they would clear.

The player tracks each point as an item on D&D Beyond named “Misfortune (STR)”, “Misfortune (DEX)” and so on, and clears them before processing a level-up. The D&D Beyond text uses plain words, with no arrows, dashes or equals signs, and a two-column d6 table.

Oath of Misfortune

Homebrew and complete, written without a named god so it stands on its own. Paladins of Misfortune hold that adversity is the only honest teacher, that comfort breeds weakness, and that hardship endured is strength earned. They face misfortune in understanding rather than fear, and they wear it.

Its tenets are Never Yield, Court Adversity and Risk Is Not Ruin. The first holds that only surrender ends anything; the third, that calculated danger is wisdom and malicious harm is not.

Jinx

The core resource, at 3rd level. Charges equal 1 + his Charisma modifier, minimum two, all regained on a long rest. Two free options on his turn, each usable once per turn and outside combat:

OptionEffect
Jinx a creatureSpend 1 charge. One creature he can see within 30 feet has Disadvantage on d20 Tests using an ability score he chooses, until the start of his next turn.
Jinx himselfBefore a d20 Test, spend any number of charges. Each shifts his critical and failure thresholds by 1 in both directions, until the start of his next turn. On a critical failure he takes 1d4 psychic damage per charge spent.

Any jinxed creature, Sol included, counts as cursed, and remove curse or greater restoration ends it. He regains 1 charge on a critical hit, on a critical failure, or on being the target of a critical hit. The self-Jinx is the character in one mechanic: a wider critical range costs a wider failure range at the same price, paid in psychic damage.

Design notes

  • Subclass features deal necrotic and psychic damage. Divine Smite stays radiant, as the base class has it.
  • He is a bruiser with no defensive level-15 feature; his defense comes from the base class and the oath spells. In Korey's words, of Ill Omen (the oath's working name): “Ill Omen isn't about changing what happened but instead about mitigating it entirely or rolling with it.”
  • Oath spells may come from any class list, as Ancients, Vengeance and Glory do.
  • The capstone's damage hits allies too: misfortune does not care who the target is (Cloak of Flies is the precedent). Allies trade the damage for the Favor.

What's hidden

  • His escape. A Watcher of the Order of the All-Father extracted Sol from Aethris, against the Order's remit to preserve worlds, not individuals. The Order then broke protocol to recommend that he be taken to Caelestis.
  • Beshaba's part. Beshaba did not arrange Sol's escape. Her reward for the oath is power, and she gives it shape through a want of her own, laid on him as a quest.
  • Who knows. Mirt, Tarto and Saerthe know Sol's background and keep it to themselves.
  • Aethris. Vecna is pushing the Fortuned's ritual toward completion from outside, and nobody on Aethris knows he is involved (Opposition: Vecna).

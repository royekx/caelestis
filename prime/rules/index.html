Table·Mechanics Reference

Rules

Two halves. Setting mechanics are rulings the cosmology implies and the system has no answer for — what happens when a player acts on a heart, an Atrium, a Monolith or a taken creature. Character subsystems are the bespoke mechanics attached to particular characters, and Ship & crew holds the ship combat rules. Every ruling here is one a DM will need mid-session with a player waiting; the terms are defined in the Glossary. The inscription tracker comes in two copies: the unlocked DM version, with every rune matched, and the player-facing one.

Hearts & Atria

Found, opened, linked are three steps with three different costs. Each resolves differently at the table.

Finding an Atrium

MethodRuling
With A.T.L.A.S.Automatic for a reader working at the operational depth. No check — the gate is the reader, not the roll. See its reading depths.
By scholarshipA downtime project, not a check. Minimum one month on the world with access to its records, then a DC 20 Intelligence (History, Religion or Arcana) check at the end of each month. Three successes locate it. Each failure costs the month and reveals one true fact about the world that narrows the search.
By being toldThe fastest route and the one the campaign should prefer. Someone always knows — a local tradition, a Seeker chapter, a Restorer survey, a thing the colony already opened.
By accidentAn Atrium is a fold, so it can be stumbled into. A world's folklore is the tell: the well that was always dry, the courtyard that is only a way through for a few minutes each year.

Residue is required to pass through

An Atrium can be found, stood in and studied by anyone. Opening it takes a piece of residue in hand, held by a creature passing through, and the piece is not consumed. Without one, the fold stays shut and the location is simply a strange place with a story attached.

The descent

Past the Atrium, the interior is made of what the world is, and it is a dungeon rather than a corridor. Run it as a site, never as a check. Guidance:

  • Scale. One session for a small or young world, two to three for an old or dense one. The deeper the history, the longer the descent.
  • Time passes oddly and travels both ways. A heart has no sky and no clock. Set a ratio when the party enters, state it plainly, and hold to it.
  • Dying inside is dying. No special rule. The interior is real.
  • The centre is the thing the world most fundamentally is. Opening happens there, and what opening looks like is that place accepting the residue and unfolding.

Opening, and what it does

  • The heart becomes reachable from outside and can hold a link. It grants no control over the world and no one on the surface notices.
  • It holds until the heart is closed, and closing a heart ends every link that runs through it.
  • It can count toward Vecna's eye. Each heart opened and linked in a mark's system is progress toward opening Vecna's eye, whoever opened it and for whatever reason.

Links

QuestionRuling
Making oneBoth hearts opened, a Monolith standing over each world, and a working performed at one centre with the other's location known. Days, not rounds. It is infrastructure.
Using oneReach either heart's centre and step through. One round. Carry what you carry; a crew and its cargo pass, a ship does not.
Who may use itAnyone who reaches either end. Links are public roads. The colony's lattice is usable against the colony.
Blocking oneClosing either heart ends the link. Short of that, a heart's centre can be held, garrisoned or made lethal, which is how a link is usually denied.
BleedBegins within months. One visible exchange per season of linked time, worsening: weather, then crops and livestock, then language and dreams, then people. It does not reverse.

Monoliths, archiving & intakes

Being archived is a campaign event

A taking is a selection made by the mechanism and it allows no save. Treat a PC being archived as a thing the table agreed to beforehand, never as an outcome of a failed roll. The consequence is permanent relocation to the Citadel, which is a different campaign until they get out.

Standing in a Zenith

  • A Monolith takes what the Zenith threatens with loss. Visitors are rarely at risk, because they can leave and the world cannot. A party that has committed to the world — defending it, bound to it, unable to withdraw — becomes eligible.
  • Gods are never taken. Nothing bound to continue is at risk, so the mechanism finds nothing to preserve.
  • Warning is generous. The strain is visible for hours before the manifestation. A party that wants out can get out.

Riding an intake

An intake runs in both directions and is open only while a taking is underway. Going in is a decision. Going out is a procedure, and it is the only way out of the Citadel.

StepRuling
PredictFrom inside the Citadel, with no sky. Requires a source: the sect's watch records, a resonance-bearer, or a fragment that senses archiving. A DC 20 check against the best available source, once per attempt window.
LocateThe outbound channel among a permanent inbound flood. DC 18 Perception or Arcana, with advantage for anyone carrying resonance.
TraverseAgainst the flow of an active cosmological event. One minute of sustained effort; three successful DC 15 Constitution saves before two failures. Failure ejects the traveller back into the Citadel, badly hurt.
ArriveAt the world whose Zenith is in progress, at the hinge of its history. The destination is unchosen. Roll or pick what kind of hinge it is — a coronation, a transcendence, a war, a collapse.
Get homeThe real problem. They arrive stripped of ship, charts and bearing. A Wayfinder solves it with time on the ground; everyone else is stranded until they find passage.

The approach to the Core

The Core's intake is a permanent omnidirectional draw, and it is gradual rather than a threshold. Three bands:

BandAt the table
CurrentA crew can work against it with effort and skill. Crossing costs time, supplies and a sequence of helm checks. Survivable and exhausting.
SlopeCommitting. A vessel can still steer, and it can no longer turn around. Everything from here is one way.
FallNothing withstands it. This is where arrivals join the inbound stream, which is also the only way into the Core from outside.

Entering the Core means joining the fall deliberately. No check opens it; the decision is the mechanic. Gods cannot make the crossing at all, because of polarity: Rykoe is the First Heart, the deepest heart there is, and the push back grows with every step until going on is unbearable. It harms a god without destroying it. That is why every power with an interest here works through mortals.

Paracausality & the Weave

Paracausality and the Weave are orthogonal. Paracausality describes what a thing's reality is; the Weave governs whether anti-magic suppresses it. A thing can sit anywhere on both axes.

At the table, use the well. Energy is groundwater: it is always there, under everything. The Weave is the well that mortals draw from to cast. An anti-magic field caps the well, and the water stays where it was. A spell slot is water already drawn, so it persists in the field and cannot be poured into a spell. The Faelight spore is a root: it pulls on the water through its own channel, apart from the well, and it holds no water of its own, so it needs a caster's slot to run.

CaseInside anti-magic
Weave-acts — ordinary spellcastingSuppressed.
A spell slotInnate stored energy. It persists; it simply cannot be spent on a Weave-act.
Innate non-Weave processes — a flower's anti-magic emission, a wisp's draining feed, spore conversionUnsuppressed, which is why a flower's own emission survives inside the field it creates.
Paracausal objects — residue and its housingsDampened, not suppressed. Residue is non-Weave by origin, so an instrument built around a piece keeps working inside an anti-magic field, faintly: its pull goes faint and its working stutters.

Paracausal resonance

A creature that is paracausal, or that carries paracausal matter, develops a felt awareness of other paracausal events, anomalies and distortions nearby.

Passive and always on
No action and no check to notice that something is there. It gives direction and intensity, never identity.
Range
Roughly a mile for a strong source, a few hundred feet for a faint one. A Monolith, an Atrium or an active intake registers from much further.
Reading it
Distinguishing what kind of thing is resonating is a DC 15 Wisdom check, and the carrier has advantage on anything matching their own bias.
Who has it
Gregory, who is paracausal himself; Boogie, carrying a Monolith fragment that is still attempting to archive; Tumak, carrying Dissolution residue. Each senses most keenly in its own register.

The taken, and detecting them

An n'ghathu is a person whose body, memories, manner and affections are intact and whose allegiance runs elsewhere. There is nothing to notice in conversation, which is the point.

The tell is the absence of a reading

Every n'ghathu carries an innate mind blank. So detect thoughts, modify memory, telepathy and divinations aimed at them fail outright rather than returning a lie — the caster experiences the spell finding nothing where a mind should be. That is the single most reliable tell in the campaign and it requires a caster to try it on a decorated officer and then believe the result.

ApproachRuling
Telepathy and mind-readingFails. The caster knows the spell failed and learns nothing about why.
Social reading — InsightNo bonus and no penalty. They are themselves. An Insight check reads a truthful person, because they are one about everything except the one thing.
Physical examinationChitin beneath the skin along the spine and the back of the skull, thin at short tenure. DC 18 Medicine with hands on the subject, or automatic on a corpse.
Detect evil and good, alignment-style magicNothing. They are not fiends, undead or aberrations; they are the person they were.
Removing oneThe tadpole is the person now. A tadpole can be removed, and removal frees the host; how is not set. If N'ghathrod's true body dies, the taken do not die: part of him lives on in each of them, and each loses a part of themselves. His lieutenants are closest to him, and each rung down the colony is further away. Given enough time, one of them could grow toward becoming another N'ghathrod.

Forbiddance, sigils & Wayfind

Fleet installations of consequence carry a permanent Forbiddance. At Caelestis this blocks teleportation in, out and within, with registered sigil sequences as the only exception.

Wayfind passes

Wayfind is not a Weave-act. It rides the heart layer, and Forbiddance is specified against Weave-based teleportation, so the ward has nothing to catch. Tumak can arrive on and leave Caelestis at will, and the Academy's security model has a hole in it that nobody has written down.

  • A mark needs no heart. Tumak can place one on the station itself, on a ship he could crew, or anywhere he chooses, and Wayfind back to it through the Forbiddance.
  • Sigil misuse is detected at the next registry reconciliation rather than instantly. The window is days.
  • Heart-links ignore Forbiddance entirely for the same reason, which matters the first time the colony reaches a warded world.

Tumak — the inscription

Wan and Tu are two separate hosts sharing one persona. The Dissolution fragment is settling in both of them at once, in two halves, and the inscription is how that shows: the world writes runes onto their bodies, one half per twin. A rune does nothing until both halves exist. Twenty positions in total.

Two tracks run side by side. Runes are what he can do: his level sets his essence runes and how far he can jump; his matched runes set his marks and passengers. Havens are where he goes: reached by travel, found by the homeworld's sensory anchors. Getting home takes adventuring, not levelling. The story behind it is on the Wayfinders' homeworld and the Runekeepers.

One in two

Tumak is Wan and Tu, two hosts sharing one persona. Wan always carries the left half of a rune and Tu always the right, so no complete rune exists on either body alone — separation locks the Wayfinder abilities without any rule saying so, because the halves are literally apart. Taken together the twins are a whole Dissolution fragment walking around in two pieces, which is a paradox the cosmology does not otherwise permit. It is the rule anyway.

The core loop

At each character level, Wan and Tu each roll a d20 against their own pool of 01–16. A rune is the pair of halves one level brings, matched or not. 17, 18 and 19 are the clan runes, bestowed whole at havens; 20, the Heart, was matched before the campaign and wakes at the Becoming.

ResultOutcome
1–16That position resolves for the rolling twin.
17–19Reroll.
20The player chooses any position that twin lacks; the point is to match.
A position that twin already holdsSlide up to the next position that twin lacks, wrapping from 16 to 01.

A position with both halves is matched and awake. Matched runes carry the per-rune boons and power the four Wayscribes. Rolled runes fill the sixteen positions by level 16; the clan runes arrive early, at havens; levels past 16 add no runes, and the inscription never passes 20. As the pools shrink, matches come more often, on the roll or on the slide.

The Pacing Rule

If a twin lands on a Vessel position while that twin still lacks a position in Soul, Maker or Walker, reroll the d20 once and keep the second result. It applies to the position he lands on, after any slide. Vessel is six of the sixteen, so without this the largest and highest-tier Wayscribe fills first and the small ones arrive last.

His classes

Rogue 3 (Soulknife) and Sorcerer (Runechild) from level 4. Both run as published, with one bridge.

  • Essence runes are the runes on his skin, one per rune, clan runes included, in place of the Runechild's count (4 now).
  • A Psionic Energy Die can charge a rune: as a bonus action, expend one die to charge one essence rune. Sorcery points charge runes as Runechild describes, including its end-of-turn charge for points spent on anything and its action to charge one rune when he has none.
  • Out of combat, charging takes no action. With sorcery points, 1 point charges 2 runes.
  • Charged runes power the Runechild's features, Wayfind and quick marks, and last until spent or until a long rest.
  • An optional haven can add a sorcery point or a Psionic Energy Die to his maximum.

Marks

  • He holds one mark per matched rune, clan runes included (1 now). A walked haven is a further mark, held for good.
  • Placing a mark where he stands takes a 1-hour ritual, or a Magic action and 2 charged runes. A mark sits on a place, a station or a ship he could crew, never on a creature or anything that can be carried. Ending a mark takes no action.
  • Looking costs no resource, as with a familiar. As a bonus action he sees, but does not hear, the area within 30 feet of one of his marks within his Wayfind reach, until the start of his next turn or until he stops. While he looks he is blind where he stands. It is for checking that a landing is clear.

Wayfind

A Magic action: expend one charged rune and teleport to one of your marks, from wherever you stand, with your passengers. It always lands on a mark. Character level sets how far; matched runes set where. At each level-up, add the new band and move on.

  1. The toll and the tax. One charged rune for any jump; distance sets the exhaustion and the mishap. Every jump spends its rune, mishap or not. A mark beyond reach fails before anything is spent, the action included. Aboard a ship within a world's air, he is on that world; in wildspace between worlds, World does not apply.
  2. His marks. Standing on one of his marks as he jumps: −25% mishap. Standing on a mark buys safety, never energy. Rune Sets give their Wayscribe spells and play no part in Wayfind.
  3. A walked haven as the target cannot mishap; leaving from one counts as standing on a mark.
  4. Passengers: willing creatures within 5 feet, numbering (matched runes + 1) ÷ 2, rounded down, at most 8; the +1 is the hidden Heart. They bring what they wear and carry. The twins are Tumak, not passengers. The player's page states the number (1 now), never the formula.
  5. While the twins are apart, Wayfind is locked. The twin with the crew is the one played. They reunite by travel, through the crew, or by the Twin Bond Compass.
  6. Off-target landings go in a random horizontal direction. One that would put him in rock, water or open sky lands him on the nearest safe ground. Wayfind is not a Weave act (Forbiddance).

Exhaustion is 2024's: −2 to every d20 test and −5 ft speed per level, death at 6, one level removed per long rest. There is no cap; the player knows the table and does the sum. A jump always resolves first, by its roll, and the exhaustion lands as it does. If that brings him to 6, he dies there, and whoever he carried is where the jump put them. From fresh, a Deep Astral jump leaves him at 5.

When he jumps how. A long jump when it is urgent and a miss is survivable. A chain of short hops when he has prepared marks: Step and Near cost no exhaustion. Onto his marks first: when a dungeon or an ambush goes wrong, jump to the entrance mark, then on from it at −25%. A look before landing. The ship when neither: ordinary travel stays the default, and Gregory's navigation with it. A cold long jump is for when he has one rune left, no time, or no marks between: runes pay the toll, and exhaustion and risk pay for distance. The player names the mark and says whether he stands on one; you hold the table and roll.

Against the published spells, short range runs a little ahead (Near at 5 against thunder step's 90 feet), to marks only and for a rune. Long range lags about two levels behind (World at 11, after teleportation circle at 9; System at 13, with teleport). From levels 7 to 10, anything beyond a day's travel is the ship or the road.

Havens and Homebound

  • Both twins present and the haven's trial passed: the haven is walked. It gives a waypoint, a reward the haven makes for this heir, and that Runekeeper's chapter of the order's history. One twin alone can find a haven but cannot take its trial.
  • Homebound needs one haven of each of the four clans, Lunarfoot's included, with the current Runekeeper's haven last; his counts for his own clan. With today's seven the shortest path is four: one of the first and fifth, one of the second and sixth, the fourth, and the seventh last.
  • A clan's rune (17, 18, 19) comes from the first haven of that clan he walks.
  • Order is free until the last. Arriving early at the current Runekeeper's haven, the trial is not yet open, and he is told to return once the others are walked.
  • At that haven the twins meet the Runekeeper and take the exchange: the Becoming. The Heart wakes and Homebound opens.
  • Homebound is a Wayfind into the folded world's heart: free, cannot mishap, any distance, at a moment the player chooses. The Zenith follows; run it from the homeworld.

The clock

Slip is the count of unmatched halves: fragment that has left the Runekeeper and holds nothing yet. A single heir's runes arrive whole, so only a split heir has it. It is 6 today. Strain is Slip minus havens walked.

StrainAt the table
0–4Steady. The voice is clear.
5–7The Runekeeper feels the fragment leaving too fast; the sensory anchors flicker at havens.
8–9The binding slips. Traces of the homeworld leak into wildspace where someone could notice.
10+Crisis: the unhiding is near or here.

From today's runes, Slip holds about 7 across levels 5 to 9 and falls to 0 by level 16, so the pressure peaks mid-campaign and levelling alone does not answer it. The unhiding: the binding gives way and the homeworld comes out of its fold. Tumak learns it in short, broken pulses from the Runekeeper and chooses: go home early by ordinary travel, which brings the Zenith early and perhaps harder, or race the remaining havens. It is the rising action's peak; homecoming is still the climax.

Running it

  • Half-runes surface through small physical prompts: itches, tingles, heat. The player drives discovery, and meets each rule in play before it goes on his sheet.
  • Matched runes arrive in pairs across two bodies, so a match is a scene between the twins, not a die result in isolation.
  • Mishaps are a story tool: a separated twin, and a haven to wake up in.

Ship & crew

How ship combat runs at this table. The players hold the same rules on Ship Mechanics, their roles on Bridge Crew Roles, and their two ships on the ship sheets of the tyrant ship and the Flighty Foundling. This section adds what only the DM needs: enemy budgets, what to do when the crew loses its ship, and the numbers to say aloud between rounds.

  • Ship stats. Use the official Spelljammer Academy (SJA) stat blocks. Hull is the stat block's hit points. Bulwark is twice the old damage threshold, and the mishap threshold is that damage threshold again (half the Bulwark maximum).
  • Encounter budget. Build enemy ships at about 1.5 times SJA's strength. A level-4 fight should last 3–4 rounds, cost the crew 20–50 Hull and roll 1–2 mishaps.
  • Sources. Bulwark, Hull and the bridge roles come from Wildjammer: More Adventures in Space; the mishap table and crew rules from Spelljammer Combat Expanded (SCE); the stat blocks and weapon ranges from SJA. Where this section differs from them, this section holds.

Turns

  • Side initiative. The Captain rolls d20 + Cha or Wis for the crew's side. The enemy leader rolls for the other side.
  • The party picks its order within its side's turn. The usual pattern is the Spelljammer first, setting the range, and then everyone else.
  • Every character keeps a full turn: action, bonus action, movement and reaction. A role adds to that turn. It gives one passive, a menu of bonus actions, and at most one reaction.
  • Roles are optional. Passives always work. A character may spend the bonus action on their own features instead of the role's.
  • Roles are fixed for the fight. If a role's holder goes down, anyone may take the seat as an action.

Range bands

BandRough distanceWhat it allows
Closeup to 60 ftGrapple and boarding are possible. Ballistae and eyes fire normally, but mangonels cannot fire. Most character attacks reach.
Medium60–200 ftEvery ship weapon fires normally. Only attacks and spells with a range of 120 ft or more reach.

  • Each helm shifts the range on its own turn, for free: it closes one band, holds, or opens one band.
  • Burn shifts one band further.
  • Penalties. A slowed ship cannot shift the band or Burn. A Crippled ship cannot Burn.
  • Weapon reach. A ballista reaches 120/480 ft. A mangonel reaches 200/800 ft and cannot hit anything within 60 ft.

Zones

  • A typical ship has four zones: Helm, Weapon Deck, Below and Rigging. The tyrant ship's zones are in The tyrant ship, below.
  • Positions are abstract by default. The Spelljammer is at the Helm, and everyone else is on the Weapon Deck. Nobody tracks zones until something marks one.
  • An event marks a zone. A mishap marks one, and so do boarders at the rail. Fixing a marked zone needs someone in it.
  • Moving costs the turn. One zone uses a character's movement. Two zones use the movement and the action (Dash).
  • Patching Bulwark works from any zone.

Weapons

  • The crew keeps every weapon loaded and aimed. This replaces SJA's separate load, aim and fire actions. A weapon's crew number is the crew it needs to stay loaded, and a weapon short of crew cannot fire.
  • Firing is an action anyone can take. The attack is d20 + proficiency bonus + Str or Dex; at an eye, the firer may use their spellcasting ability instead. Damage is the weapon's dice, and the Gunner adds a modifier.
  • Each weapon fires once a round, unless it is hot loaded (Working together, below).
  • The NPC crew fires one weapon a round. The PCs fire the rest.

Working together

  • Help. One PC uses the Help action to give another advantage on a role check or a ship weapon attack. Role checks are Quick Fix, Find Cover, the repair check and escape checks. The helper must be in the same zone or at the same eye station. This advantage does not stack with Read the Line.
  • Hot load. A weapon that has already fired this round can fire once more: one PC spends their action loading it (two actions for a mangonel), and another spends their action firing it. Each weapon fires at most twice a round, and the Gunner's Quick Load counts as that second shot. The eyes carry no ammunition and cannot be hot loaded.
  • Stand in as crew. A PC can spend their action working a gun as crew. They count as two crew members this round, which can bring a short-crewed weapon back into action.

Damage

  • Ship weapons hit Bulwark first, then Hull.
  • Character attacks and spells barely dent a hull. They are better spent on crew and officers. SCE's area table sets how many crew an area spell hits.
  • Ships are immune to poison and psychic damage.
  • A heavy hit rolls a mishap. A single hit that deals Hull damage of at least the ship's mishap threshold rolls on the mishap table. The threshold is half the Bulwark maximum: 15 for a hammerhead or a squid ship, 20 for the tyrant ship.
  • A critical hit always rolls a mishap, and doubles its dice.
  • Expect 1–2 mishaps a fight. A level-4 fight lands 2–3 hits on Hull over its 3–4 rounds, about half of them clear the threshold, and the occasional crit adds one.
  • Crippled. At half Hull or less, the ship's speed is halved and it cannot Burn.
  • Zero Hull. See The ship at 0 Hull.

Parts

  • The ship sheet lists every part: each weapon or eye, the helm, and the rigging or fins.
  • Each part is working, disabled or destroyed. A disabled part can be fixed in combat with a Quick Fix. A destroyed part cannot be fixed in combat; it takes repair days, and a destroyed helm or eye takes a port.
  • Bulwark can still be Patched whatever state the parts are in.

How a fight ends

  • An NPC ship breaks when any one of these happens: its Hull drops to half or less, two of its parts are disabled or destroyed, or its leader falls.
  • A broken ship flees, surrenders, or makes a desperate attempt to board.
  • Either side can always try to escape (Escape, below).

Hazards

  • Hazards outside combat. A drift hazard (a debris field, a storm, a color pool's edge) is a group check: each PC and the crew make one check against DC 12. All, most, few or none succeeding sets the outcome. The Navigator's Plot rerolls one failure.

Boarding

  • Boarding needs Close range, or a grapple.
  • Side turns continue. Boarders cross as a group on their side's turn, as part of their movement, and act when they land. Defenders answer on their own side's turn. A lone boarder is not swarmed before it acts, and the defenders are not overrun by a whole boarding party before their turn comes.
  • What boarding does to roles. A PC who boards the enemy ship leaves their role until they come back. A boarded ship fires only its crew weapon, as in SJA.
  • Big brawls. If a boarding fight grows large, switch to individual initiative. Ship actions then happen on the Spelljammer's turn.

Roles

Role numbers tie to proficiency bonus (PB): +2 at level 4, +4 at level 10 and +5 at level 15. Each role steps up at levels 5, 10 and 15. The level-10 figures below assume ability modifiers that are estimates. The ship rolls that Rally can boost are a ship weapon attack, a role check, a Grapple, an escape check, or a save against a ship effect.

Captain (Tumak; Cha or Wis)

Controls the order and lifts the crew.

  • Command (passive). Rolls the side's initiative. Allies who can hear the Captain have advantage on saves against being frightened.
  • Rally (bonus action). One ally gets a Command die to add to one ship roll before the Captain's next turn.
  • Order (bonus action). One ally uses their reaction to take one of their role's bonus actions.
  • Grapple (action, Close only). A Cha (Intimidation or Persuasion) check contested by the enemy captain, or against DC 12 if there is none. On a success the ships lock together and move at half speed.
  • Level 4: Command die d6; Fear eye DC 14. Level 10: Command die d10; Fear eye DC 17.
  • Steps: d8 at level 5, d10 at level 10, and d12 at level 15, when Rally also reaches two allies.
  • Against its holder's own options: Rally competes with Cunning Action and the Soulknife's bonus-action blade. At range Rally usually wins; in a deck fight Tumak's own bonus action does.

Spelljammer (Bartholomew; spellcasting ability)

Chooses the range, and with it what the fight looks like.

  • Helm (passive). Moves the ship and shifts the band for free, and senses everything on and around the ship. Can cast their own spells at anything they can see on or around the ship. Takeoff is a DC 13 spellcasting check; a second attempt succeeds automatically.
  • Passing the helm (action). Attunement passes to a willing creature as an action: the spelljamming helm’s own rule. Anyone aboard who can cast can take it, hold position, drift and answer hails while the cadets are ashore.
  • Shields Up (bonus action, spend a spell slot). +3 AC, plus 1 per slot level above 1st, until the Spelljammer's next turn.
  • Jink (bonus action). The first ship weapon attack against the ship before the Spelljammer's next turn has disadvantage.
  • Burn (bonus action). Shift the band one more step.
  • Onboard Defense (bonus action). A creature aboard makes a Wis save. On a failure it takes psychic damage and has disadvantage on its next roll.
  • Level 4: DC 14; Onboard Defense 1d6; Jink covers 1 attack. Level 10: DC 17; Onboard Defense 3d6; Jink covers 2 attacks.
  • Steps: Onboard Defense gains 1d6 at levels 5, 10 and 15. Jink covers 2 attacks from level 10. From level 15, one Shields Up a fight costs no slot.
  • Against its holder's own options: Starry Form. Waking it before initiative softens the cost.

Navigator (Gregory; Int or Wis)

Reads the fight so everyone else's rolls land.

  • Plot (passive). Once per hazard, reroll one failed check.
  • Read the Line (bonus action). The side's ship weapon attacks against one target have advantage until the Navigator's next turn.
  • Find Cover (bonus action). d20 + PB + Int or Wis against DC 13. On a success the ship has +2 AC against one ship until the end of the Navigator's next turn.
  • Find the Gap (bonus action). The side's next escape check has advantage.
  • Level 4: Find Cover +6; Slowing eye DC 14. Level 10: Find Cover +9; Slowing eye DC 17; Read the Line also crits on 19–20.
  • Steps: at level 5 Find Cover needs no check; at level 10 Read the Line crits on 19–20; at level 15 it covers two targets.
  • Against its holder's own options: very little. Gregory has few bonus actions of his own.

Gunner (Sol; Str or Dex, Cha at an eye)

Takes the enemy ship apart.

  • Gun Captain (passive). Adds his modifier to ship weapon damage. Class features that apply to weapon attacks apply to ship weapons, and Extra Attack lets him fire a second weapon.
  • Aim (bonus action). Advantage on his next ship weapon attack this turn.
  • Called Shot (bonus action, added to a shot). A hit deals half damage and adds one effect: a weapon is disabled; or the rigging is hit, so the target cannot Jink or Burn; or, on an open deck, 1d4 + PB crew drop. Through the Disintegration eye, the part is destroyed.
  • Quick Load (bonus action, PB uses per long rest). Fire the same weapon once more. It does not work on an eye.
  • Level 4: a ballista at +5 for 3d10 + 3; Quick Load 2 per long rest. Level 10: +8 with two shots; Quick Load 4 per long rest; Called Shot deals full damage.
  • Steps: none at level 5, when Extra Attack arrives. At level 10 Called Shot deals full damage. At level 15 ship weapons crit on 19–20.
  • Against its holder's own options: Divine Smite. For bonus-action spells such as Divine Smite, the table uses whatever edition it already uses for Sol.

Boatswain (Casey; Str, Dex, or Int with tools)

Keeps the ship in the fight.

  • Patch dice. PB + 2 Patch dice (d10s), refreshed on a long rest.
  • Upkeep (passive). Runs the repair days (below), and only the Boatswain repairs Hull.
  • Patch (bonus action, from any zone). Restore 1d10 + PB Bulwark, adding any Patch dice spent. Patch never repairs Hull.
  • Quick Fix (bonus action, in the zone). DC 13 to end a mishap or fix a disabled part. Anyone else can try it as an action.
  • Brace (reaction). Spend a Patch die to reduce the damage of one hit by the roll + PB.
  • Shore Up (action, Below, once per fight). Restore 2d10 + PB Hull.
  • Expel (action, tyrant ship, once per fight). Creatures in the Cargo Deck make a DC 13 Str save or are blown out through the intake.
  • Level 4: Patch about 7.5; 4 Patch dice; Brace about 7.5; Shore Up about 13. Level 10: Patch 3d10 + 4, about 20.5; 6 Patch dice; Brace about 9.5; Shore Up about 15.
  • Steps: Patch rolls one more d10 at levels 5, 10 and 15.
  • Against its holder's own options: hex and misty step. Brace still works whatever his bonus action did. Heavy hits start fires and breaches, so in most fights the Boatswain goes Below once.

Medic (Boogie; Wis or Cha)

Keeps officers and crew working.

  • Medical dice. PB + 2 medical dice (d8s), refreshed on a long rest.
  • Vigil (passive). Creatures within 30 ft add 1d4 to death saves.
  • Triage (bonus action). Spend a medical die on a creature within 5 ft. It regains the roll + PB + the Medic's modifier, and one condition ends: poisoned, frightened, or a latched wisp.
  • Muster (bonus action, in the zone). 1d4 + PB downed crew stand up.
  • Level 4: Triage about 9.5; 4 medical dice. Level 10: Triage 2d8 + 8, about 17; 6 medical dice.
  • Steps: one more die at level 5; Triage heals 2d8 at level 10; at level 15 Triage heals two creatures.
  • Against its holder's own options: healing word and Quickened Spell. Triage saves his slots and sorcery points, and the Medic is often the one free to Help or to follow the Boatswain Below.

Caps across roles

  • AC. Role bonuses to the ship's AC (Shields Up, Find Cover) add up to +5 at most.
  • Once per fight. Shore Up and Expel.

Repair

  • Bulwark refills 10 minutes after a fight.
  • Hull takes repair days, worked out of combat with the ship stopped. Work them out once: Hull missing ÷ (10 × PB), rounded up, which is 20 Hull a day at level 4 and 40 a day at level 10. Add 1d4 days for each destroyed part that can be field-repaired, which is every part except the helm and the eyes. Disabled parts are fixed along the way at no extra time.
  • The Boatswain's check. When repairs start, the Boatswain makes one DC 15 check (Str, Dex, or Int with tools). A success takes 1 day off, to a minimum of 1; a failure adds 1 day. Help gives advantage.
  • Underway, only Bulwark recovers. The crew chooses: stop and spend the days, or push on damaged.
  • Spells. A repair spell of 3rd level or higher (fabricate, stone shape on the tyrant ship, Wildjammer's repair hull) cast during repair days counts as one extra day of repair, once a day. Mending fixes one disabled part out of combat.
  • Supplies and a repair cap are not used. The time cost does that job.
  • Ports repair 50 Hull a day at 10 gp a point. They are the only place to replace a destroyed helm or eye, and they do full overhauls.
  • What a fight costs at level 4: about 20–50 Hull, so 1–3 days, plus 1d4 days for each destroyed part.

The ship at 0 Hull

Plan for it as a turn in the story.

  • Dead in space. At 0 Hull the ship cannot move, shift the band or Burn. Its weapons and eyes go quiet, and Bulwark is gone. Enemies grapple it automatically. Its air envelope holds, so the crew aboard can breathe. Once it regains 1 Hull through repair days, it moves again.
  • Destroyed outright. One of three things destroys it: further damage equal to half its maximum Hull; scuttling, which takes 10 minutes aboard; or a Fire left burning for 10 minutes. As in Wildjammer, destroying a ship takes a focused effort.
  • Abandoning ship. Flighty Foundling carries a scuttle ship, and so does the tyrant ship on the crossing: one, docked in the Cargo intake, seating six. Spells such as fly and teleportation work, and a boarded enemy ship is another way off.
  • Cut loose in space. Wildjammer's air rule: 1 minute of fresh air, then 1 minute of fouled air with disadvantage on checks, then the creature holds its breath for minutes equal to its Con modifier (minimum 30 seconds) before it suffocates. That gives roughly 2–4 minutes, or 20–40 rounds, to rescue someone.
  • Outcomes. Capture, towing, rescue by the Fleet, or limping to a rock to repair. Each one leads somewhere.
Losing the ship Losing the ship changes the conditions, not the game. A destroyed ship does not mean a dead crew. Step in and keep it real, without cushioning or punishing: a captured crew is usually worth more to an enemy than a dead one. The enemy takes the stragglers aboard, and the players' options change to finding a way off, revolt, ransom or sale, or a bargain, which is where a story forms.

Escape

  • Only from Long range. The Spelljammer spends their action on a spellcasting check contested by the pursuing Spelljammer.
  • Speed. +2 for every 5 ft the fleeing ship is faster, and −2 for every 5 ft it is slower.
  • Advantage comes from Find the Gap, or from a pursuer that is slowed or Crippled.
  • Two wins and the ship is gone. Wins are kept if the range closes, but no check can be made until the ship is back at Long.
  • Broken NPC ships use the same rule.

Fight or flee

Keep these in view on the ship sheet every round:

  • Hull now, and the repair days it already costs.
  • Destroyed parts, each worth 1d4 more days. Note which of them need a port.
  • The clock: days to the destination, who is pursuing, and how deep in hostile space the ship is.
  • Escape odds: the current band, and the speed modifier against this enemy.
  • The enemy's state: how close it is to breaking (Hull at half, two parts down, its leader dropped).

Say the price aloud: “Another round like that is two more days stopped.” The crew then weighs the fight against the clock.

The tyrant ship

The exception to the typical ship; its decks are on its vessel entry. AC 17, Hull 300, Bulwark 40, speed 40, mishap threshold 20.

  • No crew weapon. The ship has no NPC-crewed weapon, and the eyes need no crew.
  • Stations. Four of the ten great eyes answer, each at a station around the helm on the Command Deck, and all four work. A station holder sees through their eye and may cast spells through it. Using an eye takes the holder's action. An NPC can hold an empty station (+4 to hit, DC 12).
  • DCs are 8 + PB + the holder's modifier. The eyes reach 120 ft; Disintegration also reaches Long, at disadvantage.
  • Recharge. Roll each spent eye's recharge at the start of the party's turn. Every eye starts a fight charged.
  • The Medic and the Spelljammer hold no eye.
  • The Pact. At H'Catha the eye matching the hour's shut eye goes dead. Its station holder keeps their role.
  • Repairs. A destroyed eye or helm needs a port, which for this ship means beholder craft: a possible hook at H'Catha.

  • Shaft and gravity plane: crossing takes a DC 12 Dex (Acrobatics) check, or the character falls prone.
  • Hollow Deck: the repair deck. Breaches and fires land here, and Shore Up happens here.
  • Cargo Deck: the intake, and the only way in.
  • Distances. The Hollow Deck and the Cargo Deck are each two zones from the Command Deck.
  • Boarding it. Boarders must reach Close range and come in through the Cargo intake, then climb the shaft and cross the gravity plane. Expel clears the Cargo Deck once a fight. Wisps, if the crew kept them, harass everyone aboard. A PC who leaves a station silences its eye unless an NPC takes it.

Flighty Foundling

The typical ship; see its vessel entry. AC 15, Hull 400, Bulwark 30, speed 35, crew 15, mishap threshold 15.

  • Weapons: a ballista and two mangonels. The crew fires one, and the PCs fire the other two.
  • Zones: Helm, Weapon Deck, Below and Rigging.
  • Boarding happens at Close range. Losing crew silences guns.

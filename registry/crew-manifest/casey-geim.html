Casey Geim
Hadozee Warlock; zodiac-flavored magic; reads the sky

Casey is a hadozee warlock cadet at Caelestis, barefoot and composed beneath a pale green cloak stitched with constellations, wielding cold star-bright magic. Awkward and half-orbiting any conversation, he serves as the crew's bosun. A vision beyond Mirt's wardrobe pointed him toward the Viren star system, and a familiar presence has begun following him elsewhere.

PursuitFollowing a vision and a voice that told him 'Go there' — a path toward a place beyond H’Catha in the Viren star system.
Stellar Reading

Casey's stars do more than decorate his cloak. Before he speaks to a stranger, they gather above the target in a partial constellation only he can see. He has learned to read them. He has not learned to read them well.

You can read a stranger's nature in the stars that gather above them.

Choose a creature you can see that you have not yet engaged in conversation. As a bonus action, you can expend 1 of your stars to invoke a stellar reading. Your stars appear as a partial constellation in the sky above the target, visible only to you. Consulting your chart, you may attempt to name the constellation it forms.

  • Correct. You have advantage on Charisma checks made against the target for 1 minute.
  • Incorrect. You have disadvantage on Charisma checks made against the target for 1 minute.

Once the encounter begins, the window closes.

Arrival orders

Brothers in Stars

You did not think much of the stars at first. They were constant, distant, and easily overlooked—points of light that marked the night without asking to be understood. It was Polly who gave them meaning. He would pause beneath an open sky and linger there, gaze lifted, as though something above was worth more than a passing glance. You did not always see what he saw, Casey, but you stayed beside him all the same. Not for the stars, but for him. The quiet certainty in the way he looked at them made it feel as though they mattered, even if you could not say why.

The night of the starfall, the sky answered that certainty. Light crossed the heavens in long, radiant arcs, each streak deliberate, each path too precise to belong to chance. The world gathered in wonder, voices rising in awe, but Polly stood still, watching with a focus you had never seen before. Something settled into him in that moment—recognition, or perhaps resolve. When the last of the light faded, he did not try to explain what he had seen. He did not need to. There was something out there, something real, and he meant to find it.

—

Polly left not long after, following a direction only he seemed to understand. There was no urgency in it, no fear—only purpose, steady and unshaken. He spoke of returning, though not as a promise bound to time, but as something that would make sense when it came. Or, if it did not, that you would understand why. It was not a farewell marked by grief, but one tempered by distance, as though he had already stepped beyond something you could not yet see.

The days that followed settled into a quieter rhythm. His absence was present, but not hollow. It carried with it the echo of his certainty, the sense that he had not been lost, only moved further along a path that remained open. At times, you found yourself looking upward as he once had, tracing the same points of light without quite knowing what you were searching for. The sky had not changed—but something in the way you looked at it had.

—

It began without warning.

A still night. An open sky. And a presence that did not belong to memory alone. It did not arrive with sound or spectacle, but with clarity, as though something long distant had aligned just enough to be heard. The voice that followed was not spoken aloud, yet it carried with it a familiarity that needed no explanation.

"I found it."

The certainty in those words left no room for doubt. In that moment, you knew him. Not as he had been, but not gone either—changed, broadened, as though whatever he had reached had not erased him, but expanded him beyond what you had known. He did not return in form, nor did he stand before you as he once had, but his presence remained, threading through quiet moments and clear skies alike.

He showed you the path not through words, but through direction. A pull that strengthened when you followed it, a sense of alignment that brought clarity and power in equal measure. Where you stepped closer to it, the connection deepened; where you turned away, it dimmed. It was not a demand, nor even a request, but an understanding that settled into place the longer you walked it.

He could not come back to you.

But he had found something greater.

And now, he is leading you to it.

Tumak Swan
Rogue (Soulknife); searching for somewhere they cannot yet name

Tumak Swan is a slight rogue swallowed by an oversized coat, muttering reassurances to himself, whose mind often seems divided. He holds the captain's chair for the crew and keeps a curtained corner of his bunk for things he would rather not explain. He is searching for a family he cannot yet name — and has begun to manifest powers and glowing half-runes he does not understand, with a tether now pulling him toward home.

PursuitSearching for his own family, now trusting the crew to help him find them.

Arrival orders

Two Boys, One Destiny

The deck hums beneath your feet as the ship cuts through the Astral Sea, its motion steady and alive. One of you leans over the railing, the other adjusts in the same breath, balance settling into place without effort as drifting lights and distant shapes slide past in slow, silent currents. Ahead, Caelestis glows with deliberate structure, a place of order and knowledge waiting to be reached. You watch it together, sharing the same pull forward, the same quiet certainty that somewhere beyond it lies the place you came from.

The sky stretches wide, and something in it catches. A soft, luminous haze spreads across the expanse, not distant points but a continuous veil that glows like light caught beneath water. It pulls at you instantly. The memory rises with it, sharp and immediate. The trees come first this time—tall, pale trunks that twist as they grow, their bark smooth and almost glasslike, catching light in faint ripples. Their branches curl in long spirals, each one bending toward the same unseen center, as if the entire forest listens to a single, silent call.

The scent follows, vivid enough to feel present. Warm earth after rain, rich and grounding, threaded with something faintly sweet and metallic, like sap touched by lightning. It settles deep in your chest, familiar in a way that bypasses thought entirely. You breathe it in without realizing you have, and for a moment, everything aligns with it—the rhythm of your stance, the quiet adjustment between you, the way your bodies hold steady as one.

The ship tilts as it banks toward Caelestis, and the shift forces a quick correction. Your footing slips for half a breath before the other compensates, weight shifting, grip tightening, balance snapping back into place. It takes effort this time—small, controlled, practiced—and you feel the strain of holding that perfect alignment together. You adjust again, steadier now, movements syncing as the deck levels out beneath you. The moment passes, but the awareness lingers. Whatever waits ahead will not make room for you. You will have to hold your shape through it, the same way you always have—together, adjusting, correcting, moving forward as one.

A Wayfinder's Path

Tumak's runes surfaced before he understood them — half-marks, glowing, on skin he had not marked. The soul knife comes through a small portal that is a piece of the home he is looking for, and the tether he feels is a connection to that place rather than a direction on any map.

The world knows its own.

The marks read front and back, and only together. Each half is a fragment of a Lunarfoot wayfinder's script: a record of where its bearer has been and a claim on where they belong. Tumak carries two, and neither is finished.

What the rest of the script says, and who else can read it, is not yet known to him.

Bartholomew Grayson
Human; Druid; Wilder; newly arrived cadet with a natural helm affinity

Bartholomew is a human druid and wilder, a cadet at Caelestis with an uncanny natural affinity for the helm. Quiet and untroubled, he has slipped into the crew and settled as its spelljammer. When he shifts into a starry form, a two-starred constellation surfaces — a shape he carries from a fall he took as a child.

Arrival orders

Call of the Wilds

You sit at the edge of a quiet lake with your acceptance letter spread across your knees, one thumb pinning the page while the breeze keeps worrying at the corner. The water gathers the whole morning inside itself—sky, reeds, drifting cloud—until a dragonfly skims across and cuts the reflection into silver ribbons. Your eyes keep returning to the same lines: the Order of All has marked you for an additional certification—Wilder—and the Spelljamming Corps has accepted you aboard Caelestis. Out among the stars live creatures no one here has ever seen, and that alone would have been enough to stir you. But there is something else, something older and harder to name: the feeling that there is a shape inside you that you have never quite managed to reach. Ape, wolf, boar, spider—you know those roads. This one lives deeper. Your fingers dip into the lake and, for a heartbeat, your hand broadens with the weight of some half-remembered beast before settling back. For the first time in your life, the stars feel close enough to hunt for answers.

🐾

The Order reaches you first, and for the better part of a month your world narrows into quiet rooms, narrow windows, and long tables crowded with maps, sketches, and sealed notes. They teach you to read the first shiver in a place: a grove gone silent, a herd moving the wrong way, water holding too still, wind that feels watchful, wildspace that prickles across the skin before anyone can say why. A Wilder learns the early signs and speaks before others step where they should not. You listen. You learn. You study claw marks in the margins, unfamiliar feather shapes, beasts from worlds you have never walked, and every so often one of them catches in your chest for a moment before sliding loose again. Close, but not it. By the time they release you, your instincts are sharper, your questions are bigger, and that nameless shape still waits somewhere just beyond the edge of understanding.

🐾

Then Caelestis rises to meet you, all height, motion, and disciplined noise, and your first day in the Corps begins in earnest. The tower sends you where it sends every new cadet—up stairs, down halls, through forms, stations, checks, and the steady churn of orientation—until by late morning your boots know the rails and your hands know the feel of the place. Caelestis has a mood that day, and it wears it badly. Cadets lower their voices when officers pass. Locked doors draw longer looks than they should. Temporary stars change hands beneath eyes sharp enough to shave with. Twice you catch the word theft drifting through a corridor like thistledown, and once someone mutters again with the tired annoyance of a gardener finding another bite taken from the same leaf. By the time you finish your last task, lunch is starting to drift warm through the tower. You head for the simulation deck. Tarto is waiting beside a bank of pods and a table gone half to paperwork and half to clutter, all scar and posture and command, as though someone carved a bosun from old oak and taught her to glare. The room smells of warm metal, spent magic, and something faintly scorched. Her eyes find you at once. One quick look. One sharp jerk of her chin. "Grayson, is it? Good. You're just in time."

Aspects

In Vaelorix's garden Bartholomew breathed a flower that had only just bloomed, and his body wanted to become something he had never been. Since then he can call a single trait out of a creature he knows, the ape's strength or the spider's grip, without taking its form.

Two stars burn within him. Others are faint.

Each star is a place for an aspect. A creature he studies can give him a trait, and the trait finds its star. What shape the whole constellation makes, he does not yet know.

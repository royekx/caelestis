Sol Fortuna
Astral Elf Paladin of Beshaba; resilient and resourceful

Sol Fortuna is a tall, pale, dark-haired astral elf paladin of Beshaba with white eyes, steady even after simulated death. He anticipates danger before it arrives and puts himself between it and his crew as their gunner and front line. Beshaba has laughed at his shoulder, and now a curious voice speaks to him of fate and whether he has truly escaped his origin.

Arrival orders

Misfortune's Favored

The temple of Tymora was never quiet. Laughter carried through its halls in bright, easy waves, as if nothing within those walls had ever truly gone wrong. Coins rang in shallow bowls, dice clicked across polished tables, and prayers rose like conclusions already decided. Fortune was not asked for here; it was assumed, lived in, reinforced by every small success that passed without question. Each generation, one child was marked. No ceremony named it, but the pattern was always the same—small failures clustering around a single life while the rest of the city moved untouched. Those children grew into the Misfortuned, and for as long as they lived, things held. Missteps gathered where they stood. Accidents found them first. The rest of the world stayed smooth.

You were one of them, and you grew up knowing it in the quiet ways people stepped aside, in the pauses that followed your presence, in the understanding that what followed you was necessary, even if it was never welcomed. There were others before you, older Misfortuned who carried the same quiet burden, and for a time the weight was shared among you, distributed in a way that kept the world intact. But you learned faster than most what it meant to live at the center of failure, to expect the break before it came, to move with it instead of against it. Where others trusted in luck, you trusted in response, and when something went wrong, you were already in motion.

✧✦✧

The ritual promised an end to all of it. Not just prosperity, but permanence. The priests spoke of a world where nothing faltered, where every action resolved cleanly, where uncertainty itself had been removed. The Misfortuned were brought to the center, not hidden but placed exactly where the pattern required them, each one a point through which the last of the world's imbalance would pass. The structure formed around you, precise and unyielding, and for a moment it worked with a kind of perfection that felt wrong. Every motion aligned. Every word landed exactly where it should. The air grew still, as if the world had agreed to hold its shape.

And then, before anything faltered, something appeared. High above the ritual, vast and unmoving, an iridescent form hung suspended in the sky, its surface shifting with color that never settled, catching the light in ways that bent the eye and resisted focus. It did nothing. It simply was. They called it the Prism Ascendant, and the priests looked up in awe, certain they had reached what they sought. You felt something else, a sharp certainty that something had gone too far, too cleanly, with nowhere left for anything to give. You stepped back. The others did not.

When it broke, it did not shatter outward—it collapsed inward. Light fractured, magic surged beyond control, and the flawless structure unraveled all at once. The Misfortuned at the center were caught in it, the burden they had always carried tearing through them without resistance. You moved on instinct, pulling, shouting, trying to break them free, but the force was already too great. One by one, they fell, consumed in the moment the ritual reached its peak. Above it all, the Prism Ascendant remained, unchanged and unmoving, while everything beneath it came apart.

✧✦✧

When it ended, the Ascendant still hung in the sky. The temple gathered beneath it not in panic, but in reverence, their certainty unbroken, only sharpened. The ritual had reached its height. The Ascendant had appeared. What followed below—the collapse, the deaths, the instability—did not contradict that truth. It confirmed it. Something so complete would not come without cost. The Misfortuned had been taken, their purpose fulfilled, their burden resolved. It was a sacrifice, and like all sacrifices, it meant something greater had been achieved.

You were not meant to be part of what remained. You were not meant to survive. The conclusion formed as cleanly as the ritual itself: you had stepped away, broken the pattern, carried something with you that should have been released. The failure had not come from the ritual, but from its interruption. From you. The temple began to search, at first quietly, then with growing purpose. You were no longer avoided. You were required. The ritual had not failed; it had stopped short, and now there was only one Misfortuned left to complete it. A handful of those you had tried to save saw something different in what had happened, and they hid you when they could, moving you from place to place, buying time that would not last as the search closed in.

The offer came before that time ran out. A stranger who did not speak of blame or belief, who did not look to the sky or name what hung there, but who spoke instead of what you had done when everything broke—how you had moved, how you had acted without hesitation while others froze. There were places, they said, where things went wrong more often than they went right, and people were needed who could still function when they did. You had already proven you could. So you left, not as something cast out, but as something taken elsewhere, while behind you the Prism Ascendant still hangs above the temple, and the people who stand beneath it still call it perfection.

Brief Log · Voyage 004
All Things Find Their Way Home
Voyage 004 Brief
The crew clears the clockwork horrors in the tyrant ship's cargo hold and finds the broken autognome — Ostekk-6 — whose name comes off the manifest. Its wounds are mechanical, past what magic can mend. Tumak manifests a telepathic link to speak with it, and Ostekk-6 warns of a mimic aboard and a bloom spreading through the ship. Given a floating dolly to move the spelljamming helm toward the command deck, the crew routes into the access shafts and finds the garden choked with lantern-wisp flowers. They clear the flower in a hard fight — Sol and Casey both casting Arms of Hadar, Tumak throwing his first fire bolt — but the wisps remain and bite harder than expected. Tumak ends it by summoning his soul knife for the first time, feeling a tether to home as the Rune Keeper's voice curves the blade true. Bloodied, the crew decides to rest before descending.

The Clockwork Horrors
  • Combat opens the moment the crew rounds the corner — three clockwork horrors crouched over the wreckage. Sol and Casey hold the front line; Casey works his steady aim from the flank.
  • Gregory opens with magic missile, then uses Vortex Warp to teleport Ostekk-6 — the broken autognome — out of danger and behind him, pulling the helpless figure clear before pressing the attack.
  • The crew dispatches the horrors without serious injury.
Ostekk-6
  • The cargo manifest gives the autognome its name: Ostekk-6. The crew tries to heal it and learns the damage is mechanical, not arcane — its core is sound, but its body needs physical restoration they can't provide here.
  • Frustrated at not being able to speak with it, Tumak manifests a telepathic link for the first time — connecting himself, Gregory, and Ostekk-6 without understanding how he did it.
  • Through the link, Ostekk-6 warns them: a specimen aboard can take the shape of other objects — a mimic. The bloom has spread through much of the ship. The clockwork horrors are aboard as well.
Moving the Helm
  • The goal comes back into focus: get the spelljamming helm up to the command deck and install it to commandeer the ship. The crew is given a magical dolly — a floating disc that lifts the helm — but they still have to route it through the ship's access shafts.
  • The crew settles the area and takes stock. Miken, Ryeback, and Fred are among them.
  • Bartholomew shifts into a starry form — a constellation surfacing from within him, one Casey had glimpsed earlier. It holds two stars. Bartholomew recognizes it: the shape he remembered from a fall he took as a child.
The Shafts
  • Bartholomew drops a lit match down the quarters shaft to gauge its depth. It falls a long way before the light vanishes entirely. Wisps rise and perch around the opening in answer.
  • Investigating the garden shaft, the crew finds it choked with an infestation of lantern-wisp flowers. Ostekk-6 confirms they'll need to pass through the access shafts to reach the command deck — which means clearing a path.
The Flower and the Wisps
  • Sol opens with a Divine Sense, then strikes the flower with a Sacred Flame. The flower answers by releasing a swarm of wisps.
  • Sol takes the front, absorbing hits, and casts Arms of Hadar — dark celestial tentacles that catch the wisps, which fail almost every save. Casey answers with his own constellation-tinged version of the same, spreading the reach further.
  • Sol uses his Misfortune's Favor to take a burden onto himself and succeeds through it. Gregory adds magic missiles; Tumak casts fire bolt for the first time, manifesting magic he doesn't understand and finding it works.
  • The crew kills the flower — and learns the wisps remain. Ostekk-6 explains the flower produces them but isn't the only thing keeping them here. The wisps are tankier than expected, and when they strike they hit hard: Bartholomew takes 10 damage from a single shock.
All Things Find Their Way Home
  • To finish the fight, Tumak summons his soul knife for the first time — a spectral blade thrown from a small portal, his two runes flaring as it leaves. For the first time he feels a tether to home: not its location, but a connection to it.
  • As the knife looks set to miss, a voice — the Rune Keeper — says, all things find their way home, and the blade curves to strike the last wisp true.
  • The fight ends. Bloodied and freshly out of one hard encounter into another, the crew begins to understand these things are lethal — and that they should rest before pushing on toward the deck below.

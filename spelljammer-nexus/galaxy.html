Introduction to Cosmography — Fleet Navigator’s Handbook, Vol. I Chapter 1: Systems, the Galaxy and the Astral Sea
Systems

A system is a star and the bodies that circle it: its worlds, their moons, and the rock and ice that drift between them. A system is named for its primary, the star at its centre, and the name covers the space within it as well as the bodies. Realmspace is the system of Toril, and the space within it. Caelestis Station holds a fixed position there, near Toril.

Travel between worlds in one system takes days by spelljamming helm. Travel between systems takes far longer and crosses Wildspace, along lanes the Fleet has charted to a greater or lesser degree.

The charts issued to cadets cover Realmspace, and they are kept in Navigation Records. A cadet learns what lies beyond them by sailing it.

The Erevos Galaxy

Realmspace lies in the Erevos Galaxy, a great body of star systems turning around Erevos, the black hole at its core, for which the galaxy is named. Four spiral arms run out from the core, and the galaxy's systems lie along them. Realmspace lies on one arm, toward the galaxy's edge.

The United Intergalactic Fleet works within the Erevos Galaxy. It keeps one sector along each of the four arms, and the Caelestis Sector holds the arm that carries Realmspace.

The Astral Sea

The Astral Sea fills all of space: within every system, between the systems, and out beyond them. It is not empty. A diffuse magical energy runs through it, and that energy is what lets a spelljamming helm drive a ship and what keeps a ship's air envelope alive.

Navigators name a reach of the Sea by where it lies. Within a system the space carries the system's name, so a ship sailing near Toril is in Realmspace, though anyone may simply call it the Astral Sea. Between systems the Sea is called Wildspace. The Fleet's everyday word for the whole Sea is the drift, and a Driftwarden is a warden of the drift.

Priests teach that the greater gods dwell in the deep Astral Sea, far out beyond the systems where ships sail.

What Wildspace Is

Wildspace is the Astral Sea between systems: the stretch a spelljamming vessel crosses when it leaves one system for another. Within a system the same Sea goes by the system's name, as Realmspace does around Toril. The Fleet calls all of it the drift. The cosmography manual covers systems, the Erevos Galaxy and the Sea as a whole; this manual covers the crossing.

Lanes

Vessels cross Wildspace along lanes, and the Fleet grades every lane by how well it is known. A charted lane is surveyed, logged and routinely sailed, and a crossing on one is quiet more often than not. A long haul is charted but far, and supplies decide whether a crew arrives in good order. An uncharted route is sailed rarely and recorded badly; conditions change along it, and both the arrival time and the arrival point are uncertain. Some lanes are closed by standing order. A closed lane can be sailed, and the Fleet forbids it; a crew caught on one answers to the Fleet.

Current lane data is held in Navigation Records.

Currents

The drift has currents, and the charted lanes run with them. Off the lanes the currents run uncharted. Some carry a vessel inward toward a system, some hold it in place, and some carry it out along a bearing nobody has surveyed. A helm can fight a current for a while, and a crew that leaves the lane should know which kind it has entered before it decides to.

Color Pools

Color pools are portals adrift in the Astral Sea, each opening onto another plane. They open and close quickly. The Fleet charts them where it can and sails around them, and Fleet doctrine treats a pool on the course as an obstacle to clear by a wide margin.

Dead Gods and Drifting Matter

The corpses of dead gods drift through the Astral Sea, with wreckage, debris and abandoned works collected around them. On a crossing that is otherwise featureless they serve as landmarks. They are rarely empty: salvage crews, hermits and githyanki all work them, and a crew approaching one should expect company.

Gravity

Gravity in the Astral Sea does not behave as it does at a planetary surface. Planetary bodies generate gravity fields that function normally within a limited radius. Beyond those fields, which is to say for most of the time a vessel spends in transit, gravity runs parallel to the hull and pulls crew toward the nearest large flat surface at roughly the strength of surface gravity.

In practice, a crew member on the upper deck and one on the lower deck both stand "down" relative to their own surface. Objects released over the side of a vessel fall away from the ship. The midpoint of a small vessel has negligible gravity, and loose items float there. Secure your equipment, because cargo lost over the side in the drift cannot be retrieved conveniently.

Air Envelopes

Every mass of sufficient size in the Astral Sea holds an air envelope, a bubble of breathable atmosphere extending from its surface. A spelljamming vessel's envelope is proportional to the ship's mass and refreshes itself as the vessel moves. A vessel that holds still for long finds its envelope degrading, so keep the ship moving on long holds. A vessel that loses its envelope does not keep its crew alive for long; review the manual section on envelope maintenance and emergency procedure before any crossing.

When two ships make physical contact, their envelopes merge into one traversable gap of air, and that is how boarding actions begin. Treat any approaching vessel's intent as unknown until it is positively established, because allowing contact with an unidentified vessel allows a boarding action to begin.

Navigation

The spelljammer at the helm has an intuitive sense of the system's geometry, a broad directional feel for the space around the vessel. It supplements conventional navigation and does not replace it. Fine positioning, rendezvous calculations and the approach to a specific destination require charts, direct observation and a crew's navigation skill. A helm is not a map.

Fleet charts of Realmspace cover the inner system with reasonable accuracy, and the outer system less consistently. Beyond Realmspace a crew relies on Navigation Records, independent surveys or its own scouting. Do not trust inner-system chart quality in the outer system or in Wildspace.

Conditions

Asteroid fields, gravitational anomalies, debris clouds, void-adapted creatures and the occupants of other vessels all create conditions a crew must answer. Temperature beyond an envelope runs to extremes in both directions, depending on how near the vessel is to a star, and radiation varies with position.

The most consistent hazard is other ships. Pirates, raiders and hostile creatures work throughout Wildspace. Fleet doctrine for an unknown vessel is to establish identification at distance, keep separation and assume nothing. Most encounters in the drift end before weapons are drawn, and the rest need a crew that knew its work before the contact began.

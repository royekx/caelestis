Four Sectors, One Fleet

The United Intergalactic Fleet, which everyone calls the Fleet, is made of four sector branches, one along each of the Erevos Galaxy's four spiral arms. Each branch oversees a sector, a cluster of several systems, and keeps stations, satellites and waystations in its systems and in the drift between them. The branches work together, and together they make the Fleet.

Caelestis founded the Fleet about 250 cycles ago, in about Cycle 1240 of Fleet time. The sectors began as separate institutions. Caelestis proposed a combined fleet, and the others came together around it over time, so each sector keeps its own ways and its own names. The sectors differ in character as well: some are disciplined and naval, and others take any work for the coin.

The Fleet is one of the largest organised bodies of spelljammers in the galaxy, though not every spelljammer belongs to it.

Driftwardens

Everyone in the Fleet's service is a Driftwarden. The drift is the Fleet's everyday word for the Astral Sea, all of it, and a Driftwarden is a warden of the drift. Every Driftwarden is assigned to a sector and holds a rank, from the newest graduate up through officers and commanders.

Cadets train at a spelljammer academy to become Driftwardens and hold no Fleet rank until they graduate. There are only a handful of spelljammer academies in the galaxy, and the Caelestis Spelljammer Academy is one of them.

Rank shows in gems or pins. Driftwardens otherwise wear what they like, while seated officers at a station wear something closer to a uniform. Clearance is separate from rank and is carried on an access star.

Chain of Command

Each sector keeps stations at its systems or between them, each under a commander, with smaller satellites and waystations around them. A station's crew is drawn from the sector that keeps it.

A Driftwarden reports to the commander of their station. Station commanders answer to the sector's command, and the sectors answer to a council of the Fleet's highest officers, a few admirals. No single person leads the Fleet. Each sector names its command in its own way; at Caelestis it is the Bridge.

The Caelestis Sector

The Caelestis Sector is a cluster of several systems, among them Toril's. It has two parts. The Caelestis Corps is the sector's branch of the Fleet and oversees the sector, and the Caelestis Spelljammer Academy trains its cadets. Both are housed at Caelestis Station, under the Bridge.

The sector's archive is the Fleet Intelligence Archive. It holds what Caelestis knows of its own sector and some of what the other three sectors share. The sectors pass station readings between them, which is how a disturbance seen at one edge of the galaxy reaches another, but each keeps a good deal to itself.

The Work

The Fleet is not the law of the cosmos. It keeps an order of its own choosing, and it lives by contracts. Much of its work is mercantile, because it holds the easiest routes between systems: it carries shipments, protects them, and carries passengers. Its most frequent passengers, and its closest partners, are the Watchers, whom Fleet ships carry to their posts across the galaxy.

Contracts take other shapes too: excavations, escorts, retrievals, hunts. A crew starts close to its station, and its range grows with its rank. Independent spelljammers, other peoples and smaller mercantile houses work outside the Fleet, and the easiest routes are still its own.

Some worlds know about spelljamming and some do not. Work on a world that knows is done in the open. Work on a world that does not is covert: a Fleet crew blends in, deals only with the few locals who have been read in, and leaves the world's sense of the sky as it found it. Ship-shaped spelljammers exist so that a crew can come down on water and walk in through a port.

Went Overboard

In the Fleet, "went overboard" means more than a fall from a ship. A crew member who leaves the ship, willingly or not, and does not come back has gone overboard. It is the Fleet's softer way of saying killed in action.

The Device

The Fleet's device is the Erevos Galaxy seen from above and tilted a little, its four spiral arms turning around the black hole at its centre, all inside a double ring. A small eight-pointed star sits on each arm, one for each of the four sectors, and faint lines run from each star in to the centre: four branches, one Fleet.

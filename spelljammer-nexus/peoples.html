Peoples of the Drift — Field Guide to Spacefaring and Worldbound Peoples, Cadet Edition Caelestis Corps · Cadet distribution

This guide covers the peoples a Fleet crew is likely to meet, in the drift and in port. Spacefaring peoples come first and at length, the peoples of the worlds follow in brief, and the last section covers the peoples a crew should approach with care.

Each entry carries the Fleet's general posture toward that people. Cooperative peoples work alongside the Fleet as a rule. Neutral peoples deal with it on terms, Variable peoples differ from one crew to the next and are assessed on contact, and Hostile peoples are treated as hostile from first sight. A posture describes a people; the person in front of you may differ from it, and current orders outrank this guide.

Where an entry describes a fight, it uses the Academy's fixed wording for harm. A creature that shrugs off half of a kind of harm takes half of it. A creature unharmed by a kind of harm takes none of it, and one that takes double from a kind of harm takes twice the usual. Where an entry names no such kind, that people has none. The creatures and hazards of a crossing are in Perils of the Drift.

Spacefaring peoples

Astral elf

Neutral
  • Appearance. Slender, long-lived elves whose eyes carry points of light like stars.
  • Where met. Aboard elven ships and at elven holdings far out in the drift, and as individuals serving on other peoples' ships. Some Academy cadets are astral elves.
  • Temperament and customs. Astral elves keep their own courts, ranks and customs, and many find other peoples brief and hurried. They do not sleep. An astral elf rests in a waking trance of about four hours and comes out of it with a skill or a knack drawn from shared elven memory, which it holds until its next trance.
  • With the Fleet. Elven fleets keep to their own business. An astral elf on a Fleet crew is judged as any crew member is.
  • On meeting. Courtesy and patience serve best. Address an elf by rank where you know it.
  • In a fight. An astral elf can step through the air to a point it can see about thirty feet away, several times between rests, and kindles light or holy fire at will. Charms take hold of it less often, and magic cannot put it to sleep.

Autognome

Cooperative
  • Appearance. A mechanical gnome built by rock gnomes in their own likeness, with a metal casing painted with a gnome's features. It walks with a stiff gait and clanks, whirs and wheezes as it goes. No two are made of quite the same materials; magic is what gives an autognome its mind.
  • Where met. Wherever gnomes build and trade, and on ships and stations as crew.
  • Temperament and customs. An autognome is made for its maker's purpose, and most are built with three standing directives: defend gnomes attacked by others, defend itself when attacked, and protect the young of any people. The last makes no distinction of species. A flaw in an autognome's making can free it from its orders, and such autognomes wander the drift on business of their own.
  • With the Fleet. Steady crew. An autognome needs no food, drink or air, and it rests for about six hours in a motionless state that looks inert while it stays aware of everything around it, which suits it to long watches. Poison does it half harm and disease takes no hold. A mending spell repairs it, and its makers built it to take healing magic as a living body does.
  • On meeting. Speak to an autognome as to any other member of a crew.

Dohwar

Neutral
  • Appearance. Short, round, flightless bird-folk about three feet tall, who look and move like penguins and have small arms with tiny hands in place of wings. A dohwar's plumage is one bright colour, and it can change in a moment of strong feeling. Many wear a clashing mix of clothes under a hooded cloak.
  • Where met. Trading and salvaging along the lanes, working wrecks and drifting cargo, and doing business in back rooms and quiet corners of a port even when the business is lawful. Dohwar breed space swine, winged boars they ride and load like pack animals.
  • Temperament and customs. Dohwar trade hard and bargain in their own way: a dohwar seller calls out prices, again and again and often over other dohwar doing the same, until the customer buys. They are telepathic. Two dohwar can hold a silent conversation with each other and with a third creature at once, a link they call merging, so a group that looks disorganised is usually working as one. Some can skim a customer's surface thoughts during a deal, and a customer who expects it resists it better.
  • With the Fleet. Commercially neutral. A dohwar salvager on a crossing is usually after the same wreck as everyone else.
  • On meeting. Settle on your price before the bargaining starts, and keep your mind on it.

Dracon

Cooperative
  • Appearance. Big, heavy folk the size of a centaur and bulkier, with the four-legged body, tail and broad flat feet of a great plant-eating lizard, a human torso and arms, and a dragon's head.
  • Where met. Dracon began appearing at the edges of charted space about a century ago. They come from a world where they were the only thinking people, and they are still new to the drift.
  • Temperament and customs. Friendly from the first meeting, and communal. Dracon eat plants, travel in herds and can fall ill when parted from their families for long; a dracon alone often adopts a crew as its herd. A herd's leader, the kaba, is chosen by lineage through a reckoning other peoples find baffling and dracon settle in a few questions. Being new to so many peoples, a dracon may mix up elves and dwarves, and which customs go with which.
  • With the Fleet. Cooperative and glad of company, and a dracon can carry a small rider.
  • On meeting. Expect warmth, and expect questions about your family.

Giff

Neutral
  • Appearance. Hippo-headed folk eight to nine feet tall and heavily muscled, who dress smartly and move with a grace that surprises anyone expecting them to lumber. Giff swim well.
  • Where met. As mercenaries, shipmates and shock troops across the drift, and as crew and staff on stations, Caelestis among them. A giff warlord may command a small fleet from a fortified hold.
  • Temperament and customs. Giff life is organised around professional soldiering, with elaborate codes of military conduct that giff take entirely seriously. They love gunpowder: they handle firearms and explosives with expert skill and open delight, and gunpowder is a payment they prize. Giff will not fire on other giff. When two giff companies find themselves on opposite sides, both sit the fight out, and afterward the winners rescue the losers.
  • With the Fleet. As hired crew or contracted forces, giff are reliable and skilled, and they care little for an employer's politics as long as the pay arrives correctly and the terms were clear from the start.
  • On meeting. State a contract's terms plainly and up front, and keep them.
  • In a fight. Giff shoot faster and farther than anyone else: reloading barely slows them, and long range costs them nothing in accuracy. Expect a volley, then a charge; a giff shock trooper running straight at a target can bowl it off its feet. A giff on a ship's deck is very hard to knock down or shove overboard, and some carry force grenades that burst across a wide area. Their gunpowder is mundane, so their guns fire where magic fails.

Hadozee

Cooperative
  • Appearance. Tall, slender ape-like folk covered in shaggy hair, often with a thick mane, with membranes of skin joining arms, body and legs. Their feet grip as well as their hands.
  • Where met. On deck. Hadozee are among the most sought-after sailors in the drift and make up much of the crew on some ships. Many raise their young in forests or forest cities, where climbing and gliding are learned early.
  • Temperament and customs. Wanderers by nature, always after what lies past the horizon. Their language changes faster than most, because hadozee take words from every people they meet and trade them among themselves; it is notorious for its curses. Many elven peoples count hadozee as friends and open their forest cities to them.
  • With the Fleet. Favoured deckhands. A hadozee climbs rigging with both hands free, and when it falls from a height it spreads its membranes and glides to a landing unhurt. In a fight it can twist to blunt a blow.
  • On meeting. A hadozee judges a crew by how it handles its ship.

Mercane

Neutral
  • Appearance. Lanky blue giants about twelve feet tall, with elongated heads and long, spindly fingers, dressed in elegant robes.
  • Where met. Trading in the drift and in port, usually one at a time and accompanied by underlings or bodyguards. Mercanes are best known for procuring and selling magic items, spelljamming helms among them, and they trade in services, ideas and information as readily as in goods.
  • Temperament and customs. A mercane deals with anyone, fairly and reliably, provided that party has never harmed or swindled another mercane. Mercanes speak to one another mind to mind across any distance, and they use it to warn each other about dangerous or unreliable customers. A mercane once offended is next to impossible to win back. Its word, formally given in a commercial context, is reliable; its prices are rarely open to negotiation, and its motives are its own.
  • With the Fleet. Commercially neutral. Mercanes take no side in a conflict they are not party to, and they protect their interests with considerable resources.
  • On meeting. Deal honestly and pay what you agreed. A swindle against one mercane is soon known to the rest.
  • In a fight. A mercane leaves fighting to its guards where it can. Its own blades are charged with psionic force, and a cut can strike a moment's fear into the target.

Oortling

Cooperative
  • Appearance. Small grey folk about three feet tall, with large heads and big black eyes, who live inside golems about eight feet tall. The golem is manifested by a psicrystal tuned to the oortling's mind and works as its body, an extension of it, where armour would be a shell.
  • Where met. Travelling the drift in search of knowledge. Their people once hid in the icy comets at the edges of systems, living on the nutrients in the ice.
  • Temperament and customs. Curious, gentle and community-minded. Their ancestors were captives of the illithids, bred small and frail and held down by fear fed through their shared empathy, and many fled when their captors' empire fell. A golem shields an oortling from that inherited fear. A dying oortling can imprint its mind on a young oortling's psicrystal to help guide it.
  • With the Fleet. Cooperative. Inside its golem an oortling shrugs off half of psychic harm and holds its mind steady; outside it, an oortling is frail and slow.
  • On meeting. Expect questions, and answer them.

Plasmoid

Cooperative
  • Appearance. Ooze-folk with a body of translucent jelly. A plasmoid can shape itself a rough head, arms and legs, or slump into a limbless blob, and it can push out a pseudopod about ten feet long to work an object.
  • Where met. Everywhere. Plasmoids are at home in any port and on any ship, and wanderlust carries many of them from world to world with their possessions in a pack. Now and then a plasmoid swells far past the usual size as it comes of age, around twenty; such a plasmoid often ends up running a guild, a ship or a community.
  • With the Fleet. Cooperative, and useful in tight places. A plasmoid carrying nothing can squeeze through a gap an inch wide, is hard to hold and quick to slip a grip, and can hold its breath for an hour. Acid and poison do it half harm.
  • On meeting. A plasmoid's shape tells you little about its mood; ask.

Reigar

Variable
  • Appearance. Striking folk with glowing freckles and skin that changes colour, each surrounded by a glory: a cloud of glittering motes that shifts colour at random and turns blows aside. Reigar began as octopus-like creatures of a stormy sea and were remade long ago into their present form.
  • Where met. Wandering the drift in search of artistic inspiration, aboard esthetics: living ships that each reigar grows for itself. From a distance an esthetic looks like a giant glowing jellyfish or squid, and only its reigar can open its hatch.
  • Temperament and customs. Vain, bold and peerless artisans, though each reigar holds its own idea of what art is; for some, war is the highest art. Each carries a talarith, a magic item it made and that only it can use.
  • With the Fleet. Variable. A reigar may be a patron, a rival or a raider.
  • On meeting. Praise sincerely, and never mock a reigar's work. A slighted reigar is dangerous.
  • In a fight. A reigar's glory makes it hard to hit. It fights with a trident and throws bolts of frost, fire, lightning or radiant light, and once between rests its talarith calls up a duplicate of itself that fights for an hour. A hostile esthetic can silence a nearby ship's helm with a scream that lasts for days, then seize crew in acid-dripping tentacles. The esthetic is unharmed by acid.

Scro

Variable
  • Appearance. Kin of orcs and nearly identical to them, though taller, more upright and better spoken, with the bearing of soldiers.
  • Where met. Aboard their own warships and at their holdings, and as mercenaries.
  • Temperament and customs. Disciplined, militant and organised. The scro remade themselves around tactics and strategy after their forebears lost a long war against elven fleets and were driven out of their worlds; they came back, fought the elves again to a standstill and retook those worlds. Scro play games of strategy where their ancestors brawled, and they work to bring worldbound orcs onto the same path. They reach adulthood at twelve and seldom live past forty; most die in battle.
  • With the Fleet. Variable, since scro answer to their hierarchy first.
  • On meeting. Speak to the commander.
  • In a fight. Scro fight as a unit and press together on one target at a time, and their warriors bite to steady themselves in the middle of a fight. They see well in the dark.

Thri-kreen

Cooperative
  • Appearance. Insect-folk with a chitin carapace and four arms, the lower pair smaller than the upper. The carapace can change colour to match its surroundings.
  • Where met. As crew, guides and hunters. A thri-kreen tracker in the drift may be on a fugitive's trail or leading a band of pirates.
  • Temperament and customs. Thri-kreen do not sleep; they stay alert through a rest. They speak their own clicking tongue aloud and every other language through telepathy, mind to mind with willing listeners out to about 120 feet.
  • With the Fleet. Cooperative, and valued on watch.
  • On meeting. A thri-kreen's words arrive in your head; answer aloud.

Xixchil

Neutral
  • Appearance. Mantis-like folk about five feet tall with six limbs: walking legs, a pair of arms and a lower pair of scythe-bladed forelimbs. They decorate their chitin with jewels, gems and ornaments, and work armour into it.
  • Where met. In port, practising surgery and craft.
  • Temperament and customs. Raised on a world crowded with predators, xixchil hold that every living thing should improve itself, and that a body is the home of the soul, to be improved and made an expression of the one who lives in it. Many are surgeons; a xixchil surgeon can replace or add a body part, and adventurers seek them out for it. They are fine artisans of every other kind as well, and they do what is best for themselves.
  • With the Fleet. Neutral. Xixchil sell their skill to anyone who pays.
  • On meeting. Agree the price and the work before any surgery begins.

Peoples of the worlds

Worldbound peoples take their posture from their world and their rulers, so most are marked Variable. A crew meets them in port, as passengers and as crew who have taken to the drift.

Human

Variable
  • Where met. On more worlds than any other people, and in every port.
  • Temperament and customs. Short-lived, adaptable and as varied as the worlds they live on; a human's customs are those of their world.

Elves

Variable
  • Appearance. Long-lived, slender folk with pointed ears. High elves and wood elves are the most common; drow are dark-skinned elves of the deep places under a world's surface; sea elves live in the oceans; eladrin carry the moods of the seasons in their looks and temper; shadar-kai are grey, gaunt elves touched by shadow.
  • Temperament and customs. Elves trance rather than sleep and shrug off charms more readily than most. Their realms differ as much as human ones, and some elven peoples sail fleets of their own.

Dwarves

Variable
  • Appearance. Short, broad and sturdy folk, long-lived and hard to poison.
  • Where met. In mountain holds and mining settlements, and on asteroids and stations where there is metal to work.
  • Temperament and customs. Clan-minded, proud of their craft and slow to forget a debt or a wrong.

Halflings

Variable
  • Appearance. Small folk about three feet tall, quick and quiet on their feet.
  • Temperament and customs. Friendly, practical and famously lucky; a halfling in a tight spot often comes out of it better than anyone expected.

Gnomes

Cooperative
  • Appearance. Small, lively folk with sharp minds, long lives and a resistance to magic that tries to cloud their thinking.
  • Temperament and customs. Rock gnomes are tinkerers and inventors; they build the autognomes, and some have tried to drive spelljamming ships with giant space hamsters running in wheels, so far without success. Forest gnomes keep to the woods and talk with small animals.

Orcs

Variable
  • Appearance. Tall, powerful grey-green folk with prominent lower tusks, who see well in the dark.
  • Temperament and customs. Orc peoples range from raiding hordes to settled clans and city dwellers. The scro are their spacefaring kin.

Goliaths

Variable
  • Appearance. Towering mountain folk with grey, mottled skin and a giant's strength in their build.
  • Temperament and customs. Competitive and fair-minded; goliaths keep score of their own deeds and expect others to pull their weight.

Dragonborn

Variable
  • Appearance. Tall, scaled folk with dragon-like heads, who breathe a blast of elemental energy and shrug off half of the same kind of harm.
  • Temperament and customs. Clan and honour come first, and a dragonborn's word is meant to be kept.

Tieflings

Variable
  • Appearance. Folk with a fiendish heritage in their blood, showing it in horns, tails, unusual skin and solid-coloured eyes. Most shrug off half of fire.
  • Temperament and customs. As varied as humans. Many have met suspicion all their lives and expect it.

Aasimar

Variable
  • Appearance. Folk with a celestial heritage, often marked by luminous eyes or a faint inner light. They shrug off half of necrotic and radiant harm.
  • Temperament and customs. Many feel a calling, and many struggle with what it asks of them.

Genasi

Variable
  • Appearance. Folk with elemental heritage, marked by their element: air genasi carry a breeze and need little breath, earth genasi have stony skin, fire genasi run hot and shrug off half of fire, and water genasi breathe water and shrug off half of acid.
  • Temperament and customs. Genasi often share their element's temper, from the restless to the immovable.

Firbolgs

Variable
  • Appearance. Large, gentle forest folk with cow-like noses and soft voices, who can speak with plants and animals and turn briefly invisible.
  • Temperament and customs. Quiet guardians of the wild, wary of cities and of anyone who takes more than they need.

Tabaxi

Variable
  • Appearance. Cat-like folk with claws, sharp senses and bursts of great speed.
  • Temperament and customs. Curious above all; a tabaxi collects stories, places and objects, and moves on once the novelty fades.

Tortles

Variable
  • Appearance. Turtle-folk who carry their shells as natural armour and can withdraw into them, and who can hold their breath for an hour.
  • Temperament and customs. Easygoing wanderers who take each day as it comes and carry their home on their backs.

Tritons

Variable
  • Appearance. Sea folk with blue or green skin and webbed hands and feet, who breathe air and water and shrug off half of cold.
  • Temperament and customs. Dutiful guardians of the deep, proud and sometimes stiff with peoples who live above the waves.

Kenku

Variable
  • Appearance. Flightless raven-folk who speak by mimicking sounds and voices they have heard.
  • Temperament and customs. Kenku keep a sharp memory and often live on the margins of other peoples' cities.

Lizardfolk and aarakocra

Variable
  • Where met. Both live on worlds of every kind. On some gas giants, earth and water islands swirl in the winds, and aarakocra and lizardfolk live on them; the lizardfolk of such worlds are used to trading with spacefarers.
  • Temperament and customs. Aarakocra are bird-folk who fly and love open sky. Lizardfolk are scaled and practical, judge things by their use, and see little point in sentiment.

Goblinoids

Variable
  • Appearance. Goblins are small and quick, hobgoblins tall and soldierly, and bugbears big, hairy and stealthy.
  • Temperament and customs. Hobgoblin legions are disciplined and organised; goblin and bugbear bands range from raiders to settled neighbours. Goblinoids once fought long wars against elven fleets.

Kobolds

Variable
  • Appearance. Small reptilian folk who trace their line to dragons.
  • Temperament and customs. Clever trap-makers who work in numbers, and often devoted to a dragon of their own.

Changelings

Variable
  • Appearance. Pale folk with colourless eyes who can change their face, voice and build at will.
  • Temperament and customs. Many keep several identities. A changeling may be anyone in a crowd, which is why many peoples distrust them.

Harengon

Variable
  • Appearance. Rabbit-folk with long ears, quick reflexes and a powerful leap.
  • Temperament and customs. Restless, alert and fond of freedom and travel.

Owlin

Variable
  • Appearance. Owl-folk with silent feathers who fly and see well in the dark.
  • Temperament and customs. Watchful and patient.

Satyrs

Variable
  • Appearance. Goat-legged, horned folk who resist magic more than most.
  • Temperament and customs. Revellers who love music, wine and a good time, and who rarely plan past tomorrow.

Centaurs

Variable
  • Appearance. Folk with a human torso on a horse's body, fast over open ground.
  • Temperament and customs. Travellers of open country who keep to their herds and their own ways.

Minotaurs

Variable
  • Appearance. Bull-headed folk with horns they use in a charge.
  • Temperament and customs. Many are fine sailors and proud warriors.

Fairies

Variable
  • Appearance. Small winged fey folk of many shapes, with a little innate magic.
  • Temperament and customs. Whimsical and unpredictable, and keen on bargains and promises kept to the letter.

Yuan-ti

Hostile
  • Appearance. Serpent-folk, from those who can pass for human to scaled monstrosities, who resist magic and are unharmed by poison.
  • Temperament and customs. Cold, scheming and ruthless; their temples and cities work through cults and infiltration.

Locathah

Variable
  • Appearance. Fish-folk with scaled hides who breathe water and can last only a few hours out of it.
  • Temperament and customs. A hardy, long-oppressed people who have learned to look after their own.

Kuo-toa

Hostile
  • Appearance. Fish-headed folk with slick skin and bulging eyes, who live in dark waters and caverns and hate bright light.
  • Temperament and customs. Suspicious, unpredictable and fervent in their worship.

Sahuagin

Hostile
  • Appearance. Shark-like sea devils with fins and claws; the smell of blood drives them to frenzy.
  • Temperament and customs. Raiders of coasts and shipping, organised under priestesses and barons.

Ogres and giants

Variable
  • Appearance. Ogres stand about nine feet tall and are brutally strong; giants are larger still and come in many kinds, each with its own lands and temper.
  • Temperament and customs. Ogres are greedy and quick to violence, and often work as hired muscle. Giants keep their own ranks and grudges and treat small folk according to their kind.

Peoples to approach with care

Githyanki

Hostile
  • Appearance. Lean, yellow-skinned humanoids, often with dark hair bound high, in ornate armour. Their knights carry silver greatswords.
  • Where met. Raiding across the drift from their own ships and fortresses, and working the dead gods. Some ride red dragons. Githyanki in the employ of other parties are met occasionally; that reflects their availability as mercenaries, and their posture toward everyone else stays the same.
  • Temperament and customs. Githyanki were once thralls of the illithids and won their freedom by revolt. They hate mind flayers above everything else and hunt them wherever they find them. Toward worldbound peoples they show contempt and practical predation; raiding is their trade and their pride. They are disciplined, well trained and capable spelljammers.
  • With the Fleet. Fleet doctrine treats githyanki contact as hostile unless specific intelligence says otherwise.
  • On meeting. Keep separation, and keep a githyanki ship out of boarding range.
  • In a fight. A githyanki warrior's greatsword carries psychic force, so every cut lands as steel and as a blow to the mind together. A warrior can leap great distances, step through the air in a silver mist to a point it can see nearby, and veil itself from divination, each a few times a day; on a boarding, expect them on your deck before their ship touches yours. A knight's silver sword strikes harder still.

Neogi

Hostile
  • Appearance. An adult neogi has a small, spider-like body and a long, eel-like neck and head; some grow to a person's size. Hatchlings are ugly, squealing creatures about the size of a cat, with hairy, spider-like bodies and eel-like heads.
  • Where met. Aboard their spider-legged ships, slaving, usually with umber hulk thralls.
  • Temperament and customs. Neogi society is built on control. A neogi's worth is its power to dominate others, and every other thinking creature is property or a tool. Hatred is as foreign to a neogi as love, and loyalty without authority behind it is foolishness. A neogi lives about a century. When one grows weak with age, the others overpower it and poison it into a bloated mass of flesh, then lay their eggs on it; the hatchlings devour the old neogi and each other until only the strongest are left. Sometimes the young band together into a skittering swarm instead.
  • With the Fleet. Hostile without qualification. The Fleet has no record of a productive diplomatic exchange with a neogi captain and expects none.
  • On meeting. Break contact. If boarded, fight them and their thralls apart.
  • In a fight. A neogi's bite carries a venom that hurts badly and leaves the victim sick and weak; adults claw as well. A neogi can bend a creature's will and hold it as a charmed thrall for a day, speaking to it mind to mind out to a mile, and then needs rest before it can do so again. The hold breaks if the neogi dies or goes more than a mile from its thrall, and a thrall that takes harm has a fresh chance to throw it off. Neogi are hard to frighten or charm, magic cannot put them to sleep, and they climb walls and ceilings like spiders. Hatchlings bite with the same venom, and a swarm of them shrugs off half of the harm from blades, clubs and arrows.

Psurlon

Hostile
  • Appearance. Worm-like aberrations about seven feet long, with legs that end in hooves and hands with three long fingers. The eyeless head is like an earthworm's, capped by a maw ringed with teeth. Psurlons wear richly coloured robes and seldom armour.
  • Where met. In strongholds far out in the drift. About once a century psurlons come out into inhabited systems for a seven-year ceremony they call the Feast of Worlds, eating as many thinking creatures as they can before they withdraw; they prefer humans and halflings. They use magic to slip into a settlement before they strike.
  • Temperament and customs. Malevolent and long-lived, measuring their lives in thousands of years. Githyanki despise them and attack their strongholds wherever they find them.
  • With the Fleet. Hostile.
  • In a fight. A psurlon perceives everything within about 120 feet without eyes and is blind beyond that, so darkness and blinding do nothing to it; it cannot be charmed. It crushes a mind with psychic force from as far off as it can perceive, and it shrugs off half of psychic harm itself. Magic cannot read its thoughts or put it to sleep.

Beholders and kin

Hostile
  • Appearance. A beholder is a floating orb of flesh with a great central eye above a wide, toothed maw and a crown of smaller eyes on stalks. Beholders vary widely: plated or smooth-hided, with stalks jointed like a crab's or writhing like tentacles, in many hues. Their kin are smaller, and the two a crew is most likely to meet, the spectator and the gazer, are covered in Perils of the Drift.
  • Where met. In their own holdings and on worlds they hold. Their ships are carved from stone.
  • Temperament and customs. Xenophobic. Every beholder believes its own form is the ideal and that every other creature, other beholders included, resents it and plots against it, and the slightest difference in shape or colour can make two beholders lifelong enemies. Some turn that hatred into tyranny and rule other creatures as eye tyrants. A beholder judges its worth by what it has collected and never parts willingly with a treasure. A sleeping beholder closes its central eye and keeps its small eyes open and watching.
  • With the Fleet. Hostile.
  • On meeting. Stay out of its sight. If a beholder speaks to you, be respectful and brief, and leave.
  • In a fight. The central eye projects a cone of dead magic about 150 feet long. Inside it spells fail, magic items go quiet and enchanted weapons strike as plain ones, and the beholder's own eye rays fail too, so it points the cone at whatever magic threatens it and turns its rays on everything else. Each turn it fires three of its ten eye rays, chosen at random, at targets out to 120 feet, and it fires more between turns: charm, paralysis, fear, slowing, withering necrotic harm, a telekinetic grip, sleep, petrification, disintegration and death. It hovers and cannot be knocked prone. Mundane weapons, gunpowder included, work inside the cone of dead magic.

Mind flayers

Hostile
  • Appearance. Humanoids with mauve skin under a thin glaze of mucus and an octopus-like head, with four tentacles around the mouth. They are also called illithids.
  • Where met. In colonies hidden underground on the worlds they hold, and in the drift aboard nautiloids, their shell-shaped ships. Illithid worlds exist, and the Fleet keeps clear of them.
  • Temperament and customs. Illithids once held empires across many worlds and enslaved whole peoples, the githyanki among them, before those empires fell. Most mind flayers belong to a colony devoted to an elder brain: a vast brain in a pool at the colony's heart, which speaks to every mind flayer within about five miles. They live on the brains of humanoids. They reproduce by ceremorphosis: a tadpole placed in a humanoid's skull devours the host's brain and takes the body, and within about a week a new mind flayer stands where the person stood. The host's brain is eaten, and the person is gone.
  • With the Fleet. Hostile; keep clear of nautiloids.
  • On meeting. Keep your distance and break its line of sight. Mind flayers send thralls forward and hang back themselves.
  • In a fight. A mind flayer's mind blast is a cone of psychic force about sixty feet long. Everyone in it whose will fails takes heavy psychic harm and is stunned, for up to a minute; a stunned sailor can shake it off by force of will. The blast needs time to gather again before it can be used a second time. Up close, the tentacles grip and stun, and a mind flayer holding a helpless victim can extract and eat its brain. It reads surface thoughts at will, can levitate, and once a day can dominate a mind or shift itself to another plane. Spells and other magic take hold of it less often than of most creatures. Its thralls stand between it and its enemies so that it can escape.
